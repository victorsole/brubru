# Serbia's EU Accession and the Conditionality on War-Crimes Accountability

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (7-8 September 2026 — the Mladić funeral and the EU's response)**: **Ratko Mladić died on 27 August 2026** at The Hague, aged 84, while serving a life sentence. His remains were returned to Serbia and he was **buried in Belgrade on Monday 7 September 2026** with military and religious honours, **attended by senior Serbian ministers**. The **EEAS issued a Spokesperson Statement the same day**, and **President von der Leyen and High Representative Kaja Kallas both spoke publicly on 8 September**. The operative sentence of the EU statement is the one that touches accession: *"Any glorification of war criminals, genocide denial and revisionism contradict the most fundamental European values and **have no place in the EU or in EU candidate countries**."* Source: EEAS, 7 September 2026, https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/spokesperson-statement-death-ratko-mladic_en
- **What the EU statement asserts as fact**: Mladić was convicted of **war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity**; the **ICTY sentenced him to life imprisonment in 2017**, describing his crimes as among the most heinous known to humankind; he died serving that sentence for crimes against humanity and the **Srebrenica genocide**.
- **NOT decided, and must not be reported as decided**: on 4 September 2026 **MEPs** (Hannah Neumann, Saskia Bricmont and others) publicly called on the Commission to **freeze EU Growth Plan and IPA III payments**, cancel official visits to Belgrade, and condition accession progress on accountability. **The Commission has announced no freeze.** A call by MEPs is not a decision by the Commission or the Council.
- **Serbia's status: candidate country.** Potential candidate at the **Thessaloniki** European Council in **2003**; European partnership **2008**; application **2009**; **candidate status March 2012**; the **Stabilisation and Association Agreement entered into force in September 2013**; the European Council decided to open negotiations in **June 2013**; the Council adopted the **negotiating framework in December 2013**; the **1st Intergovernmental Conference on 21 January 2014** formally opened negotiations.
- **Where the negotiations actually stand: 22 of 35 chapters opened, of which only TWO are provisionally closed.** All chapters under **cluster 1 (the fundamentals)** and **cluster 4 (green agenda and sustainable connectivity)** are open. Cluster 4 opened in **December 2021**; the first political IGC under the **revised enlargement methodology** was held in **June 2021**. No new cluster has opened since.
- **Latest country assessment: the Serbia Report 2025, published 4 November 2025** by DG ENEST. Reports are annual; the 2026 report is the document to check before making any claim about current progress.
- **Who is responsible**: **Commissioner Marta Kos** (Enlargement), Directorate-General for **Enlargement and the Eastern Neighbourhood (DG ENEST)**, Director-General **Gert Jan Koopman**.

## Why war-crimes accountability is an accession question, not only a moral one

The revised enlargement methodology puts the **fundamentals cluster first**: it is opened first and
closed last, and progress in the other clusters depends on it. That cluster carries **Chapter 23
(judiciary and fundamental rights)** and **Chapter 24 (justice, freedom and security)**, together
with public administration reform, economic criteria and the functioning of democratic institutions.
Regional cooperation and good neighbourly relations sit alongside them as a standing condition of the
Stabilisation and Association Process.

The consequence is structural: **a candidate country cannot bank progress elsewhere while the
fundamentals stall.** Because Serbia has 22 chapters open and only two provisionally closed, the
process is already in the state the methodology is designed to produce — plenty opened, almost
nothing closed.

The EU's own framing on 7 September was in exactly these terms: glorification, genocide denial and
revisionism "have no place in the EU **or in EU candidate countries**". That is a values sentence
addressed at the accession relationship, not a sanction.

## The money, and what is actually at stake if conditionality bites

Two instruments were named in the MEPs' demands. Both have their own guides:

| Instrument | Guide | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Reform and Growth Facility for the Western Balkans** | `reform_and_growth_facility_western_balkans_moldova` | Payments are **reform-conditional by design**; this is the instrument where conditionality is operative rather than rhetorical |
| **IPA III pre-accession assistance** | `ipa_iii_pre_accession_assistance` | The main pre-accession funding channel |

Note that Serbia was also the subject of routine EU business in the same fortnight — the Commission
welcomed Serbia joining the **SEPA** schemes on 5 May 2026, and extended wildfire support on
28 August 2026 — so "the EU is freezing Serbia" is not an accurate description of the relationship.

## What to say when asked

- **"Has the EU frozen Serbia's funding?"** No. **MEPs called for it on 4 September 2026**; there is
  no Commission or Council decision. Say what was asked for, by whom, and that it was not granted.
- **"Has Serbia's accession been suspended?"** No. Serbia remains a **candidate country** with 22 of
  35 chapters open. Nothing in the September 2026 statements changes its formal status.
- **"What did the EU actually say?"** Quote the EEAS statement of 7 September 2026 and attribute the
  von der Leyen and Kallas remarks of 8 September to them individually. Do not merge the three into
  a single "EU position" — a spokesperson statement, a Commission President's remarks and a High
  Representative's remarks are three different instruments.
- **Never state a number of chapters closed other than TWO**, and never say "negotiations are
  advanced" — 22 opened with 2 provisionally closed is the opposite of advanced.
