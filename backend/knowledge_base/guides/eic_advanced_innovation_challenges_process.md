# EIC Advanced Innovation Challenges (AIC) — Pilot Process

## QUICK FACTS
- New 2026 pilot instrument modelled on **US ARPA** (Advanced Research Projects Agency) framework.
- Two-stage funding: **EUR 300,000 lump sum Stage 1** (max 9 months) → **up to EUR 2.5 million Stage 2** (max 30 months).
- 2026 pilot budget: **EUR 6 million** (small — pilot scale).
- 2026 Stage 1 deadline: **26 February 2026, 17:00 Brussels time**.
- 2027 Stage 2 indicative deadline: **18 June 2027, 17:00 Brussels time**.
- 2 Challenges launched in 2026 (designed by EIC Programme Managers):
  1. **Accelerating Physical AI**: Embodied Intelligence for the Next Frontier of AI-Powered Robotics
  2. **Translating Disruptive New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) into Practice**
- Designed for **research-strong but commercialisation-weak** sectors — moonshot demand-driven topics.
- Topic IDs: `HORIZON-EIC-2026-AIC-01` (Physical AI), `HORIZON-EIC-2026-AIC-02` (NAMs).

## Eligibility

- Single applicant: **start-up, SME, or research performing organisation** (RPO).
- Established in an EU Member State or Horizon Europe Associated Country.
- **Large companies cannot apply as single entities** (Stage 1).
- One concurrent proposal per applicant.

## The 2-stage process

### Stage 1 — Demonstration concept (EUR 300K lump sum, max 9 months)

- Submit at the call deadline (26 Feb 2026).
- Compact proposal (page limit set in topic page, typically 12-15 pages).
- Remote evaluation by ≥3 experts on Excellence / Impact / Implementation.
- ~3-4 months from deadline to results.
- Successful applicants run a **9-month proof-of-concept project** culminating in a deliverable (prototype, dataset, validation report).
- All Stage 1 beneficiaries automatically eligible for Stage 2.

### Stage 2 — Scale-up demonstration (up to EUR 2.5M lump sum, max 30 months)

- Open ONLY to Stage 1 beneficiaries.
- Indicative deadline: **18 June 2027**.
- Reuses + extends the Stage 1 deliverable into a near-market demonstration.
- Same Excellence / Impact / Implementation evaluation.
- Lump-sum payments tied to work-package completion.

## Evaluation criteria

The standard Horizon Europe **Excellence / Impact / Implementation** framework, with AIC-specific emphasis on:
- **Demand-driven nature**: the proposal must clearly map to a specific demand challenge identified by the Programme Manager.
- **Transformative potential**: not incremental — must enable a new category of capability.
- **Commercialisation pathway**: even at Stage 1, applicants describe how Stage 2 will mature toward market.

## What makes AIC different from Pathfinder Challenges

| Feature | AIC | Pathfinder Challenges |
|---|---|---|
| TRL range | demand-driven, can be 3-7 | strictly TRL 1-4 |
| Funding model | 2-stage (€300K → €2.5M) | single grant (up to €4M) |
| Lump sum | Yes | No (cost-based) |
| Single applicant | Yes | Yes (also small consortia) |
| Programme Manager role | Heavier — designs the challenge AND manages portfolios actively | Designs the challenge; less hands-on after |
| 2026 budget | EUR 6M (pilot) | EUR 96M |
| Origin | ARPA-inspired pilot | EIC core since 2021 |

## Role of EIC Programme Managers

AIC is the most **PM-driven** EIC instrument. The 8 EIC Programme Managers (deeptech industry experts on 4-year secondments) shape and steer the Challenges in collaboration with users + ecosystem actors. Their portfolios for 2026:

- Paolo Bondavalli — Advanced materials for energy
- Walter Eevers — Cleantech
- Carina Faber — Renewable energy conversion + alternative resources
- Hedi Karray — Artificial Intelligence (→ Physical AI Challenge)
- Isabel Obieta — Sustainable Semiconductors
- Gemma Pham — Biotechnology
- Orsolya Symmons — Health and Biotechnology (→ NAMs Challenge)
- Federica Zanca — MedTech and AI in healthcare

## Stage 2 graduation criteria

Stage 1 → Stage 2 requires:
- Completed Stage 1 deliverable on time and to spec
- Demonstrated technical milestone (defined in Stage 1 GA)
- Updated commercialisation plan
- Resubmission of Part B with Stage 2 work plan
