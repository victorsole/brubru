# ELA: European Labour Authority

## QUICK FACTS

- **Full name**: European Labour Authority (ELA).
- **Seat**: Bratislava, Slovakia (permanent seat since 1 September 2021; premises formally inaugurated November 2021).
- **Legal basis**: Regulation (EU) 2019/1149 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 June 2019 establishing a European Labour Authority (CELEX 32019R1149).
- **Type**: EU decentralised (regulatory) agency, established under the ordinary legislative procedure.
- **Founded / operational timeline**: Regulation adopted 20 June 2019; ELA began activities 17 October 2019; relocated to its Bratislava seat September 2021; reached its target of roughly €50 million annual budget and around 140 staff by 2024 (full operational capacity).
- **Executive Director**: Cosmin Boiangiu (verify current status before citing as a live fact; the ELA governance page is the authoritative source).
- **Governance**: Management Board (strategic orientation, budget and work programme approval), Executive Director (legal representative, day-to-day management), Stakeholder Group (advisory body of social partners and civil society giving opinions on the draft work programme).
- **Sponsoring Commissioner**: portfolio sits under the Commissioner for Jobs and Social Rights (Nicolas Schmit held this portfolio at ELA's 2021 inauguration; verify current Commissioner before citing).
- **Geographic scope**: EU-27 plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway (EEA/EFTA participation in labour-mobility strands).
- **Five core topic areas**: (1) posting of workers, (2) social security coordination, (3) free movement of workers, (4) road transport labour rules, (5) tackling undeclared work.
- **Core tasks**: information provision to workers, employers and social partners on labour-mobility rights and obligations; coordination of the EURES network for cross-border job matching; support to national labour inspectorates including concerted and joint cross-border inspections; mediation between Member State authorities in cross-border disputes; analysis and risk assessment of cross-border labour-mobility issues; capacity-building and knowledge-sharing among national enforcement bodies.
- **Undeclared work**: the former European Platform Tackling Undeclared Work was integrated into ELA's structure and continues its cooperation and knowledge-exchange function under the ELA umbrella.
- **What ELA does NOT do**: it has no direct sanctioning power over employers or Member States; enforcement action remains with national labour inspectorates and competent authorities. ELA supports, coordinates and mediates rather than adjudicates.
- **Related EU frameworks**: Posting of Workers Directive (96/71/EC as amended by Directive (EU) 2018/957) and the Enforcement Directive (2014/67/EU); social security coordination Regulations (EC) 883/2004 and 987/2009; the EU Talent Pool Regulation (EU) 2026/1047 designates ELA as the body operating the Talent Pool IT platform on the Commission's behalf.

## What the European Labour Authority does

ELA was proposed by Commission President Jean-Claude Juncker in his September 2017 State of the Union address as part of the European Pillar of Social Rights follow-up, with the Commission tabling its first draft regulation on 13 February 2018. The agency exists to help ensure that EU rules on labour mobility and social security coordination are enforced fairly, effectively and consistently across Member States, and to support both individuals and employers who operate across EU borders.

Unlike agencies that regulate a single market or product (EMA for medicines, ESMA for securities), ELA is a coordination and support agency: it does not create new law and does not have inspection or sanctioning powers of its own. Its value lies in bringing together national labour inspectorates, social security institutions and public employment services that would otherwise operate in isolation on cross-border cases.

### Information services
ELA is the operational layer behind labour-mobility information provided through the EU's Your Europe portal and the EURES job-mobility portal, giving workers, employers and social partners a single point of access to rights and obligations when working, posting staff or hiring across borders.

### EURES coordination
ELA hosts and coordinates the EURES network (European Employment Services), which links national public employment services, trade unions and employer organisations to facilitate cross-border recruitment and job matching.

### Support to national labour inspectorates
ELA supports Member States in conducting concerted and joint cross-border labour inspections, helping national inspectorates pool resources and expertise on cases that cross national jurisdiction (for example, posted-worker fraud or road-transport rule breaches involving hauliers registered in one Member State but operating in several).

### Mediation
Where Member States disagree on the application or interpretation of EU labour-mobility or social security coordination rules in a cross-border case, ELA can provide a mediation mechanism to help reach a resolution without recourse to formal dispute settlement.

### Tackling undeclared work
The European Platform Tackling Undeclared Work, originally a separate Commission initiative, was folded into ELA. It continues to support Member States in preventing and deterring undeclared work through peer learning, mutual assistance and awareness campaigns.

### Analysis and risk assessment
ELA produces analysis and risk assessments on cross-border labour-mobility patterns, feeding evidence back to the Commission and Member States to target enforcement resources.

## Why ELA matters for Brubru users

Posted workers, cross-border recruitment and social security coordination are recurring sources of compliance risk for any organisation with staff or contractors moving between Member States. ELA sits at the operational centre of several frameworks that Brubru already tracks:

- **Posting of workers**: employers posting staff to another Member State must comply with the host state's core terms of employment (Directive 96/71/EC as amended); ELA supports enforcement coordination between the sending and receiving Member State authorities.
- **Social security coordination**: cross-border workers and their employers rely on Regulations (EC) 883/2004 and 987/2009 (A1 certificates, single applicable legislation rule); ELA supports Member State cooperation on contested cases.
- **EU Talent Pool**: Regulation (EU) 2026/1047 designates ELA as the body operating the EU-level IT platform matching third-country jobseekers with EU employer vacancies in shortage occupations (see Brubru guide `eu_talent_pool_regulation_2026`).
- **Employer sanctions for irregular migrant employment**: enforcement against employers of irregularly staying third-country nationals sits with national authorities under the Employer Sanctions Directive framework; ELA's cross-border inspection support is a complementary enforcement layer (see Brubru guide `eu_migrant_workers_employer_sanctions`).

## Brubru user questions addressed

- "What is ELA?" -> An EU decentralised agency based in Bratislava, established by Regulation (EU) 2019/1149, that supports fair and effective labour mobility across the EU and helps coordinate enforcement of social security coordination rules.
- "Can ELA fine my company?" -> No. ELA has no direct sanctioning power; enforcement against employers remains with national labour inspectorates and competent authorities in the relevant Member States.
- "Does ELA replace national labour inspectorates?" -> No. It supports and coordinates them, including through concerted and joint cross-border inspections, but does not replace national enforcement competence.
- "Is the undeclared work platform still separate from ELA?" -> No. The former European Platform Tackling Undeclared Work has been integrated into ELA and operates as one of its core functions.
- "Who operates the EU Talent Pool platform?" -> ELA, on behalf of the European Commission, under Regulation (EU) 2026/1047.
- "Where is ELA based?" -> Bratislava, Slovakia, its permanent seat since September 2021.
