# European Democracy Shield and the EUDS special committee

## QUICK FACTS

- **LATEST (Tuesday 15 September 2026): Parliament adopted the report of the special committee on the European Democracy Shield, by 420 votes to 220 with 26 abstentions.** It is an **own-initiative report with recommendations, not legislation**: it binds nobody and creates no obligation. Rapporteur **Tomas Tobé (EPP, Sweden)**. Source: European Parliament press release 20260911IPR47453, plenary of 15 September 2026, Strasbourg.
- **The committee**: the special committee on the European Democracy Shield (**EUDS**) was set up in **December 2024** to assess hostile activities targeting the EU, specifically **foreign information manipulation and interference (FIMI)**, hybrid attacks, and coordinated disinformation campaigns.
- **Core diagnosis**: MEPs argue the EU's approach suffers from **fragmentation**, because individual Member States cannot effectively counter these threats alone. They describe foreign interference and disinformation as an attack on the "very essence of the European project", with citizens as the primary victims.
- **Named threat actors**: **Russia is identified as the primary external threat**; **Belarus, China, Iran and North Korea** are also named as active.
- **Do not confuse two things**: the **Commission's Democracy Shield strategy** (an announced Commission initiative) and this **Parliament special-committee report** responding to it. They are separate documents from separate institutions.

## What Parliament recommends

### A new EU body
- Support for creating the **EU centre for democratic resilience**, as announced by the Commission in its Democracy Shield strategy and in line with proposals Parliament tabled in the previous term.
- It should function as an independent **"centre of excellence"** against FIMI and disinformation, coordinating national actions.
- It must **not duplicate existing tools**: instead it should consolidate ongoing EU-level actions into a single entity with a clear vision, mandate, budget and capacity to act.
- **Participation should be mandatory for all Member States**, with parliamentary oversight ensured.

### Digital interference: enforce, do not legislate
- MEPs demand **rigorous enforcement of existing digital rules**, naming the **Digital Services Act** and the **AI Act**, **rather than new legislation**. This is the report's most quotable position and is frequently misreported as a call for new rules.
- Platforms should be held accountable and do more against **bot-driven election interference**.
- The report states that **freedom of expression and information are rights that protect human beings, not machines**.

### Sanctions and hybrid attacks
- Russia's frequent hybrid attacks on critical infrastructure are listed as **cyberattacks, physical sabotage, arson, espionage and signal jamming**.
- MEPs want **expanded sanctions** on the enablers of disinformation and on those helping circumvent existing sanctions.
- The report **condemns Russia's politically motivated "blacklisting"** of EU officials, journalists and MEPs as an instrument of hybrid warfare.

### EU technological sovereignty
- Reduce reliance on **foreign-controlled technology in critical sectors**, specifically naming **US tech companies and Chinese hardware**.
- A **balanced "buy European" strategy** when investing in critical infrastructure.
- Diversification to more reliable partners for **critical raw materials**.

### Electoral resilience
- Classify **electoral infrastructure as critical** infrastructure.
- A **"know your donor" principle for cryptocurrency-based political donations**.
- Measures against **malicious deepfakes and fraudulent ads**.
- **Increased protection for female candidates**.
- Cooperation to improve resilience in **candidate and partner countries**.
- Condemns the **instrumentalisation of migration** as political pressure on the EU.

### Media freedom and civil society
- Strengthen **media and AI literacy**, alongside a robust and independent civil society.
- **Long-term funding commitments** for media freedom and investigative journalism.
- **Safeguards against illegal spyware**.
- Support for civic participation.

### Societal preparedness
- Democratic resilience requires society to keep functioning in crises.
- **Regular large-scale exercises**.
- An **EU-wide crisis alert app**.
- An annual **"European Preparedness Day" on 24 February**, the anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

## Rapporteur's framing

Tomas Tobé in the plenary debate: "This report is not about regulating political opinions. It is about protecting our democracy from foreign influence operations. Russian bots are not free speech. Foreign interference is not free speech. And manipulating our elections is certainly not free speech."
