# EU Private Credit: Market Structure, Financial Stability and Policy Implications

## QUICK FACTS

- **Anchor publication (11 May 2026)**: **EPRS Briefing — "Private credit: market structure, recent developments, financial stability concerns, and policy implications"** (Ref: EPRS Briefing 11 May 2026, EP Think Tank). The briefing maps the EU private-credit market for ECON committee and policy stakeholders: market size, growth trajectory since 2015, structural shift from bank syndicated lending to non-bank direct lending, the emergence of evergreen / semi-liquid private-credit vehicles aimed at retail and high-net-worth distribution, and the regulatory toolkit gaps that follow.
- **What is private credit**: Non-publicly-traded debt provided by non-bank lenders (private credit funds, BDCs, insurance-affiliated lenders) directly to borrowers — typically mid-market companies, private-equity-backed leveraged buyouts, real-estate sponsors and infrastructure projects — outside the syndicated bank-loan and public-bond markets. Distinguishes from "public credit" (rated bonds, leveraged loans traded in the secondary market).
- **Market scale (global, ~2025-2026 vintage)**: Estimated USD 1.7-2.0 trillion globally in private-credit assets under management; Europe ≈USD 350-450 billion (~25% of global). Doubled since 2019. Growth driven by (a) post-GFC bank deleveraging (Basel III / CRR-CRD capital costs), (b) borrower demand for flexible structures, (c) institutional-investor reach for yield in a multi-decade low-rate environment (now rotating to a higher-rate environment that still favours floating-rate private credit).
- **EU-specific market structure**: France, Germany, Netherlands and Luxembourg host the largest private-credit fund domiciles; the **AIFMD** (Directive 2011/61/EU, CELEX 32011L0061) governs alternative investment fund managers; the **ELTIF Regulation** (Regulation (EU) 2015/760 as amended by ELTIF 2.0 Regulation (EU) 2023/606, CELEX 32023R0606) enables retail-eligible long-term investment funds; the **AIFMD II revision** (Directive (EU) 2024/927, CELEX 32024L0927) tightens liquidity stress-testing, leverage limits and loan-origination rules for credit-granting AIFs.
- **Key recent EU developments**:
    - **AIFMD II** (Directive (EU) 2024/927, OJ L 26.3.2024) — first time loan-origination funds are subject to a harmonised EU prudential framework. Transposition deadline: 16 April 2026. Implementing technical standards from ESMA.
    - **ELTIF 2.0** (Regulation (EU) 2023/606) — broadened the scope of eligible assets and reduced retail-investor entry barriers. Combined with AIFMD II, the EU is the most-regulated private-credit jurisdiction globally.
    - **MMF Regulation Commission Notice C/2026/02641** (13 May 2026, CELEX 52026XC02641) — clarifies portfolio rules for money-market funds, indirectly relevant where private-credit vehicles use MMF cash sleeves.
    - **DORA** (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, CELEX 32022R2554) — operational resilience requirements extend to private-credit fund managers and their ICT providers from 17 January 2025.
- **Financial stability concerns flagged by EPRS, ECB, IMF, FSB**:
    - **Valuation opacity**: private loans are mark-to-model, not mark-to-market. Stress scenarios harder to gauge.
    - **Liquidity mismatch**: evergreen / semi-liquid retail vehicles offer monthly or quarterly redemptions while underlying loans are 5-7 year illiquid. AIFMD II mandates new liquidity stress tests but does not eliminate the mismatch.
    - **Concentration**: top 5 global private-credit managers hold a disproportionate share of EU AUM; failure of one creates contagion risk.
    - **Bank-NBFI interconnections**: banks finance private-credit funds via subscription-line facilities; defaults could transmit losses back into bank balance sheets.
    - **Retailisation**: ELTIF 2.0 + AIFMD II open retail access; retail-investor protection adequacy is an open question.
- **Regulators / policy actors**:
    - **ESMA** — supervisory convergence on AIFMD II implementation, peer reviews of loan-originating fund managers
    - **EBA** — bank exposure to private-credit funds (subscription lines, fund finance, equity stakes)
    - **EIOPA** — insurance-undertaking allocations to private-credit (Solvency II framework)
    - **ECB Financial Stability Review** — semi-annual coverage since 2023
    - **ESRB** — systemic-risk macroprudential framework, NBFI working group
    - **DG FISMA + DG ECFIN** — Commission lead on AIFMD II / ELTIF 2.0 + Savings and Investments Union legislative pipeline
- **EP ECON committee work**: monetary dialogues regularly address NBFI; the 11 May EPRS Briefing is the dedicated standalone primer. Combined with the 13 May EPRS Briefings on Monetary Dialogue Dec 2026 + Feb 2026 summaries, ECON is mapping NBFI risks ahead of any new legislative initiative.

## What is private credit

Private credit refers to debt financing that is **not raised through public markets**: not via syndicated bank loans traded in the secondary market, not via publicly listed corporate bonds, not via leveraged-loan CLOs. Instead, the lender holds the loan to maturity (or sells it bilaterally to another private investor) and receives interest payments directly from the borrower. Loans are typically floating-rate (linked to EURIBOR), senior secured or unitranche, and bilaterally negotiated.

The dominant private-credit strategies in Europe:

1. **Direct lending** — middle-market corporate loans (EUR 25M-1bn ticket size) for private-equity-backed acquisitions and growth financings.
2. **Distressed debt** — purchase of non-performing loans (NPLs) from bank balance sheets or from secondary markets at a discount.
3. **Real-estate debt** — bridge loans, development finance, CRE refinancing for property sponsors.
4. **Infrastructure debt** — long-duration senior loans for renewables, transport, social infrastructure.
5. **Specialty finance** — fund finance (subscription lines), asset-based lending, royalties.

## Why this matters for EU policy professionals

Private credit sits at the intersection of three live EU policy tracks:

1. **Savings and Investments Union (SIU)** — Commission's flagship 2026-2027 priority on retail savings mobilisation. Private credit (via ELTIF 2.0) is one of the retail-eligible long-term investment vehicles. Trade-off: retail access vs investor-protection adequacy.
2. **NBFI macroprudential framework** — the ESRB and Commission are progressing a system-wide leverage and liquidity framework for non-bank financial intermediaries. Private credit is a major NBFI segment.
3. **Bank capital and CRR-CRD reviews** — Basel III / CRR3 implementation continues to shape the bank-vs-non-bank loan-origination split. The more capital banks must hold against loans, the more borrower demand migrates to private credit.
