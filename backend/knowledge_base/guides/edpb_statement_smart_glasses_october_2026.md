# EDPB statement on smart glasses and the report "The connected gaze" (13 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What:** at its latest plenary the **European Data Protection Board (EDPB)** adopted a **statement on smart glasses** and published the report **"The connected gaze. Video recording, surveillance and administering evidence in the age of smart glasses"** (EDPB news of **13 October 2026**). The report was commissioned through the Board's **Support Pool of Experts** programme on a proposal from the **French data protection authority**, and written by a digital sociologist from interviews with users and non-users.
- **Status:** the statement is an **initial statement with preliminary guidance** for users, manufacturers and service providers and a call to raise awareness. It is **not a binding act** and sets no new legal rule.
- **Who is responsible (GDPR):** a user is a **data controller** where, for example, they share photos or video on a public social media account, or use the glasses in **official, professional or political activities**. Recording **family or friends**, or using the device for personal translation in a private context, is a **purely personal activity** outside the GDPR (the household exemption), though **criminal, civil, labour or sector-specific law may still apply**.
- **Producers and providers:** a producer or the provider of a linked application or service **cannot transfer its obligations to users**; as controllers they must comply with data protection law and keep any new processing **compatible with the original purpose of collection**.
- **Transparency is the main challenge:** because smart glasses look like ordinary glasses with cameras and microphones connected to the internet, bystanders may not know they are recorded, by whom or why. The EDPB says recording should be obvious, for instance with **bright LED lights, audio signals or voice-activation cues, privacy-friendly smartphone notifications or distinctive designs**, and that warnings must be reliable, hard to ignore and kept up to date, including in public spaces. Risks are greater in **hospitals, schools and workplaces** and for **children**. It encourages **industry standards** and awareness measures.
- **Quote:** EDPB Chair **Anu Talus**: "Smart glasses should make their recording obvious."
- **Not in the statement:** no fines, no ban, no product named, no new obligation beyond the GDPR as applied.

## How to answer
"Are smart glasses allowed under the GDPR?": not banned; the GDPR applies depending on use (public or professional use makes the user a controller; private use is exempt); manufacturers stay responsible and should make recording visible. This is guidance, not a ruling.
