# Court of Justice, Case C-108/26 RX: an air traffic management decision is not in itself an "extraordinary circumstance" (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Judgment of 8 October 2026**, Case **C-108/26 RX | D (Air traffic management decision)**, Court of Justice (Court press release No 144/26). The suffix RX marks a **review** case: the Court partially reviewed a judgment of the General Court.
- **The rule (Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 on air passenger rights, CELEX 32004R0261):** an air traffic management decision does **NOT**, in itself, make an "extraordinary circumstance" that frees the carrier from paying compensation for a delay. The carrier must identify the **event that led to** the decision and show, **case by case**, that the event by its nature or origin is not inherent in the normal exercise of the carrier's activity and is beyond its actual control. It must demonstrate that the decision was taken because of a circumstance that was itself extraordinary.
- **What the General Court had said (judgment of 21 January 2026):** the decision itself, not its reasons, is decisive and it may be an extraordinary circumstance. The Court of Justice found that this interpretation harms the unity and consistency of EU law and **set that judgment aside** to the extent reviewed.
- **First time:** the press release states that for the first time the Court of Justice sets aside a judgment given by the General Court in a preliminary ruling. The General Court has jurisdiction for preliminary rulings in six areas only (VAT, excise duties, the Customs Code, tariff classification, passenger compensation and assistance for denied boarding, delay or cancellation, and emissions trading); the Court of Justice handles all others.
- **Facts:** July 2023, Izmir (Turkiye) to Warsaw, arrival more than three hours late. The aircraft's earlier flight (Katowice to Izmir) had been delayed by an air traffic management decision linked to **airspace restrictions in Hungary and Romania due to adverse weather**; after landing in Izmir a technical fault took more than two hours to repair. A specialised company holding the passenger's assigned claim asked the carrier for EUR 400.
- **Adverse weather:** may be an extraordinary circumstance when severe enough to prevent the flight being operated safely and on the planned schedule. The national court (in Poland) must check whether that was so.
- **Counting the delay:** an extraordinary circumstance can count even if alone it did not cause three hours of delay. Where the delay comes from such an event and another cause, the delay due to the extraordinary circumstance is **deducted** from the total to decide whether the three-hour threshold for compensation is reached.
