## Overview

The AI Continent Action Plan is the EU's comprehensive strategy to become a global leader in artificial intelligence. Adopted on 9 April 2025, it transforms Europe's approach from primarily regulating AI (via the AI Act) to actively building AI capacity, infrastructure, and talent.

The plan builds on six pillars: compute infrastructure (AI Factories and Gigafactories), data access (Data Union Strategy), talent development (AI Skills Academy), sectoral adoption (Apply AI Strategy), cloud capacity (Cloud and AI Development Act), and regulatory support (AI Act Service Desk).

## AI Factories

AI Factories are open, dynamic AI ecosystems formed around the public network of EuroHPC supercomputers. They consolidate:
- **Computing capacity** from EuroHPC systems
- **Data** from Common European Data Spaces
- **Talent** from universities, startups, SMEs, and industry

AI Factories serve as centralised hubs for collaboration across sectors including healthcare, manufacturing, climate, and public administration.

### AI Gigafactories

The next evolution: high-capacity AI infrastructure hubs with significantly greater compute power than current AI Factories, integrated data resources, and automation. Designed for frontier AI model development.

- **Call for interest:** Open, deadline 20 June 2025 (17:00 CET)
- **Consultation workshop:** 22 May 2025
- **Submissions to:** gigafactories@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu
- **Who can apply:** Industry, public/private investors, EU Participating States, consortia
- **Non-binding:** No financial commitment at this stage

The JUPITER exascale supercomputer in Julich, Germany is cited as a model: ranked among the top 3 globally while being "the greenest supercomputer in the world."

## InvestAI

EUR 200 billion mobilisation for AI investment, announced February 2025. Includes:
- **InvestAI Facility:** EUR 20 billion specifically for AI Gigafactories
- Public and private investment combined

## Cloud and AI Development Act

**Public consultation:** 9 April to 3 July 2025.

Key targets:
- **Triple EU data centre capacity** within 5-7 years
- **Fully meet** business and public administration cloud needs by 2035
- Remove barriers to data centre expansion
- Accelerate permitting for sustainable operators
- Expand highly secure EU-based cloud options for critical use cases
- Ensure data centres reach climate neutrality and high efficiency by 2030

This will be a legislative proposal (part of the Tech Sovereignty Package expected 27 May 2026 per College agenda).

## Apply AI Strategy

**Public consultation:** 9 April to 4 June 2025.

Blueprint for AI adoption across 13 EU strategic sectors:
1. Advanced manufacturing
2. Aerospace
3. Defence
4. Agriculture
5. Energy
6. Pharmaceutical
7. Robotics
8. Creative industries
9. Healthcare
10. Public administration
11. Financial services
12. Transport
13. Education

The consultation also gathers feedback on AI Act implementation challenges to inform guidance documents, self-assessment tools, and obligation templates.

## AI Act Service Desk

A central information hub for AI Act compliance:
- Stakeholders can ask for help and receive tailor-made answers
- Will be supported by the Apply AI consultation feedback
- Part of broader digital legislation simplification effort

## European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIHs)

EDIHs serve as one-stop shops supporting companies and public sector to adopt AI:
- Technical expertise and testing capabilities
- Financing advice and training
- Collaborate with AI Factories, Testing and Experimentation Facilities, and regulatory sandboxes
- Network across all EU Member States

## Data Labs

New initiative to aggregate and organise data from multiple sources:
- Access data from Common European Data Spaces participants
- AI Factories may offer: dataset cleaning, enrichment with synthetic data, anonymisation, competition-law-compliant data pooling

## European Data Union Strategy

Expected H2 2025. Building on the Data Act and Data Governance Act:
- Simplify legal frameworks for large-scale data sharing
- Maintain privacy and security standards
- Enable data availability for AI and innovation

## The data centre capacity target, anchored

The commitment to triple EU data centre capacity is reported with a horizon of **2035**. Cite it that way rather than as an open-ended ambition, and date the citation, because a target without a horizon rots into a claim nobody can check.

For the base it triples from: EU installed IT power grew from **10,539 MW in 2023 to 14,784 MW in 2025**, and roughly **600 of the ~2,800** recorded EU facilities are not yet operational. Full detail, including the reporting duty and the constraint that growth is now limited by power availability, grid readiness and permitting rather than capital, is in `eu_data_centres_energy_sustainability`.
