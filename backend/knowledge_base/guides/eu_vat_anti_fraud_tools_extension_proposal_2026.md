# VAT anti-fraud tools: Commission proposal to extend the reverse charge and quick reaction mechanisms to 30 June 2030

## QUICK FACTS
- **What was proposed:** a Commission proposal **adopted on 1 October 2026** (announced by DG Taxation and Customs Union on **9 October 2026**) to **extend until 30 June 2030** two optional VAT anti-fraud tools:
  1. the **optional Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)**: in a number of pre-defined fraud-sensitive sectors Member States may shift the responsibility for paying VAT from the seller to the buyer (the BUYER accounts for the VAT, not the seller), which makes certain types of fraud much harder;
  2. the **Quick Reaction Mechanism (QRM)**: under strict conditions Member States may also apply the reverse charge in other sectors where sudden and massive fraud is detected.
- **Why:** the Commission says VAT fraud is still a serious problem causing Member States to lose public money, and the tools are used mostly against **missing trader fraud**. The two tools were, in the Commission's words, "due to expire on 30 June 2026"; the proposal keeps them available for four more years while the broader long-term VAT reform is completed. It responds to calls from Member States, businesses and the European Parliament.
- **Link to VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA):** the Commission says the proposal helps countries keep tackling fraud in high-risk sectors "while preparing for new digital VAT reporting rules under the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) reform measures".
- **Status:** a Commission **proposal**, not an adopted act: it still has to be agreed by the Council (which normally acts unanimously on VAT, Article 113 TFEU) with Parliament consulted. **The document:** Proposal for a Council Directive amending Directive 2006/112/EC (the VAT Directive) as regards the extension of the application period of the optional reverse charge mechanism and of the Quick Reaction Mechanism against VAT fraud, **COM(2026) 515 final, CELEX 52026PC0515** (title read at Cellar). The interinstitutional procedure number was not read: give none.

## How to answer
Describe it as a proposal to prolong two optional tools to 30 June 2030, say it is not yet law, and explain RCM and QRM in one sentence each.
