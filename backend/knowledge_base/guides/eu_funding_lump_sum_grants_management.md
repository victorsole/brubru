# How to Manage Your Lump Sum Grants

## QUICK FACTS

- Lump sum grants have a fixed grant amount, paid out when your project is implemented as described in the grant agreement (no cost reporting required)
- Three types: Type 1a (pre-fixed total for all projects in a call), Type 1b (pre-fixed building block amounts per activity), Type 2 (project-based amount determined by individual budget)
- Used in Horizon Europe (increasing share), EIC Accelerator grant track, EIC Transition, EIC AIC, and other EU programmes
- Simpler to manage than actual costs grants; require less administration and no detailed financial records
- Payment triggers when work packages are completed, not on cost submission
- You submit a detailed budget table at proposal stage (Type 2) to justify the lump sum amount
- The calculator/detailed budget table becomes part of your proposal but NOT part of the grant agreement
- Grant agreement includes the Model Grant Agreement for Lump Sum (LS-MGA) and Annex 2 (breakdown of lump sum by beneficiary and work package)
- Technical reporting focuses on completion of work packages and deliverables, not actual costs incurred
- Periodic reports are simplified; the consolidated financial statement is automatically generated
- No requirement to keep detailed financial records for the project
- Audit and controls focus on proper implementation of the work plan and compliance with non-financial obligations
- Indirect costs ineligible if a beneficiary signs a new operating grant (pro-rata temporis deduction applies)
- Work package completion criteria: all essential work completed, equivalent work carried out, or deviations duly justified

## What lump sum funding means (versus actual costs)

Unlike actual costs grants, lump sum grants provide a fixed amount of funding regardless of what you actually spend. The payment is based on completing work packages as described in your grant agreement, not on submitting receipts or detailed cost claims.

This simplification has several practical effects:

- You do not submit cost claims or certificates on financial statements
- You do not need to keep detailed financial records for the project
- No financial audits or reviews of your actual spending occur; audits focus on technical implementation instead
- The granting authority assumes you will spend in accordance with the budget breakdown set out in Annex 2, even if the real distribution differs
- Once the lump sum is fixed in the grant agreement, it will not be questioned even if prices for goods or services change later

The guiding principle is that lump sum funding follows the standard rules and processes for actual cost grants, except for the financial reporting aspects.

## Proposal stage: the detailed budget table

When submitting a Type 2 lump sum proposal (where the amount is project-based), you must provide a detailed budget table. Depending on the programme and call, this may be a separate Excel file or an online form in Part A of the submission system.

In the detailed budget table, you must provide realistic cost estimations for each cost category:

- Personnel costs
- Subcontracting costs
- Purchase costs
- Other cost categories

The estimations must be broken down per beneficiary and affiliated entity, and per work package. These cost estimations form the basis for your proposed lump sum and will be added up to define the total lump sum amount.

Your cost estimations must:

- Be in line with eligibility rules for actual costs grants (see Article 6 of the Annotated Grant Agreement)
- Reflect your normal practices
- Be reasonable and not excessive
- Be necessary for the activities proposed

If your organisation uses Horizon Europe approved personnel unit costs, you must use the approved daily rate to estimate personnel costs. Calculate the monthly rate as: Monthly rate = Approved daily rate times (215 divided by 12).

For Horizon Europe, evaluators will have access to a personnel cost dashboard showing reference data for personnel costs by country and organisation type. If your personnel costs are high, especially above the dashboard values, you should justify them in the comment tab of your detailed budget table.

The calculator or detailed budget table will NOT become part of the grant agreement and will not be updated after the grant is signed.

## Grant agreement: the LS-MGA and Annex 2 (estimated budget equals lump sum)

After your proposal is selected, the Grant Agreement Preparation phase begins. The granting authority will use your detailed budget table (for Type 2) or your cost estimations to establish the final lump sum amount, taking into account any evaluation panel recommendations or required adjustments.

Following the evaluation results, the overall lump sum is fixed in the grant agreement. The breakdown of lump sum shares per beneficiary and per work package is included in Annex 2 of the grant agreement.

The grant agreement is based on the Model Grant Agreement for Lump Sum (LS-MGA), which incorporates standard provisions on duration, eligible costs, budget, forms of funding, reporting, and payment. The lump sum is calculated based on cost estimations and will not be questioned after signature, even if actual costs change.

Once the lump sum is fixed, the consortium is free to spend the total amount as needed, provided the project is carried out as described in the grant agreement. However, for accounting and management purposes, the granting authority assumes the distribution follows Annex 2. If there are substantial differences between the budget in Annex 2 and the real implementation, you may wish to formalise changes via an amendment.

## Reporting and payment (work package completion equals payment trigger)

Payment of the lump sum is triggered by the completion of work packages, not by cost submissions. At the end of each reporting period, you must submit a technical and financial report via the Grant Management System.

The technical report should focus on the completion of work packages and must:

- Detail who did what at the level of participating organisations (not individual staff)
- Explain and justify why each work package is marked as completed or not completed
- Describe contributions from beneficiaries, affiliated entities, associated partners, and subcontractors

The financial report is simplified and largely automated. The consolidated financial statement is automatically generated based on the status of work packages and the lump sum shares in Annex 2.

You do not need to submit:

- Cost claims or certificates on financial statements
- A "use of resources" report
- Any actual cost documentation

### Payment schedule

Payments follow the standard schedule for EU grants:

1. Pre-financing payment: a percentage of the total grant (usually 50% for Horizon Europe), paid within 30 days of signature. The coordinator distributes this among beneficiaries according to the consortium agreement.

2. Interim payments: pay the lump sum shares for work packages completed during the reporting period and accepted by the project officer. These are paid after the periodic report is assessed.

3. Payment of the balance: pays remaining lump sum shares for the implementation of the action, including any partial payments for partially completed work packages at the final report stage.

For programmes with a Mutual Insurance Mechanism (such as Horizon Europe), a small percentage of the lump sum is retained as a contribution to the Mechanism.

## Periodic reports and final report (technical content focus)

At the end of each reporting period, you submit a periodic technical and financial report. The technical report is the key document; the financial report is automated.

Your technical report should:

- Detail the activities carried out and who carried them out (per participating organisation)
- Explain the status of each work package and justify completion or non-completion
- Describe any deviations from the work plan and the reasons for them
- Confirm receipt and acceptance of deliverables
- Indicate milestones reached

You are encouraged to use the standard technical periodic report template available in the Grant Management System. Do not include tables with information on individual costs (these are not required in lump sum grants).

The financial report is generated automatically by the system based on the work package completion status and the lump sum shares in Annex 2. You do not need to provide financial data.

At the final report stage, you also have the option to declare work packages as partially completed if essential work was not carried out and not replaced with equivalent work. In this case, indicate the percentage of completion, which should correspond to the share of activities described in the grant agreement that have been carried out. The percentage is not linked to cost expenditure.

## Audit and controls under lump sum

The rules for checks, reviews, audits, and investigations are the same as for actual costs grants, except that there are no financial audits or reviews related to actual costs or resources used.

Controls focus on:

- Proper implementation of the work plan and the activities described
- Compliance with non-financial obligations, such as rules on intellectual property, ethics and integrity, and visibility of EU funding
- Achievement of project milestones and deliverables

In some programmes such as Horizon Europe, additional in-depth ex-post technical reviews are conducted to verify the proper implementation of completed work packages. These reviews:

- Take place after an interim or final payment, launched up to 2, 3, or 5 years after the payment of the balance
- Focus on the work of a single participant for which lump sum contributions were paid at the end of a given reporting period
- Analyse supporting documents that prove proper implementation (such as lab books, technical documents, prototypes, conference proceedings, and publications)
- May include interviews with participant contacts and key staff
- May include on-site visits if necessary

You have no contractual obligation to keep financial records for the project. You must keep documentation to demonstrate that the activities in Annex 1 have been carried out and by whom. This is the same technical documentation as required for all grants.

## When the lump sum is reduced (work package not completed)

If a work package is not completed as described in the grant agreement, the lump sum share associated with that work package may be reduced or rejected.

Work packages should be completed as described in Annex 1. However, a work package can be declared as completed if some elements are missing, provided that:

- All essential work has been completed, or
- Equivalent work has been carried out, or
- Deviations have been duly justified

If you cannot complete a work package as described due to technical reasons, you can:

- Report the deviation in the technical report and request acceptance; if duly justified, the deviation can be accepted along with the report, or
- Introduce an amendment to the grant agreement to propose a feasible alternative based on equivalent work

At the final report stage, you may declare a work package as partially completed. In this case, the lump sum share is paid partially, in line with the accepted degree of completion. For example, if a work package is accepted at 75% completion, the lump sum share is reduced proportionally.

Acceptable reasons for partial completion include:

- The underlying method does not work as planned, and no feasible alternative is available despite best efforts
- Key staff members are no longer able to work on the project and cannot be replaced in a reasonable timeframe
- Supply problems prevent obtaining essential consumables, and alternative methods do not work
- Force majeure such as fire, flood, or other unforeseeable events prevents completion

Unacceptable reasons for partial completion include:

- Insufficient time management or lack of effort to address delays
- Project became more expensive than budgeted, and the consortium stopped work without reporting or seeking an amendment
- Lack of justification for deviations from the work plan
- Willful non-compliance or failure to follow agreed methods

Before rejecting a work package, the project officer will give you the opportunity to provide additional information or justify the completion claim.
