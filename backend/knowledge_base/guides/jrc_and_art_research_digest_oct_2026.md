# JRC and Council ART Research Papers, 30 September to 7 October 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **What this is:** a dated digest of new science-for-policy papers from the **Joint Research Centre (JRC, the Commission's in-house science service)** and the **Analysis and Research Team (ART) of the Council's General Secretariat**, read from the publishers' own record pages on 7 October 2026. The European Parliament's STOA published nothing between 30 September and 7 October (newest item 28 September). These are research papers, **NOT Commission or Council positions**.
- **JRC148453, 30 Sep 2026, "Reducing dependencies on Critical Raw Materials with advanced materials"** (dissemination, doi 10.2760/6087861): identifies research and innovation needs to substitute critical raw materials with advanced materials; the analyses "contribute to the knowledge base for the **Advanced Materials Act, foreseen in 2026**".
- **JRC147428, 1 Oct 2026, EUR 40859, "R&I needs to reduce critical raw materials through advanced materials in construction"**: compares **43 routes across antimony, boron and cerium**; "the recurring binding constraints ... are reliability qualification and industrial scale-up, rather than the absence of candidate substitute systems"; many routes already reach TRL 7 or above; supports Action 3 of COM(2024) 98.
- **JRC148174, 6 Oct 2026, "Material streams from wind energy decommissioning at national level"** (science for policy brief): glass/carbon composite outflows projected above **60 kt a year by 2050** and rare-earth outflows about **350 t a year**; Germany, Spain and France have the largest composite streams; Germany, Sweden and France the largest rare-earth outflows. **Basis (read from the brief itself):** the model counts wind capacity installed up to 2023 and assumes a 32-year turbine lifetime; cutting the lifetime to 20 to 25 years brings decommissioning forward and raises the annual volumes of the 2040s. The brief's findings are projections, sensitive to lifetime and to future deployment.
- **JRC148053, 2 Oct 2026, EUR 40864, "EU Taxonomy Article 8 Review"** (technical report): group-level Taxonomy reporting is complicated by mixed groups and financial conglomerates; firms frequently report OpEx in aggregated form.
- **JRC147907, 6 Oct 2026, EUR 40848, "Monitoring the nutrition quality of Europe's food offer"**: five categories (breakfast cereals, bread products, fresh dairy and desserts, delicatessen meats, soft drinks) across **21 countries** from the JRC's Food and Beverages Labels Explorer (FABLE); finds wide country-to-country variation in sugars, fats, salt and fibre and says it supports reformulation incentives and updated nutrient benchmarks.
- **JRC148427, 1 Oct 2026, EUR 40873, "Drought in Europe August 2026"** (Global Drought Observatory report): drought "stable and critical in most of central Europe"; heatwaves since May; soil moisture and river flow "extensively far below normal"; "pronounced yield losses are expected".
- **JRC147710, 5 Oct 2026, EUR 40867, forest connectivity for the Nature Restoration Regulation** (algorithm document): describes the computation of the Forest Connectivity indicator, with maps and statistics for the 27 Member States and decadal change from 2000 to 2018.
- **JRC148421, 2 Oct 2026, "Exploring Federated Data Sharing in Smart Communities"** (science for policy): a role play run with the Data Spaces Support Centre in March 2026 found stakeholders "more likely to federate if the value proposition is clearly defined"; private-sector orchestration was effective for clearly bounded initiatives.
- **Council ART (listed 7 Oct 2026; paper date not stated on the listing):** "**Data centre expansion: conflicting ambitions. Potential backlash against a strategic asset**": the Council's own abstract says the industry faces "a fundamental tension" in scaling up "without exhausting land, water and energy resources" and that the debate is now "between several visions of sovereign interest". Also listed: "**Weaponised suspicion: how conspiracy theories fuel modern disinformation**". The papers themselves were not retrievable (the PDF returned HTTP 403), so only the abstracts are reported.

## FAQ
**Is a JRC report EU policy?** No. JRC reports are scientific evidence for the Commission's policymaking; they do not bind the Commission.
**Is the Advanced Materials Act adopted?** No. The JRC text only says the analyses feed the knowledge base for an Act "foreseen in 2026".
**Who publishes ART papers?** The Council General Secretariat's Analysis and Research Team; they inform Council negotiators and are not Council positions.
