# EFSD+ and the External Action Guarantee

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (EFSD+)
- Legal basis: sits inside NDICI-Global Europe, Regulation (EU) 2021/947 of 9 June 2021 (CELEX 32021R0947) -- Article 1 establishes the Instrument "including the European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (the 'EFSD+') and the External Action Guarantee"; Recital 62 describes EFSD+ as "an integrated financial package supplying financing capacity in the form of grants, technical assistance, financial instruments, budgetary guarantees and blending operations"; priority areas listed in Annex V
- Guarantee capacity: EUR 40 billion, designed to mobilise up to EUR 135 billion of public and private financing
- External Action Guarantee (EAG): the overarching risk-coverage layer that backs EFSD+ operations, Macro-Financial Assistance (MFA) loans and the Ukraine guarantee; reformed by Regulation (EU) 2026/995 of 29 April 2026 (CELEX 32026R0995, OJ L, 4 May 2026) "as regards increased efficiency of the External Action Guarantee" -- streamlines guarantee-allocation decision-making, simplifies reporting and clarifies the Commission / EEAS / implementing-partner accountability split
- Three EFSD+ investment windows: (1) **Open Architecture Guarantees** -- EUR 13 billion, open to any eligible development finance institution or international financial institution; (2) **EIB Dedicated Investment Window** (EIB Dedicated Guarantees) -- EUR 26.7 billion, backing European Investment Bank operations outside the EU in clean energy, green infrastructure and health; (3) **Member State / regional blending facilities** -- combine EU grants with loans from partner institutions to make non-bankable projects investable
- Largest implementing partner: the European Investment Bank (EIB); other implementing partners include national development finance institutions (AFD -- France, KfW -- Germany, CDP -- Italy, AECID -- Spain) and international financial institutions such as the EBRD
- Financial engine of Global Gateway (EUR 300 billion mobilisation target, 2021-2027) -- EFSD+ is explicitly named by the Commission as "one of the financing tools of Global Gateway"
- Successor instrument to the original European Fund for Sustainable Development (EFSD), established by Regulation (EU) 2017/1601, repealed and replaced by Regulation (EU) 2021/947 alongside Decision No 466/2014/EU and Council Regulation (EC, Euratom) No 480/2009
- NOT the same as EFSI (European Fund for Strategic Investments, the internal "Juncker Plan", 2015-2020) or InvestEU (the internal EU investment programme that succeeded EFSI, 2021-2027) -- both operate INSIDE the EU; EFSD+ operates OUTSIDE the EU as an external-action / development-finance instrument
- Responsible DG: DG INTPA (International Partnerships); EEAS coordinates diplomatically; Commissioner for International Partnerships Jozef Sikela holds political responsibility
- Primary source: international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/funding-and-technical-assistance/funding-instruments/european-fund-sustainable-development-plus_en

## What EFSD+ Is -- and How It Differs From EFSI, EFSD and InvestEU

The European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (EFSD+) is the EU's flagship risk-sharing and blended-finance mechanism for investment OUTSIDE the Union. It sits legally and operationally inside the Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument -- Global Europe (NDICI-Global Europe), established by Regulation (EU) 2021/947. EFSD+ does not lend money directly: it provides EU budgetary guarantees that absorb first losses on investments made by partner finance institutions in developing and emerging economies, allowing those institutions to take on riskier projects (renewable energy in the Sahel, digital infrastructure in Southeast Asia, transport corridors in Central Asia) than they otherwise could.

This is the single most common confusion point for policy professionals tracking EU financial instruments, because three similarly-named vehicles exist side by side:

- **EFSD (2017-2021, superseded)**: the original European Fund for Sustainable Development, established by Regulation (EU) 2017/1601 as part of the "External Investment Plan". Repealed by Regulation (EU) 2021/947 and folded into EFSD+.
- **EFSD+ (2021-present, this guide)**: the successor, with a much larger guarantee ceiling and a broader geographic and thematic scope (all NDICI-Global Europe partner regions plus Global Gateway priorities).
- **EFSI (2015-2020, unrelated except by acronym similarity)**: the "European Fund for Strategic Investments", the internal EU investment vehicle behind the "Juncker Plan", designed to mobilise investment INSIDE the EU during the post-financial-crisis recovery.
- **InvestEU (2021-2027, unrelated)**: the internal successor to EFSI, again focused on investment INSIDE the EU (SMEs, infrastructure, research), managed by DG ECFIN with the EIB Group as the main implementing partner.

The simple test: if the investment happens inside the EU's 27 Member States, it is EFSI/InvestEU territory. If it happens in a third country (Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Western Balkans, the Eastern Partnership), it is EFSD+/NDICI territory. Both InvestEU and EFSD+ use a similar guarantee logic (EU budget absorbs risk so partner banks lend more), which is why they are frequently conflated in press coverage and even in some stakeholder submissions.

## The External Action Guarantee (EAG)

The External Action Guarantee is the umbrella risk-coverage mechanism established alongside EFSD+ by the same Regulation (EU) 2021/947 (Recital 66: "An External Action Guarantee should be established"). It is broader than EFSD+ alone -- the EAG also backs:

- EFSD+ guarantee and blending operations (the largest share)
- Macro-Financial Assistance (MFA) loans to partner countries facing balance-of-payments crises
- The Ukraine guarantee (backing exceptional macro-financial support and reconstruction-linked lending to Ukraine)

The EAG functions as a provisioning mechanism: a share of the EU budget is set aside in a Common Provisioning Fund to cover expected losses across all guaranteed operations, so that if a guaranteed loan or investment defaults, the EU budget -- not the implementing partner -- absorbs the loss up to the guaranteed ceiling.

On 29 April 2026 the co-legislators adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/995 (CELEX 32026R0995, published in the Official Journal on 4 May 2026), the most substantive amendment to the NDICI-Global Europe architecture since the instrument's 2021 launch. Its stated purpose is "increased efficiency of the External Action Guarantee": faster Commission decision-making on guarantee allocations to implementing partners (EIB, EBRD, national development finance institutions), simplified reporting and monitoring obligations, and a clearer accountability split between the Commission, the EEAS and implementing partners. Implementation acts were expected in Q3 2026. For partners preparing Global Gateway-aligned investment proposals, the practical effect is a shorter pipeline: guarantee decisions that previously took longer inter-service cycles should clear faster once the reform is operational.

## The Three EFSD+ Investment Windows

EFSD+ guarantee capacity (EUR 40 billion total) is split across three operational windows, each with a distinct access route:

1. **Open Architecture Guarantees (EUR 13 billion).** Open to any eligible finance institution -- not limited to the EIB. This is the window through which national development finance institutions (AFD, KfW, CDP, AECID) and international financial institutions (EBRD, and others meeting the EU's pillar-assessment criteria) can bid for guarantee coverage on their own external-action lending and investment programmes. Competitive calls are launched periodically by DG INTPA.

2. **EIB Dedicated Investment Window (EIB Dedicated Guarantees, EUR 26.7 billion).** Reserved specifically for European Investment Bank operations outside the EU, covering clean energy, green infrastructure and health investments in partner countries. This is the largest single window and reflects the EIB's status as the EU's house bank for external lending.

3. **Member State / regional blending facilities.** Rather than a guarantee ceiling, this window combines EU grants with loans and equity from partner institutions to make projects investable that would not otherwise clear a commercial bankability threshold -- typically early-stage infrastructure, first-of-a-kind renewable projects, or investments in fragile and conflict-affected contexts where risk premia would otherwise be prohibitive.

## Implementing Partners

The European Investment Bank is explicitly named by the Commission as "the largest implementation partner" for EFSD+. Beyond the EIB, the roster of implementing partners spans:

- **National development finance institutions (DFIs)**: Agence Française de Développement (AFD, France), KfW Entwicklungsbank (Germany), Cassa Depositi e Prestiti (CDP, Italy), Agencia Espanola de Cooperacion Internacional para el Desarrollo (AECID, Spain), and equivalents from other Member States
- **International financial institutions (IFIs)**: European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) and others meeting EU pillar-assessment standards for indirect management of EU funds
- **Team Europe partners**: the broader "Team Europe" branding under which EU institutions, Member States and their DFIs coordinate joint external investment initiatives, avoiding duplication and maximising combined firepower on flagship projects

## Link to Global Gateway

EFSD+ is the principal financial machinery behind Global Gateway, the EU's EUR 300 billion (2021-2027) strategy to mobilise smart, clean and secure infrastructure investment worldwide as a values-based alternative to China's Belt and Road Initiative. The Commission describes EFSD+ directly as "one of the financing tools of Global Gateway": guarantee coverage under the three EFSD+ windows underwrites individual Global Gateway projects and Team Europe Initiatives (for example the Lobito Corridor in Angola-Zambia-DRC, the Trans-Caspian/Middle Corridor connectivity work, and Africa-Europe energy and digital investments). Without EFSD+ guarantee capacity absorbing first-loss risk, most Global Gateway flagship projects in fragile or lower-income markets would not clear commercial investment committees. See `global_gateway_strategy.md` for the strategy-level view, including the 2026 Regulation (EU) 2026/995 EAG efficiency reform and the Connectivity Agenda Platform.

## Blending and Guarantees: The Mechanics

Two distinct financial tools operate under EFSD+, and stakeholders should not conflate them:

- **Guarantees**: the EU budget commits to cover a defined share of losses if a guaranteed loan, investment or bond defaults. The implementing partner (EIB, a national DFI, an IFI) still originates and manages the underlying transaction; the guarantee only changes the risk calculus, letting the partner lend on terms or into markets it otherwise could not justify to its own risk committee. The EAG provisioning ratio (a percentage of guaranteed exposure set aside in the Common Provisioning Fund) determines how much EU budget is actually locked up per euro of guarantee issued -- this is why guarantees can mobilise many multiples of the headline EUR 40 billion figure (the EUR 135 billion mobilisation target).
- **Blending**: EU grant funding is combined directly with loans or equity from partner institutions in a single project financing package, typically to cover technical assistance, interest-rate subsidies, or to de-risk the earliest, least bankable phase of a project (feasibility studies, first-of-a-kind technology deployment). Blending is grant money spent, not budget contingently committed -- it does not carry the same leverage multiplier as a guarantee.

Both tools are combined in practice: a Global Gateway project may carry an EIB loan backed by an EFSD+ EIB Dedicated Guarantee, plus an EU grant blended in to cover technical assistance and de-risk the construction phase.

## Brubru Tracking Angles

For users monitoring which companies, sectors and countries receive EFSD+-backed financing, Brubru's tracking angles are:

- **Project-level disclosure**: Global Gateway and Team Europe Initiative project pages (per-country, per-sector) typically name the implementing partner (EIB, AFD, KfW, EBRD) and the guarantee window used -- useful for competitive intelligence on which DFI leads which sector in which region.
- **Guarantee allocation decisions**: post-2026/995 reform, EAG allocation decisions to implementing partners should move faster and become more frequent -- track DG INTPA and EIB external-lending announcements for new guarantee tranches.
- **Sector exposure**: the EIB Dedicated Investment Window explicitly covers clean energy, green infrastructure and health -- companies in these sectors bidding for partner-country contracts backed by EIB external lending are the most direct beneficiaries of EFSD+ risk absorption.
- **Open Architecture competitive calls**: DG INTPA periodically opens the EUR 13 billion Open Architecture window to competitive bids from eligible finance institutions -- worth tracking for clients that are themselves DFIs or that partner with DFIs on specific projects.
- **Regulatory reform tracking**: implementation acts under Regulation (EU) 2026/995 (expected Q3 2026) will set the operational detail of the "faster decision-making" reform -- monitor for changes to guarantee-application timelines that shorten the practical path from proposal to disbursement.
