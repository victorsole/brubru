# E-Evidence: Regulation (EU) 2023/1543 and Directive (EU) 2023/1544

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal instruments**: Regulation (EU) 2023/1543 on European Production Orders and European Preservation Orders for electronic evidence in criminal proceedings (CELEX **32023R1543**) + Directive (EU) 2023/1544 laying down harmonised rules on the designation of designated establishments and the appointment of legal representatives for the purpose of gathering electronic evidence in criminal proceedings (CELEX **32023L1544**)
- **Adopted**: 12 July 2023, published OJ L 191, 28.7.2023; entered into force 17 August 2023
- **Directive transposition deadline**: 18 February 2026 (missed by most Member States, see State of play below)
- **Regulation entry into application**: **18 August 2026**
- **OEIL procedures**: 2018/0108(COD) Regulation, 2018/0107(COD) Directive; Commission proposals COM(2018)225 + COM(2018)226 tabled 17 April 2018
- **Lead EP committee**: LIBE; rapporteur Birgit Sippel (S&D, Germany)
- **Legal basis**: Regulation on Article 82(1) TFEU (mutual recognition in criminal matters); Directive on Articles 53 and 62 TFEU (freedom to provide services)
- **Lead DG**: DG JUST (Justice and Consumers); Commissioner Michael McGrath (Democracy, Justice and the Rule of Law, Ireland)
- **Territorial scope**: binds all Member States except Denmark (Protocol No 22, AFSJ opt-out); Ireland participates
- **What it creates**: European Production Order (EPOC) and European Preservation Order (EPOC-PR): a judicial authority in one Member State can order a service provider (or its EU legal representative) established anywhere in the EU to produce or preserve electronic evidence directly, without going through the authorities of the provider's home state
- **Data categories covered**: subscriber data, traffic data, content data stored electronically
- **Response deadlines**: 10 days for a standard Production Order, 8 hours in emergencies
- **Decentralised IT system**: e-CODEX, used for authenticated exchanges between issuing authorities and service providers; still under active development as of mid-2026
- **State of play (July 2026)**: only a handful of Member States had transposed the Directive by the 18 February 2026 deadline (Croatia, Italy, Lithuania, Slovakia, Germany completed in March); on 27 March 2026 the Commission sent letters of formal notice to 22 Member States for incomplete transposition, opening infringement proceedings four months before the Regulation's 18 August 2026 application date

## What the e-Evidence package does

The e-Evidence package gives judicial authorities in one Member State a direct legal channel to obtain electronic evidence held by service providers established or represented anywhere in the EU, without routing every request through the authorities of the provider's home state. It replaces the slower mutual legal assistance route and complements (rather than replaces) the European Investigation Order for digital data specifically.

The package has two instruments working together:

1. **Regulation (EU) 2023/1543** creates the two order types: the European Production Order (EPOC) and the European Preservation Order (EPOC-PR): and sets the deadlines, data categories, and safeguards.
2. **Directive (EU) 2023/1544** obliges service providers offering services in the EU to designate a legal representative, or an establishment, in the Union so there is always an EU-based addressee that can receive and act on an order, even if the provider's headquarters sits outside the EU.

## The two order types

- **European Production Order (EPOC)**: compels a service provider to hand over subscriber, traffic or content data. Standard deadline is 10 days from receipt; emergency cases (imminent threat to life or safety, or a similar urgent risk) must be answered within 8 hours.
- **European Preservation Order (EPOC-PR)**: compels a service provider to preserve specified data pending a follow-up formal request (an EPOC, a mutual legal assistance request, or a European Investigation Order), so evidence is not deleted while the slower channel is used.

Both orders are issued or validated by a judicial authority (judge, court, or investigating judge; for content and some traffic data a prosecutor's order needs judicial validation) and must meet necessity and proportionality tests before issue.

## Legal representatives, scope, and liability

Directive (EU) 2023/1544 requires providers of electronic communications services, information society services that store data (social networks, online marketplaces, other hosting services), and providers of internet domain name and numbering services to designate at least one addressee in the EU for receiving, complying with, and enforcing orders. Both the designated establishment/legal representative and the service provider itself can be held jointly and severally liable for non-compliance, with penalties set at national level.

## Safeguards and the decentralised IT system

The package includes fundamental-rights safeguards, including protection of personal data, immunities and privileges, and grounds on which the state where the addressee is established can object to the execution of certain orders. The Regulation is explicitly non-exclusive (Article 32(1)): it does not replace or override mutual legal assistance, the European Investigation Order, or other cross-border evidence instruments: issuing authorities can still choose those channels.

Transmission runs through **e-CODEX**, the EU's existing decentralised justice IT system, adapted for authenticated exchanges between issuing authorities and service providers. As of mid-2026, the service-provider registration application had not yet been rolled out to all Member States, and practitioner commentary has flagged a technical transmission-capacity constraint that could complicate large or complex data requests.

## State of play (July 2026)

Implementation is running behind schedule against the 18 August 2026 application date:
- Only Croatia, Italy, Lithuania and Slovakia had transposed the Directive by the 18 February 2026 deadline; Germany followed in March 2026
- On 27 March 2026 the Commission opened infringement proceedings, sending letters of formal notice to 22 Member States for failing to communicate complete transposition, giving them two months to respond before reasoned opinions can follow
- The e-CODEX-based transmission infrastructure and service-provider registration tooling remain under active development
- Denmark is not bound (Protocol No 22 AFSJ opt-out) and has adopted parallel national measures outside the EU framework

## Interaction with adjacent instruments

| Instrument | Reference | Role vs e-Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| European Investigation Order | Directive 2014/41/EU (CELEX 32014L0041) | General cross-border evidence-gathering tool; e-Evidence provides a faster, direct-to-provider track specifically for electronic data |
| Mutual Legal Assistance | Bilateral / multilateral treaties, EU MLA Convention 2000 | Slower fallback channel; e-Evidence Regulation is explicitly non-exclusive of MLA (Article 32(1)) |
| Second Additional Protocol, Budapest Convention on Cybercrime | CoE CETS 224 | Parallel third-country framework for direct cooperation with non-EU service providers |
| UN Convention against Cybercrime | Adopted 24 December 2024; EU consent procedure 2025/0231(NLE) | Global cybercrime cooperation layer, complementary in the third-country dimension |
| GDPR | Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Underlying data-protection safeguards apply to personal data transferred under e-Evidence orders |
