# European Pillar of Social Rights — the New Action Plan (2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Wednesday 22 July 2026 — ON THE COLLEGE AGENDA TODAY):** the College of Commissioners' **2576th meeting (22 July 2026, the final College before September)** carries as a B item a **Communication on the European Pillar of Social Rights** addressed to the EP, Council, EESC and CoR — the expected **new Action Plan** for the Pillar. Source: the meeting's own order of the day, OJ(2026)2576 final (Secretariat-General, dated 20 July 2026). ⚠ **As of the morning of 22 July the adoption had not yet been announced** — describe the Communication's content only once the Commission publishes it; until then, what is confirmed is that it was on today's agenda.
- **ALSO ON TODAY'S AGENDA — a new social file opens at step one:** a **Consultation Document launching the first-phase consultation of social partners under Article 154 TFEU on possible EU action on the activation of persons facing significant barriers to enter the labour market**. Article 154 first-phase consultation is the treaty-mandated opening move before any EU social-policy proposal: the Commission consults the social partners on the *possible direction* of EU action before deciding whether and what to propose. A second-phase consultation on content follows only if the Commission proceeds (the Quality Jobs Act, whose second-phase consultation opened on 20 July 2026, shows the sequence one step further along).
- **What the Pillar is:** 20 principles and rights for fair labour markets and social protection, proclaimed by Parliament, Council and Commission at the **Gothenburg Summit, 2017**. Organised in three chapters: equal opportunities and labour-market access; fair working conditions; social protection and inclusion.
- **The first Action Plan:** COM(2021) 102 of **4 March 2021**, endorsed at the **Porto Social Summit (7-8 May 2021)** with three **2030 headline targets**:
  - **at least 78%** of people aged 20-64 in employment;
  - **at least 60%** of adults in training every year;
  - **at least 15 million fewer** people at risk of poverty or social exclusion.
- **Where the EU stands** (per DG EMPL's own dashboard, read 22 July 2026): employment **around 76%** (2026); adult training participation **around 40%** (2022 data); poverty reduction **around 3.5 million** fewer at risk (2019-2025). Employment is close to target; training and poverty are far off — the arithmetic that makes a second Action Plan consequential.
- **Why a new plan now:** the first Action Plan's committed actions ran to end-2024. The 2024-2029 Commission consulted publicly during 2025 on a **second Action Plan** (stakeholder positions from mid-2025 onward reference it), framing skills and quality jobs as the social base of competitiveness and reindustrialisation.
- Responsible Executive Vice-President: **Roxana Mînzatu** (Social Rights and Skills, Quality Jobs and Preparedness).

## How to answer common questions
- "Did the Commission adopt a new social rights action plan?" → It was **on the College agenda of 22 July 2026** (order of the day OJ(2026)2576). State the adoption as fact **only if** the Commission's announcement has been published; otherwise say the College met on it and the announcement is expected.
- "What is an Article 154 consultation?" → the two-stage treaty mechanism: first phase on the *direction* of possible EU action, second phase on the *content* of an envisaged proposal; social partners may also opt to negotiate an agreement themselves instead.
- "What are the 2030 social targets?" → 78% employment / 60% training / -15 million poverty, agreed at Porto 2021; current standing as above, always with the data-year caveats.
