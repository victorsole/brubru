## Why HOUS Was Established

Housing has been a national competence since the founding Treaties. Article 153(1)(j) TFEU explicitly excludes housing from EU legislative harmonisation. For decades, EU involvement was indirect: through the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EPBD), cohesion policy housing envelopes, and the European Semester country-specific recommendations.

From 2020 onwards this institutional boundary became politically untenable. Between 2010 and mid-2025, EU average rents rose 28.8% and house prices 60.5% in real terms. Approximately 1.3 million people are without shelter on any given night in the EU. Ten point six per cent of the urban population spends more than 40% of disposable income on housing (the EU "overburden" threshold). Young adults leave parental homes at an average age of 26.3 -- compared with 21 in the US.

These numbers entered the electoral cycle. Housing featured prominently in the 2024 EP election campaigns in Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands and Ireland. The incoming von der Leyen II Commission created a new combined portfolio: Commissioner for Energy and Housing (Dan Jorgensen, Denmark), formalising the housing dimension for the first time at College level. The 10th European Parliament responded by establishing HOUS as a dedicated 12-month special committee, signalling that cross-cutting policy coordination at EU level -- even without legislative competence -- is politically essential.

The structural driver is clear: housing supply has failed to keep pace with population and household formation in most Member States, while short-term rental platforms, financialisation of the housing stock, and underinvestment in social housing have intensified demand pressure. The HOUS mandate tasks the committee with diagnosing this multi-causal failure and translating it into actionable European-level proposals.

## Mandate Text and 12-Month Timeline

The HOUS mandate was granted by EP plenary resolution in 2025. Under Article 207 of the EP Rules of Procedure, special committees are time-limited (normally six to twelve months, renewable) and exist alongside the permanent committee structure rather than replacing it.

The mandate directs HOUS to:

- Assess the root causes of housing unaffordability across EU Member States, including demand-side (population growth, migration, short-term rentals, investor demand) and supply-side (permitting delays, construction-cost inflation, labour shortages) factors
- Examine the financial dimension: the role of institutional investors, real estate investment trusts (REITs), and mortgage instruments in housing-price dynamics
- Review the adequacy of EU funding instruments -- cohesion policy, InvestEU, the Recovery and Resilience Facility -- for housing investment
- Assess the governance framework, in particular the relationship between local, regional, national and EU levels
- Produce policy recommendations on supply, finance, tenant protection, homelessness, and cross-border coordination

The 12-month timeline runs through 2025-2026. The first semester 2026 calendar (published on the committee's page) schedules hearings from February through June, with a public hearing on National Promotional Banks and the EIB on 29 June 2026. The final deliverable -- a mid-term or final recommendations report -- is expected in the second half of 2026.

## Coordination with Permanent Committees

HOUS is a special committee, which means it does not have legislative rapporteur authority in the ordinary legislative procedure. When legislative files touch on housing -- State aid reform, cohesion regulation, building-energy standards -- the responsible permanent committee retains the lead. HOUS coordinates in four directions:

**REGI (Regional Development):** Cohesion policy is the largest single EU funding instrument with a housing component. The Common Provisions Regulation (CPR, Regulation (EU) 2021/1060, CELEX: 32021R1060) allows ERDF resources to be deployed on social and affordable housing. HOUS monitors how REGI handles the cohesion-policy housing envelope in the current programming period and what changes it proposes for MFF 2028-2034 (see `cohesion_policy_midterm_review` and `mff_2028_2034`). The "right to stay" dimension -- preventing cohesion-funded gentrification that displaces existing communities -- is a recurring HOUS-REGI tension.

**ECON (Economic and Monetary Affairs):** The financialisation of housing -- institutional investors accumulating residential stock as an asset class -- is primarily an ECON domain. HOUS requested hearings on REITs, mortgage-backed securities, and the role of the European Central Bank's asset purchase programmes in suppressing housing supply investment. State-aid rule revision for social and affordable housing (one of the European Affordable Housing Plan's most consequential actions) runs through ECON on the competition side.

**EMPL (Employment and Social Affairs):** Homelessness and social housing are labour-market-adjacent: housing insecurity correlates strongly with employment exclusion. EMPL leads on the social pillar dimension of housing and on the Council Recommendation on fighting housing exclusion (Social Package, 6 May 2026, see `eu_housing_exclusion_recommendation_2026`). HOUS provides input to EMPL on the supply side of social housing.

**LIBE (Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs):** Articles 31 and 34(3) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU -- covering the right to decent and dignified working conditions and the right to housing assistance -- anchor the fundamental-rights framing of the housing file. LIBE owns this dimension. HOUS contributes on homelessness statistics, criminalisation of rough sleeping, and access to housing for asylum seekers and migrants. A hearing on criminal activities in the housing sector (24 June 2026, with Europol and the European Public Prosecutor's Office) illustrates the HOUS-LIBE intersection on real-estate fraud, money laundering in property markets, and illegal occupancy.

## Key Files HOUS Scrutinises

**European Affordable Housing Plan -- COM(2025) 1025 final (see `european_affordable_housing_plan`)**
The Commission's first dedicated EU housing architecture. Non-legislative Communication adopted 16 December 2025 under Commissioner Jorgensen. HOUS provides the parliamentary response to this plan. The plan's four pillars (supply, investment, reform, protection) map directly onto the HOUS work programme.

**Financialisation of the housing stock**
No standalone EU legislative file yet, but HOUS has tasked its expert hearings with documenting the scale of institutional-investor ownership of residential property, short-term rental market share in housing-stressed cities, and the gap between housing as a commodity and housing as a social right. Outputs feed into the expected Commission legislative initiative on short-term rentals and into State-aid rule revision.

**Social housing and the cohesion dimension**
Cohesion policy allocations for housing under the current ERDF programming period (2021-2027). The A10-0025/2026 resolution called for doubling cohesion policy funding for affordable housing in the next period. HOUS monitors implementation and coordinates with REGI on what the MFF 2028-2034 (see `mff_2028_2034`) should provide. The cohesion-policy audit (see `cohesion_policy_audit`) tracks mid-term review findings relevant to housing investment.

**Energy poverty and the EPBD link**
The intersection of housing affordability and energy costs is a recurring HOUS hearing theme. The Energy Performance of Buildings Directive recast (Directive (EU) 2024/1275, CELEX: 32024L1275) drives renovation requirements that raise short-term costs for owner-occupiers and landlords. HOUS coordinates with ITRE and ENVI on how renovation obligations interact with housing affordability, particularly for low-income tenants.

**Council Recommendation on fighting housing exclusion (see `eu_housing_exclusion_recommendation_2026`)**
Adopted under the Social Package of 6 May 2026. This soft-law instrument addresses homelessness and housing exclusion as social policy challenges. HOUS and EMPL jointly monitor its implementation.

**Housing for students and young people**
A hearing on 4 June 2026 examined student housing supply deficits and public financial models for affordable youth housing. This connects to the Erasmus+ accommodation crisis and to the broader demographic argument that housing costs reduce labour mobility and delay household formation.

## Expected Outputs

HOUS operates under a 12-month mandate. Its primary outputs are:

1. **A10-0025/2026 -- First report (DELIVERED 10 March 2026):** EP Resolution TA-10-2026-0064 adopted with 367 votes in favour, 166 against, 84 abstentions. Own-initiative procedure 2025/2070(INI). Rapporteur: Borja Gimenez Larraz (EPP, Spain). The resolution sets out the committee's initial diagnosis and calls for the European Affordable Housing Plan, a Housing Simplification Package, a 60-day permit-issuance target, revised State aid rules for social housing, and balanced regulation of short-term rental platforms.

2. **Further own-initiative reports or contributions:** The committee may produce additional reports during its mandate on specific sub-themes (financialisation, social housing, student housing). These would follow the standard INI procedure with a designated rapporteur.

3. **Final recommendations report (expected H2 2026):** A comprehensive output synthesising the hearings, evidence gathered, and inter-committee coordination. This will feed into the Commission's Housing Alliance and Housing Summit process, as well as into the MFF 2028-2034 preparatory discussions.

4. **Contributions to inter-institutional processes:** HOUS opinions or letters to REGI, ECON, EMPL, LIBE on specific legislative files that cross the housing dimension (State aid revision, CPR review, short-term rentals legislative initiative).

## Brubru Tracking Angles

- **Public-affairs professionals** in real estate, construction, social housing, energy renovation, student accommodation and urban mobility should monitor HOUS hearing outputs and rapporteur positions. The committee's final recommendations will shape Commission legislative proposals through the Housing Alliance.
- **Regional and local authorities** engaged in ERDF housing programmes should track HOUS-REGI coordination on the next cohesion programming period (see `cohesion_policy_midterm_review` and `mff_2028_2034`). HOUS's call for direct access to EU funds by local authorities is a structural change request relevant to any subnational entity managing housing portfolios.
- **Financial institutions and investors** should monitor HOUS-ECON hearings on financialisation and the State-aid rule revision timeline. The revised framework for social and affordable housing State aid is the single most consequential near-term change for national housing investment pipelines.
- **NGOs and social-sector organisations** working on homelessness, fundamental rights or migrant housing should track HOUS-LIBE and HOUS-EMPL coordination, and the implementation monitoring of the Council Recommendation on housing exclusion (see `eu_housing_exclusion_recommendation_2026`).
- **Energy-sector stakeholders** should note the HOUS hearing on housing affordability and energy costs (24 June 2026) as a channel through which renovation-obligation design (EPBD) may be revisited in the housing-affordability frame.
