# EU Discharge Procedure (CONT, ECA, Council)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal basis**: Article 319 TFEU grants the European Parliament the power to give discharge to the Commission in respect of the implementation of the budget.
- **Financial Regulation**: Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509 of the European Parliament and of the Council governs the rules applicable to the general budget of the Union, including discharge obligations. Earlier programming periods fall under Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046.
- **ECA feed**: The European Court of Auditors publishes its Annual Report on the implementation of the EU budget (covering both the general budget and the European Development Fund) each October/November. This report, with the Statement of Assurance (DAS), is the principal audit input to the discharge procedure.
- **CONT committee**: The EP Committee on Budgetary Control (CONT) is the responsible committee. It assigns individual rapporteurs for each institution, body, agency, and joint undertaking.
- **Institutions with separate discharge**: Commission (main) + EDF, the EP itself, the Council and European Council, Court of Justice of the EU, European Court of Auditors, EESC, CoR, Ombudsman, EDPS, EEAS, plus all decentralised agencies (approximately 40) and all joint undertakings.
- **Council recommendation**: The Council issues a recommendation to the EP on whether to grant discharge, typically in February/March.
- **EP plenary vote**: Discharge is voted in EP plenary typically in April/May of year YYYY+1 for the budget executed in year YYYY. For example, the discharge for the 2023 budget is voted in 2024 (spring), and the discharge for the 2024 budget is voted in 2025.
- **Three outcomes**: Grant, Postpone (defer to October/November), or Refuse.
- **Consequences of refusal**: Politically significant. It has occurred only twice (Commission, 1984 and 1998/1999; the 1999 case triggered the Santer Commission's resignation). Refusal for agencies/bodies is more common and requires corrective action.
- **Postponement**: A decision to postpone is not a refusal but a signal of serious unresolved concerns. The institution must provide additional information by a set deadline, and a second plenary vote takes place in October/November of the same year.
- **Recent postponements (verify before citing)**: Frontex (European Border and Coast Guard Agency) has faced repeated discharge difficulties due to fundamental rights and governance concerns. Other agencies including the European University Institute (EUI) have been postponed in past discharge rounds. Always verify the current status via the CONT discharge pages before citing in any client communication.
- **ECA materiality threshold**: The ECA considers an error rate above 2% material for the Statement of Assurance; cohesion, agriculture, and external aid historically drive the highest error rates.
- **Key URL (CONT discharge portal)**: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/cont/discharge-procedure`

Guide for EU policy professionals, compliance teams, and advocacy staff tracking the annual discharge procedure and its political consequences.

## 1. Article 319 TFEU: The Constitutional Framework

Article 319 TFEU establishes the EP's discharge power:

> "The European Parliament, acting on a recommendation from the Council, shall give a discharge to the Commission in respect of the implementation of the budget."

The same article extends the procedure to "other institutions and bodies", meaning that the Council, EP itself, Court of Justice, ECA, EESC, CoR, Ombudsman, EDPS, EEAS, and all agencies and joint undertakings also fall under discharge scrutiny, each through its own dedicated CONT rapporteur.

The provision reflects a foundational accountability logic: the Commission and all bodies spending Union funds are answerable to the democratically elected Parliament, not merely to technical auditors. Discharge is the Parliament's annual political sign-off that Union funds were spent legally, regularly, and in accordance with the principles of sound financial management (economy, efficiency, effectiveness).

Where the Commission declines or partially addresses CONT's demands for information, Parliament can and does withhold discharge, either by postponement or outright refusal.

## 2. The Annual Cycle

The discharge procedure runs on a fixed annual calendar across roughly 18 months:

| Phase | Timing | Actor | Action |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| **Budget execution** | January to December (year N) | Commission + all bodies | Implementation of the annual budget |
| **Annual accounts** | By 31 March (year N+1) | Commission | Consolidated accounts transmitted to ECA and EP |
| **ECA audit** | April to September (year N+1) | European Court of Auditors | Annual audit of accounts and underlying transactions |
| **ECA Annual Report** | October/November (year N+1) | ECA | Publication of Annual Report + Statement of Assurance (DAS) + specific reports on other institutions |
| **Commission response** | October/November (year N+1) | Commission | Official response to ECA Annual Report findings |
| **Council recommendation** | February/March (year N+2) | Council | Recommendation to the EP on granting discharge |
| **CONT hearings and rapporteur reports** | October (N+1) to April (N+2) | CONT committee + rapporteurs | Questionnaires, hearings, committee vote on draft reports |
| **EP plenary vote (first round)** | April/May (year N+2) | EP plenary | Vote on grant, postpone, or refuse |
| **Second round (if postponed)** | October/November (year N+2) | EP plenary | Vote on deferred institutions/bodies |

For the 2024 financial year (budget executed in 2024), the ECA Annual Report covering that year is published in late 2025, Council issues its recommendation in early 2026, and EP votes in spring 2026.

## 3. Per-Institution Rapporteur Structure

CONT appoints a dedicated rapporteur for each entity subject to discharge. The rapporteur is responsible for drafting the discharge recommendation, leading CONT hearings, and presenting the report to plenary. Typical rapporteur assignments cover:

- **Commission** (main budget): the most politically significant; the rapporteur leads the principal discharge debate.
- **European Development Fund (EDF)**: separate from the general budget; its own rapporteur.
- **European Parliament**: the EP grants itself discharge, with a rapporteur appointed from CONT.
- **Council and European Council**: the Council has traditionally refused to cooperate with EP scrutiny of its own discharge, an institutional stand-off that led the EP to systematically postpone Council discharge since 2009.
- **Court of Justice of the EU**: separate discharge and report.
- **European Court of Auditors**: the EP audits its auditor.
- **European Economic and Social Committee (EESC)**: committee-level report.
- **Committee of the Regions (CoR)**: separate report.
- **European Ombudsman**: discharge.
- **European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS)**: discharge.
- **European External Action Service (EEAS)**: discharge.
- **Decentralised agencies**: approximately 40 agencies each have their own rapporteur, often handled in thematic clusters.
- **Joint undertakings**: public-private research partnerships under Horizon Europe, each with a dedicated report.

Note: do NOT cite specific rapporteur names in client outputs without verifying current assignments on the CONT discharge page, as rapporteurs change each legislature and with each discharge round.

## 4. The Three Outcomes

### Grant
The standard outcome. The EP formally grants discharge, confirming that it is satisfied the institution managed its budget in accordance with Union law and the principles of sound financial management. A resolution accompanies the decision, listing observations and recommendations. Grant does not mean an absence of criticism; the resolution can be highly critical in its text even where discharge is granted.

### Postpone
The EP defers the vote to October/November. Postponement signals that CONT has identified serious unresolved concerns and needs additional time and information. The institution concerned must:
- Respond in detail to CONT's outstanding questions by a specified deadline.
- Provide any outstanding documents, data, or corrective action plans.
- Appear before CONT if requested.

A second plenary vote then takes place in autumn of the same year. If the response is deemed insufficient, the EP can still refuse discharge at that stage.

### Refuse
The EP declines to grant discharge. This is the Parliament's most forceful accountability sanction. At the level of the Commission, refusal has occurred only twice in the history of the EU; the 1998 refusal (for the 1996 accounts) formed part of the chain of events that led to the Santer Commission's resignation in March 1999 and prompted the comprehensive administrative reforms under Romano Prodi (the Kinnock reforms). Refusal for agencies and smaller bodies is more common and carries less immediate political weight, but it triggers formal corrective obligations and damages the institution's reputation.

## 5. Recent Discharge Difficulties (Verify Before Citing)

**Frontex**: The European Border and Coast Guard Agency faced sustained CONT scrutiny over governance failures, serious incidents at external borders, and fundamental rights concerns. CONT refused discharge for Frontex for the 2020 financial year (voted 2022), and the situation remained contested in subsequent rounds. Always verify current status.

**Council and European Council**: The EP has consistently withheld or postponed Council discharge since 2009 on grounds that the Council refuses to submit to EP scrutiny in the same way as other institutions. The Council's position is that it is not obliged to appear before EP or submit documents. This stand-off is an ongoing inter-institutional tension.

**European University Institute (EUI)**: Subject to discharge difficulties in past rounds. Verify current status before citing.

Before referencing any agency or institution in client advice, consult the live CONT discharge pages:
- Discharge 2024: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/cont/discharge-procedure/discharge-2024`
- Discharge 2023: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/cont/discharge-procedure/discharge-2023`

## 6. ECA Error Rate Methodology and the Statement of Assurance (DAS)

The ECA's annual Statement of Assurance (DAS) is the central audit document feeding the discharge procedure. The DAS covers:

- **Reliability of the accounts**: Are the EU's consolidated accounts a fair representation of the financial position?
- **Legality and regularity of revenue**: Were own resources and other income collected in accordance with applicable rules?
- **Legality and regularity of expenditure**: Were payments made in accordance with the Treaties, the Financial Regulation, and sector-specific rules?

### Error Rate

The ECA estimates an **overall error rate** for the EU budget based on statistical sampling. The rate represents the estimated proportion of the total audited expenditure that should not have been paid because the underlying transactions did not fully comply with the applicable rules.

| Error Rate | ECA Assessment | Typical CONT Response |
|------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Below 2%** | Below materiality threshold | Generally acceptable; observations possible |
| **2 to 5%** | Material error rate | CONT requests corrective action; specific observations in discharge resolution |
| **Above 5%** | Highly material | CONT may postpone discharge; specific payment suspension may be triggered at DG level |

**Key context**: High-risk spending areas (cohesion policy, agriculture under shared management, and external aid) consistently produce higher error rates than low-risk areas (staff costs, EU institutions' own administrative expenditure). Error in cohesion policy and agriculture is the primary driver of the overall DAS qualification.

The ECA has issued a qualified opinion (rather than an adverse opinion) on the legality and regularity of payments for most recent years, meaning material errors exist but are not pervasive across the entire budget.

For the methodological detail on cohesion-specific audit sampling, error classification (quantified, non-quantified, systemic, random), and the Arachne risk-scoring tool, see `cohesion_policy_audit.md`.

## 7. Political Significance and Brubru Tracking Angles

The discharge procedure is one of the most powerful accountability mechanisms the EP holds. For advocacy professionals, it matters in several ways:

**Budget leverage**: CONT observations in discharge resolutions frequently translate into legislative proposals or Commission commitments. Tracking which agencies or programmes receive critical observations tells clients what political pressure is building.

**Agency governance**: For clients with interests in specific EU agencies (EMA, EFSA, ERA, ACER, Frontex, eu-LISA, etc.), the discharge vote and CONT rapporteur report are early-warning signals of governance or financial management concerns that may affect agency operations.

**Council scrutiny gap**: The persistent refusal of Council to cooperate with discharge scrutiny is itself a political issue relevant to advocates on institutional reform (AFCO and CONT intersection).

**MFF implications**: The ECA's annual error rates and DAS qualifications directly inform political debates on shared management reform, cohesion policy simplification, and the design of future MFF spending rules. See `mff_2028_2034.md` and `cohesion_policy_midterm_review.md`.

**RRF post-implementation scrutiny**: The Recovery and Resilience Facility adds a new dimension to discharge, as the Commission implements the RRF directly (not under shared management). CONT and ECA scrutiny of RRF disbursements feeds the general Commission discharge. See `eu_recovery_resilience_facility.md`.
