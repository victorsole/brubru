# EP Own-Initiative Proposals: INL, INI and Article 225 TFEU

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal basis:** Article 225 TFEU gives the European Parliament the right, by a majority of its component members, to request that the Commission submit a legislative proposal. The Commission retains the formal right of initiative.
- **Three distinct routes exist, each with different legal weight:**
  1. **INL** (legislative own-initiative report): a committee drafts a report calling on the Commission to bring forward a specific legislative proposal. Governed by Rule 47 of the EP Rules of Procedure (RoP) and Annex III. Requires a plenary vote. Triggers a binding Commission response obligation under the EP-EC Framework Agreement.
  2. **INI** (non-legislative own-initiative report): a committee produces a report on a policy question within its remit. A purely political signal. The Commission has no formal legal obligation to bring forward legislation; it may reply, but non-reply carries no procedural consequence.
  3. **Proposal for a Union act** (Rule 47(2) RoP): up to 10 MEPs submit a proposal directly to the President of the Parliament, with a legal basis and an explanatory statement of no more than 150 words. The President checks admissibility and refers to the responsible committee, which decides within three months on further action. Reference format: B10-NNNN/YYYY in the 10th term. Live list: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/plenary/en/proposal-for-a-union-act.html`
- **Commission obligation after an INL:** Under the EP-EC Framework Agreement (2010, updated 2026), the Commission must respond within three months with a Communication stating whether and how it intends to follow up. If it declines, it must explain in writing. This obligation does not apply to INI reports.
- **Key identification rule:** Look up the file in OEIL (`oeil.secure.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=...`). The procedure type field shows INL (legislative initiative) or INI (non-legislative initiative). An INL carries a formal Art 225 lever; an INI does not.
- **Practical consequence for public-affairs work:** An INL on your issue puts the Commission under a formal obligation to respond, creating a concrete and time-bounded advocacy window. An INI on the same issue is politically useful but provides no legal lever.
- **EP10 current INLs:** All INLs filed in the 10th term (July 2024 onwards) are listed in OEIL filtered by procedure type INL. EP10 uses the A10-/B10-/P10- reference series. Legislative powers overview: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/about-parliament/en/parliaments-powers/legislative-powers`
- **Related guide:** `ep_ec_framework_agreement_2026.md` covers the Commission's formal follow-up obligations and the bilateral architecture governing both INL responses and inter-institutional programming.

## 1. Article 225 TFEU: the constitutional framework

Article 225 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union reads, in substance: the European Parliament may, acting by a majority of its component members, request the Commission to submit any appropriate proposal on matters on which it considers that a Union act is required for the purpose of implementing the Treaties. If the Commission does not submit a proposal, it shall inform the Parliament of the reasons.

Three points define the scope of this provision:

**The Commission retains the right of initiative.** Article 225 is a request mechanism, not a grant of initiative. The Parliament cannot impose a legislative text; it can only ask the Commission to act. The Commission may decline, but must explain.

**Majority of component members.** The threshold is an absolute majority of all MEPs (at least 361 of 720 in the 10th term), not merely a majority of those voting. This is a higher bar than most plenary votes, which require only a majority of votes cast.

**The obligation to explain non-compliance.** If the Commission does not follow up, it must inform Parliament of the reasons. The EP-EC Framework Agreement of 2010 (revised in 2026, see `ep_ec_framework_agreement_2026.md`) operationalised this: the Commission commits to a formal response within three months, either presenting a proposal or providing a detailed explanation of why it does not intend to do so.

## 2. The INL pathway: Rule 47 and Annex III RoP

An INL (legislative own-initiative) is the procedural packaging that transforms an Art 225 request into an official parliamentary procedure. The committee responsible for the policy area draws up a report calling on the Commission to act. The report is voted in plenary. If adopted, the President forwards the request to the Commission.

**Rule 47 of the Rules of Procedure** governs this pathway. It sets out:
- the conditions under which a committee may request authorisation to draw up an INL report (the Conference of Committee Chairs coordinates to avoid duplication);
- the requirement that the report set out a detailed legal basis for the requested proposal;
- the referral mechanism to the Commission following plenary adoption.

**Annex III of the Rules of Procedure** (Conditions for the granting of own-initiative reports) establishes additional criteria a committee must meet before it receives authorisation to proceed, including a justification for why the issue falls within the EP's remit and why a legislative proposal is needed.

**Procedure tracking in OEIL:** An INL file carries the procedure type code INL and an OEIL reference in the format YYYY/NNNN(INL). The procedure file records committee, rapporteur, timeline, and whether the Commission has responded.

**Legislative train observatory:** The status of all pending INL procedures, and the Commission's follow-up (or lack thereof), is tracked in the Legislative Train under the relevant policy area. See `legislative_train_observatory.md`.

## 3. The INI pathway: non-legislative own-initiative

An INI (own-initiative) report is a committee report on a policy question that does not invoke Article 225 TFEU. The Parliament exercises its right to self-organise and produce political positions on any matter within its competence.

INI reports are the workhorse of parliamentary political signalling:
- resolutions on foreign policy situations;
- positions ahead of international negotiations;
- calls on the Commission to change policy direction;
- thematic assessments of existing legislation.

**The Commission is not obliged to respond with a proposal.** It may issue a Communication or a statement welcoming or noting the resolution, but this is political courtesy, not a legal obligation. The EP-EC Framework Agreement does not impose a structured response timeline for INI files.

**OEIL procedure code:** INI. Reference format YYYY/NNNN(INI).

**Distinction that matters for advocacy:** If a client's issue is covered by an INI resolution, that resolution can be cited to show political momentum, but it does not compel Commission action. If the same issue is covered by an INL, the Commission is on a formal clock.

## 4. Proposal for a Union act: the 10-MEP route

Rule 47(2) of the Rules of Procedure provides a second channel for requesting Commission action: up to 10 MEPs may jointly submit a "proposal for a Union act" directly to the President of the Parliament, without going through the committee authorisation process required for an INL.

**Requirements:**
- A statement of the legal basis in the Treaties;
- An explanatory statement of no more than 150 words;
- Submitted jointly by no more than 10 MEPs.

**Process after submission:**
- The President verifies that the requirements are met;
- The proposal is referred to the responsible committee;
- The committee decides within three months whether to proceed (for example, by incorporating the request into a scheduled INL, initiating its own INL, or declining to act);
- The proposal is published in the EP plenary portal: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/plenary/en/proposal-for-a-union-act.html`

**Reference format:** B10-NNNN/YYYY in the 10th parliamentary term.

**Difference from an INL:** A Rule 47(2) proposal is a preliminary submission that initiates a committee review process. It does not by itself generate a Commission response obligation. If the committee takes it forward and plenary adopts an INL resolution, then Art 225 is formally engaged. If the committee declines within three months, the proposal lapses.

**Live catalogue:** The EP plenary portal lists all pending proposals for a Union act by term, with PDF and DOCX downloads. As of the 10th term, active proposals cover building energy performance, emissions trading, employment classifications, and fisheries management, among others.

## 5. Commission's obligation to respond

The Commission's formal follow-up obligations derive from three instruments, layered on top of Article 225 TFEU:

**EP-EC Framework Agreement 2010 (OJ L 304, 20.11.2010):** The original inter-institutional agreement obliging the Commission to present a legislative act within 12 months of an INL resolution, or to explain why it will not do so. The three-month explanation deadline was set by the agreement.

**Common Inter-institutional Practice:** Beyond the Framework Agreement, an informal practice of exchanging letters at Conference of Presidents level has developed, particularly for INL files related to the annual Commission Work Programme.

**2026 revision of the Framework Agreement:** The updated agreement (see `ep_ec_framework_agreement_2026.md`) strengthened the response timelines and introduced structured joint programming between the EP and the Commission at the start of the legislative term, linking annual priorities to plausible INL requests.

**What the Commission must do:**
- Respond within three months with a Communication;
- Either present a proposal or explain in detail why it does not intend to do so;
- If it departs from the EP's request (for example, proposing a different legal form or scope), state this and explain the reasons.

**What the Commission need not do:** Accept the EP's framing, adopt the EP's preferred legal basis, or produce a proposal within any particular subsequent timeframe (only the response is time-bound, not the proposal itself).

## 6. How to recognise INL vs INI vs Union-act proposal in Brubru Chat

**Rule of thumb for chat answers:**

| Procedure code | Label | Commission obliged to respond? | Art 225 TFEU invoked? |
|---|---|---|---|
| INL | Legislative own-initiative | Yes, within 3 months, in writing | Yes |
| INI | Non-legislative own-initiative | No binding obligation | No |
| B10-NNNN/YYYY | Proposal for a Union act | No (committee review stage only) | Not until plenary vote on resulting INL |

**Identifying the code:** OEIL is authoritative. The procedure type field in every OEIL file is the single most reliable indicator. The EP open data portal (`data.europarl.europa.eu`) also exposes procedure types via its Notices-Rapporteur API. See `ep_documents_and_open_data.md`.

**Chat trigger examples:**
- "Has the EP asked the Commission to legislate on X?" -- look for an INL on X in OEIL
- "Does the Commission have to respond to this EP resolution?" -- determine whether the resolution is from an INL or an INI
- "What is a proposal for a Union act?" -- Rule 47(2), up to 10 MEPs, B10-reference, committee decides within 3 months

## 7. Recent INLs in the 10th term

INLs filed in the 10th term (from July 2024) are tracked in OEIL under procedure type INL. The EP's plenary procedures database (`https://www.europarl.europa.eu/plenary/en/procedures.html`) allows filtering by procedure type and parliamentary term.

Because INL procedures are committee-driven, they take several months from authorisation to plenary vote. In the first year of a new term (EP10: 2024-2025), committees are predominantly setting up and adopting their working programmes; a significant wave of INL authorisations typically follows in the second and third years of the term.

For verified current INL status: search OEIL with the query `type:INL term:10` or use the Legislative Train Observatory, which shows pending Commission responses alongside the procedure stage. See `legislative_train_observatory.md`.

**Note on citing INL references in Brubru Chat:** Always retrieve the current status from OEIL before asserting that a specific INL is "pending Commission response" or "awaiting plenary vote". Procedure stages move. A reference that was at committee stage last month may have been voted in plenary and is now awaiting a Commission Communication.
