## What the EUSF does

The EUSF helps Member States and accession countries respond to **major natural disasters** by reimbursing a share of public expenditure on emergency response operations. It is **not** a general post-disaster development fund — it covers:

- Restoring essential infrastructure (electricity, water, telecommunications, transport, education, health)
- Emergency services (rescue, shelter, food, water, hygiene)
- Temporary clean-up and preventive measures for protected cultural heritage
- Securing prevention infrastructure (dams, dykes)

It does **not** cover:
- Damages to private property (covered by national insurance)
- Loss of revenue
- Permanent reconstruction beyond pre-disaster baseline

## When the EUSF can be activated

Two activation thresholds exist:

### Major natural disaster
- **Direct damage** exceeds **EUR 3 billion** (2011 prices) OR **0.6% of beneficiary state's GNI**, whichever is lower
- Triggered by a single event; can be a flood, earthquake, wildfire, drought, public health emergency (extended scope since 2020 to include COVID-19)

### Regional disaster
- Direct damage in an NUTS-2 region exceeds **1.5% of regional GDP**
- For outermost regions: threshold reduced to 1%
- Designed to capture serious regional events that don't meet the national threshold

## How the proposal-to-payment cycle works

1. **Eligible state submits application** to the Commission within **12 weeks** of the first damage caused
2. **Commission assesses** under Reg 2012/2002 Articles 4-5 (eligibility + amount calculation)
3. **Commission proposes Council Decision + Draft Amending Budget (DAB)** allocating the funds
4. **Parliament and Council co-decide** (ordinary legislative procedure for the DAB)
5. **Pre-financing**: from January 2020, advance payments of up to 25% of the expected aid (capped at EUR 100M per case) can be released within 6 months of application — accelerated route under Reg (EU) 2020/461 ("Coronavirus response investment initiative")
6. **Final implementation report** by beneficiary state within **18 months** of grant agreement signature

## Recent EUSF cases (selected)

The EUSF has been used heavily in the 2022-2026 period given the climate disaster frequency. Notable cases:
- **2023 Greece + Italy + Slovenia floods + wildfires** — multiple Commission proposals
- **2024 Storm Boris (Central Europe floods)** — multi-state aid package
- **2024 Spain DANA floods** (Valencia + Murcia, late October 2024) — major national-threshold case
- **2025 Iberian heatwave + wildfires** (Spain + Portugal) — ongoing
- **2026 Eastern Europe + Cyprus wildfires + flood season** — current cases now batched into the **€144M proposal** announced 21 May 2026

The exact event-mapping for the **21 May 2026 €144M proposal** (Spain + Romania + Cyprus) will be in the Commission Decision text — Brubru will mirror it once published.

## Post-2027 — EUSF in the MFF 2028-2034

The Commission proposed in the **MFF 2028-2034 framework** (presented 16 July 2025) to consolidate the EUSF and the Emergency Aid Reserve into a single Solidarity and Emergency Reserve with greater flexibility. The proposal is in trilogue (negotiations expected to land mid-2027 alongside the rest of the MFF package). Cross-link with `mff_2028_2034_proposal`.
