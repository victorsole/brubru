## Overview

Rare diseases are defined in the EU as conditions affecting no more than 5 persons per 10,000 (1 in 2,000). An estimated 27-36 million people in the EU (approximately 8% of the population) live with a rare disease. Between 6,000 and 8,000 rare diseases have been identified, approximately 72-80% are genetic in origin, and around 70% manifest in childhood. About 90-95% of rare diseases currently lack an approved therapy. The average diagnostic delay ("diagnostic odyssey") is 4.7 years, with women experiencing delays 1.7 years longer than men on average.

EU competence in public health is **supporting** (Article 168 TFEU): the EU may support, coordinate, or complement national policies but **may not harmonise** the organisation, financing, or delivery of healthcare. However, the EU may rely on Article 114 TFEU (internal market) for binding measures on medicines, clinical trials, HTA, and health data governance, and Article 16 TFEU for personal data protection.

### Council Recommendation (2009)

The **Council Recommendation of 8 June 2009 on an action in the field of rare diseases** (2009/C 151/02) provided the first EU-level policy framework. It invited Member States to:
- Adopt national rare disease plans/strategies by 2013
- Designate centres of expertise
- Improve codification using ORPHAcodes
- Develop registries
- Promote European cooperation

### Commission Communication (2008)

**COM(2008) 679** -- "Rare Diseases: Europe's challenges" -- laid the strategic groundwork for EU action on rare diseases.

## European Reference Networks (ERNs)

ERNs are cross-border networks of specialised hospital centres established under Directive 2011/24/EU to pool expertise for rare, complex, and low-prevalence diseases.

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Number of ERNs | 24 |
| Specialised centres | 1,600+ |
| Hospitals | 375-380 |
| Countries | 27 Member States + Norway |
| Operational since | 2017 |
| EU funding (2023-2027) | EUR 77.4 million (EU4Health) |
| Joint Action JARDIN | EUR 18.75 million (2024-2027), integrating ERNs into national health systems |
| Virtual consultations | 4,500+ cross-border clinical panels since inception |
| IT platform | Clinical Patient Management System 2.0 (CPMS 2.0), GDPR-compliant, open-source from 2026 |
| Monitoring | First ERN Monitoring Report published 2025 (24 performance indicators) |
| ERN Board of Member States | Designates creation of new ERNs, selects healthcare providers |

### ERN Coverage Gaps

ERN coverage remains uneven. Not all Member States have centres in all 24 networks. Strengthening governance, national integration, geographical coverage, stable financing, and clearer referral pathways are priorities for the proposed EU Action Plan.

## EU Rare Disease Action Plan (In Preparation)

### Background

In **June 2024**, the Council of the EU adopted conclusions calling for a **European Action Plan on Rare Diseases**. In parallel, the EP Committee on Public Health (SANT) is preparing a **legislative own-initiative report (INL)** on an EU Rare Disease Action Plan.

- **OEIL procedure:** 2025/2130(INL)
- **Rapporteur:** Nicolas Gonzalez Casares (S&D, Spain)
- **Public consultation:** March 2025, 4,000+ responses from patients, clinicians, organisations, and public bodies
- **Plenary debate:** April 2025 with Health Commissioner Oliver Varhelyi
- **High-Level Meeting on Rare Diseases:** December 2025, Brussels -- adopted the *Declaration on the European Innovation and Care Ecosystem for Rare and Complex Diseases*

### EPRS European Added Value Assessment (February 2026)

The EPRS study **"EU Rare Disease Action Plan -- European Added Value Assessment"** (PE 774.708, February 2026) identifies **31 proposed measures** across **6 thematic areas**:

| Theme | Potential EU Added Value |
|-------|------------------------|
| **1. Diagnosis and screening** | Shorter diagnostic pathways; earlier detection; reduced avoidable disease progression and healthcare costs |
| **2. Access and affordability of therapies** | Reduced inequalities in access; improved predictability for patients and payers; lower inefficiencies from fragmented national decision-making |
| **3. Research and innovation** | Faster translation of scientific advances; more efficient use of public investment; accelerated development of diagnostics and therapies |
| **4. Data sharing and registries** | Reduced duplication and transaction costs; stronger evidence for clinical, regulatory, and policy decisions; improved system efficiency |
| **5. Patient and family support, social inclusion and care** | Reduced socio-economic burden on patients and carers; improved continuity of care; mitigated impacts on employment, education, and informal care |
| **6. Governance and cross-border coordination** | Reduced inefficiencies from fragmented expertise; better use of EU instruments; more equitable access to specialised care across Member States |

### Key Proposed Measures (Selection)

**Diagnosis and screening:**
- Support upward convergence of newborn screening (NBS) rates (currently 2-93 diseases screened across EU)
- Fund comparative diagnostic performance studies across Member States
- Strengthen Orphanet-ERN collaboration framework
- Strengthen telemedicine and virtual case discussion tools (CPMS 2.0)

**Access and affordability of therapies:**
- Pool demand and coordinate post-marketing evidence generation
- Voluntary solidarity mechanisms for equitable access
- Closer alignment between HTA and regulatory processes for ultra-rare conditions
- Support medicine repurposing for rare diseases

**Research and innovation:**
- Pool resources and align research agendas via ERDERA partnership
- Support shared research infrastructures
- Embed patient involvement throughout the research cycle
- Accelerate gene therapies, cell therapies (CAR T-cell), and next-generation sequencing diagnostics

**Data sharing and registries:**
- Promote common standards and systematic use of ORPHAcodes
- Federated data sharing infrastructures integrated with EHDS
- Strengthen ERDRI (European Rare Disease Registry Infrastructure)

**Patient and family support:**
- Shared frameworks for disability recognition
- Capacity-building for psychosocial support
- Person-centred integrated care pathways
- EU-wide evidence generation on socio-economic impacts

**Governance and cross-border coordination:**
- Strengthen ERN governance, national integration, and legal status
- Clearer referral pathways for virtual consultations
- Improved reimbursement arrangements for cross-border care
- Stable, long-term financing mechanisms for ERNs

## Orphan Medicinal Products

The **Orphan Regulation (EC) No 141/2000** provides incentives for developing medicines for rare diseases:
- 10-year market exclusivity (under revision in pharmaceutical package)
- EMA protocol assistance and fee waivers
- Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products (COMP) at EMA reviews applications
- ~3,010 orphan designations and 261+ marketing authorisations by end of 2024

The **revised EU pharmaceutical legislation** (political agreement December 2025) introduces:
- New "8+1(+1)(+1)" regulatory data protection formula
- Breakthrough therapy designation (relevant for gene/cell therapies)
- Revised orphan incentive structures
- Transferable exclusivity vouchers (TEVs) for antimicrobial resistance

Orphan drug prices are estimated to be ~8x higher than drugs for common diseases. Access to reimbursed orphan medicines remains uneven across Member States.
