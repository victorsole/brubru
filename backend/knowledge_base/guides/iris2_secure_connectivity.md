## Legal Basis: Regulation (EU) 2023/588

The Union Secure Connectivity Programme was established by Regulation (EU) 2023/588 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 March 2023, covering the period 2023-2027 and forming part of the wider EU Space Programme framework (Regulation (EU) 2021/696). IRIS2 is the operational name given to the constellation delivered under this Regulation. The Regulation sets the legal, budgetary and governance framework: it designates the Commission as programme owner, entrusts operational tasks to EUSPA, and establishes the public-private partnership model used to deliver the infrastructure.

IRIS2 was formally announced by the Council of the EU in November 2022 and the Regulation was adopted roughly four months later, reflecting the political urgency attached to secure connectivity following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine and growing concern over dependence on non-EU satellite operators.

## Governance

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **European Commission (DG DEFIS)** | Programme ownership, policy direction, budgetary oversight, international agreements (for example the EU-Norway-Iceland secure connectivity cooperation) |
| **EUSPA** (EU Agency for the Space Programme, Prague) | Operational management, market uptake, IRIS2 procurement support, and home of the Security Accreditation Board (SAB), which acts as the IRIS2 security accreditation authority |
| **ESA** | Technical and system-engineering support for development, drawing on decades of satcom and constellation expertise |
| **SpaceRISE consortium** | Concessionaire: designs, deploys and operates the infrastructure under a 12-year concession contract |
| **Member States** | National security accreditation input, PRS-style governmental user access arrangements, co-funding via ESA optional programmes |

## Constellation Architecture

IRIS2 is a multi-orbit system, combining the low-latency advantages of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) with the wide, stable coverage of Medium Earth Orbit (MEO):

| Shell | Altitude | Satellites | Band | Purpose |
|-------|----------|------------|------|---------|
| **MEO** | ~8,000 km | 18 | Ka-band | Governmental communications, wide-area coverage |
| **LEO High** | ~1,200 km | 264 | Ku- and Ka-band | Low-latency broadband, governmental and commercial services |
| **LEO Low** | 400-750 km | ~10 (or more) | -- | Incubation of future technologies |

Total: approximately 290 satellites, positioned to extend coverage over Europe, Africa and other partner regions, with resilience against jamming, spoofing and cyberattack built into the design.

## The SpaceRISE Concession

On 16 December 2024, the Commission and ESA signed a 12-year concession contract in Brussels with the SpaceRISE consortium to design, deliver and operate IRIS2. SpaceRISE is anchored by three European satellite operators -- **SES** (Luxembourg), **Eutelsat** (France, following its merger with OneWeb) and **Hispasat** (Spain) -- supported by an industrial base including Airbus Defence and Space, Thales Alenia Space, OHB, Telespazio, Deutsche Telekom, Orange, Hisdesat and Thales SIX. This is a public-private partnership (PPP) model: the concessionaire finances, builds and operates the constellation, recovering costs through a mix of EU/ESA payments for governmental services and commercial revenue.

## Services

**Governmental services** (restricted to authorised EU and Member State users):
- Encrypted, resilient communications for EU institutions and embassies
- Defence and crisis-management communications
- Border and maritime surveillance support
- Critical-infrastructure protection (energy grids, transport networks)
- Humanitarian aid coordination

**Commercial services** (open market):
- Broadband connectivity for underserved rural, remote, maritime and Arctic areas ("connectivity dead zones")
- B2B satellite trunking and backhaul
- Connectivity for transport, energy and banking sectors
- Cloud-based and IoT services

## Relationship to Other EU Space Programme Components

IRIS2 is the third pillar of EU space sovereignty alongside Galileo (satellite navigation) and Copernicus (Earth observation), all governed under the umbrella EU Space Programme Regulation (EU) 2021/696. It succeeds and expands on **GOVSATCOM**, the EU's first-generation governmental satellite communications pooling scheme, which IRIS2 absorbs and scales up. IRIS2 also integrates the space and ground segment of the **European Quantum Communication Infrastructure (EuroQCI)**, enabling quantum-secure transmission of cryptographic keys across the network -- a first for a sovereign satellite constellation of this scale.

Synergies are expected between IRIS2, Copernicus and Galileo for combined applications such as maritime emergency response and natural-disaster management, where secure communications, positioning and Earth observation data need to work together in real time.

## Strategic Rationale

- **Digital and technological sovereignty**: reducing EU dependence on non-EU satellite constellations, notably Starlink (US) and emerging Chinese mega-constellations
- **Resilience**: maintaining governmental and critical-infrastructure connectivity when terrestrial networks are degraded, attacked or unavailable
- **Territorial coverage**: closing connectivity gaps in rural, remote, maritime and Arctic regions of the EU and partner states
- **Defence and security synergies**: dual-use capacity supporting both civilian resilience and defence communications, linked to the wider European Defence Union effort
- **International outreach**: on 26 March 2026, DG DEFIS announced an agreement extending secure connectivity cooperation to Norway and Iceland (EEA EFTA states), strengthening Arctic coverage
