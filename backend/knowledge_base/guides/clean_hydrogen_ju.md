## Overview

The Clean Hydrogen Joint Undertaking is the EU's institutionalised public-private partnership dedicated to hydrogen research, technology development and demonstration. It sits at the layer between EU hydrogen *policy* (the Hydrogen Strategy, RED III renewable targets, RFNBO rules, the Gas and Hydrogen Market Decarbonisation Package) and the *beneficiaries* who actually build electrolysers, storage, pipelines and end-use applications. Brubru's existing energy guides (`eu_energy_policy`, `clean_energy_investment_strategy`, `citizens_energy_package`) cover the policy and investment-strategy layer; this guide fills the funding-delivery-body gap.

The JU is one of ten Joint Undertakings established under the Single Basic Act, Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085 of 19 November 2021, which sets common rules for all Horizon Europe institutionalised partnerships (Clean Hydrogen, Clean Aviation, Circular Bio-based Europe, Europe's Rail, Global Health EDCTP3, Innovative Health Initiative, Key Digital Technologies (Chips JU absorbed it in 2023), Single European Sky ATM Research 3, Smart Networks and Services, and the Metrology Partnership).

## Lineage: from FCH JU to Clean Hydrogen JU

| Period | Name | Legal basis | Framework programme | Budget |
|--------|------|--------------|----------------------|--------|
| 2008-2013 | Fuel Cells and Hydrogen Joint Undertaking (FCH JU) | Council Regulation (EC) 521/2008, 30 May 2008 | FP7 | EUR 940 million |
| 2014-2020 | Fuel Cells and Hydrogen 2 Joint Undertaking (FCH 2 JU) | Council Regulation (EU) 559/2014, launched 9 July 2014 | Horizon 2020 | EUR 1.33 billion |
| 2021-2027 | Clean Hydrogen Joint Undertaking (Clean Hydrogen Partnership) | Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085, 19 November 2021 | Horizon Europe | EUR 1 billion EU + matched private, over EUR 2 billion total |

Each generation has widened scope: FCH JU focused on fuel cells and hydrogen for transport and stationary power; FCH 2 JU added cross-cutting activities and scaled demonstration projects; the Clean Hydrogen JU explicitly folds in climate-neutrality and Green Deal objectives, plus the Hydrogen Valleys and European Hydrogen Safety Panel programme lines, and (from 2025) hydrogen sustainability and circularity work.

## Governance and members

Three members constitute the Joint Undertaking:
1. **The European Union**, represented by the European Commission.
2. **Hydrogen Europe**, representing the fuel cell and hydrogen industries (close to 60+ industry members historically, now larger).
3. **Hydrogen Europe Research**, representing the research community (60+ research organisation members historically).

Only members of Hydrogen Europe and Hydrogen Europe Research can contribute financially (in-kind or in cash) to the private side of the partnership; non-members cannot contribute directly or indirectly. Successful grant beneficiaries who are members contribute roughly 3.5% of their total grant toward the JU's administrative costs (the "programme contribution" mechanism).

Governance runs through a **Governing Board** (adopts the Annual Work Programme each year, referencing Article 33 of the JU Financial Rules) and an Executive Director supported by a Programme Office that manages calls for proposals, grant agreements and monitoring.

## Budget and funding structure

- EU contribution: **EUR 1 billion** for 2021-2027 under Horizon Europe (including EUR 30.2 million for administrative costs).
- Private members' contribution: matched at roughly a 1:1 ratio via in-kind contributions to projects and to "Additional Activities" (IKAA), pushing the total partnership budget to **over EUR 2 billion**.
- REPowerEU top-up: **EUR 200 million** (May 2022) earmarked specifically for Hydrogen Valleys, again matched by private partners.
- Funding is delivered through annual calls for proposals (Horizon Europe grants), published and evaluated by the Programme Office.

## Mission and focus areas

Per Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085, the Clean Hydrogen JU's main objective is to contribute to the EU's climate neutrality goal, the European Green Deal, the European Climate Law and the implementation of the EU Hydrogen Strategy. Its Strategic Research and Innovation Agenda (SRIA 2021-2027, adopted February 2022) organises activities around:
- Renewable hydrogen production (principally electrolysis)
- Hydrogen storage, transmission and distribution
- Hydrogen end-uses in transport (heavy-duty, maritime, rail, aviation-adjacent research)
- Hydrogen end-uses in clean heat and power for buildings and industry
- Cross-cutting activities (education, standards, socio-economic analysis)
- Hydrogen Valleys (regional ecosystem deployment)
- Hydrogen safety (via the EHSP) and, from 2025, hydrogen sustainability and circularity

As with all Joint Undertakings under the Single Basic Act, the JU also carries the common objective of strengthening the Union's scientific and technological capacity and contributing to a reinforced European Research Area.

## Hydrogen Valleys

Hydrogen Valleys are regional or local ecosystems where multiple hydrogen end-uses (mobility, industry, energy/heat) share common production, storage and distribution infrastructure, typically anchored to dedicated renewable energy sources. They are the most visible, most fundable and most frequently asked-about Clean Hydrogen JU programme line.

- **2030 target**: at least 50 Hydrogen Valleys operational or under construction across the EU.
- **Current portfolio** (verify live): the Clean Hydrogen Partnership has supported 27 Hydrogen Valley projects across 23 European countries, 26 still ongoing, representing over EUR 1.6 billion in total project cost and EUR 328 million in JU funding. Split roughly 9 large-scale (over 4,000 tonnes/year) and 18 small-scale (over 500 tonnes/year).
- **REPowerEU boost**: EUR 200 million added specifically to double the number of Hydrogen Valleys, matched by private partners.
- **Supporting infrastructure**: the Hydrogen Valleys Platform 2.0 (global data hub on advanced valley projects), the H2V Facility (Project Development Assistance toward Final Investment Decision), annual Hydrogen Valleys Days conferences, and the H2V Knowledge Centre (h2v.eu, launched 2025).

Hydrogen Valleys calls run through the JU's standard annual Calls for Proposals; funding stacks with national and regional (including Cohesion and RRF) resources in many Member States.

## European Hydrogen Observatory

Relaunched in October 2023 at `observatory.clean-hydrogen.europa.eu`, the European Hydrogen Observatory is the JU's reference data platform, tracking EU hydrogen production, demand, investment, trade, infrastructure and policy developments. It publishes datasets and reports used across the Commission, industry and research community as the go-to source for hydrogen market intelligence, separate from (but linked to) the JU's own project-level Knowledge Hub data-collection tool (which replaced the earlier TRUST platform).

## European Hydrogen Safety Panel (EHSP)

Launched in 2017 under the FCH 2 JU, the EHSP continues its activity under the Clean Hydrogen JU (formal continuation confirmed for 2025 onward), coordinated by a consortium led by IDOM and ARIEMA handling administrative, financial and technical management.

- **Role**: provides independent safety expertise to the Clean Hydrogen JU at both project level (reviewing individual grant applications and demonstrators) and programme level (cross-cutting safety guidance).
- **Function**: acts as a knowledge hub for hydrogen safety, promoting scientifically backed safety measures and hydrogen-safety education, aiming to make hydrogen technologies as safe as conventional energy carriers.
- **HIAD interface**: the panel's work connects to the Hydrogen Incident and Accident Database (HIAD), a JRC-run database (Joint Research Centre, Institute for Energy and Transport) collecting reported hydrogen safety events; FCH-funded demonstration projects have been committed to reporting into HIAD since 2013.
- **Standards interface**: EHSP expertise feeds into and draws from CEN-CENELEC (EU standardisation) and ISO/TC 197 (the international technical committee for hydrogen technologies), helping align EU-funded demonstrators with emerging technical standards.
- **Contact**: ehsp-chju@idom.com; dedicated site ehsp.eu.

## Annual Programme Review

The Governing Board adopts an **Annual Work Programme** each year (in accordance with Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085 and Article 33 of the JU's Financial Rules), published after adoption. This sets out the mission statement, scientific priorities per focus area (production, storage/distribution, transport end-use, heat/power end-use, cross-cutting, Hydrogen Valleys, hydrogen safety, and hydrogen sustainability/circularity), the operational budget, and the coming year's calls for proposals. A Programme Review Report (an annual technical progress review against the SRIA) is also published, distinct from the Work Programme itself.

## Adjacent EU hydrogen instruments (not part of the JU)

These are frequently confused with the Clean Hydrogen JU but sit in separate legal and institutional tracks:

- **European Hydrogen Bank**: a financing instrument launched in 2022 to unlock private investment in renewable hydrogen production via competitive "premium" auctions, run by CINEA (the Commission's executive agency), not the Clean Hydrogen JU. First pilot auction (launched November 2023, closed February 2024): nearly EUR 720 million awarded to 7 projects from 132 bids across 17 countries. A third auction round awarded over EUR 1 billion (announced 7 May 2026, see `eu_energy_policy` guide). An "auction-as-a-service" scheme lets Member States channel national top-up funding through the same mechanism (Spain, Lithuania, Austria announced over EUR 700 million combined, 18 November 2024).
- **IPCEI Hydrogen** (State aid track, DG COMP, Article 107(3)(b) TFEU): four Important Projects of Common European Interest -- Hy2Tech (approved 15 July 2022, up to EUR 5.4 billion public support / EUR 9 billion private investment unlocked, 15 Member States), Hy2Use (approved September 2022, up to EUR 5.2 billion / EUR 7 billion), Hy2Infra (approved 16 February 2024, up to EUR 6.9 billion / EUR 5.4 billion, 7 Member States), and Hy2Move. Combined: 99 companies across 16 Member States plus Norway, up to EUR 18.9 billion State aid expected to unlock over EUR 27.1 billion additional private investment.
- **RFNBO Delegated Act**: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/1184 (CELEX 32023R1184) -- sets the methodology (additionality, temporal correlation, geographic correlation) for renewable hydrogen and other Renewable Fuels of Non-Biological Origin under RED III. Under review in 2026 following industry pressure (see `eu_energy_policy` guide).
- **EU Hydrogen Strategy** and **Gas and Hydrogen Market Decarbonisation Package** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1789 and Directive (EU) 2024/1788, establishing ENNOH, the European Network of Network Operators for Hydrogen) set the overarching policy and market framework the JU operates within.

Do not conflate JU grant funding (Horizon Europe R&I, competitive calls, EUR 1 billion 2021-2027) with Hydrogen Bank auction premiums (production subsidy, CINEA) or IPCEI State aid (national public funding approved under EU competition rules). They are complementary but legally and administratively distinct.
