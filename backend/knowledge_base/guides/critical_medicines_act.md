# Critical Medicines Act (COM(2025) 102, procedure 2025/0102(COD))

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: a proposed EU Regulation "laying a framework for strengthening the availability and security of supply of critical medicinal products as well as the availability of, and accessibility of, medicinal products of common interest, and amending Regulation (EU) 2024/795". Short name: Critical Medicines Act (CMA).
- **Reference**: COM(2025) 102 final, Strasbourg 11 March 2025. Procedure 2025/0102(COD). EEA relevance. Amends Regulation (EU) 2024/795 (the STEP Regulation).
- **Legal basis**: Article 114 TFEU (internal market), taking as a base a high level of health protection (Article 114(3) TFEU). (Single legal basis, Article 114, not Article 168.)
- **Responsible**: European Commission, DG SANTE; Commissioner Olivér Varhelyi (Health and Animal Welfare).
- **Lead EP committee**: SANT (Public Health). **Rapporteur: Tomislav Sokol (EPP, Croatia)**, appointed 2 May 2025. Public Health Committee Chair: Adam Jarubas (EPP, Poland).
- **Shadow rapporteurs**: Tiemo Wolken (S&D), Ondrej Knotek (PfE), Aurelijus Veryga (ECR), Vlad Vasile-Voiculescu (Renew), Tilly Metz (Greens/EFA), Catarina Martins (The Left), Christine Anderson (ESN).
- **Opinion committees**: ENVI (rapporteur Michele Picaro, ECR), ITRE (Oliver Schenk, EPP), IMCO (Marion Walsmann, EPP).
- **STATUS (late May 2026): PROVISIONAL AGREEMENT.** Council and Parliament reached a provisional inter-institutional deal on 11-12 May 2026 under the Cyprus Council Presidency. It is NOT yet adopted: it must still be endorsed and then formally adopted by both institutions after legal-linguistic revision. Distinguish carefully: provisional trilogue deal, not law yet.
- **Why it exists**: endemic shortages of critical medicines (antibiotics, insulin, painkillers) and heavy EU dependence on non-EU suppliers for active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). A CHESSMEN joint action found over 50% of reported shortages stem from manufacturing issues. The EU Union List of Critical Medicines (first published December 2023, updated December 2024) contains 276 critical active substances. A pilot of 11 critical molecules found non-EU API dependence for 4 of them.
- **Four objectives**: (1) facilitate investment in EU manufacturing capacity for critical medicines, their active substances and key inputs; (2) lower supply-disruption risk by incentivising supply-chain diversification and resilience in public procurement; (3) leverage aggregated Member State demand via collaborative procurement; (4) support diversification through strategic international partnerships.
- **Key mechanisms**: Strategic Projects (recognised at Member State level; fast-track permitting, priority status, access to national and EU funding); demand-side procurement rules (resilience requirements, award criteria beyond price / MEAT, an "EU preference" favouring suppliers manufacturing a significant share in the EU); collaborative and joint procurement; a Critical Medicines Coordination Group (Commission plus Member States); international strategic partnerships; medicines of common interest (MPCI); orphan medicines brought into scope of strategic projects and collaborative procurement.
- **Headline trilogue change**: the threshold of Member States needed to ask the Commission to procure on their behalf was reduced from 9 to 5.
- **EU preference (procurement)**: companies that produce medicines and their active ingredients in Europe will be favoured in procurement procedures, with flexibility for contracting authorities; firms receiving support must prioritise EU-market supply.
- **Budget**: indicative EUR 83.02 million for 2026-2027 under Heading 2b, redeployed within the EU4Health envelope, plus EUR 1.4 million for EMA and EUR 5.5 million under Heading 7 (staff and missions). Strategic Projects may also draw on EU4Health, Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe programme.
- **EP first reading**: position adopted 20 January 2026 with 503 votes in favour (report A-10-2025-0272; adopted text P10_TA(2026)0001). **Council general approach**: 2 December 2025. Trilogues: February and March 2026. Provisional deal: 11-12 May 2026.
- **Relation to other files**: complements the revision of the EU pharmaceutical legislation (the "pharmaceutical package", 2023/0131(COD) Regulation and 2023/0132(COD) Directive), builds on the EMA extended mandate (Regulation (EU) 2022/123) and the European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP), the Union List of Critical Medicines, and the Critical Medicines Alliance (launched April 2024, 300+ organisations, Strategic Report 28 February 2025).
- **Brubru explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers)**: https://brubru.beresol.eu/critical-medicines-act/index.html
- **Official sources**: OEIL procedure file 2025/0102(COD); Commission page health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/critical-medicines-act_en; EPRS briefing "Critical Medicines Act" (May 2025, ref 772842) and plenary briefing (January 2026, ref 782598); Council press release 12 May 2026; EP press release 11 May 2026 (20260511IPR43110).
- **Why it matters for industry / EFPIA**: shifts EU procurement toward resilience and EU-manufacturing preference, creates a fast-track investment route (Strategic Projects), and formalises joint procurement. Directly relevant to shortage management, supply-chain resilience and the competitiveness of EU pharmaceutical manufacturing.

## Overview

The Critical Medicines Act is the EU's industrial-policy answer to medicine shortages. Where the revised pharmaceutical legislation strengthens marketing-authorisation-holder obligations to prevent and report shortages, the CMA works on the supply side: it tries to bring manufacturing of critical medicines and their active ingredients back into, or closer to, Europe, and to make Member State procurement reward security of supply rather than only the lowest price.

It rests on three pillars: enabling investment (Strategic Projects with fast-track permitting and access to funding), shaping demand (procurement rules with resilience requirements and an EU-manufacturing preference, plus collaborative and joint procurement), and coordination (a Critical Medicines Coordination Group and international strategic partnerships). The scope centres on the Union List of Critical Medicines, with several provisions extended to medicines of common interest that are insufficiently available across several Member States, and (after Parliament's input) to orphan medicines for rare diseases.

The proposal responds to EU dependence on a limited number of non-EU suppliers (notably in China and India) for active pharmaceutical ingredients and finished medicines, a vulnerability exposed during the COVID-19 pandemic and sharpened by geopolitical instability. As of late May 2026 the file is at provisional-agreement stage: co-legislators struck a deal on 11-12 May 2026 under the Cyprus Presidency. It still needs formal endorsement and adoption.

## Complete chapter-by-chapter overview

### Chapter I -- General provisions
Objectives, subject matter and scope, plus the key definitions used throughout (critical medicine, medicinal product of common interest, strategic project, security of supply). Scope centres on the Union List of Critical Medicines (established under the proposed Pharmaceutical Regulation), with some provisions reaching medicines of common interest facing market-access issues in several Member States.

### Chapter II -- Strengthening the EU's security of supply
Establishes security of supply and availability of critical medicines as an EU strategic objective requiring a coordinated approach between Member States and the Commission.

### Chapter III -- Enabling conditions for investment (Strategic Projects)
- **Section I -- Recognition**: criteria and procedure for recognising a project as a Strategic Project; a designated Member State authority assesses and confirms, on request, whether a project meets the criteria.
- **Section II -- Priority status and permitting**: Strategic Projects in the public interest get priority status and fast-tracked permit-granting; they may request the status of highest national significance and a coordinated or joint environmental-assessment procedure, plus administrative, regulatory and scientific support.
- **Section III -- Financial incentives**: Member States may prioritise financial support for Strategic Projects that address a supply-chain vulnerability; EU funding may be available under the current MFF; information on supported projects is exchanged via the Critical Medicines Coordination Group.

### Chapter IV -- Demand-side measures
- **Section I -- Award criteria and procurement requirements**: imposes procurement requirements other than price (most economically advantageous tender, MEAT); in specific cases, where justified by a vulnerability analysis, contracting authorities apply requirements that favour suppliers manufacturing a significant portion of the critical medicine in the EU (the "EU preference"), respecting the Union's international commitments; Member States develop national programmes for security of supply via procurement, pricing and reimbursement; any contingency-stock requirements must be proportionate and respect transparency and solidarity.
- **Section II -- Collaborative procurement**: a framework for Member States to request Commission support for cross-border procurement, procurement on behalf of or in the name of Member States, and Joint Procurement.

### Chapter V -- Critical Medicines Coordination Group
Composed of the Commission and Member State representatives; facilitates application of the Regulation, including strategic orientation for financing Strategic Projects, cooperation on national procurement policies, collaborative-procurement initiatives, advice on the priority order for vulnerability evaluation, and discussion of strategic partnerships.

### Chapter VI -- International cooperation
Requires the Commission to investigate establishing strategic partnerships with third countries and organisations to diversify supply.

### Chapter VII -- Final provisions
Contains the amendments to Regulation (EU) 2024/795 (STEP).

### Chapter VIII -- Information, evaluation, entry into force
Obliges market actors to provide the information needed to apply the Regulation; sets the evaluation timeline (Commission evaluation five years after the date of application and every five years thereafter); sets entry into force and application dates.

## What the provisional agreement (11-12 May 2026) settled
- **Public procurement**: an obligation for contracting authorities to apply resilience-related requirements for critical medicines; an "EU preference" approach with flexibility for contracting authorities to incentivise EU-based manufacturing of critical medicines and their active ingredients.
- **Collaborative procurement**: Member States can join forces to procure; the threshold to request the Commission to procure on their behalf was reduced from 9 to 5 Member States.
- **Contingency stocks**: requirements must be transparent and respect solidarity and proportionality; new measures let Member States share information; the existing Voluntary Solidarity Mechanism (VSM) is clarified for exchanging stock data and voluntarily reallocating critical medicines.
- **Orphan medicines**: scope expanded so orphan medicinal products (conditions affecting no more than 5 in 10,000 people in the EU) can benefit from strategic projects and collaborative procurement.

## Impact assessment and key figures
- No formal ex-ante impact assessment (the Commission cited urgency); a staff working document summarising evidence followed within three months of publication.
- ~800,000 people directly employed by the EU pharmaceutical sector.
- ~70% of medicines dispensed in Europe are generics; production of generic inputs has increasingly moved outside Europe.
- Over 50% of reported shortages are caused by manufacturing issues (CHESSMEN joint action): manufacturing ~50.6%, unexpected increased demand ~16.7%, commercial reasons ~11.2%, distribution ~10.6%, others ~6.9%, quality ~2.8%, regulatory ~1.3%.
- Union List of Critical Medicines: 276 critical active substances.
- Pilot vulnerability assessment of 11 critical molecules: significant non-EU API dependence for 4 of the 11.
- Call for Evidence (30 January 2025): 121 valid contributions; business associations 26%, companies 25%, NGOs 22%; Belgium most represented (32%), Germany 15%, France 7%, Italy 6%, Spain 5%.

## Stakeholder context
The Critical Medicines Alliance (300+ organisations, including industry, patients, health professionals and Member States) recommended a European list of critical vulnerable medicines, a European investment plan combining EU funding and State aid, a harmonised contingency-stocks framework, virtuous public-procurement practices (MEAT criteria and more joint procurement), and partnerships with non-EU countries. EFPIA frames the CMA as essential support for EU reindustrialisation and competitiveness alongside the pharmaceutical package, while stressing it should preserve Europe's pharmaceutical innovation leadership and avoid excessive burden; the file is closely watched for how the "EU preference" and resilience requirements reshape national tenders.
