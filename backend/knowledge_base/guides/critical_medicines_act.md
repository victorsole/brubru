## Overview

The Critical Medicines Act addresses medicine shortages and supply chain vulnerabilities in the EU. It was announced in the Commission's 2025 Work Programme and the proposal was published on 11 March 2025. The Act complements the EU Pharmaceutical Legislation Reform by focusing specifically on supply chain resilience rather than the broader regulatory framework for medicines.

The proposal responds to growing concerns about EU dependence on a limited number of suppliers (often in China and India) for active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and finished medicines, exposed during the COVID-19 pandemic and worsened by geopolitical instability.

## Four Pillars

### 1. Industrial Strategic Projects
- Designation of manufacturing projects in the EU that create, increase, or modernise production capacity for critical medicines or their active substances
- Financial incentives and easier access to EU funds for designated projects
- Covers both new capacity and modernisation of existing facilities
- Includes projects contributing to roll-out of key manufacturing technologies

### 2. Public Procurement for Resilience
- New rules to strengthen supply chain resilience through public procurement
- Criteria beyond lowest price: supply security, diversification of sources, EU manufacturing capacity
- Applies to critical medicines and "other medicines of common interest"

### 3. International Strategic Partnerships
- Formal partnerships between the EU and third countries, groups of countries, or international organisations
- Aim: broaden supply chains and reduce dependence on single suppliers
- Diversification away from concentrated API sources

### 4. Critical Medicines Alliance
- Multi-stakeholder platform bringing together industry, Member States, health authorities
- Coordinates implementation of the Act
- Maintains and updates the Union list of critical medicines

## Union List of Critical Medicines

The Act establishes a formal list of medicines considered critical for EU supply security. The list is maintained and updated by the Critical Medicines Alliance based on criteria including:
- Patient health impact of shortages
- Concentration of supply sources
- Manufacturing complexity
- Substitutability

## Stakeholder Positions

**EFPIA** (European Federation of Pharmaceutical Industries) supports the core objectives but emphasises the need for the Act to maintain Europe's pharmaceutical innovation leadership alongside supply security. They call for the Act to be integrated with broader industrial policy and avoid creating excessive regulatory burden.
