# EU Foreign Direct Investment Screening Regulation

## QUICK FACTS
- **EPRS At a Glance (10 May 2026, EP Think Tank portal)**: "**Screening of foreign investments in the EU**" — briefing for the May plenary on the recast Foreign Direct Investment Screening proposal, the Member State + Commission cooperation mechanism, and the outbound-investment review track.
- **Current Regulation**: **Regulation (EU) 2019/452** of 19 March 2019 establishing a framework for the screening of foreign direct investments into the Union (CELEX **32019R0452**) — applicable since 11 October 2020
- **Recast proposal (under negotiation)**: **COM(2024) 23 final** — Proposal for a Regulation on the screening of foreign investments in the Union, repealing and replacing Reg (EU) 2019/452 (procedure 2024/0017(COD))
- **Adopted by Commission**: 24 January 2024 (part of the Economic Security package together with the Outbound Investment White Paper, the Dual-Use Goods proposals, and the Research Security proposal)
- **Lead EP committee**: INTA (International Trade); ITRE + JURI opinions
- **Council configuration**: FAC TRADE (Foreign Affairs Council, Trade configuration)
- **Lead DG**: DG TRADE (Trade)
- **Commissioner**: Maroš Šefčovič (Trade and Economic Security, Slovakia)
- **Legal basis**: Articles 207 + 114 TFEU (common commercial policy + internal market)
- **Type**: Regulation (directly applicable; full harmonisation of the screening-cooperation mechanism)

## What the 2019 Regulation does (current legal framework)

Regulation (EU) 2019/452 does **not** harmonise national FDI-screening rules. Each Member State remains free to adopt — or not adopt — its own national FDI-screening regime. The Regulation establishes a **cooperation mechanism** by which:
- Member States that adopt national screening mechanisms must notify the Commission + other Member States of FDI under screening
- The Commission + any Member State can issue **opinions / comments** on a screening case in another Member State
- Member States retain final decision-making authority on individual cases
- The Regulation lists illustrative factors (Article 4) Member States may consider: critical infrastructure, critical technologies + dual-use items, supply of critical inputs, access to sensitive information including personal data, freedom + pluralism of media, ownership/control by a foreign government or state-owned enterprise

As of May 2026, **24 of 27 Member States have national FDI-screening regimes** (up from 14 in 2019); Greece, Bulgaria, and Croatia remain holdouts.

## Why the recast (COM(2024) 23)

The 2023 Commission review of Reg 2019/452 (COM(2023) 590) flagged five categories of gap:

1. **Mandatory screening minimum**: not all Member States screen; the recast proposes that ALL Member States must operate an FDI-screening mechanism with a **minimum scope** covering specified critical sectors
2. **Intra-EU transactions with foreign ultimate beneficial owners**: the 2019 Regulation excluded intra-EU FDI; the recast proposes that intra-EU FDI by EU subsidiaries of non-EU undertakings should be in scope
3. **Mandatory sectoral coverage**: the recast lists sectors that ALL Member States MUST screen — military + dual-use, critical raw materials, semiconductors, AI, quantum, biotech, advanced connectivity (5G/6G), aerospace
4. **Improved cooperation mechanism**: tighter timelines, harmonised information requirements, streamlined Commission opinion procedure
5. **Greenfield investment**: explicit inclusion of greenfield projects (not only M&A)

## State of play (May 2026)

- Council adopted a General Approach in late 2024 under the Hungarian presidency, accepting most of the Commission proposal with carve-outs on the intra-EU UBO chain
- EP INTA committee adopted its report in mid-2025; co-rapporteur identity to confirm in the current EP committee tracker
- Trilogues opened during the Polish presidency (H1 2025) and continued into the Belgian / Cypriot rotation
- The **10 May 2026 EPRS at-a-glance** is the canonical EP-side state-of-play briefing for the May plenary

## Wider Economic Security context

FDI screening is one pillar of the **Economic Security Strategy** (JOIN(2023) 20, 20 June 2023) alongside:
- **Outbound investment review** (Commission White Paper COM(2024) 24 — not yet a legislative proposal)
- **Export controls** (Reg (EU) 2021/821 on dual-use items — under review)
- **Research security** (Council Recommendation 2024/3014 on enhancing research security)
- **Critical technologies list** (Commission Recommendation 2023/2113 of 3 October 2023, 10 critical technology areas)
- **Anti-coercion Instrument** (Reg (EU) 2023/2675)
- **Foreign Subsidies Regulation** (Reg (EU) 2022/2560)
- **International Procurement Instrument** (Reg (EU) 2022/1031)

The economic-security package is structurally about **de-risking** without **decoupling** — the framing introduced by the 2023 Commission Communication and reinforced in the 2025 Competitiveness Compass.
