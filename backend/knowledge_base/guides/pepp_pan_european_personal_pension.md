# Pan-European Personal Pension Product (PEPP) and IORP II Pension Framework

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: the EU's two supplementary (non-state) pension frameworks -- personal pensions (PEPP) and occupational/workplace pensions (IORP II)
- PEPP: Regulation (EU) 2019/1238 (CELEX 32019R1238), adopted 20 June 2019, OJ L 198, 25.7.2019, applicable from 22 March 2022
- PEPP nature: a voluntary, portable personal-pension product with an EU-wide passport, sold alongside national personal-pension products
- PEPP providers (6 types): credit institutions, life insurers, IORPs, portfolio-management investment firms, UCITS management companies, EU AIFMs -- register with a national competent authority (3-month review); EIOPA runs the central register at pepp.eiopa.europa.eu
- Basic PEPP (default option): costs capped at 1% of accumulated capital per year, a capital guarantee or equivalent risk-mitigation technique, provider switching every 5 years at capped cost
- PEPP portability: national "sub-accounts" let savers keep one contract while moving Member State, but full sub-account coverage across all 27 states has lagged
- PEPP take-up since March 2022: very low -- only 21 entities showed interest in a Feb 2022 EIOPA survey; fewer than 100,000 holders EU-wide by end-2025
- IORP II: Directive (EU) 2016/2341 (CELEX 32016L2341), adopted 14 December 2016, in force 12 January 2017, transposed by all Member States; recasts the 2003 IORP Directive
- IORP II scope: governs Institutions for Occupational Retirement Provision (IORPs) -- workplace (second-pillar) pension funds
- IORP II core rules: governance system, prudent-person investment rule, cross-border activity (one IORP for a multi-country employer group), member pension-benefit statements
- Supervisor: EIOPA (Frankfurt) for supervisory convergence on both frameworks; national competent authorities for day-to-day supervision
- Supplementary pensions package (Commission, 20 November 2025): COM(2025) 842 / procedure 2025/0362(COD) amends IORP II + Insurance Distribution Directive 2016/97; COM(2025) 840 / procedure 2025/0363(COD) amends PEPP; plus a Recommendation on pension tracking, dashboards and auto-enrolment
- PEPP reform direction: removes the mandatory 1% fee cap and advice requirement for Basic PEPP to widen provider appeal, while keeping consumer-protection safeguards
- IORP II reform direction: better solvency-margin capture of market/longevity risk, simpler cross-border mergers, a duty to explain material underperformance against benchmark
- Context figure: only 1 in 5 EU working-age people have an occupational or personal pension (Commission, 20 Nov 2025)
- Both files sit in Pillar 4 (pension capital) of the Savings and Investments Union -- see `savings_and_investment_union`
- Status (verify live): both proposals were in early Council/EP (ECON) scrutiny through mid-2026 -- check OEIL 2025/0362(COD) and 2025/0363(COD) before citing a vote or adoption date

## Why two separate frameworks

The EU distinguishes three pension "pillars": Pillar 1 is state/statutory pensions (a Member State competence, outside EU harmonisation); Pillar 2 is occupational/workplace pensions, governed by IORP II; Pillar 3 is personal/individual pensions, which PEPP supplements at EU level alongside pre-existing national personal-pension products. IORP II regulates the fund vehicle an employer or sector sets up; PEPP regulates a specific portable product an individual can buy directly, independent of any employer relationship. Both frameworks answer to EIOPA but sit in different parts of a saver's life: IORP II is about how a workplace pension fund is governed and supervised, PEPP is about giving an individual a single product that survives a job or country change.

## PEPP structure

A PEPP provider must offer a **Basic PEPP** as the default investment option unless the saver actively chooses otherwise. The Basic PEPP is deliberately simple: capped costs (1% of accumulated capital per year, all fees included), and either a capital guarantee at the point of retirement or a risk-mitigation technique calibrated to achieve a similar outcome. Providers may also offer up to five additional, more complex investment options, but these sit outside the Basic PEPP's protections.

Two features distinguish PEPP from ordinary national pension products:
- **The EU passport**: once registered with one national competent authority, a provider can market the same PEPP across all Member States, subject to notifying local supervisors and adapting to national sub-account requirements.
- **National sub-accounts**: because personal-pension tax treatment remains a Member State competence, a single PEPP contract is designed to hold country-specific "compartments" so that moving to another Member State does not force the saver to open a new product, only a new sub-account within the same contract. In practice, sub-account availability across all 27 Member States has lagged, undermining the portability promise that was PEPP's main selling point, and is one of the specific gaps the November 2025 review proposal targets.

## IORP II structure

IORP II applies to occupational pension funds ("IORPs"), not to the employers or scheme members directly. Its central obligations are:
- **Governance**: a system of governance proportionate to the IORP's size and complexity, including risk-management, internal-audit and (where relevant) actuarial functions, fit-and-proper requirements for those running the fund, and an own-risk assessment.
- **Prudent-person investment rule**: assets must be invested in the best long-term interests of members and beneficiaries, predominantly on regulated markets, with derivatives used only to reduce risk or facilitate efficient portfolio management, and no fixed quantitative asset-allocation limits beyond a prudence test.
- **Cross-border activity**: an IORP can run occupational schemes for a sponsoring undertaking established in a different Member State, provided it obtains prior authorisation from its home supervisor and satisfies the host Member State's social and labour law.
- **Information to members**: standardised pension benefit statements telling members and beneficiaries what they are entitled to and how their fund is performing.

## The November 2025 supplementary pensions package

On 20 November 2025 the Commission adopted a package intended to move both frameworks from Communication-level ambition (the March 2025 Savings and Investments Union Communication) into concrete legislative text:
- A **Directive proposal**, COM(2025) 842 / procedure 2025/0362(COD), amending IORP II (Directive 2016/2341) and the Insurance Distribution Directive (2016/97), aimed at strengthening solvency-margin adequacy for IORPs that underwrite biometric risk or guarantee performance, simplifying cross-border merger and transfer procedures, and requiring funds to explain material underperformance against their stated benchmark.
- A **Regulation proposal**, COM(2025) 840 / procedure 2025/0363(COD), amending the PEPP Regulation, aimed at removing the mandatory 1% fee cap and mandatory-advice requirement for the Basic PEPP (both identified as barriers to provider entry), while reinforcing product-governance and comparability rules to preserve consumer protection.
- A **Recommendation** to Member States on pension tracking systems, pension dashboards, and auto-enrolment into supplementary pension schemes (with an opt-out preserved), designed to raise the currently low share of the working-age population with any supplementary pension coverage.

Industry reaction has split along familiar lines: asset managers and insurers (EFAMA, Insurance Europe, PensionsEurope) broadly welcomed the package as addressing a genuine scale problem, while some national pension-fund associations pushed back on the proposed performance-benchmarking disclosure duty as potentially encouraging short-termism or index-hugging. Both files follow the ordinary legislative procedure, with ECON as the European Parliament's lead committee and ECOFIN as the Council configuration.
