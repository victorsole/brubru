# Affordable Housing Act (proposed Regulation)

## QUICK FACTS
- **PROPOSED Wednesday 9 September 2026. It is a PROPOSAL, not law.** Nothing in it binds anyone yet. It now goes to the European Parliament and the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure.
- **Brubru deep dive (public, six languages)**: https://brubru.beresol.eu/affordable-housing-act/
- Full name: **Regulation establishing a framework for measures in Member States to safeguard housing affordability and availability (Affordable Housing Act)**. **COM(2026) 599 final**, procedure **2026/0268(COD)**, legal basis **Article 114 TFEU**, EEA relevance.
- **What it actually does, and this is the most misunderstood point: it does NOT regulate housing.** It regulates how a public authority must **justify** restricting the non-primary use of housing. It creates **no new power** to restrict anything, does not require anyone to act, and does not prescribe any measure. Housing policy stays national, regional and local.
- **The housing stress test (Article 6)**, the operative core. An area qualifies when: **price-to-income ratio is 8 or higher** (eight years of disposable income per capita to buy an average dwelling), **AND** the ratio has **risen over the most recent 10 years** of data, **AND** stress is **unlikely to ease within 3 years** judged on population, supply and demand. **If the ratio reaches 10 or higher, the rising-trend condition does not apply.**
- **Annex methodology**: dwelling prices at **NUTS 3**, disposable income per capita at **NUTS 2**. Commission supplies the series through its **Mapadomo** housing database. Authorities may use finer data reaching the same thresholds. Thresholds and methodology are amendable by **delegated act**.
- **Scope (Article 2)**: only measures restricting (a) short-term accommodation rental services and (b) acquisition or use of land and residential property **not used as a primary residence**, and only when justified by housing affordability and availability.
- **A host's own primary residence can NEVER be restricted under this Regulation** (Article 5(2)). That is a floor, not a ceiling: authorities may go further under national law, but not through this instrument.
- **Key conditions**: three years of demonstrated significant adverse effect (Art 9); no less restrictive alternative (Art 10); short-term-rental measures must target scale, frequency or commercial character AND the authority must be **enforcing Regulation (EU) 2024/1028**, including removing unregistered listings; acquisition rules are **never retroactive**; vacancy measures must exempt justified non-occupation (work, study, health, ongoing succession, legal impediment, uninhabitability).
- **Duration**: maximum **five years**, reviewable and extendable, withdrawn without undue delay if conditions lapse (Art 12). Everything must be **published before entry into force**, including the assessment, exact territory and duration (Art 13).
- **Out of scope**: rent regulation in tenancy contracts, housing benefits, taxation of general application, building standards, general zoning and spatial planning, inheritance, gifts and family transfers. Also out of scope: measures taken for other overriding public interest reasons (public policy, security, health, consumer protection, environment, cultural heritage).
- **It displaces the Services Directive** for in-scope short-term-rental measures, including removing the **Article 15(7) notification obligation**.
- **All 17 articles, one line each**: 1 subject matter; 2 scope; 3 disapplies the Services Directive including its Article 15(7) notification duty; 4 ten definitions, where **acquisition excludes** inheritance, matrimonial property regimes, division of an estate, corporate reorganisations not changing beneficial ownership, and a creditor realising security; 5 the two permitted measures, host's primary residence excluded; 6 the housing stress test; 7 territory no wider than necessary, from a district to a rural area; 8 non-discrimination on nationality or place of establishment; 9 necessity, and the three-year adverse-effect proof; 10 proportionality, seven cumulative requirements; 11 procedural safeguards and effective judicial review, including challenging the authority's own assessments; 12 five-year maximum, review, withdrawal; 13 publication before entry into force; 14 measures adopted before entry into force are untouched; 15 exercise of the delegation, two-month objection extendable by two; 16 Commission evaluation after five years; 17 entry into force on the twentieth day, directly applicable.

## Why the EU is involved at all

Housing policy is a national, regional and local competence. But a restriction on who may let, buy or
leave empty a dwelling engages **Articles 49, 56 and 63 TFEU** (establishment, services, capital), so
it is already governed by Union law. The Court of Justice has accepted that safeguarding housing
affordability can be an overriding reason in the public interest justifying such restrictions, most
prominently in **Cali Apartments** (Joined Cases C-724/18 and C-727/18, 22 September 2020). What has
never existed is a **method**: how to demonstrate stress, how to show proportionality, how wide to
draw the map.

The consequence, quantified in the impact assessment: **several hundred to low thousands of cases**
across the Union, nearly 100 against a single Barcelona plan, **420 in Paris frozen pending Cali
Apartments**, and **EUR 15,000 to EUR 164,000 per case** for the defending authority.

## Complete article-by-article overview

**Chapter 1, general provisions (Articles 1 to 4)**

- **Article 1, subject matter.** A common framework so that measures safeguarding housing affordability
  and availability are justified, transparent, proportionate and subject to review.
- **Article 2, scope.** Two families of measure only, and only where housing affordability is the reason:
  short-term accommodation rental restrictions, and restrictions on acquiring or using land and
  residential property that is not a primary residence. A measure resting solely on some other
  overriding public-interest ground falls outside.
- **Article 3, relationship with other Union acts.** Short-term rental measures are taken out of Directive
  2006/123/EC, expressly including the Article 15(7) notification. Measures that merely apply the
  registration chapter of Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 are untouched.
- **Article 4, definitions.** Ten of them. A competent authority may be national, regional or local.

**Chapter 2, the measures (Articles 5 to 13)**

- **Article 5, requirements.** The two permitted measures, plus the rule that a short-term rental
  restriction may only reach accommodation not provided by the host in their own primary residence.
- **Article 6, assessment of housing stress.** Ratio at or above 8; risen over the most recent 10 years;
  unlikely to ease within 3 years on population dynamics, supply and demand. At 10 or above the trend
  test drops away. Objective, transparent, verifiable data; Annex methodology; finer local data allowed
  if consistent with the thresholds. Thresholds and Annex amendable by delegated act.
- **Article 7, territorial scope.** No wider than necessary: district, municipality, metropolitan area,
  functional urban area, agglomeration, rural area, or any part of one.
- **Article 8, non-discrimination.** No direct or indirect discrimination on nationality, nor on a legal
  person's Member State of establishment.
- **Article 9, necessity.** Significant adverse effect on availability or affordability over at least
  three years before adoption, for both families. Vacancy measures cannot apply where non-occupation is
  justified: work, education or training, health, ongoing succession, legal impediment, uninhabitability.
- **Article 10, proportionality.** Seven cumulative requirements: territorial limitation and calibration
  to different stress levels; no less restrictive alternative within a reasonable period; legal certainty
  and legitimate expectations; short-term rental measures calibrated to scale, frequency or commercial
  character; the authority must actually apply and enforce Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 in the area,
  including suspending and withdrawing registration numbers and ordering removal of unregistered
  listings; acquisition restrictions apply only to property acquired after the measure applies; vacancy
  measures calibrated on objective criteria such as dwellings held by one owner, duration of
  non-occupation, or acquisition by legal persons. Transitional arrangements where existing lawful
  situations are affected.
- **Article 11, procedural safeguards and judicial review.** Effective review, expressly including the
  ability to challenge the authority's own assessments under Articles 6 to 10.
- **Article 12, duration and review.** Five years maximum, extendable after review; first review within
  five years; withdrawal without undue delay once the conditions lapse.
- **Article 13, publication.** Before entry into force, covering adoption, extension, amendment and
  withdrawal: the assessments, the precise territorial scope of area and measure, and the duration.

**Chapter 3, final provisions (Articles 14 to 17)**

- **Article 14, existing measures.** Does not apply to measures adopted before entry into force, though
  an authority may review them against it.
- **Article 15, exercise of the delegation.** Indeterminate period; revocable at any time by Parliament
  or Council; Member State experts consulted first; two-month objection period, extendable by two.
- **Article 16, evaluation.** Five years after entry into force, a report to Parliament, the Council, the
  EESC and the Committee of the Regions.
- **Article 17, entry into force.** Twentieth day after publication, binding in its entirety and directly
  applicable.

**A cross-reference that does not work.** Article 15 points the delegated power at Article 6(3) four
times, and Article 15(1) reads "the conditions laid down in this Article 6(3)". Article 6(3) is the duty
to use objective data; the actual empowerment is **Article 6(5)**. A drafting slip in the published
proposal, of the kind corrected at first reading.

## The politics behind the timing

In **March 2024, 43 MEPs** wrote asking the Commission to stop enforcement against local short-term
rental rules, following a Member State coalition letter in December 2023; mayors asked the same.
**Those enforcement actions are on hold.** The Act is the way out of that standoff.

The Commission opened **one infringement procedure and three pre-infringement dialogues**; the 2019
infringement against the **Brussels-Capital Region** is pending, and the same requirements are before
the Court in **Smartflats, C-813/24**.

## Where it comes from

Announced in **von der Leyen's State of the Union of September 2025**; flagship of the **European
Affordable Housing Plan** (COM(2025) 1025 final, 16 December 2025); European Council conclusions of
23 October 2025; European Parliament Special Committee on the Housing Crisis final report, 10 March
2026. The impact assessment received a **negative Regulatory Scrutiny Board opinion on 5 June 2026**
and a positive opinion with reservations on 17 July 2026.

## What to say when asked

- **"Is this law?"** No. Proposed 9 September 2026, now with Parliament and Council.
- **"Does the EU now control housing?"** No. It sets the conditions for justifying restrictions that already fall under EU law. The decision whether to act stays local.
- **"Is it a ban on Airbnb?"** No. The Commission's own consultation concluded that **short-term rental activity is not the principal driver of housing stress**, and the Act says restrictions alone cannot resolve shortages.
- **"Can my own home be restricted?"** Not under this instrument.
- **Never state an application date.** Entry into force is twenty days after publication; there is no deferred application date, and it is not adopted.
