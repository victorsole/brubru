## Overview

The Common Agricultural Policy is the oldest and largest EU policy, accounting for roughly one-third of the total EU budget. The current 2023-2027 CAP introduced a fundamentally new delivery model: rather than applying uniform EU-wide rules, Member States design and submit CAP Strategic Plans that set out how they will achieve the EU's common objectives using the available funds. This shifts accountability from Brussels to national capitals while maintaining common objectives and rules on eligible expenditure.

The 2023-2027 CAP was designed to be greener than its predecessor, with mandatory eco-schemes and strengthened conditionality. In practice, the January-February 2024 European farmers' protests led to a rapid simplification package (Regulation 2024/1468) that rolled back several environmental requirements, most notably weakening GAEC 1 (crop rotations) and GAEC 8 (non-productive areas on arable land).

## Direct Payments and Conditionality

Pillar I direct payments are conditional on compliance with basic rules grouped under:

- **Statutory Management Requirements (SMR):** environmental, food safety, animal welfare EU laws (cross-compliance)
- **Good Agricultural and Environmental Condition (GAEC) standards:** 9 standards covering soil, water, biodiversity

The 2024 simplification package (Regulation 2024/1468) made key changes: GAEC 1 (crop rotation obligation) was made optional at farm level; GAEC 8 (obligation to maintain non-productive areas and features) was weakened; and farms under 10 hectares were exempted from GAEC controls entirely.

Eco-schemes (Pillar I) offer top-up payments to farmers adopting voluntary practices above conditionality baseline: precision farming, organic practices, agroforestry, reduced pesticide use, high-welfare animal housing. At least 25% of national EAGF envelope must be reserved for eco-schemes; Member States can carry over unspent eco-scheme funds in the first two years.

## CAP Simplification 2024

Regulation (EU) 2024/1468, adopted 14 May 2024 following the farmers' protests, introduced:

- Exemption of farms under 10 hectares from controls and penalties (covering approximately 65% of farms by number but only approximately 9% of agricultural area)
- GAEC 1 made voluntary at farm level (no mandatory crop rotation)
- GAEC 8 weakened: reduced ratio of non-productive features from 4% to 3%, temporary grassland exemption
- Force majeure and exceptional circumstances provisions broadened
- Simplification of advance payment rules and penalty calculations
- Extended deadlines for CAP Strategic Plan amendments

Critics, including European Environment Agency and BirdLife Europe, warned the simplification undermines the CAP's green architecture and risks breaching EU biodiversity and climate commitments.

## CAP in the Next MFF (2028-2034)

The Commission's MFF 2028-2034 proposal merges Pillar I and Pillar II into a single programming instrument: "National and Regional Partnership Plans" (NRPPs). This would fundamentally change the CAP architecture by integrating agricultural policy with cohesion policy, regional policy, and Just Transition Fund into a single national plan per Member State.

The merged approach is proposed alongside an approximately 10% cut to the total agricultural envelope in real terms. Farm organisations (Copa-Cogeca) have strongly opposed both the merger and the cut. The European Parliament AGRI committee has signalled resistance. Final negotiations expected 2025-2026.
