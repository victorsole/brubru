## Why the EPPO Exists

Before 2021, fraud against the EU budget was investigated by OLAF (European Anti-Fraud Office) which could only make **administrative** recommendations to national authorities. National prosecutors then decided whether to launch criminal proceedings — leading to:

- Inconsistent enforcement across MS
- Low prosecution rates (estimated 10-20% of cases)
- Cross-border VAT carousel fraud largely unprosecuted
- EUR 50+ billion/year estimated EU budget fraud (Commission, 2013)

The EPPO adds a **supranational criminal prosecutor** directly competent for EU financial interest crimes.

## Jurisdiction — What Crimes?

### Material Competence (Article 22)
The EPPO investigates and prosecutes:

1. **Crimes affecting the EU's financial interests** (PIF crimes) as defined in Directive (EU) 2017/1371:
   - EU expenditure fraud (grants, cohesion, CAP, NRRP/RRF)
   - EU revenue fraud (customs, VAT where cross-border >= EUR 10 million)
   - Money laundering of PIF crime proceeds
   - Active and passive corruption of EU officials
   - Misappropriation of EU funds by officials
   - Organised crime groups committing PIF offences

2. **Inextricably linked offences**: ancillary crimes (e.g. forgery) connected to PIF offences, provided the financial damage >= administrative sanctions

### Personal Competence (Article 23)
- Natural persons
- Legal persons (where MS law recognises corporate criminal liability)

### Monetary Thresholds
- **VAT fraud**: cross-border VAT fraud causing damage >= EUR 10 million (Article 22(2))
- **Other PIF crimes**: no fixed threshold; EPPO can investigate even small cases
- **Priority cases**: organised crime, high-value fraud, RRF/NGEU fraud

## Structure

### Central Office (Luxembourg)
- **European Chief Prosecutor**: appoints Permanent Chambers, represents EPPO externally
- **Deputy European Chief Prosecutors** (2): assist Chief Prosecutor
- **College** (22 members): one European Prosecutor per participating MS; oversees general operations, adopts guidelines
- **Permanent Chambers** (15): 3-member panels supervising operational case monitoring, deciding on indictment/dismissal
- **European Prosecutors**: one per MS, based at HQ, supervise specific investigations

### Decentralised Level
- **European Delegated Prosecutors (EDPs)**: prosecutors based in each participating MS
- EDPs retain dual roles: EPPO cases + national prosecutor functions
- Conduct investigations and prosecutions in national courts, applying national procedural law

## European Chief Prosecutor Succession (2026 process)

Laura Codruta Kovesi's 7-year mandate (June 2019 -- June 2026) is ending. The appointment procedure:

1. **Call for applications**: issued by Commission
2. **Selection Panel** (Article 14): 12 members -- former CJEU judges, former members of national supreme courts, national prosecutors of highest competence -- appointed jointly by Council and Commission
3. **Shortlist**: Panel draws up shortlist of candidates
4. **EP-Council agreement**: Chief Prosecutor appointed by **common accord** of European Parliament and Council from the shortlist
5. **Term**: 7 years, non-renewable

**LIBE draft report 17 April 2026** (reference 2025/0803(NLE)): LIBE committee's opinion on the appointment, typically after a public hearing with the shortlisted candidate(s). The EP and Council must then reach common accord.

## Key Institutional Relationships

- **OLAF** (European Anti-Fraud Office): administrative investigations; EPPO has priority for criminal cases. Working arrangement signed July 2021.
- **Eurojust**: judicial cooperation; EPPO can rely on Eurojust facilities.
- **Europol**: operational analysis and information exchange.
- **Frontex**: border fraud detection.
- **National prosecution services**: coordination via EDPs.
- **Commission (DG BUDG, DG HOME)**: policy dialogue and funding.

## Enhanced Cooperation Mechanism

Because Denmark, Ireland, Hungary, and (until 2023) Sweden refused participation:
- EPPO was established under **enhanced cooperation** (Article 20 TEU + Article 86 TFEU)
- Non-participating MS cooperate with EPPO via mutual legal assistance (MLA) treaties
- Hungary's non-participation particularly problematic given high RRF/NGEU fraud risk; 2025/2026 Orban defeat by Magyar may accelerate Hungary joining

## Current Challenges

1. **Hungary's non-participation**: EUR 20 billion+ Hungarian NGEU funds largely outside EPPO oversight (Magyar may change this — see `hungary_election_2026_magyar`)
2. **Resource constraints**: case load growing faster than budget
3. **Investigative powers divergence**: EDPs apply national procedural law, creating inequalities
4. **Anti-corruption extension**: debate on extending EPPO to all corruption cases affecting EU budget (Anti-Corruption Directive)

## Anti-Corruption Directive (2023/0135(COD))

The EPPO's material competence may be extended through:
- **Anti-Corruption Directive** proposal (COM(2023) 234, May 2023): harmonises corruption offences across EU; currently in trilogue (as of 2026)
- Could broaden "PIF crimes" scope to include all corruption causing budget damage
