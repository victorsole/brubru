# Indicative Audit Programme (EU 2021-2027)

## QUICK FACTS
- Commission ex-post audits and OLAF risk-based selection within 2 years of final payment (MGA Art. 13, 24, 25)
- 10-15 beneficiaries per audit cycle across all EU programmes; sampling includes ~10 cost items per audit (MUS: Monetary Unit Sampling)
- Audit scope: personnel costs (A.1-A.3), SME owners and volunteers (A.4-A.5), subcontracting (B.), travel and subsistence (C.1), equipment (C.2), other goods and services (C.3)
- Auditor checklist categories: accounting system, payroll procedures, time recording, procurement, travel policy, equipment depreciation, financial controls, segregation of duties, payment authorisation
- Common findings: missing time records or signed declarations; ineligible personnel costs (bonuses, double funding); subcontracting without proper tender or conflict-of-interest checks; equipment with missing depreciation calculation or physical verification; VAT not deducted from claimed costs; costs charged during grant suspension
- Contradictory phase: beneficiary receives draft audit report with proposed rejection decisions; may respond with evidence within a specified period; final report issued after assessment of contradictions
- Follow-up remedies: administrative recovery (grant reduction), OLAF investigation (potential fraud referral), second-level audit triggers
- Key source: EU Audits: Indicative Audit Programme (IAP) V1.1, April 2026, based on the 2021-2027 Model Grant Agreement (all EU programmes for Horizon Europe, Erasmus+, Customs, Justice, Health, Internal Security, Civil Protection, Digital Europe, etc.)

## Audit Ecosystem

The EU uses multiple audit layers to ensure compliance and cost eligibility:

### Commission Ex-Post Audits
- Conducted by the Directorate-General responsible for the programme (e.g. DG Research for Horizon Europe, DG Education for Erasmus+)
- Risk-based selection of beneficiaries using EDES (Early Detection and Exclusion System) flags, historical audit findings, financial viability risk, and materiality thresholds
- Typical cycle: 10-15 beneficiaries selected per year across a given programme
- Audit window: up to 2 years after final payment has been made to the beneficiary (MGA Article 13)
- Standards applied: ISA 230, 240, 315, 320, 330, 500, 600, 610, 620 (International Standards on Auditing)

### OLAF (European Anti-Fraud Office)
- Conducts independent investigations if fraud or systemic irregularity is suspected
- Triggered by audit findings or third-party allegations
- Authority to issue protection orders and conduct unannounced inspections
- Issues binding recovery recommendations

### European Court of Auditors (ECA)
- Acts as external auditor of the EU budget
- Can perform audits in parallel with Commission ex-post audits
- Has contractual access to project files (MGA Article 13)
- Issues annual reports to the European Parliament

### National Audit Bodies
- Where applicable under national law, national supreme audit institutions may audit grant recipients
- Coordinate with Commission auditors through formal protocols

## How Beneficiaries Are Selected

Risk-based selection uses multiple criteria:

### Primary Risk Factors
- Previous audit findings on the beneficiary (yes/no history of irregularities)
- Financial viability assessment (LFV report: qualified audit opinions, losses, going concern issues, litigation)
- Reliance on EU funding (percentage of organisation's revenue from EU grants; operational dependency)
- Participation in synergy calls or joint initiatives (linked projects increase complexity)
- EDES flagging (automated pre-screening for exclusion criteria, adverse ECB/EBU/EPPO information)

### Accounting and Control Risk Factors
- No formalised internal procedures for payroll, procurement, or travel
- Weak segregation of duties (same person initiates, authorises, and records purchases)
- No time-recording system or cost accounting system
- Accounting software lacks project-level analytics (cannot isolate costs per action)
- Outsourced accounting without clear audit trail
- Significant staff turnover in project management or accounting roles

### Materiality Thresholds
- Beneficiaries with EU contributions above EUR 430,000 require a Certificate on Financial Statements (CFS) from an independent external auditor, adding a control layer
- Higher contributions trigger mandatory audit during the programme cycle

## Audit Scope

The IAP covers six standard cost categories and special cost forms. On-the-spot visits typically occur 12-24 months after project completion.

### Standard Cost Categories (Actual Costs)

#### A.1 Employees, A.2 Natural Persons with Direct Contract, A.3 Seconded Personnel

**What is audited:**
- Time recording systems (monthly declarations, signatures, compliance with MGA requirements)
- Salary slips, employment contracts, proofs of payment
- Daily rate calculation methodology and accuracy (actual personnel costs / maximum declarable day-equivalents)
- Cross-check time records against payroll accounts and bank statements
- Verification that costs are identified and verifiable in the participant's statutory accounting books

**Key MGA Rules:**
- Daily rates calculated per person per reporting period, using the formula: {actual personnel costs within reporting period} / {maximum declarable day-equivalents}
- Horizontal ceiling: maximum 215 days per year per person across all EU and Euratom grants (or pro-rata based on part-time percentage)
- Personnel cost components must derive from national law, collective labour agreement, or employment contract
- Ineligible items: arbitrary bonuses dependent on budget availability, dividend payments, commercial targets, indirect costs, costs already reimbursed by social security

#### B. Subcontracting

**What is audited:**
- Subcontract procedures (call for tender, tender evaluation, justification for choice)
- Pricing: market price verification, no conflict of interest
- Qualifications of subcontractor matching the scope of work
- Existence of signed agreements and evidence of service delivery
- Proper invoice and payment documentation
- Compliance with national public procurement law (where applicable)

**Key MGA Rules:**
- Subcontracting must be mentioned in Annex 1 of the Grant Agreement or approved via a periodic report (simplified approval for minor deviations within 10% of budgeted amount)
- Subcontracts may not be awarded to beneficiaries or affiliates unless they are usual providers and priced at market conditions
- Subcontractors must have the same contractual obligations imposed on them by the grant agreement
- Deductible VAT must be excluded from claimed costs
- No subcontracting costs charged during grant suspension

#### C.1 Travel and Subsistence

**What is audited:**
- Travel policy (formalised internal procedures for reimbursement: per diems versus actual costs)
- Invoices (receipts, hotel bills, airline tickets, etc.)
- Evidence that the travel occurred and was directly linked to the action (minutes, attendance lists, facility reservations)
- Reconciliation of travel dates against time records of travelling staff
- Breakdown of costs for reasonableness checks (no upgrades, no private entertainment)

**Key MGA Rules:**
- Travel must occur during the action duration (not during grant suspension)
- If per diems are used, costs claimed must not exceed the per diem allowance
- If actual costs are used, all invoices must be addressed to the participant
- For combined personal and project travel, only the additional cost of project-only travel is eligible
- Ineligible: private entertainment, gifts, special meals, minibar, TV rentals, laundry, tips, entertainment expenses
- Deductible VAT must be excluded

#### C.2 Equipment

**What is audited:**
- Purchasing procedures ensuring best value for money and no conflict of interest
- Depreciation method (asset acquisition value, applicable rate, useful life per IAS standards)
- Physical existence of the equipment (serial number verification, photos for remote locations)
- Rate of use allocation to the project (usage overview tables, logbooks)
- Depreciation only during audited period and action duration (not before delivery date)
- Residual value and potential asset sales properly recorded

**Key MGA Rules:**
- Depreciation calculated on the acquisition value (excluding VAT, indirect costs, bank charges, exchange losses)
- Only depreciation accumulated during the action duration and pro-rata for rate of use is eligible
- Equipment must be necessary for the project and meet principles of economy and efficiency
- Physical inspection required; for assets sold, proceeds must be declared as revenues
- Financing costs (leasing fees, interest) are not eligible
- No depreciation during grant suspension
- For programmes with durability clauses (LIFE, AMIF, EUAF), post-project obligations must be verified

#### C.3 Other Goods, Works and Services

**What is audited:**
- Purchasing procedures (calls for tender, price comparison, conflict of interest assessment)
- Invoices and bank statements (amount and date of payment)
- Valuation method for consumables (FIFO, LIFO consistency with usual practices)
- Proof demonstrating the link to the action (delivery notes, invoices)

**Key MGA Rules:**
- Internal procedures must be objective and not refer only to EU grants
- Goods already in stock must be valued using the participant's usual practice
- Deductible VAT must be excluded
- Indirect costs are not eligible
- No double-charging against other beneficiaries or other grants

### Special Cost Categories

**A.4 SME Owner Unit Costs** and **A.5 Volunteer Unit Costs** are audited using special formulas and do not require individual time records. Audits focus on eligibility of the person and compliance with the declared unit cost rate.

## Documents the Auditor Expects

### From Internal Systems
The Commission audit office retrieves:
- LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative) contact details
- Previous audit reports and Financial Viability risk assessments
- Grant Agreement, amendments, and periodic reports
- Financial statements and Certificates on Financial Statements (CFS)
- Detailed cost reporting tables from periodic reports
- Deviations reports (budgeted versus declared costs)

### From the Beneficiary (Letter of Announcement)

The auditor requests supporting documentation within 2-4 weeks:

**Internal Procedures Document:**
- Formalised cost accounting practices, payroll policy, travel policy, purchasing and subcontracting procedures, depreciation policy
- Proof that procedures are applied consistently regardless of funding source

**Personnel Costs (for sampled individuals):**
- Full list of project staff (names, periods, functions, contract types)
- Salary slips and proofs of payment (bank statements, payroll accounts)
- Time records or declarations (signed monthly by employee and supervisor)
- Employment contracts, direct contracts, or secondment agreements
- Social security records and leave documentation

**Subcontracting and Purchases (for sampled contracts):**
- Call for tender and received tenders (or justification if single-source)
- Justification for choice of subcontractor
- Signed subcontracts or purchase orders
- Original invoices and proofs of payment
- Evidence that services were delivered (delivery notes, inspection reports, acceptance forms)

**Travel and Subsistence:**
- Invoices (receipts, hotel bills, airline confirmations) for sampled travel items
- Proof of attendance (minutes, attendance lists, facility reservations)

**Equipment:**
- Inventory of durable assets
- Delivery notes and certificates of first use
- Depreciation calculation method (description of policy and thresholds)
- Usage overview tables showing hours or percentage allocation to the action
- For physically remote assets: confirmation from site with photos and asset registration

**Internally Invoiced Goods (if applicable):**
- Internal procedures describing the methodology
- Logbooks for units used on the action
- Description of the cost pool used for calculation
- Total units allocated for the calculation period

## Typical Audit Findings

### Top Categories (from ISA 500 evidence collection during on-the-spot visits)

#### 1. Missing or Unreliable Time Records
- Monthly declarations signed only by employee, not by supervisor
- Time recorded in paper formats without date or signature
- Discrepancies between time records and payroll system entries
- No evidence of time recorded for claimed days (e.g., annual leave, sick leave taken but days declared as project work)
- Parallel time systems (weekly hours and monthly days) with inconsistencies

**Auditor response:** Rejection of corresponding personnel cost or request for alternative evidence (interviews, job descriptions, logbooks) to substantiate time allocation.

#### 2. Ineligible Personnel Costs
- Arbitrary bonuses dependent on project budget availability (not eligible; eligible only if based on overall organisational financial performance)
- Dividend payments to employees (not eligible)
- Costs paid by another EU-funded project simultaneously (double funding; full rejection)
- Incomplete salary components (missing statutory contributions, collective agreement amounts)
- Indirect costs allocated to direct personnel (project managers charged as direct cost when they should be overhead)

**Auditor response:** Full rejection of the ineligible amount. In case of double-funding, entire personnel cost rejected.

#### 3. Inadequate Subcontracting Documentation
- No call for tender for subcontracts above materiality threshold (typically EUR 20,000+, depending on programme)
- Subcontract awarded to another consortium member or affiliate without justification
- No evidence that services were actually delivered (missing delivery notes, inspection reports)
- Conflict of interest not assessed (e.g., subcontractor is related to participant via family or financial ties)
- No signed subcontract, only email exchanges

**Auditor response:** Rejection of subcontracting cost or recovery of full amount if fraud is suspected (OLAF referral).

#### 4. Equipment: Missing Depreciation Calculation or Physical Verification
- Depreciation rate applied without reference to useful life or IAS standards
- Depreciation charged before equipment delivery date
- No physical inspection completed; auditor cannot confirm equipment exists or is in use
- Rate of use not calculated (e.g., equipment used 20% for action, but 100% depreciation charged)
- Asset sold during project but proceeds not declared as revenue

**Auditor response:** Reduction of equipment cost by the amount of ineligible depreciation. Equipment without physical verification entirely rejected.

#### 5. Insufficient VAT Deduction
- VAT left in claimed costs when participant is VAT-registered and can recover it
- National law on VAT deductibility not consulted

**Auditor response:** Rejection of VAT component from claimed costs.

#### 6. Costs Charged During Grant Suspension
- Time records show days worked during a period when the grant was suspended (e.g., due to investigation or data request response delay)
- Subcontracting or equipment depreciation invoiced during suspension period

**Auditor response:** Rejection of corresponding cost.

#### 7. Insufficient Documentation for Sampling
- Critical supporting documents (invoices, contracts, payroll records) not available at audit site
- Digital records not authorised by national law (electronic signatures not authenticated)
- Accounting records for the action not identifiable in the general ledger or cost accounting system

**Auditor response:** For items lacking critical evidence, auditor performs alternative procedures (interviews, third-party confirmations) or rejects the item.

#### 8. Double Charging or Allocation Errors
- Same cost declared under two different cost categories
- Cost allocated to the project but also charged to another action
- Indirect costs misclassified as direct costs (e.g., IT support charged as direct personnel)

**Auditor response:** Full rejection of the duplicated amount.

## Contradictory Phase and Final Audit Report

### Contradictory Phase (Beneficiary's Right of Response)

1. Auditor issues draft audit report (DAR) listing proposed cost rejections and recovery amounts
2. Beneficiary receives the DAR and has 30 calendar days to respond with:
   - Written explanations
   - Additional evidence (documents not previously submitted)
   - Comments on the auditor's assessment
   - Requests for clarification

3. Auditor reviews the response and assesses:
   - Is the evidence sufficient to overturn or reduce the proposed rejection?
   - Does the beneficiary's explanation reveal a systemic error or isolated incident?

### Final Audit Report (FAR)

The Final Audit Report includes:
- Audit scope, objectives, and methodology
- System assessment findings (controls, risk factors)
- Detailed cost eligibility findings per category (with sampling basis documented)
- Total amount at risk or proposed recovery
- Beneficiary's response and auditor's counter-arguments (if applicable)
- Recommendation for action (no recovery, recovery, second-level audit, OLAF referral)

### Key Metrics in FAR
- **Total eligible costs audited:** EUR amount of sampled items
- **Total ineligible costs found:** EUR amount (categorised by finding type)
- **Extrapolation factor (if applicable):** if sample-based, auditor may project findings across unaudited population
- **Confidence level:** auditor specifies whether finding is certain (100% confirmed) or estimated

## What Follows: Recovery, OLAF, and Escalation

### Administrative Recovery

If the FAR identifies ineligible costs:

1. **Demand for recovery** issued to the beneficiary, typically within 60 days of FAR
2. **Recovery amount** = ineligible costs + interest (accrued from date of final payment)
3. **Beneficiary's remedies:**
   - Pay the recovery amount
   - Request a second-level audit (rare; only if methodology dispute)
   - Lodge a complaint with the administrative review body (programme-specific)

### OLAF Investigation Triggers

If the FAR identifies potential fraud:
- Deliberate falsification of documents
- Systematic double-funding
- Conflict of interest not disclosed
- Management override of controls

The audit report is referred to OLAF. OLAF conducts a parallel investigation and may:
- Issue a binding recovery decision
- Bar the beneficiary from future EU funding (blacklisting)
- Refer to national prosecution authorities for criminal prosecution

### Systemic Findings

If multiple beneficiaries in a programme show the same finding:
- Second-level audits triggered on other similar beneficiaries
- Programme guidance updated to prevent recurrence
- Training provided to beneficiaries (webinars, updated FAQ)

## Audit Checklists and Key Control Categories

The Indicative Audit Programme uses a structured checklist approach covering:

### Preparation Phase
1. Overview of participant (financial statements, budget, deviation report, grant history)
2. Identification of non-standard grant agreement clauses affecting cost eligibility
3. Grant type and budget category classification (actual costs, unit costs, lump sum)
4. Verification that required certificates (CFS, CoMUC, SPA) have been submitted
5. Verification of participant roles (coordinator, beneficiary, affiliated entity)
6. Deviations check (grant name, accounting records ownership, payment routing)

### System Checks
1. Accounting system ownership and outsourcing
2. Accrual versus cash basis; cost accounting system present
3. IT systems supporting general and cost accounting (integration, certification status)
4. Project-level cost allocation capability and double-charging detection
5. Transaction recording frequency and audit trail documentation
6. Reconciliation procedures between project and general ledger accounts
7. Payment authorisation and supervisory review procedures
8. Financial statements approval workflow before portal submission
9. Payroll procedures and time-recording system (mandatory, frequency)
10. Procurement procedures for subcontracting and equipment purchases
11. Travel and equipment policies (documented, enforced)
12. Digital and electronic signature authorisation (compliance with national law)

### Risk Assessment
- Participant type and business risk
- Independent audit of annual accounts
- Group structure complexity (intercompany invoicing)
- Financial stability (losses, going concern, qualified opinions)
- EU funding dependency (percentage of revenue)
- Previous audit findings or EDES flags
- Management experience and staff turnover

### Sampling (On-the-Spot Audit)

**Standard sampling:** Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS) with minimum 10 items (at least one per cost category)

**Item definition:**
- Personnel cost = 1 person
- Subcontracting = 1 contract
- Travel = 1 person/event
- Other direct costs = 1 line item in cost breakdown

**Adaptations:**
- Horizon Europe ERC grants: sample extended by +1 person (PI often 50% of personnel costs)
- CEF grants: sample extended due to large subcontracting categories
- High-risk participants: sample enlarged
