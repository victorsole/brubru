# EU-Australia Trade Agreement

## QUICK FACTS
- Signed: 24 March 2026 in Canberra by President von der Leyen and PM Anthony Albanese
- Type: Free Trade Agreement (FTA) + Security and Defence Partnership
- Procedure: Council authorisation + EP consent required for ratification
- Responsible DG: DG TRADE
- Responsible Commissioner: Maros Sefcovic (Trade and Economic Security)
- EP lead committee: INTA (International Trade)
- Council configuration: FAC (Trade)
- Legal basis: Article 207 TFEU (Common Commercial Policy)
- Negotiations launched: 2018, paused 2023, relaunched 2025
- Context: First major new FTA concluded under von der Leyen II mandate
- Related guide: `eu_trade_policy.md` (full EU trade policy framework)

## Overview

On 24 March 2026, the EU and Australia concluded a comprehensive Free Trade Agreement alongside a Security and Defence Partnership. President von der Leyen visited Australia (23-25 March 2026) and signed the deal with Prime Minister Anthony Albanese at the Australian Parliament.

The agreement signals EU trade diversification away from China dependency and reinforces the EU's Indo-Pacific strategy. Australia is characterised as a "trusted, like-minded partner" sharing democratic values and rules-based international order commitments.

## Trade Agreement Key Elements

### Market Access
- Elimination of tariffs on industrial goods
- Improved access for EU agricultural exports (wine, dairy, spirits, geographical indications)
- Services liberalisation (financial, telecommunications, maritime, professional services)
- Government procurement market opening

### Geographical Indications (GIs)
- Protection for EU GIs in Australia (wine, spirits, agricultural products)
- Significant for EU producers (Champagne, Prosciutto di Parma, Feta, etc.)

### Sustainability Chapter
- Binding commitments on Paris Agreement implementation
- Labour standards (ILO core conventions)
- Environmental protection provisions

### Digital Trade
- Data flow provisions with privacy safeguards
- Ban on forced technology transfer
- Source code protection

## Security and Defence Partnership

Alongside the trade deal, the EU and Australia established a Security and Defence Partnership covering:
- Defence industrial cooperation
- Cyber security collaboration
- Maritime security in the Indo-Pacific
- Counter-terrorism cooperation
- Crisis management coordination

## Trade Statistics

- Australia is the EU's 20th-largest trading partner
- Two-way trade in goods: approximately EUR 50 billion annually
- EU exports to Australia: machinery, vehicles, pharmaceuticals, chemicals
- Australia exports to EU: mining products, agricultural goods, energy

## Ratification Process

1. Legal scrubbing and translation (2026)
2. Council authorisation to sign
3. European Parliament consent (INTA committee lead)
4. Provisional application (trade provisions)
5. Full ratification by all Member State parliaments (for mixed agreement provisions)
