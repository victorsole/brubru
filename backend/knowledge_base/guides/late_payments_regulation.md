# Late Payments Regulation

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Regulation on combating late payment in commercial transactions (replacing Directive 2011/7/EU)
- Commission proposal: COM(2023)533, 12 September 2023
- Procedure: [2023/0323(COD)](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2023/0323(COD))
- Legislative Train: [Late Payments Directive revision](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-europe-fit-for-the-digital-age/file-late-payments-directive-revision)
- Type: Regulation (directly applicable, replaces Directive 2011/7/EU)
- Responsible DG: DG GROW (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs)
- EP lead committee: IMCO (Internal Market and Consumer Protection)
- EP rapporteur: Ivars Ijabs (Renew, Latvia), appointed 3 October 2024. Previous rapporteur: Roza Thun und Hohenstein (Renew, Poland)
- Shadow rapporteurs: Regina Doherty (EPP), Tsvetelina Penkova (S&D), Jorge Martin Frias (PfE), Reinis Poznjaks (ECR), Reinier van Lanschot (Greens/EFA)
- Consulted committees: ITRE and JURI (both declined to give opinions)
- **Current status (22 September 2026): BLOCKED IN COUNCIL, not withdrawn and not adopted.** Parliament adopted its first-reading position on 23 April 2024 (T9-0299/2024). OEIL's Stage reached reads **"Awaiting Council's 1st reading position"**. A majority of Member States opposed the proposal in a non-paper of 7 June 2024 and the Council has produced no general approach in over two years. The file carried over the parliamentary term: business resumed 13 November 2024.
- Part of: SME Relief Package (September 2023)
- **The current rapporteur voted AGAINST the position he now carries.** The roll-call annex to report A9-0156/2024 records the IMCO vote of 20 March 2024 as 33 in favour, 10 against, 2 abstentions. Renew split three to four: Gozi, Thun und Hohenstein and Yon-Courtin in favour; Ansip, Boto&#537;, Hahn and **Ivars Ijabs** against. Ijabs became rapporteur on 3 October 2024. The EPP also split, eight to four. Do not describe Parliament as united on this file.
- **Committee stage in numbers**: rapporteur's draft report **PE756.002** (14 November 2023, Thun und Hohenstein) carried **25 amendments**. Members then tabled **380 amendments**, numbered 26 to 405, in **PE757.130** (26-223) and **PE757.363** (224-405). Beware the trap: **405 is the highest amendment NUMBER, not the count** -- the first 25 are the rapporteur's own. Article 3 (payment periods) drew 54 amendments, more than any other provision.
- **What Parliament actually changed on payment terms**: the Commission proposed a flat, non-derogable **30 days** for both B2B and public-authority transactions. Parliament's adopted text keeps 30 days as the default (Article 3(1)) but adds **60 days where expressly agreed in the contract between undertakings** (Article 3(1a), Amendment 40) and **up to 120 days for slow-moving or seasonal goods** (Article 3(1b), Amendment 41). This softening is the single biggest difference between the proposal and Parliament's position.
- **The rapporteur's explanatory statement** (PE756.002) set out four aims: convert the Directive into a Regulation; keep a 30-day standard while allowing agreed flexibility; impose **payment-practice reporting obligations on large undertakings** so SMEs can see how big buyers behave; and defer application, with an **extra twelve months of grace for micro-undertakings when they are the debtor**.
- **Two national parliaments filed reasoned opinions on subsidiarity**, both on 9 January 2024: the **Italian Chamber of Deputies** (PE757.362) and the **Swedish Parliament** (PE757.364). Neither triggered a yellow card, but both foreshadowed the Council's resistance.
- **The 2025 Commission survey, presented to IMCO on 26 January 2026** (DG GROW): 7 July to 25 September 2025, **3,503 replies**, half from micro-enterprises or the self-employed; Germany 719, Italy 610, Portugal 555, Spain 387, France 360, Poland 335. **73% still report late payments**, systemic for 30%; almost 60% occur B2B. The top cause named is **lack of sanctions and ineffective enforcement**, not illiquidity. **Almost 48% want a maximum payment cap and prefer the shorter one: 30.71% at 30 days against 17.13% at 60 days**, with 27% for the status quo. Respondents split evenly on Regulation versus revised Directive but a large majority want reinforced, harmonised rules. Calls to keep the present B2B framework came primarily from a single sector: **books**. The evidence therefore supports the Commission's stricter cap over Parliament's 60-day flexibility.

## Why a Regulation Instead of a Directive?

The Commission proposed a Regulation (directly applicable in all Member States) instead of revising the existing Directive 2011/7/EU because:
- The directive allowed too much national variation in transposition
- Enforcement was inconsistent across Member States
- SMEs still face average payment delays of 11+ days beyond agreed terms
- Thousands of SMEs go bankrupt annually waiting for invoices to be paid

## Key Provisions

### Payment Deadlines
- **Default maximum: 30 calendar days** from invoice receipt (both B2B and G2B)
- **B2B flexibility: up to 60 days** when mutually agreed in writing
- **Retail sector: up to 120 days** for slow-moving or seasonal goods

### Automatic Penalties
- Interest accrues automatically from the day after payment deadline expires
- Flat fee compensation scaled by invoice value:
  - EUR 50 for invoices up to EUR 1,500
  - EUR 100 for invoices EUR 1,501 to EUR 15,000
  - EUR 150 for invoices above EUR 15,000
- Restrictions on waiving rights when debtor is a public authority or large undertaking

### Enforcement
- Independent enforcement authorities in each Member State
- Anonymous complaints permitted
- Expedited title procedures reduced from 90 to 60 calendar days
- Annual public reporting on payment practices

### New Institutions
- **European Observatory of Late Payments**: monitors practices, provides guidance to Commission
- Contracting authority reporting requirements on payment timeliness

### Special Provisions
- Micro-undertakings: 12-month additional grace period as debtors
- Insurance contract payments included (reversing original exclusion)
- Consignment contracts permitted under freedom of contract
