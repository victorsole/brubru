## Why a Regulation Instead of a Directive?

The Commission proposed a Regulation (directly applicable in all Member States) instead of revising the existing Directive 2011/7/EU because:
- The directive allowed too much national variation in transposition
- Enforcement was inconsistent across Member States
- SMEs still face average payment delays of 11+ days beyond agreed terms
- Thousands of SMEs go bankrupt annually waiting for invoices to be paid

## Key Provisions

### Payment Deadlines
- **Default maximum: 30 calendar days** from invoice receipt (both B2B and G2B)
- **B2B flexibility: up to 60 days** when mutually agreed in writing
- **Retail sector: up to 120 days** for slow-moving or seasonal goods

### Automatic Penalties
- Interest accrues automatically from the day after payment deadline expires
- Flat fee compensation scaled by invoice value:
  - EUR 50 for invoices up to EUR 1,500
  - EUR 100 for invoices EUR 1,501 to EUR 15,000
  - EUR 150 for invoices above EUR 15,000
- Restrictions on waiving rights when debtor is a public authority or large undertaking

### Enforcement
- Independent enforcement authorities in each Member State
- Anonymous complaints permitted
- Expedited title procedures reduced from 90 to 60 calendar days
- Annual public reporting on payment practices

### New Institutions
- **European Observatory of Late Payments**: monitors practices, provides guidance to Commission
- Contracting authority reporting requirements on payment timeliness

### Special Provisions
- Micro-undertakings: 12-month additional grace period as debtors
- Insurance contract payments included (reversing original exclusion)
- Consignment contracts permitted under freedom of contract
