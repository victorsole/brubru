# CJEU Landmark Article 2 TEU Judgment: Commission v Hungary (Anti-LGBTI Law, 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Court of Justice of the European Union landmark judgment in **Commission v Hungary** concerning Hungary's 2021 law restricting access to LGBTI-related content (the so-called "child protection" / anti-LGBTI law)
- EPRS coverage: **At a Glance** (5 May 2026) — "Hungary's anti-LGBTI law and EU values: The CJEU's landmark Article 2 TEU judgment"

## Why this judgment matters

For the first time, the CJEU has elevated **Article 2 TEU** from a programmatic / interpretive value clause into a **directly justiciable yardstick** in an infringement procedure. Until this judgment, Article 2 had been used as:

- An interpretive aid for operational provisions (e.g. Article 19(1) second paragraph TEU on judicial protection in Commission v Poland)
- A trigger for Article 7 TEU political proceedings
- A linkage in Conditionality Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092

The CJEU now confirms that breach of Article 2 TEU values can ground an Article 258 TFEU infringement finding **independently** when the Member State measure systematically violates the values listed (human dignity, freedom, democracy, equality, rule of law, human rights, including rights of persons belonging to minorities).

This unlocks a more direct enforcement path for the Commission against Member State measures that don't violate a specific operational EU rule but do violate the Union's foundational values.

## The Hungarian law in scope

Hungarian Act LXXIX of 2021 (commonly cited as the "anti-LGBTI law"):

- **Education**: Restricts content that "promotes or portrays" homosexuality or gender reassignment to those under 18; affects sex education, school library curation, school events
- **Advertising**: Bans depicting LGBTI persons in advertising visible to minors
- **Broadcast media**: Restricts LGBTI-themed films and TV shows from prime-time slots accessible to minors
- **Bookshops**: Requires sealed packaging or restricted-section placement for LGBTI-themed books accessible to minors
- **Listed venues and events**: Restricts LGBTI-themed gatherings within proximity of schools and churches

The law was framed as "child protection" but in substance restricts LGBTI visibility, expression, and access to information.

## EU legal framework and breaches found

The CJEU judgment found Hungary in breach of:

1. **Article 2 TEU** — values of equality, non-discrimination, respect for human rights including rights of minorities (FREE-STANDING, the landmark element)
2. **Article 21 EU Charter of Fundamental Rights** — non-discrimination on grounds of sexual orientation
3. **Article 11 EU Charter** — freedom of expression and information (including the right to receive information)
4. **Article 14 EU Charter** — right to education
5. **Audiovisual Media Services Directive (Directive (EU) 2018/1808)** — content classification and editorial independence
6. **e-Commerce Directive (Directive 2000/31/EC)** — country-of-origin principle
7. **Internal Market freedoms** — free movement of goods (books, films), services (advertising), and establishment
8. **Anti-Discrimination Directives** — Race Equality Directive 2000/43/EC + Employment Equality Directive 2000/78/EC analogical application

## What the CJEU has now established

1. **Article 2 TEU values are operational legal norms**, not merely programmatic statements — they can ground an Article 258 TFEU declaration of breach and (in a follow-up Article 260 stage) periodic penalty payments
2. **Systemic Member State measures** that combine to undermine Union values fall within CJEU jurisdiction even when individual operational provisions remain isolated breaches
3. **Article 7 TEU** is **not the exclusive enforcement route** for value-based breaches — the Commission retains the Article 258 TFEU pathway
4. **Conditionality Regulation 2020/2092** can be activated in parallel — financial sanction + judicial declaration are complementary, not alternatives
5. **Member State legislative discretion** on family policy and child protection is bounded by Article 2 TEU values when the measures discriminate against protected groups

## Political context

The judgment lands days after Hungary's **12 April 2026 general election** in which Péter Magyar's Tisza coalition defeated Viktor Orbán's Fidesz, ending 16 years in power. The post-election Magyar government has signalled willingness to:

- Repeal or amend Act LXXIX of 2021 to restore compliance
- Restore judicial independence (Article 19 TEU enforcement track)
- Seek release of EUR 19 billion frozen Cohesion + RRF funds (Conditionality Regulation linkage)

Commission technical meetings with the Magyar team began 19 April 2026 on the frozen-funds file. The CJEU judgment increases the Commission's leverage and provides a clear roadmap for legislative repeal as a precondition for funds release.

## Implications for other Member States

The Article 2 TEU pathway is now available against any systemic measure that:

- Targets a protected minority (sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, ethnicity, disability)
- Combines multiple legal instruments to restrict expression, access to information, education, or services
- Ostensibly pursues a legitimate aim (e.g. child protection) while in substance discriminating

Member State measures plausibly within radar:

- **Slovakia**: 2024-2025 measures restricting NGO civic-space and media independence
- **Romania**: judicial reform tensions
- **Italy**: 2024-2025 surrogacy ban + restrictions on rainbow families' parental rights recognition
- **Russia / Kremlin-aligned legislative copying**: any Member State adopting "foreign agent"-style or LGBTI-restriction laws

The Commission has not announced new Article 258 TFEU cases on the Article 2 TEU pathway as of 5 May 2026 — but the option is now operational.

## Procedural follow-up

After today's declaratory judgment:

- Hungary must **comply** within a reasonable period (typically 6-12 months)
- If non-compliance: **Article 260 TFEU** stage — Commission seeks lump-sum + periodic penalty payments
- Magyar government's pre-emptive repeal would moot the Article 260 stage and unlock the frozen-funds pathway
- Conditionality Regulation 2020/2092 review — Commission can update the finding of breach in light of repeal progress
