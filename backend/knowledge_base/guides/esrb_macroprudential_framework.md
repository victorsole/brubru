# European Systemic Risk Board: Warnings, Recommendations, Macroprudential Stance

## QUICK FACTS
- Body: European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB), established by Regulation (EU) No 1092/2010 (CELEX 32010R1092)
- Mandate: Macroprudential oversight of the EU financial system; prevention and mitigation of systemic risk
- Host and chair: Hosted by the European Central Bank in Frankfurt; General Board chaired by the ECB President
- Current Chair: Christine Lagarde (ECB President); First Vice-Chair Olli Rehn (Governor, Suomen Pankki); Second Vice-Chair Verena Ross (ESMA Chair)
- Decision-making body: General Board -- voting members are the ECB President and Vice-President, national central bank governors of all 27 Member States, a European Commission representative, and the Chairs of the EBA, EIOPA and ESMA, plus the Chairs of the Advisory Scientific and Advisory Technical Committees; non-voting members include national supervisory authorities, EFTA (Iceland, Norway, Liechtenstein) observers, the SRB Chair and the ECB Supervisory Board Chair
- Other bodies: Steering Committee (prepares General Board meetings); Advisory Scientific Committee (ASC, independent experts); Advisory Technical Committee (ATC, technical-level national and EU authority staff); Secretariat provided by the ECB
- General Board meets at least quarterly (March, June, September, December)
- Instruments: warnings and recommendations (both operate on a "comply or explain" basis addressed to the EU as a whole, one or more Member States, one or more ESAs, or one or more national authorities), opinions, risk dashboards, occasional/working papers, stress-test input
- Coverage: banking, insurance, non-bank financial intermediation (NBFI), real estate, market infrastructure, and cross-cutting systemic risks (cyber, climate, geoeconomic fragmentation)
- Distinct from: the ESAs (EBA/ESMA/EIOPA -- microprudential + conduct, see `financial_supervision_eba`), the Single Supervisory Mechanism / ECB Banking Supervision (microprudential supervision of significant euro-area banks), and the Single Resolution Board (resolution planning and execution, macroprudential ESRB does not resolve institutions)
- Recent flagship work (verify before citing as live): warning on frontier AI models and cyber resilience of the financial system (7 July 2026); report on bank/non-bank financial intermediation linkages (12 February 2026); report on financial stability risks from geoeconomic fragmentation (22 January 2026); recommendation on third-country multi-issuer stablecoin schemes ESRB/2025/9 (20 October 2025)
- Countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB): the ESRB issues guidance and recommendations on the CCyB framework under CRD/CRR; national designated authorities set the actual buffer rate for their jurisdiction
- Key sources: `https://www.esrb.europa.eu/about/tasks/html/index.en.html`, `https://www.esrb.europa.eu/home/html/index.en.html`, EUR-Lex CELEX 32010R1092

## What the ESRB Is and Why It Exists

The European Systemic Risk Board was created as part of the post-2008 financial crisis reform of EU financial supervision, alongside the three European Supervisory Authorities (EBA, EIOPA, ESMA). Where the ESAs supervise individual firms and sectors (microprudential supervision), the ESRB looks at the financial system as a whole (macroprudential oversight): it identifies and prioritises systemic risks -- risks that can disrupt the financial system so severely that they have serious negative consequences for the internal market and the real economy -- and issues warnings and recommendations to address them.

The ESRB has no direct enforcement powers and cannot fine institutions or set binding rules. Its influence works through the "comply or explain" mechanism: an addressee that does not follow a recommendation must publicly justify why not, creating reputational and political pressure to act.

Legal basis: Regulation (EU) No 1092/2010 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 November 2010 on European Union macroprudential oversight of the financial system and establishing the European Systemic Risk Board (CELEX 32010R1092), part of the same legislative package as the three ESA-founding regulations.

## Governance Structure

| Body | Composition | Role |
|------|-------------|------|
| General Board | ECB President (Chair) + ECB Vice-President, all 27 national central bank governors, a European Commission representative, EBA/EIOPA/ESMA Chairs, ASC and ATC Chairs (voting); national supervisors, EFTA observers, SRB Chair, ECB Supervisory Board Chair (non-voting) | Decision-making body; adopts warnings, recommendations and opinions; meets at least quarterly |
| Steering Committee | Subset of General Board members | Prepares General Board meetings, ensures continuity between meetings |
| Advisory Scientific Committee (ASC) | Independent academic and market experts | Provides scientific advice and research input on systemic risk |
| Advisory Technical Committee (ATC) | Technical-level staff from national central banks, supervisory authorities, the Commission and the ESAs | Provides technical analysis underpinning General Board decisions |
| Secretariat | Provided by the ECB | Day-to-day administrative and analytical support |

The ECB provides analytical, statistical, logistical and administrative support to the ESRB Secretariat, reflecting the ESRB's status as an EU body hosted by, but legally distinct from, the ECB.

## Instruments

**Warnings**: Public alerts on the identification of significant systemic risks, addressed to the EU, one or more Member States, one or more ESAs, or one or more national supervisory authorities. Warnings do not require a specific policy response but flag a risk that addressees are expected to monitor and act on if it materialises.

**Recommendations**: Formal, more prescriptive instruments than warnings, typically calling for specific policy action within a defined timeline, addressed to the same range of possible recipients. Recommendations follow the "act or explain" (comply-or-explain) principle: addressees must report back to the ESRB, the Council and the European Parliament on the action taken or provide adequate justification for inaction.

**Opinions**: Issued on macroprudential matters, including on national authorities' notifications of macroprudential measures (for example, notifications under the CRR/CRD systemic risk buffer and CCyB provisions) and on EU legislative proposals with financial stability implications.

**Risk dashboards, reports and papers**: The ESRB publishes a quarterly Risk Dashboard, an Annual Report, thematic reports (real estate, NBFI, climate, cyber, crypto), and Occasional/Working Papers through its Secretariat and Advisory Scientific Committee.

## Thematic Coverage

**Real estate**: The ESRB has issued country-specific warnings and recommendations on residential real estate vulnerabilities across multiple Member States, monitoring house-price growth, household indebtedness and bank exposure concentration. Commercial real estate lending is a recurring focus of ESRB analytical work given post-pandemic valuation corrections in several markets.

**Non-bank financial intermediation (NBFI)**: A growing share of ESRB work addresses systemic risk building up outside the banking sector -- investment funds, insurers, pension funds, money market funds and other non-bank entities -- including leverage in alternative investment funds and interconnectedness (linkages) between banks and the NBFI sector.

**Countercyclical capital buffer (CCyB) and other macroprudential capital tools**: Under the Capital Requirements Directive/Regulation framework (see `crr_capital_requirements_regulation`), national designated authorities set CCyB rates for their jurisdiction; the ESRB issues recommendations and methodological guidance on the CCyB framework and reviews national notifications through its Advisory Technical Committee.

**Crypto-assets and cyber risk**: The ESRB has extended its monitoring to crypto-asset markets (including stablecoin systemic risk, for example the October 2025 recommendation on third-country multi-issuer stablecoin schemes) and to cyber risk, including a 2026 warning on the financial stability implications of frontier AI models' cyber capabilities.

**Climate-related systemic risk**: The ESRB and ECB jointly publish reports assessing climate-related risks to financial stability, feeding into stress-testing methodologies used by the ECB and the ESAs.

**Geoeconomic fragmentation**: More recent ESRB analytical work (2026) has examined financial stability risks stemming from geopolitical fragmentation of trade and financial flows.

## How the ESRB Differs From Other EU Financial Bodies

| Body | Function | Scope |
|------|----------|-------|
| ESRB | Macroprudential oversight; system-wide risk identification | Whole financial system, all sectors, all 27 Member States |
| EBA / EIOPA / ESMA | Microprudential and conduct supervision, single rulebook, supervisory convergence | Banking / insurance-pensions / securities-markets, EU-wide (see `financial_supervision_eba`) |
| ECB Banking Supervision (SSM) | Direct microprudential supervision of significant euro-area banks | Euro area significant institutions |
| Single Resolution Board (SRB) | Resolution planning and execution for failing banks | Banking Union significant/cross-border banks |

The ESRB does not supervise individual firms and does not have resolution powers; it sits above the supervisory and resolution architecture, warning and recommending rather than authorising or enforcing.
