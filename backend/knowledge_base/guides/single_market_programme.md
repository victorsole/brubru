# Single Market Programme (SMP) 2021-2027

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal base**: Regulation (EU) 2021/690 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 28 April 2021 establishing a programme for the internal market, competitiveness of enterprises (including small and medium-sized enterprises), the area of plants, animals, food and feed, and European statistics (the "Single Market Programme"), CELEX 32021R0690. Applicable from 1 January 2021 (retroactive at entry into force), repeals Regulations (EU) No 99/2013, (EU) No 1287/2013, (EU) No 254/2014 and (EU) No 652/2014.
- **Budget**: **EUR 4.21 billion** (precisely EUR 4,208,041,000 in current prices) over the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), plus a further **EUR 2 billion** available indirectly through the **InvestEU SME Window** guarantee (not SMP grant money, but the same policy family: see cross-link below).
- **Six predecessor programmes consolidated into one framework**: (1) **COSME** (Competitiveness of Enterprises and SMEs); (2) the **Consumer Programme**; (3) the **food-chain financial framework** (plant, animal, food and feed health/safety spending, ex-Regulation 652/2014); (4) the **European Statistical Programme (ESP)**; (5) the **financial reporting and auditing** framework (support to IFRS/EFRAG-type standard-setters and audit oversight bodies); (6) **technical standardisation financing** (support to CEN, CENELEC and ETSI, and to European standard-setting generally). The SMP replaces six previously separate legal instruments with a single set of rules, one governance structure and simplified reporting.
- **Six strands / specific objectives** (with indicative 2021-2027 envelopes): (a) making the internal market more effective, including market surveillance (~EUR 452 million); (b) strengthening the competitiveness and sustainability of enterprises, especially SMEs, and enhancing market access (~EUR 1 billion): this is the direct COSME-successor strand, delivered largely through the **Enterprise Europe Network (EEN)** and **Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs (EYE)**; (c) ensuring the effective functioning of the internal market through high-quality European standards (~EUR 221 million); (d) promoting the interests of consumers and ensuring a high level of consumer protection (~EUR 200 million); (e) contributing to a high level of human, animal and plant health along the food chain and animal welfare (~EUR 1.7 billion, by far the largest strand); (f) developing, producing and disseminating high-quality European statistics (~EUR 552 million).
- **Implementing bodies**: the **European Innovation Council and SMEs Executive Agency (EISMEA)** runs the enterprise-competitiveness/SME, standardisation, market-surveillance and consumer-protection strands on behalf of DG GROW and DG JUST; the **European Health and Digital Executive Agency (HADEA)** runs the food-chain (plant, animal, food and feed) strand on behalf of DG SANTE; **Eurostat** itself, together with the European Statistical System (national statistical institutes), implements the statistics strand directly (not delegated to an executive agency). Overall political ownership sits with **DG GROW** (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs) as lead DG, with DG SANTE, DG JUST and Eurostat as co-owners of their respective strands.
- **Link to InvestEU**: the SMP's SME strand is deliberately paired with the **InvestEU SME Window**: the SMP provides grants, advisory support (EEN, EYE) and market-access services, while InvestEU's guarantee (delivered mainly via the EIF and national promotional banks) provides debt and equity financing to the same SME population. See `investeu_programme.md` for the guarantee mechanism.
- **2026 annual work programme**: the Commission adopts an annual SMP work programme (implementing decision) setting out the year's calls for proposals and calls for tenders per strand; check the Funding & Tenders Portal (SEDIA) for the live 2026 calls under each strand.
- **Sources**: Regulation (EU) 2021/690 (EUR-Lex, CELEX 32021R0690); Commission funding-programme overview page; EISMEA programme page (read July 2026).

Regulation (EU) 2021/690 is the umbrella EU funding instrument (2021-2027, EUR 4.2 billion) that folds six previously separate programmes into one framework: SME competitiveness (COSME's successor), consumer protection, food-chain safety, European statistics, financial reporting/auditing oversight, and technical standardisation. It is the "one law, one budget line" answer to what used to be six overlapping funding streams with six different sets of rules.

## What the Single Market Programme is and what it replaced

Before 2021, EU funding for internal-market-adjacent policy areas ran through six separate, individually legislated programmes, each with its own eligibility rules, reporting formats and multiannual cycle:

1. **COSME** (Programme for the Competitiveness of Enterprises and small and medium-sized enterprises, Regulation (EU) No 1287/2013): the flagship SME support programme, funding the Enterprise Europe Network and access-to-finance instruments.
2. The **Consumer Programme**: consumer protection, product safety cooperation, consumer organisation capacity-building.
3. The **food-chain financial framework** (Regulation (EU) No 652/2014): funding for animal disease control, plant health, official controls and the veterinary/phytosanitary emergency reserve.
4. The **European Statistical Programme (ESP)** (Regulation (EU) No 99/2013): the multiannual funding basis for Eurostat and the European Statistical System.
5. The **financial reporting and auditing** framework: Union financing for bodies developing and endorsing international accounting standards (e.g. EFRAG) and for public-interest audit oversight.
6. **Technical standardisation financing**: Union support to the European standardisation organisations (CEN, CENELEC, ETSI) for developing harmonised European standards that underpin CE marking and the internal market's "New Approach".

Regulation (EU) 2021/690 (28 April 2021) merges all six into a single legal base, a single multiannual budget envelope (EUR 4.21 billion, 2021-2027) and a single set of financial and governance rules, while preserving each strand's distinct specific objective and indicative budget share. The stated rationale (recitals to the Regulation): fewer parallel rulebooks, more flexibility to reallocate between strands if priorities shift, and a simpler entry point for beneficiaries who used to have to navigate six different funding logics.

## The strands and specific objectives

The SMP Regulation sets out **six specific objectives**, each corresponding to one of the strands above:

| Strand | What it funds | Indicative budget |
|---|---|---|
| Internal market functioning & market surveillance | Governance tools for the single market; market surveillance authorities' cooperation and joint actions against non-compliant products | ~EUR 452 million |
| SME competitiveness & market access | Enterprise Europe Network, Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs, cluster support, industrial ecosystem resilience, SME internationalisation | ~EUR 1 billion |
| Standardisation | Grants to CEN, CENELEC and ETSI to develop and maintain European standards | ~EUR 221 million |
| Consumer protection | Consumer organisation capacity, product safety cooperation (Safety Gate/RAPEX ecosystem), financial-services consumer representation | ~EUR 200 million |
| Food chain (plant, animal, food and feed) | Animal disease eradication and surveillance, plant health emergency measures, official controls co-financing, veterinary/phytosanitary emergency fund | ~EUR 1.7 billion (largest strand) |
| European statistics | Eurostat's operating budget and the European Statistical System's joint statistical production | ~EUR 552 million |

## Implementing bodies: EISMEA, HADEA, Eurostat

The SMP is not run by a single body. Implementation is split by strand:

- **EISMEA** (European Innovation Council and SMEs Executive Agency) manages the strands closest to enterprise policy: SME competitiveness/market access, standardisation, market surveillance/internal market governance, and consumer protection. EISMEA's own programme page states it "will only manage part of the Single Market Programme covering competitiveness of enterprises, governance of the internal market and standardisation and consumers." EISMEA also runs the European Innovation Council (EIC) and parts of Horizon Europe, so SMP sits alongside those in its portfolio: see `eu_commission_executive_agencies.md`.
- **HADEA** (European Health and Digital Executive Agency) manages the food-chain strand (plant, animal, food and feed health and safety, animal welfare) on behalf of DG SANTE. HADEA also runs EU4Health and parts of Digital Europe, so the food-chain strand of SMP is one line among several in its mandate.
- **Eurostat** implements the statistics strand directly, without delegation to an executive agency, working through the European Statistical System (the network of national statistical institutes and central banks). See `eurostat_statistics_production.md` for how that production pipeline works day-to-day.

Political ownership (policy design, work programme adoption, evaluation) sits with the relevant DG: **DG GROW** for the internal-market/SME/standardisation strands (and overall lead DG for the Regulation), **DG SANTE** for food chain, **DG JUST** for the consumer-protection strand, and Eurostat's own Director-General function for statistics.

## SME support: Enterprise Europe Network and Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs

The SME strand is the direct institutional successor to COSME and is the strand most visible to individual businesses:

- **Enterprise Europe Network (EEN)**: the world's largest support network for SMEs with international ambitions, combining local business-support organisations (chambers of commerce, development agencies, technology centres) across the EU and beyond into a single access point for internationalisation advice, technology transfer and partner search, and EU funding navigation.
- **Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs (EYE)**: a cross-border exchange programme placing new or aspiring entrepreneurs with experienced entrepreneurs in another participating country for a working stay, to transfer knowledge and business skills.
- **Cluster support and industrial ecosystem resilience**: funding for European cluster organisations and for building resilience across the 14 industrial ecosystems the Commission tracks under its industrial strategy.
- **SME internationalisation and market access**: help navigating trade barriers, IP protection outside the EU, and access to non-EU markets.

## Consumer protection strand

Funds the capacity of European and national consumer organisations to participate in EU policymaking, supports the product-safety cooperation network that underpins the **Safety Gate (RAPEX)** rapid alert system, and finances consumer representation in EU financial-services policymaking. See `eu_product_safety_consumer.md` for the substantive product-safety and market-surveillance rules this strand's cooperation networks help enforce.

## Food chain strand

By value, the largest single strand (~EUR 1.7 billion of the EUR 4.21 billion total). Funds: emergency and routine measures against animal diseases (including compensation-adjacent cost-sharing with Member States), plant health surveillance and eradication campaigns, co-financing of Member State official controls along the food chain, and a standing veterinary/phytosanitary emergency reserve for outbreak response. Managed by HADEA on behalf of DG SANTE. This strand is the direct continuation of the pre-2021 food-chain financial framework (Regulation (EU) No 652/2014).

## Statistics and standardisation strands

- **Statistics**: funds Eurostat's core operating budget and the European Statistical System's joint statistical infrastructure (methodology, quality frameworks, data collection tools) that underpins every official EU statistic Brubru cites (GDP, trade, employment, environment, digital economy). See `eurostat_statistics_production.md`.
- **Standardisation**: funds CEN, CENELEC and ETSI to develop and maintain the harmonised European standards that give manufacturers a presumption of conformity with EU product legislation (the "New Approach"/CE-marking system) and that DG GROW databases like NANDO and TRIS reference. See `dg_grow_databases.md`.

## Link to the InvestEU SME Window

The SMP is grant and advisory money; it does not lend or guarantee. For SMEs needing debt or equity finance rather than a grant, the complementary instrument is the **InvestEU SME Window** (one of InvestEU's four policy windows), which channels the EU budget guarantee through the EIF and national promotional banks to crowd in loans, guarantees and equity for SMEs and small mid-caps. The two instruments are designed to be used together: EEN/EYE (SMP) for advice, internationalisation and networking; InvestEU SME Window for the financing itself. See `investeu_programme.md` for the guarantee mechanism, implementing partners and how a project becomes InvestEU-backed.

- **Tenderator**: SMP calls for proposals and calls for tenders (SME strand, consumer strand, standardisation strand) are published on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal (SEDIA) under EISMEA; food-chain calls under HADEA. Brubru's Tenderator should track both agencies' SMP-tagged calls.
- **My EU Bubble: Policy Interests**: relevant to users tagged Internal Market/SME, Consumer Protection, Food Safety/Agri, or Statistics/Data policy interests.
- **EU Law Comply**: the SMP itself is a funding instrument, not a compliance obligation, but its standardisation strand output (harmonised European standards) and its food-chain official-controls co-financing are directly relevant to compliance questions in those sectors.
- **Chat**: users asking "what happened to COSME" or "where did the Consumer Programme go" should be pointed to the SMP as the consolidating successor instrument, with the specific strand that matters to them.
