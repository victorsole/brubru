## Context

La Rochelle is a small Atlantic coast city in western France. Its airport (now officially Aéroport La Rochelle Ile de Ré) is a category D facility, meaning it handled under one million passengers a year throughout the period under investigation. The Chambre de Commerce et d'Industrie de La Rochelle operated and owned the airport, a common arrangement for French regional airports at the time. CCI is established by statute as a public-law body under Article L 710-1 of the French Commercial Code, giving it a dual nature as both a commercial operator and a quasi-public institution.

From 2001 onwards the CCI concluded a series of agreements with low-cost carriers to stimulate traffic and tourism. The most significant were with Ryanair, which operated routes to Dublin, London, Liverpool and other UK and Irish destinations, and with Ryanair's wholly-owned subsidiary Airport Marketing Services (AMS), which provided promotional services via the Ryanair.com website. Jet2.com also entered into a marketing agreement in 2008.

A competitor complaint in 2012 prompted the Commission to open a formal investigation under Article 108(2) TFEU covering all public funding to the airport and all airline incentive agreements. After nearly a decade of investigation, document exchange and economic analysis, the Commission issued its final decision on 26 July 2022.

## Structure of the Decision

The operative part has eight articles:

- **Article 1:** Compatible State aid (investment grants, repayable advances, underinvoicing 2001-2005, local authority promotional subsidies) under Article 107(3)(c) TFEU
- **Article 2:** Measures not constituting State aid (FIATA safety/security subsidies; CCI underinvoicing 2006-2012 under proper allocation key; Rochefort airport management contract; Ryanair lighting-charge exemption; Flybe and Aer Arann non-selective discounts; alleged Buzz/easyJet payments)
- **Article 3:** Incompatible State aid -- Ryanair/AMS marketing services agreements 2003-2011
- **Article 4:** Incompatible State aid -- Jet2 marketing services agreement 2008
- **Article 5:** Recovery obligation, compound interest, four-month deadline
- **Articles 6-8:** Recovery method, France's reporting obligations, annual report requirement

The decision is accompanied by extensive economic analysis (over 500 recitals), including multiple NPV tables and a full incremental profitability model.

## Substantive Assessment

### Article 107(1) TFEU: Four conditions

All four State aid conditions were examined for each measure:

**State resources and imputability (recitals 231-242).** The CCI's decisions were imputable to the French State because CCI is a public-law body subject to administrative supervision and financed in part through parafiscal levies. The financial resources used -- including operating budgets that funded airline payments -- are State resources. There is no exception for public bodies that also carry on commercial activities.

**Selective advantage.** The Commission examined whether each measure conferred a selective economic advantage on its recipient that it would not have received under market conditions. For the airport funding this turned on whether France acted as a prudent public authority/market economy operator. For the airline deals it turned on whether a rational private airport would have concluded the same agreements (the MEO test).

**Distortion of competition and effect on trade.** La Rochelle operates routes that compete with routes from Bordeaux, Nantes and Poitiers. Ryanair and Jet2 operate across borders. Even at a small airport, intra-EU trade effects are present.

**SGEI/Altmark rejection (recitals 253-256).** France argued that the airport performed a service of general economic interest and that the funding was Altmark-compliant compensation. The Commission rejected this: La Rochelle is accessible by rail and motorway; it is not isolated. The fourth Altmark condition (cost benchmark via competitive tender or independent efficiency analysis) was clearly not met.

### The Market Economy Operator test for airline deals

The Commission chose the **incremental profitability approach** over a comparative benchmark (recitals 485-495). The test asks: would a private airport operator, seeking to maximise profit, have concluded this agreement given the incremental revenues it expected to earn and the incremental costs it accepted?

Incremental revenues included: additional landing and passenger fees; additional retail, food and beverage, car parking and other non-aeronautical revenues generated by the airline's passengers (single-till approach).

Incremental costs included: additional staff, handling, ground services, security, plus the marketing payments themselves.

The Commission calculated the NPV of each agreement at a 6% discount rate. Every agreement with Ryanair/AMS returned a **negative NPV in both the base and the most favourable stress scenario** (Tables 18 and 19). The Jet2 agreement likewise returned a negative NPV. A rational private operator would not have concluded these deals.

The Commission noted that the CCI prepared no ex ante business plans or profitability projections before signing (recital 533). This absence of contemporaneous documentation reinforced the finding.

### Ryanair and AMS as a single economic entity (recitals 432-438)

Ryanair and AMS (its wholly-owned marketing subsidiary) always contracted with the airport together or in immediate sequence. AMS provided promotional services on Ryanair.com. The Commission treated them as one economic unit for State aid purposes. Recovery was ordered jointly and severally.

### Compatible aid to the airport

**Investment grants** (approx. EUR 2.986 million, 2001-2005): compatible under the 2005 Community Guidelines and Article 107(3)(c). Aid intensity of 50.8% was within the permissible ceiling. The investment was necessary (safety and capacity) and proportionate.

**Repayable advances** (approx. EUR 7.06 million, 2001-2012): compatible as operating aid. La Rochelle is a genuine category D airport with structural operating losses it cannot overcome unaided. Regional connectivity value justifies limited operating aid. Aid was proportionate to the financing gap.

**Local authority promotional subsidies:** compatible on the same basis. These covered the airport's own promotional obligations, not the airline agreements.

### Measures not constituting State aid

Subsidies for FIATA safety/security tasks (public-remit reimbursement on a cost-coverage basis), the Rochefort airport management contract (awarded by competitive tender), the Ryanair daytime-flight lighting charge exemption (applied to all daytime flights, non-selective), and Flybe/Aer Arann discounts (under a non-discriminatory general scheme) were found not to constitute State aid.

## Legal Lineage

The 2014 Aviation Guidelines (OJ C 99, 4.4.2014) replaced the 2005 Community Guidelines on financing of airports and start-up aid to airlines departing from regional airports. The 2014 Guidelines introduced the incremental profitability test as the standard MEO test for airport-airline agreements, the category-based approach to investment and operating aid, and the framework for start-up aid to new routes. The La Rochelle decision is one of a series of Commission decisions applying this framework to French regional airports (see also decisions on Angouleme, Nimes, Montpellier, Pau, Carcassonne and others arising from complaints about low-cost carrier agreements in the 2010s and 2020s).
