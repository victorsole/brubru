# Unit Cost Decision: Personnel Costs (Horizon Europe / Euratom)

## QUICK FACTS

- Personnel costs are the largest eligible cost component in Horizon Europe and Euratom programmes, making their simplification a priority
- The unit cost method calculates an hourly rate based on each beneficiary's average historical personnel costs per annual work unit (AWU)
- Standard formula: total staff costs in last full financial year divided by annual work units, then divided by 215 productive days per year
- Valid for at least two years; beneficiaries may request updates every two years (updates apply to new proposals, not ongoing grants)
- Beneficiaries must obtain an independent certified audit certificate before unit cost approval by the granting authority
- A country-specific cap applies to prevent unrepresentative results, set at EUR 9,618 monthly cost divided by 18 days
- Maximum day-equivalents charged per person per calendar year is 215 days, accounting for absence such as parental leave
- Once approved, the unit cost must be used consistently in all subsequent Horizon Europe and Euratom proposals
- A beneficiary may revert to actual costs once, but cannot re-request the unit cost option for the remainder of the programmes
- Governed by Articles 6 and 21 of Regulation 2021/695 (Horizon Europe) and Article 6 of the Euratom regulations
- Audit controls include verification of calculation parameters, cross-checks against statutory accounts, and withdrawal authority for misuse

## The Standard Hourly-Rate Formula

The daily rate unit cost is calculated using the following formula:

**Daily rate = (Total staff costs / Annual work units) / 215 days**

Where:
- **Total staff costs** are recorded in accordance with accounting standards of the country where the beneficiary is established and reported under the heading "staff costs" in the statutory accounts
- **Annual work units (AWU)** represent the number of persons who worked full-time in the last full financial year, including fractional counts for part-time and seasonal workers
- **215 days** represents the standard productive working days in a year across the EU

Beneficiaries use a wizard on the Funding and Tenders Portal to calculate the indicative unit cost before submission. The calculated daily rate becomes the amount multiplied by day-equivalents worked on the action to determine eligible reimbursement.

## Productive Hours and Day Equivalents

### Standard Productive Days

The unit cost methodology uses **215 productive days per year** as the standard baseline for all beneficiaries across the EU.

### Application of Day-Equivalents

Eligible direct personnel costs for all beneficiary staff are determined by multiplying the approved daily rate unit cost by the number of day-equivalents worked on the action. These must be recorded in accordance with Article 6.2 of the Horizon Europe General Model Grant Agreement.

### Annual Ceiling

The total number of day-equivalents a person can declare in EU grants during a single calendar year cannot exceed **215 days**. Time spent on parental leave may be deducted from this ceiling to ensure compliance with the double-funding prohibition.

## Components of Personnel Costs

### Eligible Elements

Personnel costs under the unit cost method include:

- **Gross salary**: Base wages and salaries of staff members
- **Employer's social contributions**: Employer-paid social insurance and pension contributions
- **Additional remuneration**: Bonuses, allowances, and other compensation directly related to employment

### Cost Basis

The unit cost is calculated using the **total expenditure of the beneficiary for all its staff**, regardless of whether they work directly on research and innovation actions. This ensures the unit cost reflects the true average cost per worker across the organisation.

### Currency Conversion

When a beneficiary's statutory accounts are recorded in a currency other than the euro, the total personnel costs are converted to euros at the average of the daily exchange rates published in the C series of the Official Journal of the European Union (ECB website) calculated over the corresponding financial year.

## Categories NOT Covered by Unit Costs

The following personnel-related costs are **not** covered by the standard unit cost method and require separate treatment:

- **Subcontracting**: Personnel costs related to external contractors or subcontractors must be declared separately as actual subcontracting costs
- **Seconded personnel**: Staff seconded from external organisations are not eligible under the unit cost; separate actual cost documentation applies
- **Consultants**: Consultant fees and related professional services costs do not fall under the unit cost category

These categories may use alternative eligible cost methods, including actual costs, lump sums, or other unit costs, as defined in Article 8 of the Horizon Europe General Model Grant Agreement.

## Documentation Required

### Audit Certificate

Before approval, the beneficiary's Legal Entity Appointed Representative (LEAR) must provide an **independent certified audit certificate** that:

- Is prepared by a qualified external auditor (independent and complying with Directive 2006/43/EC) or, for public bodies, by a competent independent public officer
- Covers the annual personnel costs and annual work units for the last full financial year
- Is drawn up using the template published on the Funding and Tenders Portal
- Confirms compliance with the applicable rules for unit cost calculation
- Verifies the figures are drawn from statutory accounts or other official supporting documents

### Supporting Records

Beneficiaries must maintain records demonstrating:

- **Payslips and salary records**: Evidence of gross salary, employer contributions, and additional remuneration for all staff
- **Payroll records**: Full payroll documentation establishing the relationship between declared unit cost parameters and actual costs
- **Annual accounts**: Statutory accounts or equivalent records from which total staff costs are derived
- **Time records**: Documentation linking individual staff members to day-equivalents worked on EU-funded actions, consistent with the 215-day annual ceiling

The granting authority may request clarification or additional supporting documents during the approval process and retains the right to conduct ex-post checks, reviews, audits, or investigations.

## Country-Specific Caps

### Cap Calculation

To prevent unrepresentative results due to unusual organisational structures or remuneration practices, a country-specific cap applies:

**Country cap = (EUR 9,618 multiplied by country-specific correction coefficient) / 18 days**

### Basis for the EUR 9,618 Reference

The EUR 9,618 monthly cap was derived from analysis of 7,793 Horizon Europe grant agreements signed before June 2023 (excluding unit cost and lump sum agreements). These grants provided funding for 1.9 million person-months, and the estimated budget for 95 per cent of these person-months was below EUR 9,618. When the calculated daily rate results in monthly costs exceeding the cap for a given country, the cap value replaces the calculated rate.

### Country Correction Coefficients

The country correction coefficients are based on Comparative Price Levels published by Eurostat and are the same coefficients used for unit costs under Marie Sklodowska-Curie Doctoral Network actions. Coefficients vary by country to reflect differences in living costs and wage levels across the EU.

## Consistency Requirement and Grant Agreement Coverage

### Mandatory Use

Once a unit cost is approved for a beneficiary, the beneficiary **must use that unit cost consistently in all subsequent Horizon Europe and Euratom proposals** submitted after the approval date.

### Application to Existing Proposals

A beneficiary may use an approved unit cost in proposals already submitted (before approval) only if:
- The proposal is not yet awarded
- The proposal will be awarded after the approval date of the unit cost
- The consortium and granting authority agree to adapt the budget if necessary

### Grant Agreements Already Awarded

Grant agreements that were awarded or signed at the time of unit cost approval **cannot be amended** to introduce the unit cost, even if the unit cost would have been beneficial to that grant.

### One-Time Reversion to Actual Costs

A beneficiary may revert to actual costs (Option b: a combination of eligible costs actually incurred, average personnel costs, and SME owner unit costs) **once**, such as where the unit cost did not deliver the envisaged administrative simplification. This change is valid only for future grants; ongoing grants are not affected. Once a beneficiary reverts, it **cannot request the unit cost option again for the remainder of the Horizon Europe and Euratom programmes**.

## Update and Validity

### Validity Period

The approved unit cost is valid for at least two years from the approval date.

### Update Requests

Beneficiaries may submit a unit cost update request every two years after the approval date, until 31 December 2027. The updated request must follow the same rules and procedures as the initial request, including submission of a new certified audit certificate.

### Application of Updated Costs

An updated unit cost applies to all the beneficiary's Horizon Europe and Euratom proposals invited to grant preparation after the new approval date. However, **ongoing grant agreements cannot be amended to increase the unit cost** once signed.

## Link with Average Personnel Costs Decision

The unit cost method under this decision complements the average personnel costs approach and may be combined with it. A beneficiary may elect to use:

- **Option A**: A unit cost calculated in accordance with its historical data (the method under this decision)
- **Option B**: A combination of eligible costs actually incurred, average personnel costs, and SME owner unit costs

This flexibility allows beneficiaries to select the approach that best fits their administrative capacity and grant portfolio.

## Common Audit Issues

### Data Verification Mismatches

Audits frequently identify discrepancies between the figures entered in the unit cost wizard, the certified audit certificate, and the beneficiary's statutory accounts or other official supporting documents. The granting authority verifies these on a limited risk-sample basis before approval.

### Actual Costs Significantly Lower Than Unit Cost

If an audit, review, check, or investigation (including checks under Article 25.3 of the Horizon Europe General Model Grant Agreement) establishes that a beneficiary's actual costs are **recurrently and significantly lower** than the approved unit cost, the granting authority may withdraw the unit cost (with effect for future grants).

### Misuse and Incorrect Declarations

Audit findings frequently relate to:
- Charging more than 215 day-equivalents per person per calendar year
- Misuse of the unit cost in proposals or contexts for which it was not approved
- Failure to maintain supporting time and payroll records linking declared costs to actual work performed

### Concealed or Misrepresented Information

If the granting authority finds that incorrect data, concealed information, or misrepresented information was used to calculate the unit cost (for example, inflated personnel costs or understated work units), it may **withdraw the unit cost** with retroactive effect for ongoing grants, future grants, and closed grants that remain auditable.

### Non-Compliance with Audit Certificate Requirements

Audit findings often identify instances where the submitted certification did not comply with the template requirements, was not provided by a qualified independent auditor, or did not verify compliance with applicable calculation rules.
