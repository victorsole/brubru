# EUSPA: EU Agency for the Space Programme

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Union Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA)
- Headquarters: Prague, Czech Republic
- Established: 12 May 2021, under Regulation (EU) 2021/696 (the EU Space Programme Regulation)
- Predecessor: European GNSS Agency (GSA), itself a reorganisation (2010) of the European GNSS Supervisory Authority created in 2004
- Executive Director: Rodrigo da Costa (verify current at point of use)
- Governance: Administrative Board (Member States + Commission + European Parliament observer) sets strategy and budget; Executive Director is the legal representative and day-to-day manager; EU Space Security Accreditation Board (Space SAB) is a separate, functionally independent body within EUSPA
- Sponsoring DG: DG DEFIS (Defence Industry and Space); design and in-orbit procurement for most components remains with the European Space Agency (ESA) under delegation agreements
- Core operational mandate: Galileo (EU satellite navigation) and EGNOS (satellite-based augmentation) service operation; GOVSATCOM secure government satellite communications; growing role in IRIS2 secure connectivity procurement and service delivery; security accreditation for all EU Space Programme components; downstream market development and Copernicus market uptake
- Galileo services operated: Open Service (OS), High Accuracy Service (HAS, free since January 2023), Open Service Navigation Message Authentication (OS-NMA, live since 2023), Public Regulated Service (PRS, encrypted, government-only), Search and Rescue (SAR, feeds Cospas-Sarsat), Safety of Life (SoL); a Commercial Authentication Service (CAS) is planned
- Staff: roughly 350 (growing with IRIS2 and expanded Copernicus responsibilities)
- Downstream market coverage: around 18 GNSS and Earth observation application sectors tracked in the EUSPA EO and GNSS Market Report, including agriculture, aviation, maritime, rail, road/automotive, location-based services (LBS), timing and synchronisation, drones/UAS, and emergency response
- Related programme-level guide: `eu_space_programme.md` (Galileo, Copernicus, IRIS2, SSA/SST, budgets, EU Space Act)

EUSPA is the EU's operational space agency: it does not design or build satellites (that sits mainly with ESA under Commission delegation) but runs the services once they are in orbit, accredits the security of every EU Space Programme component, and pushes downstream market uptake so that European businesses actually use Galileo, EGNOS, GOVSATCOM, Copernicus and (increasingly) IRIS2 data and signals.

## Legal basis and governance

### Founding regulation

EUSPA was created by the EU Space Programme Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2021/696, which entered into force in 2021 and merged the governance of Galileo, EGNOS, Copernicus, GOVSATCOM and SSA/SST into a single legal framework. The Agency itself replaces and legally succeeds the European GNSS Agency (GSA), broadening the GSA's GNSS-only mandate (Galileo and EGNOS) to cover the full EU Space Programme. IRIS2 secure connectivity, established later by Regulation (EU) 2023/588, subsequently assigned EUSPA a growing procurement and service-delivery role alongside the Commission and the SpaceRISE public-private consortium.

### Governance bodies

- **Administrative Board**: one representative per Member State, two Commission representatives, and a European Parliament observer without voting rights. Sets the Agency's work programme, budget and strategic priorities and appoints the Executive Director.
- **Executive Director**: legal representative of the Agency, responsible for day-to-day management, implementation of the Administrative Board's decisions and reporting to the Commission and Parliament. Verify the current name before citing in outward-facing material, as the mandate is time-limited.
- **EU Space Programme Security Accreditation Board (Space SAB)**: established within EUSPA but functionally independent of the Agency's other bodies, precisely because it must be able to reach accreditation decisions (including on PRS access and GOVSATCOM security) without operational or budgetary pressure from the same management line that runs the services.

### Split of responsibilities with the Commission and ESA

- **European Commission (DG DEFIS)**: overall programme owner, budget holder, and policy lead; adopts implementing and delegated acts under Regulation (EU) 2021/696.
- **ESA**: design, development and in-orbit validation of space and ground segment infrastructure, procured on the Commission's behalf under delegation agreements (this is why satellite manufacturing and launch procurement questions point to ESA, not EUSPA).
- **EUSPA**: operations once systems are declared operational: service provision, security accreditation, and market development: plus an expanding role in IRIS2 governmental service procurement.

## Galileo: operations and service portfolio

EUSPA operates the Galileo constellation's service layer from two Galileo Control Centres (Oberpfaffenhofen, Germany and Fucino, Italy) and coordinates the Galileo Service Centre, the operational interface for PRS authorisation, service status and user support. Current and planned Galileo services:

| Service | What it does | Access |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| Open Service (OS) | Free positioning, navigation and timing for mass-market receivers | Public, global |
| High Accuracy Service (HAS) | Sub-20cm accuracy correction data, free since January 2023 | Public, global |
| Open Service Navigation Message Authentication (OS-NMA) | Cryptographic authentication of the open navigation message to detect spoofing | Public, requires compatible receiver firmware |
| Public Regulated Service (PRS) | Encrypted, jamming/spoofing-resistant signal for critical infrastructure and government use | Authorised government users only, via national PRS Authorities |
| Search and Rescue (SAR) | Distress beacon localisation feeding into Cospas-Sarsat | Global, integrated with maritime/aviation SAR systems |
| Safety of Life (SoL) | Integrity monitoring for certified civil aviation and maritime navigation | Certified users |
| Commercial Authentication Service (CAS) | Authentication service for commercial/high-value applications | Planned, not yet operational: verify status before citing |

EGNOS, the European Geostationary Navigation Overlay Service, is operated alongside Galileo and improves the accuracy and integrity of GNSS signals over Europe, principally for civil aviation approach procedures and other safety-of-life applications.

## GOVSATCOM

GOVSATCOM pools and shares secure satellite communications capacity for EU and Member State government users, covering crisis management, border and maritime surveillance, and critical infrastructure protection. EUSPA operates the GOVSATCOM hub function, allocating capacity and coordinating access on behalf of authorised users, while satellite capacity itself is sourced from both Member State assets and, increasingly, IRIS2.

## Role in IRIS2

IRIS2 (Infrastructure for Resilience, Interconnection and Security by Satellite), governed by Regulation (EU) 2023/588, is delivered mainly through the SpaceRISE public-private consortium under Commission oversight, with initial services targeted for 2027 and full operational capability by 2029. EUSPA's role is on the demand and service-delivery side: coordinating governmental user requirements, feeding into GOVSATCOM-style secure service provision once IRIS2 capacity comes online, and applying its security accreditation function to IRIS2 governmental services. For the programme-level budget, timeline and strategic rationale, see `eu_space_programme.md`.

## Security accreditation

The Space SAB issues security accreditation decisions for every EU Space Programme component: Galileo PRS receiver manufacture and use, GOVSATCOM, and (as it matures) IRIS2 governmental services. Accreditation determines who may build, sell or operate equipment that touches the encrypted or government-restricted layers of these systems, which is why PRS receiver manufacturers and GOVSATCOM capacity providers must engage with EUSPA's accreditation process, not only with national authorities.

## Market development and downstream uptake

EUSPA runs the EU's principal downstream space market intelligence function, publishing the EUSPA EO and GNSS Market Report and running innovation and matchmaking activity (including CASSINI hackathons and mentoring, aligned with the EIC and Horizon Europe) to help start-ups and established firms build products on Galileo, EGNOS and Copernicus. It also carries Copernicus market uptake responsibilities alongside its GNSS mandate, even though Copernicus space and ground segment operations remain with ESA and EUMETSAT.
