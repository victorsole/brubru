# Single-Use Plastics Directive (SUPD)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (published Wednesday 9 September 2026, dated 8 September — THE COMMISSION GUIDELINES ARE UPDATED)**: **Commission Notice, *Updating Commission guidelines on single-use plastic products in accordance with Directive (EU) 2019/904*** — OJ C series **C/2026/4813**, CELEX **52026XC04813**, ELI http://publications.europa.eu/resource/eli/C/2026/4813/oj. It **replaces the original guidelines at OJ C 216, 7 June 2021, p. 1**. A Notice is **interpretative guidance, not law**: it does not change Directive (EU) 2019/904 and creates no new obligation, but it is what national authorities and enforcement bodies will read when deciding whether a given product is in scope.
- **What the updated guidelines cover**, section by section: the meaning of **'plastic'** (polymer; 'can function as a main structural component'; natural polymers **not chemically modified**) and of **'single-use plastic product'** (wholly or partly plastic; single-use; refillable and reusable nature); a dedicated section on the **relation between Directive (EU) 2019/904 and the Packaging Directive 94/62/EC**; and product criteria with illustrative examples for **food containers, packets and wrappers, cutlery, plates, straws and stirrers, beverage containers, beverage bottles and cups (including caps, covers and lids), lightweight plastic carrier bags, cotton bud sticks, balloons and balloon sticks, sanitary towels, tampons and applicators, wet wipes**, and tobacco filters.
- **The genuinely useful part for a compliance reader** is section 4.5, which gives the criteria for **telling adjacent categories apart** — food container vs beverage container, food container vs cup for beverages, beverage container vs beverage bottle vs cup, food container vs packet or wrapper, plate vs food container. Category assignment is what determines which obligation applies.

## The 10 SUP Product Categories

The Directive targets the 10 single-use plastic products most frequently littered on EU beaches (based on 2016 Joint Research Centre beach monitoring data), plus fishing gear:

### Article 5 -- Market Restrictions (BANS from 3 July 2021)

Placing on the market prohibited:
1. **Cotton bud sticks** (except medical devices)
2. **Cutlery** (forks, knives, spoons, chopsticks)
3. **Plates**
4. **Straws** (except medical devices)
5. **Beverage stirrers**
6. **Balloon sticks** (except industrial/professional use)
7. **Food containers** made of expanded polystyrene (EPS)
8. **Beverage containers** made of EPS, including caps and lids
9. **Beverage cups** made of EPS, including caps and lids
10. **Oxo-degradable plastic products**

### Article 4 -- Consumption Reduction

Member States must achieve an **ambitious and sustained reduction** by 2026 (compared to 2022) in:
- Single-use plastic cups (with lids)
- Single-use plastic food containers for ready-to-eat meals

No specific percentage, but measures must include reuse systems, alternatives, economic instruments.

### Article 6 -- Product Requirements

- **Tethered caps**: from 3 July 2024, plastic bottle caps and lids must remain attached to the container (Annex E, Part C)
- **Recycled content**: PET bottles >= 25% recycled content from 1 January 2025; all beverage bottles >= 30% from 1 January 2030

### Article 7 -- Marking Requirements

Visible marking for 5 categories informing consumers of:
- Appropriate waste management options
- Avoidance of littering
- Plastic content

Applies to: menstrual products, wet wipes, tobacco filters, beverage cups, balloons.

### Article 8 -- Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

Producers bear costs for:
- Waste management (collection, treatment, disposal) of 8 product categories
- Awareness-raising measures
- Litter clean-up

Covered categories: food containers, packets and wrappers, bottles, cups, tobacco filters, balloons, wet wipes, fishing gear.

### Article 9 -- Separate Collection Targets

For plastic beverage bottles (up to 3 litres):
- **77% collection by 2025**
- **90% collection by 2029**

### Article 10 -- Awareness-Raising

Consumer information campaigns on:
- Littering impacts
- Reusable alternatives
- Waste management systems

### Article 11 -- Coordination

Directive (EU) 2019/904 + Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, Reg (EU) 2025/40) + Waste Framework Directive (2008/98/EC) form an integrated framework.

## April 2026 Commission Update

On 16 April 2026, DG ENV published Member State progress on the Article 9 separate collection target:

**10 Member States ahead of schedule** (exceeding 77% collection of SUP beverage bottles by 2024 data):
- Germany, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, Netherlands, Austria, Latvia, Lithuania, Sweden, Croatia

**Key drivers of success**:
- Deposit Return Schemes (DRS) operational in 9 of the 10
- Modern collection infrastructure
- Consumer awareness and strong EPR schemes

**Concerns**:
- 17 MS still not on track; risk of missing 90% target by 2029
- Southern Member States (Italy, Spain, Greece) below 60% in some regions
- Enforcement varies widely

## Relationship to PPWR (Regulation (EU) 2025/40)

The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (adopted 15 January 2025) overlaps with SUPD on:
- Plastic beverage bottle recycled content (PPWR takes over from SUPD Article 6(5) from 2030)
- Tethered caps
- Deposit Return Schemes (PPWR Article 50 mandates DRS for bottles and cans from 2029)
- Food service packaging (PPWR Article 26 restrictions on single-use packaging)

PPWR gradually replaces some SUPD provisions; both remain in force.

## Transposition State

- **27 MS have transposed**, but quality and enforcement vary
- **Infringement proceedings**: Commission has opened cases against several MS for incomplete transposition or weak implementation
- **Directive rather than Regulation**: allows Member States to go further (some do: France's Anti-Waste Law, Germany's SUPV, Spain's Law 7/2022 on Waste)

## Interaction with EU Plastics Strategy

- **Plastics Strategy (2018)**: COM(2018) 28 -- strategy for plastics in a circular economy
- **PPWR** (2025/40): packaging rules
- **Microplastics Regulation** (Commission Reg (EU) 2023/2055): restricts microplastics under REACH
- **Marine Strategy Framework Directive** (2008/56/EC): descriptor 10 marine litter
- **CSRD** (2022/2464): corporate sustainability reporting on plastics

## Penalties

Article 14 -- Member States lay down penalties. Must be effective, proportionate, dissuasive.

Examples:
- **France**: up to EUR 100,000/day for non-compliance with tethered caps or bans
- **Germany**: fines up to EUR 100,000
- **Spain**: Law 7/2022 -- minor infractions EUR 2,001-100,000; serious EUR 100,001-1,000,000; very serious EUR 1,000,001-3,500,000

## EPR Schemes

Producers must join or establish EPR schemes paying for:
- Waste management (up to full cost coverage)
- Beach clean-up costs (Article 8(3))
- Awareness-raising

Examples:
- **France**: CITEO for packaging, ALCOME for cigarette butts
- **Germany**: Duales System, cigarette butt EPR operational from 2022
- **Netherlands**: Afvalfonds Verpakkingen
