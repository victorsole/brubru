# EU Returns Policy Reform (Return Regulation)

- Full name: Regulation establishing a common system for the return of third-country nationals staying illegally in the Union
- Commission proposal: [COM(2025)0101](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/docs_autres_institutions/commission_europeenne/com/2025/0101/COM_COM(2025)0101_EN.pdf), 11 March 2025

- Type: Regulation (directly applicable, replaces Directive 2008/115/EC)
- Legal basis: TFEU Article 79(2)
- Lead DG: DG HOME (Migration and Home Affairs)
- Commissioner: Magnus Brunner
- EP lead committee: LIBE (Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs)

## Why a Regulation Instead of a Directive?
The Commission proposed upgrading from a Directive (2008/115/EC) to a directly applicable Regulation because:
- National transposition created divergent return systems across Member States
- Low effective return rates (only ~20% of return decisions enforced)
- Need for mutual recognition of return decisions across all Member States via SIS
- Complements the Pact on Migration and Asylum (adopted May 2024)

## Key Provisions

### Return Decisions
- Mandatory issuance to all third-country nationals without right to stay
- Shared across EU via Schengen Information System (mutual recognition)
- European Return Order: new EU-level cross-border enforcement instrument

### Voluntary Departure
- Maximum 30 days (no guaranteed minimum period)
- Numerous exclusion grounds: failure to cooperate, absconding risk

### Detention
- 5 grounds (expanded from 2 under Directive 2008/115/EC): absconding risk, hampering return, security risk, identity verification, non-compliance
- Maximum detention: 24 months (up from 18 under current Directive)
- Unaccompanied minors and families: detention only as last resort
- Alternatives: reporting obligations, residence requirements, financial guarantees, electronic monitoring

### Entry Bans
- Extended durations (up to 10 years)
- Mandatory for non-compliance or security risks
- Potentially permanent for security threats

### Return to Third Countries
- Member States may remove individuals to third countries via bilateral agreement
- No requirement for prior link to that country
- Unaccompanied minors excluded from third-country agreements

### Fundamental Rights
- Independent monitoring during removal operations
- Non-refoulement principle upheld
- Country of return selection considers: origin country, transit routes, safe third country status
