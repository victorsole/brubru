# Pact for Skills

## QUICK FACTS
- Initiative: Pact for Skills -- EU-wide shared engagement model for skills development
- Type: Voluntary, non-binding partnership framework (not a legal instrument)
- Launched: 10 November 2020 by the European Commission
- Lead DG: DG EMPL (Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion)
- Commissioner: Roxana Minzatu (EVP Social Rights and Skills, Quality Jobs and Preparedness)
- Policy context: European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan; European Skills Agenda COM(2020) 274
- Milestone (16 April 2026): **Over 10 million workers trained under the Pact for Skills** (DG EMPL announcement)
- Number of partnerships: 25+ large-scale skills partnerships across industrial ecosystems
- Signatories: 2,300+ organisations (companies, trade unions, public authorities, training providers, sectoral associations)
- Funding mechanisms: not a funding instrument itself; leverages ESF+, Erasmus+, Horizon Europe, Digital Europe, JTF, InvestEU
- Target sectors: 14 industrial ecosystems identified in New Industrial Strategy (COM(2020) 102 and COM(2021) 350)
- Core principle: Public-private cooperation with joint commitments on upskilling and reskilling
- Accompanied by: European Year of Skills (9 May 2023 -- 8 May 2024)
- Related: Individual Learning Accounts (Council Rec 2022/C 243/03), Micro-credentials Recommendation (Council Rec 2022/C 243/02)

## What is the Pact for Skills

The Pact for Skills is a **shared engagement model** for skills development in Europe. It brings together public and private stakeholders to invest in large-scale upskilling and reskilling actions in strategic sectors.

Unlike regulation, the Pact is based on **voluntary commitments**. Signatories subscribe to four key principles and join one or more large-scale skills partnerships.

### Key Principles
1. **Promote a culture of lifelong learning for all**
2. **Build strong skills partnerships** — multi-stakeholder, including social partners
3. **Monitor skills supply/demand and anticipate skills needs**
4. **Work against discrimination and for gender equality and equal opportunities**

### Signatories' Commitments
- Identify upskilling/reskilling needs
- Commit to concrete quantitative targets (number of workers trained, budget)
- Publish a yearly progress report
- Cooperate with other partnership members

## The 10 Million Milestone (April 2026)

On 16 April 2026, DG EMPL announced that Pact for Skills signatories have collectively **trained over 10 million workers** since 2020.

Breakdown (approximate):
- **Digital skills**: 3.2 million workers
- **Green skills**: 2.4 million workers
- **Sectoral/technical skills**: 3.0 million workers
- **Transversal skills** (leadership, communication): 1.4 million workers

Countries contributing most: Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Poland, Netherlands.

## Individual Learning Accounts (ILA)

Council Recommendation of 16 June 2022 on individual learning accounts (Rec 2022/C 243/03):
- Personal training rights regardless of employment status
- Transferable across jobs and Member States
- Aligned with micro-credentials
- Implementation state: France (CPF since 2015), Belgium, Netherlands, Austria, Spain, Portugal advanced; Italy and Germany developing

## Micro-credentials

Council Recommendation of 16 June 2022 on micro-credentials for lifelong learning (Rec 2022/C 243/02):
- Small, verifiable units of learning outcomes
- Europass digital wallet integration
- Quality standards: European Credit Transfer System (ECTS)
- Providers: universities, companies, sectoral bodies

## Apply AI Strategy (October 2025)

The Apply AI Strategy (COM(2025) 723) identifies skills gaps as a key barrier to AI adoption. The Pact for Skills -- Digital partnership is central to addressing:
- AI fluency for civil servants
- AI engineering skills (shortage of 500,000 in EU by 2030)
- Cross-sectoral AI literacy

See `apply_ai_strategy_public_sector` for details.

## Critiques and Limitations

- **Voluntary nature**: no binding commitments, no penalties for under-delivery
- **Measurement inconsistency**: signatories self-report; methodologies vary
- **SME participation**: dominated by large multinationals and sectoral federations
- **Gender gap**: digital skills training skews male; women overrepresented in low-pay care skills
- **Informal workers**: platform workers, gig economy poorly covered
- **EU budget MFF 2028-2034**: skills fragmented across multiple headings; no standalone Skills Fund yet
