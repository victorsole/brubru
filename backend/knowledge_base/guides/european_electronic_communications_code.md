## Context

The EECC is the largest overhaul of EU telecoms law since the 2002 framework. It consolidates four separate directives into a single instrument, extends scope to over-the-top (OTT) interpersonal services, prioritises very high capacity network connectivity as a core regulatory objective, and fully harmonises end-user rights across the Union.

The legal base (Article 114 TFEU) reflects the Code's dual purpose: removing internal market barriers created by divergent national telecoms frameworks, and establishing a stable investment environment for VHCN roll-out. The 2002 framework had been amended multiple times (notably by the 2009 "Better Regulation" package — Directives 2009/136/EC and 2009/140/EC) but had not been recast; fragmented implementation created significant regulatory divergence, particularly on spectrum and end-user rights. The EECC addresses this through full harmonisation on end-user rights (Article 101) and a much more detailed spectrum chapter.

---

## Structure

The Code has four Parts, 127 articles and thirteen Annexes:

- **Part I (Articles 1–41)**: Framework — objectives, definitions, institutional set-up (NRAs, independence, BEREC), general authorisation regime, information and consultation, dispute resolution, security
- **Part II (Articles 42–83)**: Networks — market entry and deployment (spectrum rights, rights of way, sharing), access and interconnection (symmetric access, SMP market analysis, ex ante remedies including functional separation and co-investment), retail regulation
- **Part III (Articles 84–115)**: Services — universal service, numbering, end-user rights
- **Part IV (Articles 116–127)**: Final provisions — delegated acts, implementing acts, review, transposition, repeal, entry into force

## Substantive Provisions

### Part I — Framework

#### Classification of electronic communications services (Articles 1–2)

The EECC introduces a three-tier classification replacing the broad concept of "electronic communications service" in the 2002 Framework Directive:

1. **Internet access services**: as defined in Regulation (EU) 2015/2120.
2. **Interpersonal communications services**: enable direct exchange between finite numbers of persons. Subdivided into:
   - **Number-based**: connect to the public switched telephone network via nationally or internationally assigned numbers (e.g. voice calls, SMS). Subject to full obligations under Parts II and III.
   - **Number-independent**: do not use public numbers (e.g. WhatsApp, Signal, Skype when connecting only to other Skype users). Subject to lighter obligations; brought into scope for the first time.
3. **Signal-conveyance services**: pure carriage (e.g. broadcasting transmission), not interpersonal. Subject to access regulation but not to end-user rights provisions.

Purely cable TV services, broadcasting content and editorial content remain outside scope. The ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC) is expressly saved.

#### Very high capacity networks (Article 2(2))

A network qualifies as a VHCN if it either:
(a) consists wholly of optical fibre elements at least to the distribution point at the serving location (FTTP, FTTB, FTTC with fibre-to-the-cabinet where appropriate); or
(b) is capable under usual peak-traffic conditions of delivering similar network performance in terms of available downlink and uplink bandwidth, resilience, error-related parameters, and latency and its variation.

#### NRA independence (Articles 5–9)

NRAs must be functionally and legally independent from all market players. Structural separation between regulatory and ownership/operational functions is required where the state retains ownership. Heads must be appointed for at least three years through open transparent selection on merit; dismissal only on pre-established grounds. NRAs must have separate publicly disclosed budgets and adequate staffing for BEREC participation. Only appeals bodies under Article 31 may overturn NRA decisions on appeal.

#### General authorisation (Articles 12–19)

The 2002 Authorisation Directive's general authorisation regime is maintained and tightened. No individual licence required; entry is subject only to notification (a simple declaration). Conditions must be those in Annex I only; they must be sector-specific and proportionate. Competent authorities must issue a one-week confirmation. Administrative charges must reflect only administrative costs of managing the authorisation system, transparently published. Undertakings with combined EU annual telecoms turnover below EUR 50 million are exempt from accounting separation requirements.

#### Security (Articles 40–41)

Providers of public networks and publicly available services must implement proportionate technical and organisational security measures (including encryption, including end-to-end where appropriate). Significant security incidents must be notified to the competent authority without undue delay. Competent authorities may require security audits (at operator's cost), issue binding instructions, and obtain CSIRT assistance. Annual summary reports go to the Commission and ENISA. The Commission may adopt implementing acts on specific security measures and notification formats.

### Part II — Networks

#### Spectrum rights (Articles 45–56)

The EECC introduces a substantially reformed spectrum chapter:

- **Technology and service neutrality**: spectrum use rights may not be restricted by technology or service type unless specified circumstances apply (harmful interference, public health, efficiency, spectrum sharing needs).
- **Minimum 15-year duration** for harmonised wireless broadband spectrum; regulatory predictability must be assured for at least 20 years. At least two years before expiry, the NRA must conduct a forward-looking review; extension must be granted unless specific criteria are not met (Article 49(2)).
- **Renewal**: competitive/comparative selection procedure required in limited-rights bands; assessment must consider market objectives, fulfilment of conditions, competition, and technological evolution (Article 50).
- **Transfer and lease**: spectrum rights must be transferable and leasable with minimum procedural burden; refusal requires justification (Article 51).
- **Peer review before 5G auctions**: a voluntary RSPG Peer Review Forum mechanism allows Member States to comment on each other's draft selection procedures before award (Article 35).
- **5G bands**: by 31 December 2020, Member States must take measures to allow use of sufficiently large blocks of the 3.4–3.8 GHz band and at least 1 GHz of the 24.25–27.5 GHz band (Article 54).
- **30-month rule** (Article 53): Member States must allow spectrum use within 30 months of adoption of the relevant harmonisation measure.
- **Small-area wireless access points** (Article 57): may not require individual town-planning permits if they comply with physical/technical characteristics set by Commission implementing act (by 30 June 2020). Operators have the right to host small cells on public authority infrastructure (street furniture, light poles) on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms, published at a single information point.

#### Geographical surveys (Article 22)

NRAs must conduct a survey of broadband network geographical reach by 21 December 2023, updated at least every three years. Surveys may include forecasts up to three years ahead covering VHCNs and planned upgrades to at least 100 Mbps. Based on survey results, competent authorities may designate areas with no VHCN, invite deployment declarations, and manage demand coordination to avoid overbuilding. BEREC guidelines by 21 June 2020.

#### Access and interconnection (Articles 59–82)

**Symmetric access (Article 61(3))**: Irrespective of SMP designation, NRAs may impose access to wiring and cables inside buildings or up to the first concentration/distribution point where replication is economically inefficient or physically impracticable. NRAs may extend this beyond the first concentration point where high and non-transitory barriers to replication exist (subject to Commission/BEREC review). BEREC guidelines by 21 December 2020.

**SMP and market analysis (Articles 63–67)**: SMP = dominance equivalent. NRAs define markets following the Commission Recommendation (reviewed by 21 December 2020) and the SMP guidelines, using a three-criteria test for markets outside the Recommendation. Market reviews must be completed within five years (three years where triggered by a revised Recommendation or for new Member States). Transnational markets may be defined by Commission decision.

**SMP remedies (Articles 68–82)**:

Key instruments available to NRAs on SMP-designated undertakings:

- **Transparency** (Article 69): reference offers, including prices, accounting information, technical specifications, migration conditions. BEREC minimum criteria by 21 December 2019.
- **Non-discrimination** (Article 70): equivalence of inputs (EoI) is the surest method; same timescales, terms, conditions, systems and processes for all access seekers including the operator itself.
- **Accounting separation** (Article 71): separate accounts for wholesale and internal transfer activities.
- **Civil engineering access** (Article 72): access to buildings, entry points, cables, antennae, towers, poles, masts, ducts, manholes, cabinets — irrespective of the relevant market under analysis.
- **Network element access** (Article 73): local loop unbundling, active/virtual network elements, open interfaces, co-location, wholesale services for resale, interconnection, associated services (identity, location, presence).
- **Price control** (Article 74): cost-orientation, cost-accounting, price-squeeze prevention where competition is insufficient. Reasonable return on capital, accounting for investment risk (particularly for VHCNs). Annual independent verification of cost-accounting systems.
- **Voice termination rates** (Article 75): by 31 December 2020 the Commission must adopt a delegated act setting single maximum Union-wide mobile and fixed voice termination rates, reviewed every five years. (Adopted as Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/654.)
- **Co-investment in new VHCNs** (Article 76): SMP undertakings offering open co-investment commitments (meeting Annex IV criteria, open access, fair terms, at least one agreement signed) may have those commitments made binding by the NRA. Once binding, the NRA refrains from imposing additional SMP access obligations on the new VHCN elements. Commitments must be binding for at least seven years.
- **Functional separation** (Article 77): last resort, exceptional remedy. Prior Commission approval required. Vertical integration split into operationally independent wholesale access entity.
- **Wholesale-only undertakings** (Article 80): where the SMP undertaking has no retail presence, only non-discrimination, specific access and fair/reasonable pricing obligations apply (not the full SMP toolkit).
- **Legacy copper migration** (Article 81): SMP undertakings planning decommissioning must notify NRA in advance; NRA ensures transparent timetable and conditions, and that an alternative access product of at least comparable quality is available.

### Part III — Services

#### Universal service (Articles 84–91)

**Article 84** requires Member States to ensure all consumers have access at affordable prices to:
- Adequate broadband internet access at a fixed location (definition based on national bandwidth levels assessed using BEREC best practices report due by 21 June 2020, supporting at least the minimum set of services in Annex V); and
- Voice communications services at a fixed location.

Member States may extend to mobile services to ensure full social and economic participation.

**Article 85** requires Member States to ensure affordability for low-income or special-needs consumers through direct support (vouchers/payments) or requiring providers to offer social tariffs/packages. Exceptional designation procedures only where this is an excessive burden.

**Article 86** allows designation of providers where market and public policy tools are insufficient, through efficient, objective, transparent, non-discriminatory designation procedures.

Providers designated under Article 86 may be compensated for unfair net cost burdens. Cost calculation uses methodology in Annex VII, taking into account intangible benefits of being a universal service provider.

#### Numbering (Articles 93–97)

NRAs control national numbering plans. Non-geographic numbers must be made available for use throughout the Union (Article 93(3)). Rights of use must be granted within three weeks (six weeks for competitive procedures). The standard international access code remains 00. Member States may agree cross-border shared numbering plans. Free access to the 116000 missing children hotline must be ensured (Article 96).

#### End-user rights — full harmonisation (Articles 98–115)

Article 101 imposes **full harmonisation**: Member States may not maintain or introduce end-user protection provisions diverging from Articles 102–115. A transition period until 21 December 2021 allows retention of stricter national provisions that were in force on 20 December 2018, provided they are proportionate.

**Article 102 — Contract information and summary**: Before being bound, consumers must receive all statutory information plus a concise contract summary (maximum one A4 page; maximum three for bundles) covering service characteristics, price, duration, renewal/termination conditions, accessibility for disabled users, and internet access quality summary. Commission must adopt a contract summary template by 21 December 2019. Providers must also notify consumers before consumption limits are reached.

**Article 103 — Comparison tools**: Competent authorities must ensure at least one independent free comparison tool for prices and quality of internet access services and number-based interpersonal communications services. Third parties may reuse published information free of charge in open data formats.

**Article 105 — Contract duration and termination**:
- Maximum 24-month commitment period (Member States may mandate shorter maxima).
- After automatic renewal: termination at any time on one month's notice with no cost beyond service charges during notice.
- Providers must notify one month in advance of automatic renewal and simultaneously give best tariff advice.
- Annual best tariff information required.
- Recurring significant discrepancy between actual and contracted internet service performance = grounds for free termination.
- On early termination: no compensation beyond pro rata value of retained subsidised terminal equipment.

**Article 106 — Switching and number portability**:
- Continuity of internet access service during switching; maximum one working day loss of service.
- Number portability within one working day.
- Receiving provider leads the process.
- Compensation mechanism for delays.

**Article 109 — Emergency communications and 112**:
- All end-users of number-based interpersonal communications services must be able to access emergency services free of charge via 112 and national emergency numbers.
- Caller location information (geographic coordinates for mobile; physical address for fixed) must be made available free of charge immediately upon emergency call set-up.
- Commission may adopt delegated acts on emergency communications compatibility, interoperability, quality and reliability (first delegated act by 21 December 2022).
- BEREC maintains database of E.164 numbers of Member State emergency services.

**Article 110 — Public warning system**: By 21 June 2022, Member States must ensure that public warnings for imminent or developing major emergencies or disasters are transmitted by mobile number-based interpersonal communications service providers to all end-users in affected areas. Equivalent systems are allowed if assessed as such by reference to BEREC guidelines (by 21 June 2020).

**Article 111 — Disabled access**: Competent authorities must specify requirements ensuring equivalent access for persons with disabilities, including as regards prices, tariffs and quality.

## Lineage

### Repealed directives (effect from 21 December 2020)

| CELEX | Directive | Common name |
|---|---|---|
| 32002L0019 | 2002/19/EC | Access Directive |
| 32002L0020 | 2002/20/EC | Authorisation Directive |
| 32002L0021 | 2002/21/EC | Framework Directive |
| 32002L0022 | 2002/22/EC | Universal Service Directive |

The ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC, CELEX 32002L0058) was NOT recast; it remains in force and the EECC is expressly without prejudice to it.

The Radio Spectrum Decision (Decision No 676/2002/EC) is retained as the legal basis for technical harmonisation measures; the EECC supplements and references it throughout.

### Simultaneously adopted companion legislation

- **Regulation (EU) 2018/1971** — new BEREC Regulation (replaces Regulation (EC) No 1211/2009)

- **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/654** (OJ L 135, 19.4.2021): single maximum Union-wide voice termination rates (fixed and mobile), adopted under Article 75
- Emergency communications delegated act (Article 109): due by 21 December 2022
- Annex adaptations (Article 116): adaptation of Annexes V, VI, IX, X, XI to reflect technological or social developments
