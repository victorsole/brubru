# Court of Justice, Case C-706/23: workers with several contracts keep full pro rata rights (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Judgment of 8 October 2026**, Case **C-706/23 | Şcoala gimnazială "Mihai Eminescu"**, Court of Justice (Court press release No 142/26). A preliminary ruling requested by the Court of Appeal of Iaşi (Romania).
- **Holding:** the entitlements of a person employed **full time** who also works **part time paid by the hour** must be calculated on the basis of the work performed and the income received under **both** contracts. EU law **precludes** national legislation that calculates paid annual leave pay and meal allowances **only** on the full-time permanent contract.
- **Three instruments applied (exactly three):** the Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC (at least four weeks of paid annual leave, CELEX 32003L0088), the part-time work framework agreement (Directive 97/81/EC, CELEX 31997L0081) and the fixed-term work framework agreement (Directive 1999/70/EC, CELEX 31999L0070).
- **Facts:** between 2019 and 2022, fourteen Romanian teachers on full-time permanent contracts also worked part time on fixed-term contracts, paid by the hour, in the same schools from September to June. Their annual leave pay and meal allowances were computed only on the full-time contract. Their trade union sued; the Vaslui Regional Court agreed in 2023 and the employer appealed.
- **Reasoning in brief:** each such employee is both a "fixed-term worker" and a "part-time worker" under the framework agreements; the meal allowance is part of remuneration and so an "employment condition". The ban on less favourable treatment applies even when an employee accumulates two contracts, the difference in treatment has no objective justification and the **pro rata temporis** principle was not applied correctly.
- **The right to annual leave** of at least four weeks is a fundamental principle of EU social law with no derogation; during leave the worker must receive pay comparable to periods of work.
- Source (Court press release): https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-10/cp260142en.pdf
