# EU-Philippines Free Trade Agreement (FTA)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Tuesday 22 September 2026): SUBSTANTIAL AGREEMENT reached.** The European Union and the Philippines reached "substantial agreement" in their negotiations for a Free Trade Agreement (FTA). Announced by **Maroš Šefčovič** (Commissioner for Trade and Economic Security) and **Maria Cristina Aldeguer-Roque** (Philippine Secretary of Trade and Industry) after a video call. Commission press release IP/26/1929: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1929
- **Status in one line: substantial agreement at political level, NOT formal conclusion, NOT signed, NOT in force.** The two sides still have to "finalise the negotiations", including "determining its implementation and finalising technical details". Formal conclusion is expected "in the coming months" (joint statement STATEMENT/26/1922). Signature is foreseen in **2027**: President von der Leyen "looks forward to returning to the Philippines in 2027 to sign the FTA" (readout READ/26/1927).
- **Leaders' call (Monday 21 September 2026)**: President **Ursula von der Leyen** spoke with President **Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr.**, "just three years after" her **2023 visit to Manila**, when the two leaders agreed to relaunch the negotiations. Readout: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/read_26_1927
- **Headline market access**: the FTA will liberalise **over 94% of tariff lines, covering more than 97% of bilateral trade**, on both sides. No product-by-product tariff schedule has been published yet.
- **Agri-food (Commissioner for Agriculture and Food Christophe Hansen)**: tariff preferences on key EU export interests "such as pigmeat", and protection for **almost 200 EU Geographical Indications (GIs)**. Key EU agri-food exports: pork, poultry, dairy, spirits. EU agri-food exports about EUR 2 billion against EUR 1.4 billion of imports (Šefčovič remarks, SPEECH/26/1932).
- **Other chapters announced**: Philippine **government procurement** opened to foreign bidders "for the first-time ever"; intellectual property; digital trade (with data privacy and consumer protection); sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) measures and technical barriers to trade (TBT); energy and raw materials; a "very ambitious" **Trade and Sustainable Development (TSD) chapter**, with **human rights and the Paris Agreement as essential elements** of the agreement.
- **Trade figures**: goods EUR 17.6 billion (2025), services EUR 10.3 billion (2024). EU is the Philippines' **fourth-largest** trading partner (8.3% of its goods trade). EU foreign direct investment (FDI) stock in the Philippines EUR 15.4 billion; Philippine FDI stock in the EU EUR 2.4 billion. Philippines population: 113 million.
- **History**: negotiations launched **22 December 2015**; second round **February 2017**, then on hold; stocktaking exercise announced **July 2023**; resumption announced **18 March 2024** (IP/24/1504); sixth round Manila **18-22 May 2026**; substantial agreement **22 September 2026**.
- **Trade preferences today**: the Philippines benefits from the **Generalised Scheme of Preferences plus (GSP+)** since **December 2014**, a unilateral EU arrangement (NOT a trade agreement) that removes tariffs on two thirds of product categories, conditional on 27 core international conventions. GSP runs under Regulation (EU) No 978/2012, extended to **31 December 2027** by Regulation (EU) 2023/2663.
- **EU-only or mixed?** NOT yet published. No Commission document has said whether the FTA will be an EU-only agreement or a mixed agreement. Do not assert either.
- **Lead**: Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security (DG TRADE). European Parliament committee for consent: Committee on International Trade (INTA).

## What was agreed on 22 September 2026

Source: Commission press release IP/26/1929, "Substantial agreement on EU-Philippines trade deal" (https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1929), and DG TRADE's Philippines page (https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/eu-trade-relationships-country-and-region/countries-and-regions/philippines_en).

The Commission describes the outcome as a "substantial agreement at political level". The mutual benefits it lists:

- **Tariffs and market access**: elimination of tariffs on both sides; over 94% of tariff lines liberalised, covering more than 97% of bilateral trade. EU industrial exports are led by machinery and appliances, transport equipment, medicines and medical appliances.
- **Agri-food**: key EU exports are meat products (pork, poultry), dairy and spirits. Commissioner Hansen: "We have reached an agreement on tariff preferences on key EU export interests such as pigmeat, and secured protection for almost 200 of our Geographical Indicators."
- **Government procurement**: clear rules, opening the Philippine government procurement market to foreign bidders for the first time.
- **Intellectual property**: better protection, including a "commercially meaningful list" of EU GIs.
- **Digital trade**: rules facilitating digital trade while guaranteeing data privacy and consumer protection.
- **SPS and TBT**: transparent rules on sanitary and phytosanitary measures and technical barriers to trade.
- **Sustainability**: respect of human rights and the Paris Agreement made essential elements of the agreement, plus a "very ambitious" TSD chapter.
- **Energy and raw materials**: provisions aimed at a level playing field to facilitate sustainable investment, in particular in renewable energy.

**Services and investment**: the press release says the agreement will improve "conditions for exporters, service suppliers and investors on both sides". It publishes no services schedule and says nothing about investment protection or investor-state dispute settlement. Do not describe investment-protection content: none has been published.

**Not yet published**: the legal text, tariff schedules, the GI list, rules of origin, staging periods and any sensitive-product arrangements. Figures beyond those above should not be stated.

Commissioner Šefčovič's press remarks (SPEECH/26/1932, https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/speech_26_1932) add: EU exporters already sell over EUR 8 billion a year to the Philippines; semiconductors are cited as a supply-chain example; renewable energy as an investment area.

## Negotiation history

Source: DG TRADE, EU-Philippines agreement page (https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/eu-trade-relationships-country-and-region/countries-and-regions/philippines/eu-philippines-agreement_en).

- **October 2015**: joint scoping exercise concluded.
- **November 2015**: Member States authorise the Commission to open negotiations.
- **22 December 2015**: negotiations launched.
- **February 2017**: second negotiating round; talks then put on hold.
- **30 June 2022**: the Marcos administration takes office (IP/24/1504).
- **July 2023**: the EU and the Philippines announce a technical stocktaking exercise to assess whether to resume talks. This followed President von der Leyen's visit to Manila in 2023 (readout READ/26/1927). The stocktaking concluded at the end of 2023.
- **18 March 2024**: resumption of negotiations formally announced (IP/24/1504, https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_24_1504).
- **18-22 May 2026**: sixth round of the resumed negotiations, Manila (EU Delegation to the Philippines: https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/philippines/sixth-round-negotiations-european-union-%E2%80%93-philippines-free-trade-agreement-eu-ph-fta_en).
- **21 September 2026**: von der Leyen and Marcos call.
- **22 September 2026**: substantial agreement.

A Sustainability Impact Assessment (SIA) was carried out in support of the negotiations (linked from the DG TRADE page).

## GSP+ and what changes

Since December 2014 the Philippines has had GSP+ preferences: full tariff removal on two thirds of product categories, subject to monitoring of 27 core conventions on human and labour rights, environmental protection and good governance. In 2023, 73% of the Philippines' GSP+-eligible exports to the EU used the preferences (DG TRADE Philippines page). GSP+ is unilateral and can be withdrawn by the EU; an FTA is a reciprocal, bilateral treaty. The GSP Regulation (EU) No 978/2012 applies until 31 December 2027 (Regulation (EU) 2023/2663, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2663/oj); a successor GSP regulation is being negotiated (see `eu_gsp_new_generation`). How GSP+ and the FTA preferences will interact after entry into force has not been published.

## Next steps

Confirmed by primary sources:
1. Negotiators finalise the text and technical details (IP/26/1929); formal conclusion "in the coming months" (STATEMENT/26/1922).
2. Signature foreseen in **2027**, in the Philippines (READ/26/1927).

Standard EU procedure for trade agreements (Article 218 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, TFEU), not yet specific to this file: legal scrubbing, translation into the EU languages, Commission proposals for Council decisions on signature and conclusion, European Parliament consent (INTA leads), then Council conclusion and entry into force. No Commission proposal for this agreement has been tabled yet. Whether national ratification will be needed depends on whether it is EU-only or mixed, which has NOT been published.

## Place in the EU's Indo-Pacific FTA network

The EU aims for bilateral agreements with members of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) as building blocks towards a future region-to-region EU-ASEAN agreement (region-to-region talks launched 2007, paused 2009).

| Partner | Status |
|---|---|
| Singapore | FTA in force (2019) |
| Vietnam | FTA in force (2020) |
| Indonesia | Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA): negotiations concluded 23 September 2025; proposals to Council 29 June 2026 (see `eu_indonesia_cepa`) |
| Philippines | Substantial agreement 22 September 2026 (this guide) |
| Thailand | Negotiating ("making strides", Šefčovič, 22 September 2026) |
| Malaysia | Negotiating ("engaging intensively", Šefčovič, 22 September 2026) |

Beyond ASEAN, the EU has agreements with Japan, Korea, New Zealand and Australia, and concluded negotiations with India (see `eu_trade_policy`). The readout notes the Philippines holds the ASEAN chairmanship and that 2027 marks the 50th anniversary of EU-ASEAN relations. It also records EU solidarity with the Philippines "in the face of aggressive maritime claims".
