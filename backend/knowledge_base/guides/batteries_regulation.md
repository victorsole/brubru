## Overview

The EU Batteries Regulation is the world's first "cradle to grave" regulatory framework for a single product category: it governs the whole life cycle of every battery placed on the EU market, from the sustainability of raw-material sourcing, through carbon footprint, recycled content, performance, durability and safety, to labelling, a digital battery passport, and finally collection, treatment, recycling and material recovery once the battery becomes waste. It replaces Directive 2006/66/EC, which the co-legislators judged too thin (mainly collection targets and a handful of substance bans) for a market that has been transformed by electromobility and grid-scale energy storage.

Because a Directive would have allowed 27 different national implementations and fragmented the internal market, the co-legislators chose a Regulation instead (recital 10): uniform rules apply directly in every Member State without transposition. The dual legal basis reflects this split personality - Article 114 TFEU (internal market) covers the product rules in Chapters II to VII and IX to XII, while Article 192(1) TFEU (environment) covers the waste-management rules in Chapter VIII (Articles 54 to 76).

The Regulation introduces five categories of battery - portable, starting/lighting/ignition (SLI), light means of transport (LMT), industrial, and electric vehicle - and imposes a graduated set of obligations depending on category, chemistry and capacity. It is one of the most technically dense pieces of EU product legislation in force: it delegates dozens of methodologies (carbon-footprint calculation, recycled-content verification, recycling-efficiency formulas) to future Commission delegated and implementing acts, several of which are still being adopted years after entry into force.

## Scope and the battery categories

Article 1 sets the scope: the Regulation applies to all batteries placed on the market or put into service in the Union, whether produced in the Union or imported, whether sold on their own or incorporated into another product (appliances, vehicles, light means of transport). It excludes batteries built into equipment for Member States' essential security interests or war material, and batteries designed for space equipment (Article 1(5)).

Article 3 defines five battery categories (with the strictest-applicable-category rule where a battery could fall under more than one):

- **Portable battery** - sealed, weighs 5 kg or less, not designed for industrial use, and not an EV, LMT or SLI battery. A sub-category, "portable battery of general use", covers standardised formats such as AA, AAA, D, C, button cell and 9V.
- **SLI battery (starting, lighting, ignition)** - specifically designed to supply electric power for starting, lighting or ignition, also usable for auxiliary or backup purposes in vehicles, other transport or machinery. Typically the conventional car battery.
- **LMT battery (light means of transport)** - sealed, weighs 25 kg or less, designed to provide traction for wheeled vehicles powered by an electric motor alone or combined with human power (e-bikes, e-scooters, type-approved category L vehicles). New category created by this Regulation; did not exist under Directive 2006/66/EC.
- **Industrial battery** - designed for industrial uses, or repurposed for industrial use, or any other battery weighing more than 5 kg that does not fall into another category. Includes stationary battery energy storage systems (grid or private/domestic storage).
- **Electric vehicle battery** - specifically designed to provide traction power in hybrid or electric vehicles of category L (over 25 kg) or of categories M, N or O. New category, split out of "industrial" under the old Directive because of its scale and growth.

## Structure

The Regulation runs Articles 1 to 96 across 14 Chapters:

- **Chapter I - General provisions (Art. 1-5).** Subject matter and scope, objectives, definitions, free movement of compliant batteries, and the general condition that batteries may only be placed on the market or put into service if they meet the sustainability, safety, labelling and information requirements.
- **Chapter II - Sustainability and safety requirements (Art. 6-12).** Substance restrictions (Art. 6), carbon footprint declaration/labelling/threshold (Art. 7), recycled content (Art. 8), performance and durability for portable batteries of general use (Art. 9) and for rechargeable industrial/LMT/EV batteries (Art. 10), removability and replaceability of portable and LMT batteries (Art. 11), safety of stationary battery energy storage systems (Art. 12).
- **Chapter III - Labelling, marking and information requirements (Art. 13-14).** Labels, the separate-collection symbol, heavy-metal symbols, the QR code, and battery-management-system data on state of health and expected lifetime.
- **Chapter IV - Conformity of batteries (Art. 15-20).** Presumption of conformity via harmonised standards, common specifications, conformity assessment procedures (Modules A, D1, G), the EU declaration of conformity, CE marking.
- **Chapter V - Notification of conformity assessment bodies (Art. 21-37).** Notifying authorities, requirements for notified bodies, notification procedure, coordination between notified bodies.
- **Chapter VI - Obligations of economic operators (Art. 38-46).** Duties of manufacturers, cell/module suppliers, authorised representatives, importers, distributors and fulfilment service providers; traceability along the supply chain.
- **Chapter VII - Battery due diligence (Art. 47-53).** Scope threshold (economic operators below EUR 40 million turnover are exempt), management-system requirements, risk-management obligations, third-party verification by a notified body, public disclosure, and recognition of equivalent third-party due diligence schemes.
- **Chapter VIII - Management of waste batteries (Art. 54-76).** Competent authorities, the register of producers, extended producer responsibility, collection obligations and targets (portable, LMT, SLI/industrial/EV), distributor take-back, treatment, recycling-efficiency and material-recovery targets, shipment rules, and reporting to Member States and the Commission.
- **Chapter IX - Digital battery passport (Art. 77-78).** Passport content and access tiers, technical design requirements (interoperability, decentralised storage, security).
- **Chapter X - Union market surveillance and safeguard procedures (Art. 79-84).** National procedure for non-compliant or risk-presenting batteries, the EU safeguard procedure, formal non-compliance, non-compliance with due diligence obligations.
- **Chapter XI - Green public procurement and substance-restriction procedure (Art. 85-88).** Award criteria for public procurement of batteries; the REACH-aligned procedure (via ECHA) for adding or amending substance restrictions in Annex I.
- **Chapter XII - Delegated powers and committee procedure (Art. 89-90).**
- **Chapter XIII - Amendments (Art. 91-92).** Amends Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (market surveillance) and Directive 2008/98/EC (Article 8a(7), extended producer responsibility schemes).
- **Chapter XIV - Final provisions (Art. 93-96).** Penalties, review clauses, repeal of Directive 2006/66/EC, entry into force and application.

Annexes I to XIV supply the technical detail: Annex I (substance restrictions), Annex II (carbon-footprint methodology elements), Annex III (performance/durability parameters, portable batteries), Annex IV (performance/durability parameters, industrial/LMT/EV batteries), Annex V (safety parameters, stationary battery energy storage systems), Annex VI (labelling: general information, separate-collection symbol, QR code), Annex VII (battery-management-system parameters), Annex VIII (conformity assessment modules and technical documentation), Annex IX (EU declaration of conformity model), Annex X (due diligence: raw materials, risk categories, international instruments), Annex XI (collection-rate calculation methodology), Annex XII (treatment requirements, recycling-efficiency targets, material-recovery targets), Annex XIII (battery passport content and access tiers), Annex XIV (minimum requirements to distinguish used batteries from waste batteries for shipment purposes).

## Substantive obligations by theme

**Sustainability and carbon footprint (Article 7).** Electric vehicle batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh, and LMT batteries must be accompanied by a carbon footprint declaration, calculated in kg CO2-equivalent per kWh of total energy delivered over the battery's expected service life, broken down by life-cycle stage. The obligation is staggered by category and rolls out in three layers over time: first a declaration, then a labelled performance class, then (last) a maximum life-cycle carbon footprint threshold that non-compliant batteries cannot exceed.

**Recycled content (Article 8).** From 18 August 2028, industrial batteries above 2 kWh, EV batteries and SLI batteries containing cobalt, lead, lithium or nickel in active materials must be accompanied by documentation disclosing the recovered share of each metal (LMT batteries follow from 18 August 2033). From 18 August 2031, those batteries must meet binding minimum recycled-content shares; from 18 August 2036, higher minimum shares apply, extended to LMT batteries too. Battery manufacturing waste counts toward the target; manufacturing scrap that is reused in the same process does not.

**Performance and durability (Articles 9-10).** Portable batteries of general use (excluding button cells) must meet minimum electrochemical performance and durability values from 18 August 2028, set by a Commission delegated act. Rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh, LMT batteries and EV batteries must be accompanied from 18 August 2024 by documentation of their performance and durability parameters, with binding minimum values following later (18 August 2027 for industrial, 18 August 2028 for LMT).

**Safety (Article 12).** Stationary battery energy storage systems - not covered by any other EU product law before this Regulation - must be tested against the safety parameters in Annex V and any additional hazards specific to the technology, with mitigation instructions for foreseeable failure modes such as fire or explosion.

**Labelling and information, plus the digital battery passport (Articles 13-14, 77-78).** All batteries must carry a label with general information, capacity data (rechargeable batteries), minimum average duration (non-rechargeable portable batteries), the separate-collection symbol (from 18 August 2025), and heavy-metal symbols where cadmium or lead exceed threshold concentrations. From 18 February 2027, every battery must also carry a QR code. For LMT batteries, industrial batteries above 2 kWh, and EV batteries, that QR code links to a digital battery passport (Article 77) - an electronic record covering the battery model and the individual unit, including carbon footprint, material composition, supply-chain origin, repair/repurposing/dismantling information, and (for stationary storage, LMT and EV batteries with a battery-management system) real-time state-of-health data. Access is tiered: some information is public, some restricted to notified bodies/market surveillance authorities/the Commission, and some restricted to parties with a legitimate interest (repairers, remanufacturers, recyclers, the purchaser).

**Supply-chain due diligence (Chapter VII, Articles 47-53).** Economic operators placing batteries on the market with turnover of EUR 40 million or more (individually or as part of a consolidated group) must, from 18 August 2025, adopt a battery due diligence policy covering cobalt, natural graphite, lithium, nickel and their compounds (Annex X); identify and manage social and environmental risks in their supply chain, drawing on OECD/UN/ILO due diligence standards; have their policy third-party verified and periodically audited by a notified body; and publish an annual public due diligence report. The Commission can recognise industry due diligence schemes as equivalent.

**Removability and replaceability (Article 11).** From 18 February 2027, any person placing on the market a product incorporating a portable battery must ensure the battery is readily removable and replaceable by the end-user using commonly available tools, with narrow derogations (washable/splash-exposed appliances, medical imaging devices, continuity-of-power or data-integrity needs). LMT batteries must be removable and replaceable by an independent professional. Software cannot be used to block replacement with a compatible battery, and spare batteries must remain available for at least five years after the last unit of the powered equipment is placed on the market.

**End of life: extended producer responsibility, collection, recycling and material recovery (Chapter VIII, Articles 54-76).** Producers bear extended producer responsibility for the batteries they place on the market for the first time in each Member State: they must register in a national producer register, finance and organise free take-back and collection networks (individually or through a producer responsibility organisation), and meet binding collection targets. Collected waste batteries cannot be landfilled or incinerated for energy recovery; they must go to a permitted facility for preparation for re-use, preparation for repurposing, or recycling, meeting the recycling-efficiency and material-recovery targets in Annex XII. Producers and waste management operators must report annually to national competent authorities, who in turn report aggregated, machine-readable data to the Commission.

## Key Numbers

- **Structure:** 96 Articles, 14 Chapters, 14 Annexes.
- **Due-diligence turnover threshold:** economic operators with turnover below EUR 40 million (unconsolidated or consolidated group) are exempt from Chapter VII (Article 47).
- **Collection targets, portable batteries (Article 59(3)):** 45% by 31 December 2023; 63% by 31 December 2027; 73% by 31 December 2030.
- **Collection targets, LMT batteries (Article 60(3)):** 51% by 31 December 2028; 61% by 31 December 2031.
- **Recycling efficiency targets, Annex XII Part B:** by 31 December 2025 - 75% lead-acid batteries, 65% lithium-based batteries, 80% nickel-cadmium batteries, 50% other batteries; by 31 December 2030 - 80% lead-acid, 70% lithium-based.
- **Material recovery targets, Annex XII Part C:** by 31 December 2027 - 90% cobalt, copper, lead and nickel; 50% lithium; by 31 December 2031 - 95% cobalt, copper, lead and nickel; 80% lithium.
- **Recycled content minima from 18 August 2031 (Article 8(2)):** 16% cobalt, 85% lead, 6% lithium, 6% nickel (in active materials, industrial >2kWh, EV and SLI batteries).
- **Recycled content minima from 18 August 2036 (Article 8(3)):** 26% cobalt, 85% lead, 12% lithium, 15% nickel (extended to LMT batteries).
- **Carbon footprint declaration applies from:** 18 February 2025 (EV batteries); 18 February 2026 (rechargeable industrial batteries >2kWh); 18 August 2028 (LMT batteries); 18 August 2030 (industrial batteries with external storage) - Article 7(1).
- **Maximum life-cycle carbon footprint threshold applies from:** 18 February 2028 (EV); 18 February 2029 (industrial); 18 August 2031 (LMT); 18 August 2033 (industrial with external storage) - Article 7(3).
- **Battery passport and universal QR code:** from 18 February 2027 (Articles 13(6) and 77(1)).
- **Removability and replaceability of portable/LMT batteries:** from 18 February 2027 (Article 11, per Article 96(2)(a)).
- **Separate collection symbol on all batteries:** from 18 August 2025 (Article 13(4)).
- **Battery due diligence obligations apply:** from 18 August 2025 (Article 48(1)).
- **Waste management/EPR Chapter VIII applies:** from 18 August 2025 (Article 96(2)(c)); Directive 2006/66/EC repealed the same date.

## Lineage

- **2006** - Directive 2006/66/EC (batteries and accumulators, waste batteries and accumulators) establishes the first EU-wide battery framework, repealing Directive 91/157/EEC.
- **2018** - Commission Communication "Europe on the Move" sets out the Strategic Action Plan on Batteries, targeting a European battery value chain.
- **11 December 2019** - European Green Deal Communication commits to legislation for a safe, circular and sustainable battery value chain.
- **10 December 2020** - Commission proposal for the Batteries Regulation (COM(2020) 798 final), OEIL 2020/0353(COD).
- **14 June 2023** - European Parliament adopts its position.
- **28 June 2023** - Council decision.
- **12 July 2023** - Regulation signed at Strasbourg.
- **28 July 2023** - published as OJ L 191, 28.7.2023, p. 1.
- **17 August 2023** - entry into force.
- **18 February 2024** - general date of application begins.
- **18 August 2025** - Chapter VIII (waste management/EPR) applies; due diligence obligations apply; Directive 2006/66/EC repealed.
- **18 February 2027** - digital battery passport live; universal QR code mandatory; removability and replaceability of portable/LMT batteries in force.
- **2028-2036** - staggered recycled-content minima, carbon-footprint thresholds and higher recycling/material-recovery targets phase in.

The Regulation sits alongside the Critical Raw Materials Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1252) - both respond to the same strategic-autonomy driver (cobalt, lithium, nickel, natural graphite dependency), with the CRMA addressing extraction/processing capacity and the Batteries Regulation addressing recycled content and end-of-life recovery of the same materials. It also complements the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (which will eventually set eco-design requirements for phones/tablets containing batteries) and the End-of-Life Vehicles framework (Directive 2000/53/EC, which governs SLI and EV battery removal from scrapped vehicles).
