# Commission Recommendation on housing affordability and supply in areas under housing stress

## QUICK FACTS
- **ADOPTED Wednesday 9 September 2026. C(2026) 6318 final**, legal basis **Article 292 TFEU**, signed by Commissioner **Dan J&oslash;rgensen**. A Recommendation is **not binding**: it creates no obligation and no deadline.
- **It is the supply half of a two-part package.** The other half is the proposed **Affordable Housing Act** Regulation, adopted the same day, which governs how restrictions are justified. This Recommendation says what to do instead of, and alongside, restricting.
- **The line it exists to make:** *"Restrictions alone cannot solve housing shortages."* The Commission is explicit that demand-side measures on short-term rentals, second homes and vacancy cannot fix a structural supply problem.
- **The central instrument is the HOUSING ACCELERATION PLAN**: a local plan for the stressed area **and its commuting zone and functional urban area**, based on real data, resourced with money and staff, coordinated across levels of government and with residents, and integrated with existing plans rather than added beside them.
- **The money, and the gap.** The Commission estimates **an additional EUR 150 billion a year** is needed over the next decade on top of current public funding and private investment. Against that, the EU has mobilised **at least EUR 47.7 billion** under the current MFF through cohesion policy, InvestEU, LIFE, the single market programme, Horizon Europe and NextGenerationEU, plus **EUR 4.7 billion** reprogrammed under the cohesion mid-term review. New possibilities are expected under the **2028-2034 MFF**.
- **Seven sections**: subject matter; housing acceleration plans; accelerating construction and renovation through permitting, procurement and modern methods of construction; increasing supply of housing below market prices; better use of the existing building stock; investing in land and buildings; mobilising funding and financing.
- **Concrete asks worth knowing**: **60-day permitting** timeframes where relevant and tacit approval for some steps; interoperable digital permitting with **Building Information Modelling**; **openBIM as a legally recognised submission format**; local **targets** for housing below market prices with inclusive zoning and density bonuses; a **20-year minimum** designation for publicly subsidised affordable housing under Decision (EU) 2025/2630; land banks, public land agencies and **long-term public leasehold** where the authority keeps the land; **revolving funds**; reduced tax distortion between owner-occupied and rental housing.

## What it recommends, section by section

**Housing acceleration plans.** Tailored to local conditions, or integrated into existing plans.
Must cover the stressed area **plus commuting zones and functional urban areas**, so that underused
housing outside the pressure point can be made viable through transport investment. Must be based on
data, coordinated with other policies (housing exclusion, urban planning, tourism, transport, climate
and heat resilience, energy renovation), and adequately resourced.

**Permitting, procurement, construction methods.** Prioritise approvals for affordable and social
housing and for renovation, adaptation, extension, repurposing and subdivision, including allowing
deviations from local zoning where substantive standards are met. Work towards maximum processing
times, **enforcing 60-day permitting where relevant**. Use the flexibilities already in EU public
procurement law: framework contracts, joint procurement, accelerated procedures, architectural
competitions, and division into lots to let SMEs bid. Support **modern methods of construction**,
offsite and modular, and harmonised European standards under Regulation (EU) 2024/3110.

**Housing below market prices.** Legally recognise the diverse providers that exist across the Union,
including **cooperatives, community land trusts and limited-profit and non-profit providers**, several
of which lack legal recognition in some Member States and therefore cannot access public support or
land. Set local targets, use inclusive zoning and density bonuses, and keep subsidised housing
designated for at least the **20-year minimum** where Decision (EU) 2025/2630 requires it.

**Existing building stock.** Revise building codes, zoning, tax systems, rental law and co-ownership
law so that renovation, repurposing, densification and subdivision become possible rather than
obstructed. Encourage letting rooms to students and apprentices, and voluntary right-sizing schemes.

**Land and buildings.** Acquire, readjust and release land; separate land from building ownership;
use resale restrictions and mandatory buy-back; create **public land agencies, land banks and
development corporations**; retain ownership through long leases when making land available.

**Funding and financing.** National hubs within the **Pan-European Investment Platform** for
affordable and sustainable housing; blended public-private finance and de-risking; revolving funds;
long-term concessional lending via national and regional promotional banks, the **Council of Europe
Development Bank**, the **EBRD** and the **EIB Group**.

## What to say when asked

- **"Is this binding?"** No. It is a Recommendation under Article 292 TFEU. No obligation, no deadline, no penalty.
- **"What is a housing acceleration plan?"** A local supply plan the Commission encourages, not a legal requirement, covering the stressed area and its commuting zone.
- **"How much money comes with it?"** None that is new. It points at existing instruments and at the **EUR 150 billion annual gap** against **EUR 47.7 billion** mobilised.
- Do not describe it as part of the Regulation. They are **two separate instruments** adopted on the same day, with different legal bases and different force.
