# European Affordable Housing Plan (EAHP)

## QUICK FACTS

- Initiative: European Affordable Housing Plan -- the EU's first-ever dedicated plan to support all governance levels in delivering affordable housing
- Commission Communication: COM(2025) 1025 final
- Date presented: **16 December 2025**
- Lead Commissioner: **Dan Jorgensen (Commissioner for Energy and Housing, Denmark)**
- Lead DG: DG ENER (Energy and Housing) acts as site-manager; cross-cuts DG REGIO (cohesion + Right to Stay), DG EMPL (homelessness, social housing), DG COMP (State aid revision), DG GROW (construction productivity), DG MOVE (urban mobility), DG ENV (Bauhaus + circular construction)
- Press: IP/25/3048; Q&A QANDA/25/3049; Factsheet FS/25/3050
- Site: housing.ec.europa.eu/european-affordable-housing-plan_en
- Type: Communication (non-legislative) -- frames the architecture and feeds individual legislative + funding instruments downstream
- Adoption process: prepared with contributions from the **Housing Advisory Board** (expert group), which delivered its recommendations to Commissioner Jorgensen on **20 November 2025**
- Cross-link in the Commission's spring social agenda: complementary to the **Anti-poverty Strategy 2026** (College adoption 6 May 2026, see `eu_anti_poverty_strategy_2026`), the **Council Recommendation on fighting housing exclusion** (see `eu_housing_exclusion_recommendation_2026`), and the **EP HOUS Committee resolution A10-0025/2026** of 10 March 2026 (see `eu_housing_crisis`)
- 2026 follow-up actions: **Housing Summit**, **Housing Alliance launch**, **complementary Citizens Energy Package** (energy poverty + bills)
- Underlying right framing: housing as a fundamental right and cornerstone of human dignity (Article 31 of the Charter and Article 34(3) of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the EU)

## What the Plan is

The European Affordable Housing Plan is the Commission's first comprehensive, EU-level architecture for the housing-affordability and housing-availability crisis affecting Member States. It is a **non-legislative Communication** that frames the EU's role across four pillars and feeds downstream legislative + financial + State aid instruments to be adopted in 2026 and beyond. The plan is institutionally located under Commissioner Jorgensen (Energy and Housing), formalising the new combined portfolio in the von der Leyen II Commission.

## The Four Pillars

### Pillar 1 -- Boosting Housing Supply

- Strengthen productivity and innovation in the construction sector through advanced materials, offsite/modular construction methods, and digitalisation
- New **Housing Simplification Package**: cut red tape and accelerate permitting (links to the EP HOUS Committee's 60-day permit-issuance target adopted 10 March 2026 in A10-0025/2026)
- Combine affordability, sustainability and quality through innovative techniques aligned with the **New European Bauhaus**
- European Strategy for Housing Construction (forthcoming, ongoing)

### Pillar 2 -- Mobilising Investment

- New **Pan-European Investment Platform** for housing, anchored by the European Investment Bank (EIB) in partnership with national and regional promotional banks plus international financial institutions
- **Revise State aid rules** to enable faster public support for social and affordable housing without prior Commission notification (this is the most consequential single action because it directly unlocks Member-State and regional spend)

### Pillar 3 -- Enabling Immediate Support and Driving Reforms

- **Legislative initiative on short-term rentals** in housing-stressed areas (regulatory floor for tourist-rental impact on local rents; complements the EP HOUS Committee's call for "balanced regulation of short-term rentals")
- Tackle speculation and promote market transparency
- Member State **structural reforms** in spatial planning, social housing, taxation (Country-Specific Recommendations under European Semester will reflect housing priorities)

### Pillar 4 -- Protecting the Most Affected

- Mobilise **student and youth housing** investment + innovative models for disadvantaged youth
- Expand **social housing** stock + targeted measures to **prevent and address homelessness**
- Cross-link with the **Council Recommendation on fighting housing exclusion** of 6 May 2026 (Social Package, see `eu_housing_exclusion_recommendation_2026`)

## What is NOT in the Plan

- **No EU-level quantitative targets** (no headline figure for new units to be built or rents to be lowered)
- **No new EU funding line dedicated to housing** -- the plan re-channels existing instruments (Cohesion Policy / ERDF, RRF, EIB) and adds the Pan-European Investment Platform on top
- **No mandatory rent control** at EU level (that remains a Member-State competence)
- **No revision of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive** (EPBD, Directive (EU) 2024/1275 -- separate file, see `energy_performance_buildings_directive`)
- **No quantified timeline** beyond the 2026 milestones (Housing Summit + Housing Alliance + Citizens Energy Package)

## Why this matters for users

- **Public-affairs professionals** in the construction, real-estate, social-housing, urban-mobility, and energy sectors must align positioning with the Plan's four pillars and the State-aid revision timeline
- **Member-State + regional officials** can plan ERDF + EIB pipeline against the Pan-European Investment Platform; revised State aid rules unlock notification thresholds that today block social-housing top-ups
- **MEPs + EP rapporteurs** working on housing files (HOUS Committee + ECON, EMPL, ENVI, REGI) will reference the Plan in own-initiative reports and amendments to existing files
- **Investor-facing teams** can map deals against the Plan's "innovative models" pillar (modular, prefabricated, student housing, youth housing) and prepare for the Housing Alliance ecosystem
