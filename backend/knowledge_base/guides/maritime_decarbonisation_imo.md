# Maritime Decarbonisation -- EU Policy and IMO MEPC 84

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Decarbonisation of maritime transport -- EU instruments (ETS for shipping, FuelEU Maritime) and International Maritime Organization (IMO) framework (Marine Environment Protection Committee sessions)
- EPRS Briefing (20 April 2026): "On the path towards decarbonisation of maritime transport -- Key issues at stake at the IMO MEPC 84" -- EPRS analysis ahead of MEPC 84 session. Ref: EPRS_UNK_863c37a1ac87
- IMO MEPC 84: Marine Environment Protection Committee 84th session, scheduled spring 2026, London
- IMO target: net-zero GHG from international shipping "by or around" 2050 (adopted MEPC 80, July 2023); indicative checkpoints 20%/30% reduction by 2030, 70%/80% by 2040 (vs 2008)
- IMO Net-Zero Framework (adopted MEPC 83, April 2025): global fuel standard + economic measure (levy); entry into force expected 2027 after adoption at MEPC 84 or 85
- EU ETS extension to shipping: Directive (EU) 2023/959, CELEX 32023L0959, extending EU ETS (Directive 2003/87/EC) to maritime emissions from 1 January 2024 (phased-in: 40% 2024, 70% 2025, 100% 2026)
- FuelEU Maritime: Regulation (EU) 2023/1805, CELEX 32023R1805, entered into force 1 January 2025. GHG intensity limits on energy used by ships above 5,000 GT calling at EU ports
- FuelEU intensity trajectory: -2% (2025) → -6% (2030) → -14.5% (2035) → -31% (2040) → -62% (2045) → -80% (2050) vs 2020 baseline
- Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR): Regulation (EU) 2023/1804, CELEX 32023R1804, shore-side electricity supply mandate at TEN-T ports from 2030
- Responsible DG (EU): DG MOVE (Maritime Directorate), DG CLIMA (ETS coordination)
- Responsible Commissioner: Apostolos Tzitzikostas (Sustainable Transport and Tourism), Wopke Hoekstra (Climate)
- EP lead committee: TRAN (Transport and Tourism), with ENVI on climate aspects
- Council configuration: Transport, Telecommunications and Energy Council (TTE)

## IMO MEPC 84 Key Issues (per EPRS)

1. **Adoption of Net-Zero Framework**: global fuel standard (GHG intensity limits) + economic measure (levy/flexibility mechanism). MEPC 83 approved in April 2025; formal adoption expected MEPC 84 or 85
2. **Levy design**: per-tonne CO2 price, revenue allocation (climate finance, just transition for developing states, R&D for alternative fuels)
3. **Flexibility mechanism**: banking, borrowing, trading of compliance units
4. **Interaction with regional measures**: coordination with EU ETS + FuelEU Maritime (avoiding double regulation)
5. **Alternative fuels certification**: well-to-wake emissions methodology (LNG, methanol, ammonia, hydrogen, biofuels)
6. **Small-island developing states (SIDS) + LDCs**: transition support, capacity-building
7. **Port state measures**: detention for non-compliance, penalties

## EU Position at MEPC 84

- Support for ambitious global measures that deliver at least EU ETS + FuelEU level of stringency
- Flexibility: willingness to adjust EU regional measures if global measures deliver equivalent outcomes
- Revenue allocation: push for majority of levy revenues to climate finance (Green Climate Fund + Loss and Damage Fund)
- Technology neutrality with lifecycle (well-to-wake) assessment, not just tank-to-wake
- Strong safeguards against shifting emissions (offshoring to non-EU ports)
