# China BEV Countervailing Duties (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754)

## QUICK ANSWERS

**What does this Regulation do?** Imposes definitive countervailing duties (CVD) on imports of new battery electric vehicles (BEVs) for passenger transport originating in China, on top of the standard 6.5% customs duty. Definitive duties range from 7.8% (Tesla Shanghai) to 35.3% (SAIC and all non-cooperating companies). It replaces the provisional measures from Regulation (EU) 2024/1866.

**Who pays what?**

| Producer / TARIC additional code | Definitive duty |
|---|---|
| Tesla (Shanghai) Co., Ltd / C189 | **7.8%** |
| BYD Group / C184 | **17.0%** |
| Geely Group / C185-C187 | **18.8%** |
| Other cooperating companies (full list in Annex) / C190 | **20.7%** |
| SAIC Group / C188 | **35.3%** |
| All other companies (non-cooperating) / C179 | **35.3%** |

**Total landed duty:** these CVD rates are *additional* to the existing 6.5% MFN customs duty, so e.g. a SAIC BEV faces 6.5% + 35.3% = **41.8% total duty** on its CIF value at the EU border.

**Did provisional duties get collected?** **No.** Article 2 releases all provisional-duty securities back to importers because the Commission only found a *threat* of material injury, not actual material injury — Article 16(2) of the Basic Regulation forbids retroactive collection in that situation.

**Are EU-built electric vehicles affected?** No. The duties apply only to BEVs *originating in China* (manufactured there and exported). EU-built BEVs from any brand (including Tesla Berlin, BMW iX, Volkswagen ID series, etc.) are unaffected.

**What about hybrids / PHEVs?** Out of scope. The regulation covers only pure battery electric vehicles charged exclusively from an external source. Plug-in hybrids, mild hybrids, and range-extended EVs are excluded.

## OVERVIEW

This is the EU's first major countervailing-duty action in the green-transition era and is one of the most significant trade-defence cases in EU history by economic weight. The investigation was opened *ex officio* by the European Commission on **4 October 2023** (notice C/2023/160) without a prior industry complaint, which is unusual — the Commission initiated on its own motion based on evidence of subsidised imports. The probe ran for 13 months: provisional duties shipped 8 July 2024 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1866) and definitive duties on 29 October 2024.

The central legal finding is **threat of material injury under Article 8(8) of the Basic Regulation**, not actual material injury. This is technically important: it means the duties are forward-looking (preventing imminent harm) rather than remediating realised harm. The legal consequence is that all provisional-duty securities collected between July and October 2024 are *released* rather than collected — no retroactive recovery. The Article 16(4) conditions for retroactive collection (critical circumstances, massive imports, risk of undermining definitive measures) were not met, so registration-period imports (under Regulation (EU) 2024/785) also avoid duty.

The investigation found three categories of countervailable subsidies: (a) preferential financing from policy banks and state-owned commercial banks at below-market rates; (b) direct grants and tax revenue foregone (NEV subsidies, CIT reductions for high-tech enterprises, VAT exemptions); and (c) provision of goods and services for less than adequate remuneration (LTAR) — batteries (LFP cells), land use rights, electricity, aluminium.

Five undertaking offers (one collective via CCCME, individual offers from BYD, Geely, SAIC, others) were **all rejected** for five reasons including product complexity (segment heterogeneity makes a minimum-import-price unworkable), monitoring risk, and SAIC's non-cooperation.

## STRUCTURE

The Regulation is a casework decision, not a framework law. Its structure:

- **Preamble** (1432 recitals): full case file — initiation, sampling, subsidy findings programme-by-programme, injury analysis, causation, Union interest, undertaking rejection
- **Article 1**: imposes definitive countervailing duties (the operative duty table)
- **Article 2**: releases provisional-duty securities
- **Article 3**: entry into force
- **Annex**: list of "other cooperating companies" eligible for the 20.7% weighted-average rate

## SUBSTANTIVE ELEMENTS

### Product scope (recital 1 and CN classification)

The product is "new battery electric vehicles, i.e. motor vehicles for the transport of persons, which are propelled exclusively by one or more electric motors, which have a battery pack attached to the vehicle and which are designed to be charged exclusively from an external power source (fully electric vehicles), classifiable under CN code ex 8703 80 10 (TARIC code 8703 80 10 10)".

Explicitly excluded:
- Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs)
- Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs)
- Range-extended EVs where ICE is primary propulsion
- BEVs for goods transport (CN 8704 area)
- Used/second-hand BEVs

The Commission confirmed EU-produced BEVs and Chinese-imported BEVs are "like products" under Article 2(c) of the Basic Regulation: same technical characteristics, same end use.

### Sampling and individual examination (Article 27 of the Basic Regulation)

Three Chinese exporter groups were sampled: **BYD, Geely, SAIC** (largest export volumes to the EU during the IP). Three EU producers were sampled to represent the EU industry.

**Tesla (Shanghai) Co., Ltd** requested *individual examination* under Article 27(3) and was granted. Its 7.8% rate is calculated specifically on Tesla's own subsidy benefits, separately from the sampling.

The Commission applied **facts available under Article 28** in three instances:
1. **SAIC** declined to cooperate fully, particularly on certain input suppliers. Facts available materially raised SAIC's rate to 35.3%.
2. **Geely** received partial facts available for non-cooperating intra-group entities.
3. **Government of China (GOC)** declined to cooperate on several subsidy programmes; facts available used for benefit calculations.

### Subsidy programmes found countervailable (recitals 530-820 approximately)

**Category A — Preferential financing (LTAR):**
- Loans from policy banks: China Development Bank, Export-Import Bank of China, Agricultural Development Bank of China
- Loans from state-owned commercial banks (SOCBs) at below-benchmark rates
- Bonds issued at below-benchmark yields

External benchmarks (international rates for comparable risk profiles) were used to calculate the benefit, because Chinese domestic rates are distorted by state intervention.

**Category B — Direct grants and revenue foregone:**
- New Energy Vehicle (NEV) purchase subsidies passed through to producers
- Technology innovation grants
- Industrial development fund grants
- Export-promotion grants
- R&D subsidies
- Corporate income tax (CIT) reductions for high-technology enterprises (25% standard rate cut to 15%)
- CIT "two-free, three-half" holidays for new investments
- VAT exemptions / reduced rates on inputs
- Import duty exemptions on inputs used in exports

**Category C — Provision of goods and services for LTAR:**
- **Batteries (LFP cells in particular)**: identified as one of the most significant subsidy categories. Battery cells and packs sourced from Chinese suppliers (CATL, BYD captive, others) at below-benchmark prices. International market data used to construct the benchmark where available.
- **Land use rights (LURs)**: BEV production facilities pay below-market for land rights. Benchmark constructed from comparable international land transactions or less-distorted Chinese reference points.
- **Electricity**: industrial electricity tariffs in certain provinces below unsubsidised market levels.
- **Aluminium**: state-influenced supply chains providing aluminium and other raw materials below market.

### Lesser duty rule — does NOT apply

Unlike anti-dumping law, the Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation **does not contain a "lesser duty rule"**. Countervailing duties are imposed at the full subsidy rate found, without testing whether a lower rate would suffice to remove injury. The Regulation confirms this explicitly. This is why duties match the subsidy rates exactly (17.0%, 18.8%, 35.3%, 7.8%).

### Injury analysis (recitals 820-1080 approximately)

The Commission found **threat of material injury**, not actual material injury (Article 8(8) of the Basic Regulation). Key indicators:

| Indicator | Trend over period considered (2020 to IP end) |
|---|---|
| Chinese BEV import market share in EU | low single digits in 2020 → ~25% in IP |
| Price undercutting by Chinese imports | 12.7% PCN-weighted average during IP |
| EU industry profitability | positive in 2020-2021 → -20.5% by Q3 2023 |
| EU industry sales volume | grew in absolute terms but lost market share |
| Capacity utilisation | declined relative to projected BEV market growth |
| Employment | pressure on BEV-dedicated lines |
| Capital investment | substantial; ROI threatened by surge of subsidised imports |

The threat finding rests on: (a) the trajectory of Chinese market share, (b) sustained price-undercutting at PCN level, (c) deteriorating EU profitability, (d) growing Chinese export capacity, and (e) the conclusion that without intervention, material injury would materialise imminently.

### Causation and non-attribution (Article 8(6) of the Basic Regulation)

Causal link established: subsidised Chinese imports → threat of material injury to EU industry. Six alternative causes were assessed and rejected as causation-breaking:
1. Macro-economic conditions (post-pandemic): contributory but not break-causing
2. EU producers' own pricing/model decisions: not sufficient
3. Consumer-preference theories: rejected; Chinese brands gaining EU acceptance on price competitiveness
4. Other third-country imports: negligible / market-priced
5. Structural EU production-cost differentials: real but additional to subsidy effect
6. Other minor factors: did not break causation

### Union interest test (Article 31 of the Basic Regulation)

- **EU industry**: directly benefits from measures; supports strategic transition objectives
- **Importers/dealers**: margin compression but bearable
- **Consumers**: short-term price effects offset by long-term EU industry viability
- **Green Deal / Fit for 55**: a viable EU BEV industry is needed to deliver the EU's industrial transition; subsidised competition threatens this

Conclusion: measures are in the Union interest.

### Undertakings — all five rejected

Article 13 of the Basic Regulation allows exporters to offer price undertakings (commitments to a minimum import price, MIP) in lieu of duties. Five offers were received:
1. **CCCME collective offer** (China Chamber of Commerce for Import and Export of Machinery and Electronic Products)
2. **BYD individual**
3. **Geely individual**
4. **SAIC individual**
5. **Other cooperating** group offer

All five were rejected for five reasons:
1. **Product complexity**: BEVs are heterogeneous (segments, battery sizes, equipment levels) — a single MIP per producer would be unworkable
2. **Monitoring risk**: high circumvention risk, complex chassis-level tracking
3. **SAIC non-cooperation**: insufficient basis to set a workable MIP
4. **Insufficient coverage**: undertakings would leave significant non-cooperating volumes uncovered
5. **WTO-compatibility concerns**: certain proposed structures raised consistency questions with WTO rules on price undertakings

The case stands within the EU's broader **trade defence framework** (Reg 2016/1036 anti-dumping + Reg 2016/1037 anti-subsidy + Reg 2015/478 safeguards + Reg 2022/2560 Foreign Subsidies Regulation).

There is **no parallel anti-dumping case** on the same product in this Regulation — anti-dumping requires a separate finding of below-cost pricing, which the Commission did not pursue here.

## SUBSIDY-RATE METHODOLOGY: KEY CONCEPTS

- **LTAR (Less Than Adequate Remuneration)**: when the government provides goods/services or buys them at prices that confer a benefit relative to market terms. Used here for batteries, land, electricity, aluminium.
- **External benchmarks**: used when Chinese domestic prices are distorted by state intervention. International market data, third-country data, or non-distorted reference points.
- **Facts available (Article 28)**: when an interested party (producer or GOC) declines to cooperate or provides insufficient information, the Commission may use facts available, including the highest cooperating rate.
- **Sampling (Article 27)**: when there are too many exporters/producers to individually examine, the Commission selects a representative sample; non-sampled cooperating companies get a weighted-average rate.
- **Individual examination (Article 27(3))**: a non-sampled producer can request its own investigation; granted to Tesla in this case.

## ANSWER PRINCIPLES FOR THE CHATBOT

1. **Always link the deep-dive explainer URL** when answering about this regulation, the China BEV duties, BYD/Geely/SAIC/Tesla rates, or the underlying anti-subsidy procedure.
2. **Use plain language**: "additional duty", not "ad valorem countervailing measure"; "Chinese-made electric cars", not "BEVs originating in the PRC".
3. **Give the duty rate by company** when asked. The rates are the headline fact most users want.
4. **Clarify EU-built BEVs are unaffected** — many users confuse Chinese ownership with Chinese origin (BMW iX5, Tesla Berlin, VW ID series are EU-built).
5. **Explain "threat of injury" vs "actual injury"** when relevant — it's the central legal hook and explains why provisional duties were released.
6. **Avoid speculation about appeal proceedings** — the user can check pending Court of Justice cases via OEIL; do not invent outcomes.
7. **Cite recital numbers sparingly**, only for procedurally important findings (Article 8(8) threat finding, Article 16(2) consequence, Article 28 facts-available applications).
