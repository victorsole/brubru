# European Council of 15 and 16 October 2026: agenda from the President's invitation letter

## QUICK FACTS
- **Update (13 October 2026), two things read today:** (a) the **General Affairs Council met in Luxembourg on 13 October** to prepare this summit; its published programme (Council media advisory of 12 October) listed preparation of the European Council, the **MFF 2028-2034 (public session)**, omnibus simplification packages (state of play), the **Article 7 procedure concerning Hungary** and conclusions on inclusive democratic participation; **published results, read at headline level on 14 October 2026:** the Council **approved conclusions on inclusive democratic participation, and societal and community resilience**, and that evening the accession conferences **opened negotiations with Ukraine and Moldova on the acquis related to internal market, competitiveness and inclusive growth** and **Albania provisionally closed chapter 20 (enterprise and industrial policy)**; the results of the Article 7 item, the MFF debate and the omnibus packages were **not read**; the press briefing on the European Council was set for Tuesday 13 October at 14:30. (b) **A letter on regulatory burden, as REPORTED by POLITICO (12 October), which saw it:** the leaders of **Germany (Friedrich Merz), Italy (Giorgia Meloni) and Belgium (Bart De Wever)** wrote to **President Costa**. Four separate requests, as POLITICO lists them: (1) an **"emergency brake" mechanism** so that EU capitals can **intervene to stop new legislation** from Brussels that they say is too burdensome for governments and businesses (the report describes the brake only as this power to STOP new legislation, NOT as a pause or a time-limited measure: it gives no duration, trigger, voting rule or procedure for it); (2) a **"priority rule"** to **suspend "certain regulatory areas for a specified period"** (only this one carries a time element); (3) to **"significantly accelerate planning and approval procedures"**; (4) **further ambitious simplification packages**, with the Commission easing the burden "first and foremost" on **small and medium-sized businesses**. POLITICO's framing: the aim is to stop unnecessary regulatory burdens from causing permanent losses of European industrial capacity, and the call echoes demands made after the March 2026 competitiveness summit. **The letter itself had not been published in the sources read: treat all of this as POLITICO's report, not an official text.** **Beyond what is listed here the report states nothing, so add nothing:** not the letter's place on the summit agenda (POLITICO says only that it comes days before the meeting, where leaders will debate the long-term budget, energy prices and Ukraine), not a legal basis, not a procedure, not any detail of how the brake would work.
- **Status (9 October 2026): PREVIEW.** Based on the invitation letter from European Council President **António Costa**, published on **8 October 2026 (16:30)**. The meeting has not taken place and **no conclusions have been adopted**; do not describe outcomes.
- **When and where:** starts **Thursday 15 October 2026 at 10:00** in Brussels and is due to end with lunch on **Friday 16 October**.
- **Agenda items named in the letter (the order below follows the letter):**
  1. **Energy prices and competitiveness:** guidance to the Commission on "targeted and temporary measures" to ease the impact of high energy prices on citizens and businesses, while continuing to reduce dependence on fossil fuels; review of the 2026-2027 competitiveness strategy, the **"One Europe, One Market" agenda**, together with the President of the European Parliament; and the work to reduce global macroeconomic imbalances affecting European industry.
  2. **Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) 2028-2034:** negotiations "enter a decisive phase" in October; the aim is to consolidate key elements for a **final agreement by the end of the year**.
  3. **Ukraine:** continued support against Russia's war of aggression; the letter says it is important to hear from President Zelenskyy on the country's operational needs for the winter, given Russian attacks on critical infrastructure; stock-taking of Ukraine's accession progress, "by then hopefully having opened further clusters".
  4. **Russia's hybrid attacks and defence readiness:** implementation across all strands, including critical military capability gaps.
  5. **Migration (strategic discussion):** arrivals of irregular migrants have decreased significantly; leaders will look at crisis preparedness and instrumentalisation.
  6. **Enlargement and reforms (strategic discussion):** fed by the Commission communication "Preparing for a wider Union"; the letter highlights the accession Treaty with **Montenegro** and the aim to conclude negotiations with it **by the end of 2026**.
  7. **Middle East:** latest developments and their impact on freedom of navigation and maritime security, important for energy, fertiliser and food supply chains.
- **Update (12 October 2026):** (a) on the MFF item, the Irish presidency tabled a new negotiating box on 10 October and Parliament's lead MEPs rejected it the same day (see `mff_2028_2034`); (b) the Council announced a **press briefing on the European Council meeting for Tuesday 13 October 2026 at 14:30**; (c) China is not an item in the letter, but Commissioner Šefčovič said after the second EU-China Trade and Investment Consultations (8-9 October) that President von der Leyen would discuss the outcome and next steps with leaders at the upcoming European Council (see `eu_china_trade_investment_consultations_2026`); (d) on 9 October the Council adopted the defence simplification package (Omnibus V), relevant to the defence readiness item (see `eu_defence_readiness_omnibus_v_and_agile_2026`). The status stays PREVIEW: no conclusions have been adopted.
- **Related decisions this week:** the Council prolonged the Russia hybrid-activities sanctions on 8 October (see `eu_russia_hybrid_destabilisation_sanctions_regime`); the Commission and ENTSOG assessed gas supply for the winter (see `eu_gas_winter_2026_2027_supply_outlook`).
