# Petitions to the European Parliament (PETI)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the right of any person or organisation to bring a **complaint or request** to the European Parliament on a matter that falls **within the European Union's fields of activity** and **affects them directly**. Handled by the **Committee on Petitions (PETI)**.
- **Legal basis**: **Article 24 and Article 227 TFEU** and **Article 44 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights**; the right was established by the Maastricht Treaty.
- **Who can petition**: every **EU citizen**; every natural person **residing** in a Member State; and any **company, organisation or association with its registered office in a Member State**. Individually or jointly with others.
- **Subject-matter (admissibility) test**: the petition must (1) concern an **EU field of activity** and (2) **directly affect** the petitioner. Inadmissible if it is purely a **national competence**, a personal grievance unrelated to EU law, or mere disagreement with a lawful decision.
- **How to submit**: online via the **Petitions Web Portal** (petitions.europarl.europa.eu) - create an account, read the preliminary information, complete the checklist, fill the form (**title, text, topic(s), language, country, confidentiality level, and whether you represent an association**), attach documents, and submit. Petitions may also be sent by post. Available in the **24 official EU languages** (Catalan is not an official EU language, so a petition must be lodged in one of the 24, e.g. Spanish or English).
- **How it is handled (5 steps)**: (1) **registration** and a petition number; (2) written **acknowledgement** (PETI may ask for more information); (3) the PETI **secretariat prepares a summary** with recommendations; (4) PETI **Members decide admissibility** and next actions; (5) admissible petitions are **published** (with confidentiality exceptions).
- **What PETI can do with an admissible petition**: ask the **European Commission** to investigate or provide information; request **opinions** from other parliamentary committees; hold a **hearing** with the petitioner present; prepare a **report**; conduct a **fact-finding visit**; suggest tools such as **SOLVIT**; refer to other bodies; or "any other appropriate solution". Inadmissible petitions are filed with written notice.
- **What it does NOT do**: a petition raises and investigates an issue; it does **not by itself change EU law**. Manage expectations accordingly.
- **Alternatives / related tools**: a **written question** tabled by an MEP; the **European Citizens' Initiative** (Regulation (EU) 2019/788, 1 million signatures across at least 7 Member States to ask the Commission to propose law); a **complaint to the European Ombudsman** (maladministration by EU bodies); **SOLVIT** (cross-border problems with national administrations); a **complaint to the European Commission** (Member-State breach of EU law); **Have Your Say** public consultations.
- **Annual cycle**: PETI adopts an **annual report** on its activity and the petitions received.

## Overview

Any individual or organisation can ask the European Parliament to look at a problem or a request that touches EU competence and affects them. It is the most accessible direct channel between citizens (and civil-society organisations) and the Parliament. The Committee on Petitions screens every petition for admissibility, then can press the Commission for answers, hold hearings, or report. It is a tool of scrutiny and visibility rather than legislation.

For an organisation (an association, NGO or movement with an EU registered office) it is a legitimate advocacy instrument: a well-drafted, clearly admissible petition puts an issue on the Parliament's record and can trigger Commission action.

## Admissibility in practice
A strong petition makes the EU dimension explicit. Tie the subject to a concrete EU competence or instrument (a treaty article, a regulation, a directive, an EU programme), and explain how the petitioner is affected. Avoid: matters that are purely national, abstract political statements, or requests the Parliament has no power over. State the **specific action requested** (investigate, legislate, fund, protect a right).

## How to write a petition (structure that works)
1. **Petitioner and standing**: who you are and why you are affected / have a legitimate interest.
2. **Subject**: one clear sentence.
3. **EU competence hook**: the treaty article / regulation / programme that makes this an EU matter.
4. **Facts**: concise, dated, verifiable.
5. **The ask**: the specific action requested of the Parliament / Commission.
6. **Annexes**: supporting evidence.
Keep it factual and proportionate; the admissibility decision turns on the EU-competence link, not on rhetoric.
