# ESMA: Direct Supervision and Oversight Mandates (MiCA, DORA CTPPs, ESAP, EU CTP, Benchmarks)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Body:** European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA), based in **Paris**
- **Founding regulation:** Regulation (EU) No 1095/2010 (CELEX 32010R1095), as amended (notably by the ESAs Review, Regulation (EU) 2019/2175)
- **Type:** One of the three European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs) in the European System of Financial Supervision (ESFS), alongside EBA (banking, Paris) and EIOPA (insurance/pensions, Frankfurt)
- **Operational since:** 1 January 2011, following the 2009 de Larosière report
- **Mission (three statutory objectives):** enhance investor protection, promote orderly financial markets, safeguard financial stability
- **Core functions:** single rulebook (RTS/ITS), supervisory convergence (peer reviews, Q&A, no-action letters, Common Supervisory Actions), direct supervision of specific entity types and, increasingly, **new oversight roles created by post-2019 legislation** that push ESMA from coordinator toward direct EU-level supervisor.
- **Long-standing direct supervision (since 2011-2022):** Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs); Trade Repositories (TRs, under EMIR and SFTR); Securitisation Repositories (SRs); EU critical Benchmark Administrators + single-entry point for third-country benchmark administrators; Data Reporting Services Providers (APAs and ARMs, MiFIR Articles 48a-48n); Tier 2 (systemically important) third-country CCPs under EMIR.
- **New mandates phasing in 2025-2027:** Consolidated Tape Providers (CTPs) for bonds, equities and OTC derivatives (MiFIR review, Regulation (EU) 2024/791); Lead Overseer role (shared with EBA/EIOPA) for critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs) under DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554), with the first list of **19 designated CTPPs published November 2025**; operator of the European Single Access Point (ESAP, Regulation (EU) 2023/2859), where Collection Bodies began feeding data **July 2026** with public access from **July 2027**; registration and supervision of **External Reviewers of European Green Bonds** (Regulation (EU) 2023/2631), mandatory from **21 June 2026**; and authorisation and supervision of **ESG rating providers**, from **2 July 2026**.
- **Live legislative proposal (not yet adopted):** European Commission package COM(2025) 941-943 of 4 December 2025 (procedures 2025/0381-0383(COD), part of the Savings and Investment Union) would transfer **direct supervision of all crypto-asset service providers (CASPs)** under MiCA from national competent authorities (NCAs) to ESMA. ECB backed the proposal April 2026; ECON held a public hearing 5 May 2026 and rapporteurs published draft reports 11 June 2026; the file entered ECON committee scrutiny 2 July 2026. Currently, ESMA does **not** directly supervise CASPs: NCAs do, including "significant" CASPs under MiCA Title V Chapter 5.
- **Verify live:** current CTP designations (bonds and equities, beyond the OTC derivatives award), the CTPP list, and the MiCA supervisory-transfer proposal's progress before citing exact status in outward-facing material.

## QUICK ANSWERS

**Is ESMA now a "direct supervisor" like a national regulator?** Increasingly, yes, for a growing but still bounded list of entity types. ESMA has never supervised banks or insurers, and it still does not directly supervise most investment firms, trading venues or crypto-asset service providers: those remain with NCAs. What has changed since the 2019 ESAs Review is a steady expansion of EU-level "single supervisor" pockets for entities whose business is inherently cross-border or systemic: credit rating agencies, trade repositories, benchmark administrators, data reporting providers, and now consolidated tape providers, critical ICT providers (jointly with EBA/EIOPA), green bond external reviewers and ESG rating providers.

**What is the Consolidated Tape Provider (CTP) mandate?** The 2024 MiFIR review (Regulation (EU) 2024/791) creates a mandatory EU consolidated tape, a single continuous live data stream of trades across all EU venues, for bonds, equities and OTC derivatives (in that broad sequencing). ESMA runs the public selection procedure, then authorises and directly supervises the winning provider for each asset class under a renewable licence (five years for the OTC derivatives award). ESMA selected **Etrading Software (Netherlands) B.V.** as CTP for OTC derivatives, announced 6-7 July 2026.

**What is ESMA's role under DORA?** DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) gives the three ESAs, acting jointly through their Joint Committee on the recommendation of the Oversight Forum, the power to designate ICT third-party service providers as "critical" (CTPPs) and to assign each a **Lead Overseer**: the ESA whose sub-sector holds the largest share of assets among the CTPP's financial-entity clients. ESMA is Lead Overseer for CTPPs whose exposure is concentrated in capital-markets and post-trade entities; EBA leads for banking-concentrated CTPPs; EIOPA for insurance-concentrated ones. The first list of 19 designated CTPPs was published in November 2025 (cloud, hyperscale and core technology-services providers including Amazon Web Services EMEA, Google Cloud EMEA, Microsoft Ireland Operations, IBM, Oracle Nederland, SAP, Bloomberg, LSEG Data and Risk, and several systems-integrator/telecom firms). The Joint Oversight Network (JON) coordinates the three Lead Overseers; the Lead Overseer issues yearly oversight plans and can recommend (and, as a last resort, block) subcontracting arrangements.

**Does ESMA directly supervise crypto firms under MiCA?** Not yet. Under MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) as it stands, national competent authorities authorise and directly supervise all crypto-asset service providers, including "significant" CASPs under Title V Chapter 5 (enhanced reporting obligations, information shared with ESMA/EBA). ESMA's current MiCA role is coordination: maintaining the EU-wide register of authorised CASPs and issuers, drafting technical standards and guidelines, and running Common Supervisory Actions (CSAs) with NCAs, for example the CSA on CASPs' digital operational resilience for custody launched 8 July 2026. The Commission's 4 December 2025 SIU legislative package (COM(2025) 941-943) proposes to change this by transferring direct CASP supervision to ESMA; it is under negotiation in Parliament and Council and is not law.

**What is ESMA's role in ESAP?** ESMA is mandated by Regulation (EU) 2023/2859 to build and operate the European Single Access Point, the EU's free public portal for financial, capital-markets and sustainability disclosures. ESMA does not collect data directly from companies; it ingests feeds from national and EU "Collection Bodies" in machine-readable format and exposes them through one searchable portal. Collection Bodies began submitting from July 2026; public access follows from July 2027, with further phases in January 2028 and January 2029. See the dedicated guide `esap_european_single_access_point`.

**What is the EU Green Bond mandate?** Under the European Green Bond Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/2631), firms wishing to act as "External Reviewers" (assessing whether a bond meets the EuGB standard) must register with and be supervised by ESMA. A transitional period ran from 21 December 2024 to 21 June 2026; registration (fee EUR 40,000) became mandatory from 21 June 2026, and ESMA published its first register of authorised external reviewers around 22 June 2026.

**What is the ESG ratings mandate?** From 2 July 2026, ESMA authorises and directly supervises ESG rating providers operating in the EU, focused on governance, transparency and conflict-of-interest avoidance in ratings methodology.

**Where does the money come from?** ESMA's new direct-supervision activities (CRAs, TRs, SRs, benchmark administrators, DRSPs, CTPs, EuGB external reviewers, ESG rating providers) are funded largely by supervisory fees charged to the supervised entities, not general EU budget appropriations, consistent with the "polluter pays" funding logic the ESAs Review entrenched.

## OVERVIEW

ESMA was created on 1 January 2011 as part of the European System of Financial Supervision, the post-financial-crisis architecture recommended by the 2009 de Larosière report. For most of its history ESMA's role was indirect: writing binding technical standards, coordinating national supervisors, and directly supervising only a narrow set of infrastructure entities (credit rating agencies from the outset, then trade repositories under EMIR).

The 2019 ESAs Review (Regulation (EU) 2019/2175) began a shift toward direct EU-level supervision by transferring data reporting services providers (APAs, ARMs) and critical-benchmark oversight to ESMA from 1 January 2022. Since then, a wave of separate sectoral legislation (DORA in 2022, the European Green Bond Regulation in 2023, the ESAP Regulation in 2023, the MiFIR review in 2024, and now the pending Savings and Investment Union supervisory-convergence package from December 2025) has added or proposed further ESMA oversight roles, each targeting entities whose cross-border or systemic nature makes fragmented national supervision inefficient or risky: consolidated market-data providers, critical ICT third-party providers to the whole financial sector, green-bond external reviewers, ESG rating providers, and (if the pending proposal is adopted) all crypto-asset service providers.

The result is a patchwork rather than a single unified "ESMA supervises everything" model: some entity types are supervised end-to-end by ESMA (CRAs, TRs, SRs since their creation), some are jointly overseen with the other two ESAs (DORA CTPPs), some ESMA merely coordinates while NCAs retain the front line (MiCA CASPs today), and some are brand-new single-EU-licence regimes ESMA runs from scratch (CTPs, EuGB external reviewers, ESG rating providers).

## LONG-STANDING DIRECT SUPERVISION (pre-2022)

| Entity type | Legal basis | ESMA role |
|---|---|---|
| Credit Rating Agencies (CRAs) | CRA Regulation (EC) No 1060/2009 | Registration + direct supervision across the EU; independence, governance, methodology, conflict management |
| Trade Repositories (TRs) | EMIR (Regulation (EU) No 648/2012) + SFTR (Regulation (EU) 2015/2365) | Authorisation + supervision of derivatives and securities-financing transaction-data repositories |
| Securitisation Repositories (SRs) | Securitisation Regulation (EU) 2017/2402 | Registration + oversight of securitisation disclosure data |
| Tier 2 third-country CCPs | EMIR 2.2 (Regulation (EU) 2019/2099) | Enhanced supervisory requirements for systemically important third-country central counterparties |

## MANDATES TRANSFERRED OR EXPANDED BY THE 2019 ESAs REVIEW (from 1 January 2022)

| Entity type | Legal basis | ESMA role |
|---|---|---|
| Data Reporting Services Providers (APAs, ARMs) | MiFIR Articles 48a-48n | Direct authorisation, supervision, data-gathering and fining powers (transferred from NCAs) |
| Critical benchmark administrators | Benchmarks Regulation (EU) 2016/1011, as amended | Direct supervision of EU critical benchmarks; single EU entry point for third-country benchmark administrators |

## MiCA TODAY: WHY ESMA IS NOT (YET) THE CASP SUPERVISOR

MiCA's Title V, Chapter 5 already has a category of "significant" crypto-asset service providers subject to enhanced obligations, but the direct supervisor for every CASP today, significant or not, remains the national competent authority in the CASP's home Member State. ESMA's current MiCA footprint is: maintaining the EU-wide public register of authorised issuers and CASPs; drafting Regulatory and Implementing Technical Standards and Guidelines; running supervisory-convergence tools such as Common Supervisory Actions with NCAs (for example the CSA on CASPs' digital operational resilience for custody, launched 8 July 2026); and, if the pending Commission proposal is adopted, becoming the direct EU-level supervisor of all CASPs, mirroring the model already used for CRAs and TRs. Until adoption, treat any claim that "ESMA supervises crypto exchanges" as inaccurate for the current legal position; the correct framing is that ESMA coordinates while NCAs supervise, with a live legislative proposal to change that.
