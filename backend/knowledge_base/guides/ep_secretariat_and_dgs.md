# The European Parliament's Secretariat and Directorates-General

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: the permanent civil service of the European Parliament (EP), distinct from the 720 Members of the European Parliament (MEPs) and from political group staff. It prepares, organises and supports every parliamentary activity: plenary sittings, committee work, legislative drafting support, translation, interpretation, security, IT, finance and communication.
- **Who heads it**: the **Secretary-General**, currently **Alessandro Chiocchetti**, in office since **1 January 2023**. He was appointed by Parliament's Bureau on 12 September 2022, succeeding Klaus Welle (Secretary-General 2009 to 2022). The Secretary-General is appointed under **Rule 240 of the EP Rules of Procedure**.
- **Two Deputy Secretaries-General**: Markus Winkler (also Director-General of DG PRES) and Anders Rasmussen (also Director-General of DG EPRS).
- **Structure**: roughly **15 Directorates-General (DGs)** plus the **Legal Service** and the Office of the Secretary-General. The Directorates-General are led by Directors-General who report to the Secretary-General.
- **Important correction to older sources**: **DG IPOL (Internal Policies) no longer exists.** It was split up effective **January 2025** as part of the "Parliament 2024" administrative reform, into four successor DGs (**DG ECTI**, **DG CASP**, **DG IUST** and **DG BUDG**). Many external references, including older Wikipedia pages, still describe the pre-2025 DG IPOL structure; that structure is outdated for 2025 onward.
- **Committee secretariats** now sit inside DG ECTI, DG CASP, DG IUST, DG BUDG or DG EXPO, depending on which committee they serve. They are the procedural gatekeepers of every legislative file: they run the calendar, track amendment-tabling deadlines, compile amendments for committee votes, and give drafting and procedural support to the rapporteur and shadow rapporteurs.
- **Staff numbers**: the Secretariat proper (officials, temporary and contract staff, excluding political groups and MEPs' personal staff) numbers roughly **4,000**. Adding political-group staff brings the combined Secretariat-plus-groups figure to roughly **8,400** (official EP FAQ figure). A further roughly **2,000 Accredited Parliamentary Assistants (APAs)** work directly for MEPs and sit outside both of those figures. Total EP-wide staff footprint is therefore in the region of **10,400**.
- **Three official places of work**: **Strasbourg** (the legal seat, hosting 12 monthly plenary part-sessions including the budget session), **Brussels** (additional part-sessions, all committee meetings, political group activity) and **Luxembourg** (home to the bulk of the General Secretariat's administrative departments, concentrated in the Konrad Adenauer building, with roughly 3,000 to 3,200 staff).
- **Legal basis for the three seats**: the Edinburgh European Council decision of 11-12 December 1992, formalised as **Protocol No 6** annexed to the Treaties (referenced by **Article 341 TFEU**), and upheld by the Court of Justice in **Case C-345/95** (1 October 1997).
- **Official portal**: [the-secretary-general.europarl.europa.eu](https://the-secretary-general.europarl.europa.eu/en)
- **Legal Service**: not a Directorate-General, reports directly to the Secretary-General and Parliament's political bodies. Advises on legal questions and represents Parliament before the EU courts and national courts. Jurisconsult: Freddy Drexler (confirmed September 2025).

## What the Secretariat is, and why it is distinct from "Parliament" in the political sense

When people say "the European Parliament", they usually mean the 720 elected MEPs, the political groups, or plenary votes. The **Secretariat is different**: it is Parliament's permanent, politically neutral administration, the EU institution's own civil service. It does not vote on legislation and has no political mandate; its job is to make the political institution function, day to day, across three cities and 24 official languages.

The Secretariat is led by the **Secretary-General**, who is the EP's most senior official, appointed by the **Bureau** (the President plus 14 Vice-Presidents, chaired by the President, with the five Quaestors in an advisory role) under Rule 240 of the Rules of Procedure. The Secretary-General chairs the Secretariat's senior management and answers to Parliament's political bodies (the Bureau and the Conference of Presidents) rather than to any single political group. See `ep_bureau_conference_of_presidents` for how those bodies work.

Alessandro Chiocchetti (born 1968, Moena, Italy; degree in Political Science and International Law, University of Padua) is a career EP official. Before becoming Secretary-General he was Head of Cabinet to President Roberta Metsola (from January 2022), Director for Legislative and Committees' Coordination in the then-DG IPOL (2019-2022), Deputy Head of Cabinet to President Antonio Tajani (2017-2019), and held roles in the Secretariat of the Conference of Committee Chairs and the cabinets of previous Secretaries-General Klaus Welle and Harald Römer going back to 2007. His appointment in September 2022 was politically contested at the time (the Greens/EFA and parts of the S&D group opposed it), which produced press coverage and a subsequent complaint to the European Ombudsman about the appointment process; that dispute is now historical context rather than a live governance issue.

## The Directorates-General: what each one does

Parliament's administration is organised into Directorates-General, each responsible for a distinct function. As of 2026 the structure is:

**DG PRES (Presidency)**, led by Deputy Secretary-General Markus Winkler. Supports the President, the Bureau, the Conference of Presidents and the Quaestors; runs plenary sittings, protocol, mail and archives.

**DG ECTI (Economy, Transformation and Industry)**, led by Michael A. Speiser since January 2025. One of the three thematic successor DGs to the former DG IPOL. Provides the committee secretariats and policy departments for ECON, IMCO, the FISC subcommittee, ITRE, ENVI and SANT: economic and monetary affairs, the internal market, industry, research and energy, environment and public health.

**DG CASP (Cohesion, Agriculture and Social Policies)**, led by Agnieszka Walter-Drop. A second successor DG to the former DG IPOL. Serves REGI, AGRI, PECH, TRAN, EMPL and the HOUS special committee: regional development, agriculture, fisheries, transport, employment and social affairs, and housing.

**DG IUST (Citizens' Rights, Justice and Institutional Affairs)**, led by Leticia Zuleta de Reales Ansaldo. The third successor DG to the former DG IPOL. Serves PETI, FEMM, CULT, LIBE, JURI, AFCO and the EUDS special committee: petitions, women's rights and gender equality, culture and education, justice and home affairs, legal affairs, constitutional affairs, and the European Democracy Shield file.

**DG BUDG (Budgetary Affairs)**, led by Monika Strasser. The fourth successor DG created from the former DG IPOL in the same January 2025 reform. Serves BUDG and CONT: the EU's annual budget procedure and budgetary control. Distinct from DG FINS, which handles Parliament's own internal finances.

**DG EXPO (External Policies of the Union)**, led by Pietro Ducci. Serves AFET, DEVE, INTA and SEDE, and provides the co-secretariat for Parliament's interparliamentary delegations and joint parliamentary assemblies (ACP-EU, Euro-Mediterranean, EuroLat and others). See `ep_intergroups_and_delegations`.

**DG EPRS (European Parliamentary Research Service)**, led by Deputy Secretary-General Anders Rasmussen. Parliament's in-house research and analysis service: briefings, impact assessments, at-a-glance notes and legislative-train tracking for MEPs. The **STOA panel** (Science and Technology Options Assessment) sits within DG EPRS. See `eprs_european_parliamentary_research_service` and `stoa_science_technology_panel`.

**DG COMM (Communication)**, led by Christian Mangold. Press service, spokesperson's office, public information offices in the member states, and Parliament's public-facing web presence.

**DG PART (Parliamentary Democracy Partnerships)**, led by Sanna Lepola. A newer DG focused on cooperation with national parliaments and democracy-support partnerships.

**DG PERS (Personnel)**, led by Ellen Robson. Human resources across all Directorates-General, recruitment and staff training.

**DG INLO (Infrastructure and Logistics)**, led by Anna Grzybowska. Manages Parliament's buildings in Strasbourg, Brussels and Luxembourg, member-state liaison offices, and equipment.

**DG TRAD (Translation and Clear Language)**, led by Valter Mavrič. Written translation of Parliament's documents into and out of the EU's official languages.

**DG LINC (Logistics and Interpretation for Conferences)**, led by Juan Carlos Jiménez Marín. Meeting-room logistics and interpretation services for every institutional meeting.

**DG FINS (Finance)**, led by Didier Klethi. Parliament's own budget, accounting and treasury, and MEPs' financial and social entitlements. Distinct from DG BUDG, which handles the EU's annual budget as a legislative matter.

**DG ITEC (Information Technologies and Cybersecurity)**, led by Lorenzo Mannelli. IT infrastructure and cybersecurity across the institution, organised into five directorates.

**DG SAFE (Security and Safety)**, led by Guy Mols. Protection of people, assets and information across Parliament's premises, including fire and first-aid prevention.

**Legal Service** (not a DG), Jurisconsult Freddy Drexler. Advises Parliament's political bodies and the Secretariat on legal questions and represents Parliament before the Court of Justice of the European Union and national courts.

Note on sourcing: the exact Bureau decision reference formalising the January 2025 DG IPOL split has not been pinned to a single published document number; "effective January 2025" is corroborated by the DG ECTI leadership's own official biography ("since January 2025") and by 2023 reporting on the reform's origins, but should be treated as reasonably solid rather than as a precisely cited legal instrument.

## Committee secretariats: the procedural gatekeepers

For a lobbyist or policy professional, the single most operationally important fact about the Secretariat is this: **every EP committee has a dedicated secretariat**, staffed inside whichever DG now owns that committee (DG ECTI, DG CASP, DG IUST, DG BUDG or DG EXPO, per the mapping above), and that secretariat is where the *procedural* decisions on a legislative file actually get made day to day.

Committee secretariats:

- Draft the committee's calendar and agenda in coordination with the chair and the committee coordinators.
- Track and enforce procedural deadlines, most importantly the **deadline for tabling amendments**, which appears on the committee's own "latest documents" page for each file.
- Collect, number and compile amendments ahead of a committee vote.
- Provide drafting and procedural assistance to the rapporteur and shadow rapporteurs, meaning they are often the practical point of contact for turning a political position into legislative text.
- Since the "Parliament 2024" reform (July 2024 Bureau/Conference of Presidents decisions, effective from the start of the 2024-2029 term), a proposal can be assigned to a single committee or to a small group of up to three committees, and a new Rule 214 allows temporary legislative committees for cross-cutting files. Both changes are managed through the secretariats.
- Each **Policy Department**, housed within the relevant successor DG per committee cluster, produces studies and briefings on MEPs' request, complementary to DG EPRS's institution-wide research function.

Practically, this means: if you are trying to understand when an amendment deadline falls, how quickly a rapporteur's office can respond to a counter-proposal, or which department to approach for background analysis on a file, the committee secretariat, not the political group, is usually the right door to knock on. See `ep_committees_overview` for the current list of standing and special committees (22 standing committees plus SEDE and SANT subcommittees plus the EUDS and HOUS special committees created in the 2024-2029 term).

## The three official places of work

Parliament is legally required to work across three cities, a peculiarity written into the Treaties rather than a historical accident it has since abandoned:

- **Strasbourg** is the official **seat** of Parliament. Twelve monthly plenary part-sessions, including the annual budget session, must be held there.
- **Brussels** hosts additional plenary part-sessions, all standing and special committee meetings, and the bulk of political group activity.
- **Luxembourg** is the base for the **General Secretariat's back-office departments**. Historically this included DG TRAD; today the majority of Luxembourg-based Secretariat staff, an estimated 3,000 to 3,200 people, are concentrated in the purpose-built **Konrad Adenauer building** on the Kirchberg plateau.

This three-seat arrangement dates to the **Edinburgh European Council of 11-12 December 1992**, where member states unanimously fixed the seats of the institutions. It was later formalised as **Protocol No 6 on the location of the seats of the institutions**, annexed to the Treaties and referenced by **Article 341 TFEU**. The Court of Justice confirmed the validity of the arrangement in **Case C-345/95** (France v Parliament, judgment of 1 October 1997).

## Why this matters to a policy professional

Understanding the Secretariat's structure changes how you plan an advocacy campaign:

- **Timing runs through the committee secretariat, not the political group.** Amendment deadlines, vote scheduling and the practicalities of getting a rapporteur's office to engage are set and managed by Secretariat staff inside DG ECTI, DG CASP, DG IUST, DG BUDG or DG EXPO, depending on the file's committee.
- **Research requests have two separate channels.** DG EPRS (including STOA) is the institution-wide, standing research service available to any MEP. Policy Departments, housed within the successor DGs, serve their specific committee cluster on request. Knowing which one to approach, and when, saves time.
- **The DG IPOL retirement is a live knowledge gap for anyone still relying on older briefings, org charts or press cuttings.** Any source describing "DG IPOL" as Parliament's internal-policy DG is describing the pre-January-2025 structure. For files in economic, industrial, environmental or health policy, the correct current reference is DG ECTI; for cohesion, agriculture, transport or social policy, DG CASP; for justice, rights, institutional and constitutional files, DG IUST; for the EU budget procedure, DG BUDG.
- **The three-seat structure has real logistical consequences.** A meeting confirmed for "Strasbourg" is a plenary-week event; a meeting in "Brussels" is far more likely to be a committee session, which is where most substantive legislative work, and most lobbying access, actually happens.
