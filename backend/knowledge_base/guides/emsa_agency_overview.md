# EMSA: European Maritime Safety Agency

## QUICK FACTS
- Agency: European Maritime Safety Agency (EMSA)
- Headquarters: Lisbon, Portugal (Praca Europa 4, Cais do Sodre)
- Legal basis: Regulation (EC) No 1406/2002 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 June 2002 establishing a European Maritime Safety Agency, CELEX 32002R1406
- Mandate amendments: Regulation (EU) No 100/2013 (2013, expanded pollution-response and coast-guard-support role); Regulation (EU) 2016/1625 of 14 September 2016 (OJ L 251, 16.9.2016), CELEX 32016R1625, amending Regulation (EC) No 1406/2002 to strengthen cooperation between EMSA, Frontex (European Border and Coast Guard Agency) and the European Fisheries Control Agency (now EFCA) on coast guard functions. Regulation (EU) 2016/1624 (the Frontex-establishing Regulation of the same year) is the companion instrument that created the shared coast-guard-cooperation duty; it was itself later repealed and replaced by Regulation (EU) 2019/1896. Verify currency before citing if a newer EMSA-specific mandate revision is adopted
- Executive Director: Maja Markovcic Kostelac, in post since 1 January 2019 (previously State Secretary, Croatian Ministry of the Sea, Transport and Infrastructure); verify currency, as the mandate is time-limited and subject to Administrative Board renewal or replacement
- Governance: an Executive Director runs the Agency under the oversight of an Administrative Board with representatives from Member States, the Commission and industry observers
- Staff and budget (as published): roughly 290 staff; 2024 budget approximately EUR 93.2 million
- Core mandate: ensure a high, uniform and effective level of maritime safety and maritime security in the EU; prevent and respond to pollution caused by ships and by oil and gas installations; support the Commission and Member States in developing, applying and evaluating EU maritime law; provide technical assistance for maritime digitalisation and surveillance
- Key digital systems: SafeSeaNet (EU-wide vessel traffic monitoring and information exchange network), the European Maritime Single Window environment (EMSWe, Regulation (EU) 2019/1239, CELEX 32019R1239, for electronic reporting by ships calling at EU ports), CleanSeaNet (satellite Earth-observation service detecting oil spills and monitoring marine pollution), Equasis (open-source database on the safety and quality record of the world merchant fleet), THETIS (the inspection-support database underpinning EU port state control, including the Paris MoU), LRIT (Long-Range Identification and Tracking of ships), and an RPAS (remotely piloted aircraft system, i.e. maritime drone) surveillance service
- Copernicus Maritime Surveillance: EMSA delivers Earth-observation products (satellite imagery, vessel detection) under the EU's Copernicus programme to support Member State maritime monitoring, environmental protection and law-enforcement authorities
- Pollution response: EMSA runs a Network of Stand-by Oil Spill Response Vessels under commercial contracts; vessels continue normal commercial trading and must be ready to sail within 24 hours of activation; combined network storage capacity is roughly 60,000 cubic metres, with individual vessel capacity averaging around 3,600 cubic metres; vessels can be mobilised anywhere in European waters and shared sea basins
- Coast Guard cooperation: EMSA, Frontex and EFCA (European Fisheries Control Agency) jointly support national coast guard authorities through the Coast Guard Functions Forum, sharing surveillance data, assets (including aerial surveillance) and expertise rather than creating a single EU coast guard
- CISE: EMSA contributes to the Common Information Sharing Environment, which links the systems of authorities responsible for maritime surveillance across sectors and borders so relevant information can be exchanged in near-real time
- Relationship to IMO/EU rule implementation: EMSA provides technical support, ship inspections and visits to Member States and the Commission on the practical implementation of IMO conventions and EU maritime law, including the EU ETS extension to shipping and FuelEU Maritime (see `maritime_decarbonisation_imo.md` for the decarbonisation policy detail; this guide covers EMSA as the operational agency, not the IMO/ETS/FuelEU policy files themselves)
- Distinguish from Frontex: EMSA is not a border-control or law-enforcement agency; it supports maritime safety, pollution response and digital surveillance infrastructure that other bodies (Frontex, EFCA, national coast guards) also draw on for their own mandates

EMSA is the EU's technical agency for maritime safety, security, pollution prevention and response, and maritime digitalisation. It does not replace Member State maritime authorities; it provides technical assistance, shared digital infrastructure and operational response capacity that Member States and the Commission use to apply EU and international maritime law consistently.

## What EMSA Is and Is Not

EMSA is an EU decentralised agency established in the aftermath of the Erika (1999) and Prestige (2002) tanker disasters, which exposed gaps in EU-wide maritime safety and pollution-response capacity. Its founding Regulation gives it a support role: it assists the Commission and Member States rather than regulating shipping directly. Enforcement (port state control inspections, flag state duties, casualty investigation) remains a Member State competence; EMSA provides the shared databases, inspection-support tools, satellite services and, where requested, technical visits that make that enforcement more consistent across the Union.

## Safety and Pollution Response

EMSA supports port state control through THETIS, the database underpinning ship inspections in EU ports and the wider Paris Memorandum of Understanding region, helping inspectors target higher-risk vessels. Equasis gives regulators, insurers, charterers and the public a consolidated view of a ship's safety and quality history.

For pollution response, EMSA's Network of Stand-by Oil Spill Response Vessels is a contracted, commercially operated fleet on 24-hour readiness across European sea basins, backed by CleanSeaNet satellite detection of oil spills. This combination lets a coastal state request rapid assistance without every Member State maintaining its own dedicated response fleet.

## Digitalisation and Surveillance

SafeSeaNet is the backbone EU vessel-traffic monitoring network, feeding data to Member State maritime authorities and to EU-level situational awareness. The European Maritime Single Window environment (EMSWe) digitises the reporting formalities ships must complete when calling at EU ports, replacing fragmented national paper and electronic systems with a harmonised EU-wide interface.

EMSA's surveillance toolkit extends into aerial and satellite capability: RPAS (drone) services and Copernicus Maritime Surveillance imagery support tasks ranging from pollution monitoring to fisheries control and border surveillance support for other agencies, delivered through the Common Information Sharing Environment (CISE) so relevant authorities can access the same data rather than each agency tasking separate assets.

## Coast Guard Functions Forum

Since the 2016 mandate strengthening (Regulation (EU) 2016/1625), EMSA, Frontex and EFCA are required to cooperate closely on coast guard functions, sharing surveillance information, multipurpose assets (vessels, aircraft, satellite feeds) and joint operations where their mandates overlap at sea. This is a cooperation duty between three separate agencies, not the creation of a single EU coast guard body; each agency retains its own distinct legal mandate (maritime safety for EMSA, border management for Frontex, fisheries control for EFCA).
