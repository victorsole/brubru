# Comitology Register and Delegated Acts Register

## QUICK FACTS

- **Topic:** Article 290 delegated acts and Article 291 implementing acts -- the two registers where users track secondary EU legislation
- **Volume:** Approximately 1,500 to 2,000 implementing acts and 300 to 500 delegated acts are adopted each year, far exceeding the volume of primary legislative acts (roughly 100 to 200 Ordinary Legislative Procedure files per year)
- **Comitology Register** -- `https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/comitology-register/screen/home` -- covers Article 291 TFEU implementing acts; lists all committees, agendas, draft implementing acts, voting records, summary records, EP and Council scrutiny documents; managed by DG COMM; legal basis: Regulation (EU) No 182/2011 (the Comitology Regulation)
- **Interinstitutional Register of Delegated Acts (REGDEL)** -- `https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/regdel` -- covers Article 290 TFEU delegated acts; joint register of the Commission, European Parliament and Council; shows the full lifecycle from planning through expert group consultation, Commission adoption, EP and Council scrutiny, to OJ publication; launched December 2017; available in all 24 EU official languages; users can subscribe for file-specific notifications
- **Key legal difference:** Delegated acts (Art. 290) *supplement or amend non-essential elements* of a law -- EP and Council can veto by objection within 2 months (extendable). Implementing acts (Art. 291) *ensure uniform application* of a law -- Member States control via comitology committee, EP and Council have limited scrutiny rights only
- **Who uses them:** Trade associations, regulatory affairs professionals, lobbyists and legal counsel monitoring secondary rules on product standards, financial services, environmental limits, customs procedures, food safety thresholds, and similar technical matters
- **Commission information hub:** `https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/adopting-eu-law/implementing-and-delegated-acts_en`

## Article 290 vs Article 291 TFEU: The Legal Distinction

The Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union creates two distinct channels through which the Commission adopts secondary acts after primary legislation has been agreed.

**Article 290 TFEU -- Delegated acts**

A primary legislative act (a regulation or directive adopted by Parliament and Council) may explicitly delegate power to the Commission to adopt non-legislative acts that supplement or amend non-essential elements of that act. The delegation must define its objectives, content, scope and duration. The Commission then prepares the draft, consults expert groups of Member State representatives and publishes the draft for a four-week stakeholder feedback window on the Have Your Say portal. Once the Commission adopts the act, Parliament and Council each have a standard two-month scrutiny period (extendable by a further two months on request) to object. Absence of objection equals entry into force.

Key identifiers in the OJ: "Commission Delegated Regulation", "Commission Delegated Directive", "Commission Delegated Decision".

**Article 291 TFEU -- Implementing acts**

Where uniform conditions for implementation of a legally binding act are needed, that act may confer implementing powers on the Commission. A committee of Member State representatives assists the Commission under the Comitology Regulation (EU) No 182/2011, which replaced the predecessor framework of Council Decision 1999/468/EC.

Key identifiers in the OJ: "Commission Implementing Regulation", "Commission Implementing Decision", "Commission Implementing Directive".

**Core contrast:**

| Aspect | Art. 290 Delegated | Art. 291 Implementing |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| Purpose | Supplement / amend non-essential elements | Ensure uniform application |
| Control mechanism | EP or Council objection right | Comitology committee (Member States) |
| Scrutiny period | 2 months + 2 optional | Committee opinion before adoption |
| Register | REGDEL | Comitology Register |
| Annual volume (approx.) | 300 to 500 acts | 1,500 to 2,000 acts |

## The Comitology Register

**URL:** `https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/comitology-register/screen/home`

### What the register contains

- A list of every comitology committee, with its legal basis and rules of procedure
- Agendas for all committee meetings
- Draft implementing acts submitted to committees (references are always public; the document itself is published once it leaves the pre-decisional stage)
- Voting results, including each Member State's position in the Appeal Committee since the 2022 amendment to the Comitology Regulation
- Summary records of meetings and written consultations
- Documents forwarded to the European Parliament for information or scrutiny
- Annual reports on the work of comitology committees

### The two comitology procedures

**Advisory procedure:** The committee delivers an opinion; the Commission "shall take the utmost account" of it but is not bound. Used for less sensitive policy areas.

**Examination procedure:** The committee must deliver a positive opinion by qualified majority for the Commission to adopt the act. If no opinion is delivered or the opinion is negative, the Commission refers the matter to the Appeal Committee. If the Appeal Committee also fails to deliver a positive opinion, the Commission may adopt the act in most cases, but cannot in certain sensitive areas (GMOs, plant protection products). The Appeal Committee can meet at ministerial level for politically sensitive files.

### EP and Council scrutiny rights over implementing acts

Under Article 11 of the Comitology Regulation, Parliament and Council may indicate at any time that a draft implementing act exceeds the implementing powers conferred in the basic act. The Commission must then review the draft. This is a procedural check, not a veto.

Under the same regulation, Parliament may adopt a resolution stating that a draft implementing act exceeds the implementing powers; the Commission must respond within 3 months. The Parliament has used this mechanism most actively on GMO authorisation files.

### How to search the Comitology Register

Use the register's search function at `https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/comitology-register/screen/procedures`. You can filter by:
- Committee name or reference
- Policy area (DG responsible)
- Procedure type (advisory or examination)
- Meeting date range

Each committee page lists all associated implementing acts, their status, meeting documents and voting records.

## The Interinstitutional Register of Delegated Acts (REGDEL)

**URL:** `https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/regdel`

### What the register contains

REGDEL is a joint tool maintained by the Commission, the European Parliament and the Council. It aggregates data from the three institutions' own IT systems into a single lifecycle view for each delegated act.

For each file REGDEL shows:
- Planning: the Commission's intention to adopt a delegated act
- Expert group consultation: which expert group was consulted, meeting dates
- Stakeholder feedback: link to the Have Your Say portal entry
- Commission adoption: date and reference
- Transmission to EP and Council: the formal notification starting the scrutiny clock
- Scrutiny status: whether either co-legislator has raised objections, requested extension, or let the deadline expire
- Entry into force and OJ publication reference

### The scrutiny mechanism in detail

After the Commission formally notifies Parliament and Council:

1. The standard scrutiny period is **2 months** from the date of notification.
2. Either institution may extend the period by a further **2 months** by unilateral notification before the original deadline expires.
3. Either institution may adopt a reasoned objection by a majority of its members (Parliament) or by qualified majority (Council) within the scrutiny period.
4. If an objection is adopted, the delegated act does not enter into force. The Commission may then revise and resubmit, or withdraw.
5. If neither institution objects and no extension is requested, the act enters into force at the end of the 2-month period or on any later date specified in the act.

### Using REGDEL for lobbying and monitoring

Trade associations and legal counsel use REGDEL to:
- Set alerts when the Commission plans a delegated act under a specific empowerment clause (e.g., under DORA, the AI Act, CSRD, CBAM)
- Track the expert group consultation phase to request participation
- Monitor the 4-week Have Your Say feedback window for public input
- Watch the EP and Council scrutiny clock to coordinate objection campaigns

REGDEL's notification subscription function lets registered users follow individual files by email.

## How to Find a Specific Implementing or Delegated Act

### You know the basic act (e.g., GDPR, AI Act, REACH) and want to find all secondary acts under it

1. Go to REGDEL (`https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/regdel`) and search by the title or CELEX number of the basic act. This returns all delegated acts empowered by that act.
2. Go to the Comitology Register (`https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/comitology-register/screen/procedures`) and search by the responsible DG or policy area. Each committee shows the basic acts it works under.

### You have a specific OJ reference (e.g., "Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/XXXX")

- Search EUR-Lex (`https://eur-lex.europa.eu`) by the OJ reference or the act title. The EUR-Lex record links back to the REGDEL file and the comitology procedure where applicable.

### You want to track upcoming acts not yet adopted

- REGDEL's planning entries show acts the Commission intends to adopt, often before any formal proposal is published.
- The Commission's "Implementing Acts and Delegated Acts" tracking page at `https://commission.europa.eu/law/tracking-law-making_en` gives an overview of acts in the pipeline.

## Practical Chat-Routing Rules

**Send the user to the Comitology Register when:**
- They ask about a specific implementing regulation, implementing decision or implementing directive
- They ask which Member State voted how on a specific Commission measure
- They want the minutes or agenda of a comitology committee meeting
- They want to know whether Parliament has issued a scrutiny resolution on an implementing act
- The file involves technical thresholds, product standards, customs tariffs, food safety limits, phytosanitary measures, and similar uniform-application matters

**Send the user to REGDEL when:**
- They ask about a specific delegated regulation, delegated directive or delegated decision
- They want to know whether Parliament or Council has objected to a delegated act, or whether the scrutiny clock is still running
- They are monitoring the lifecycle of a delegated act under DORA, AI Act, CSRD, CBAM, MiFID II, SFDR or similar major regulations
- They want to subscribe to notifications for a specific empowerment clause

**General guidance:**
- Both registers are publicly accessible without a login for read-only searches.
- REGDEL subscription notifications require a European Commission authentication account (EU Login).
- For the full text of adopted acts, always direct users to EUR-Lex, not the registers themselves.
- The Comitology Register links documents to each lifecycle stage; EUR-Lex is the authoritative source for the final adopted text.
