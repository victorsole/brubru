# Digital Omnibus on AI: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744

## QUICK FACTS
- **Brubru deep-dive explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers):** https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2026-1744_aiomnibus/index.html
- **What it is:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of the European Parliament and of the Council of **8 July 2026** "amending Regulations (EU) 2024/1689, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2023/1230 as regards the simplification of the implementation of harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Digital Omnibus on AI)". CELEX **32026R1744**. ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj. It is **binding law, in force**, not a proposal.
- **Dates:** signed 8 July 2026; **published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026**; **in force on the third day after publication, Monday 27 July 2026** (Article 4). Legal basis Article 114 TFEU.
- **Procedure:** **2025/0359(COD)** (Omnibus VII), verified on OEIL on 5 Oct 2026: "Procedure completed". Commission proposal **COM(2025)0836** of 19 November 2025; IMCO/LIBE joint committee, rapporteurs **Arba Kokalari (EPP, IMCO)** and **Michael McNamara (Renew, LIBE)**; Parliament first-reading texts T10-0098/2026 (26 March 2026) and T10-0198/2026 (16 June 2026); Council adopted on 29 June 2026. Opinions: ECB, EESC (18 March 2026), Committee of the Regions (7 May 2026), EDPS and EDPB joint opinion (20 January 2026).
- **It does NOT replace the AI Act.** It amends it (Article 1, 43 numbered changes), plus aviation safety Regulation 2018/1139 (Article 2) and machinery Regulation 2023/1230 (Article 3).
- **New application dates it sets (Article 113 of the AI Act, as amended):** high-risk AI systems under **Annex III from 2 December 2027**; high-risk AI systems embedded in **Annex I products from 2 August 2028**; the **new prohibitions on intimate-image and child-abuse-material AI from 2 December 2026**; **Article 50(2) marking of synthetic content: systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must comply by 2 December 2026**; Articles 102 to 110 apply from 27 July 2026. The AI Act's general date of application stays 2 August 2026. **Grace periods and deadlines also set:** Article 111(2) gives providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems **intended for use by public authorities until 2 August 2030** to comply (systems already on the market are caught only if significantly changed); notified bodies already designated under Annex I Section A sectoral law must **apply for AI Act designation by 28 January 2028** (Article 43(3)); Commission guidelines on combining AI Act and sectoral requirements are due by **1 August 2027** (Article 96(1)(g)).
- **Two new prohibitions (Article 5(1)(ba) and (bb)):** AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate images, video or audio of an identifiable person without explicit consent, and AI systems that generate child sexual abuse material (except where a "without right" defence applies under national law). A system is caught where that output is its intended purpose, or a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome without adequate technical safeguards (Article 5(1a)).
- **Biggest enforcement change: new Articles 75a to 75d.** The **AI Office** becomes **exclusively competent** for AI systems built on a general-purpose AI model by the same provider (with exceptions) and for AI systems that are, or are integrated into, a **very large online platform or search engine** under the DSA. It gets **all market-surveillance powers** of Regulation 2019/1020 Article 14(4) and 16(3), including prohibiting or restricting a system, ordering withdrawal or recall, and requiring access to an online interface to be restricted, plus investigations, inspections, binding commitments (Article 75b), non-compliance decisions, fines and **periodic penalty payments up to 5% of average daily income or turnover per day** (Article 75c), Court of Justice unlimited jurisdiction over fines, and publication of decisions (Article 75d).
- **Is there an EU "AI kill switch"?** No: the Omnibus adds no shutdown capability duty and no emergency shutdown order. The nearest tools are the AI Office's withdraw, recall and restrict powers after an investigation, the existing Commission request power over general-purpose models (Article 93(1)(c) of the AI Act) and the human-oversight "stop" button for high-risk systems (Article 14(4)(e)). The terms "shut down" and "kill switch" do not appear in the Omnibus.
- **Small mid-caps (SMCs):** the simplifications for SMEs now extend to small mid-cap enterprises (definition by reference to Recommendation 2025/1099), including technical documentation, quality management and a fine cap "whichever is lower" (Article 99(6a)).
- **Annex XIV (new):** codes for notified-body designation by type of AI system, including **AIH 0401, "AI systems based on other emerging AI technologies, including Agentic AI"**.

## Overview
The Omnibus is a **simplification and timing package**, not a rewrite. Recitals explain the reasons: standards were delayed, the AI Act's high-risk obligations would otherwise have applied from 2 August 2026 without the tools to comply, SMEs and small mid-caps carry disproportionate cost, and the supervision of AI built on general-purpose models needed a single enforcer. It also adds two prohibitions in response to non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material produced with AI.

## Structure (4 articles)
- **Article 1: amendments to the AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689)**, points (1) to (43).
- **Article 2: amendments to Regulation 2018/1139** (civil aviation): when the Commission adopts delegated or implementing acts on AI safety components, it must take AI Act Chapter III Section 2 into account.
- **Article 3: amendments to Regulation 2023/1230** (machinery): (the delegated acts must reflect AI Act Chapter III Section 2 and Articles 17, 19, 72 and 73; the Commission's delegation runs five years from 27 July 2026); delegated acts adding health and safety requirements for high-risk AI in machinery, to apply by 2 August 2028; high-risk AI complying with AI Act standards is presumed compliant until machinery standards exist.
- **Article 4: entry into force** on the third day after publication.

## Substantive changes to the AI Act (Article 1)
- **Scope and definitions (points 1 to 4):** support for SMCs and SMEs; for Annex I Section B products only Article 6(1), Article 60a and Articles 102 to 112 apply; Article 2(13) lets the Commission limit some requirements for Annex I Section A products by delegated act **by 2 August 2027** where sectoral law gives equivalent protection; the "safety component" definition is narrowed (fulfils a safety function, or its failure endangers health and safety); new definitions of SME and SMC.
- **AI literacy (point 5, Article 4):** providers and deployers must take measures to support AI literacy of their staff, and the text expressly does not require them to guarantee any specific level; the Commission and Member States support it, and the Board adopts recommendations.
- **Bias detection (point 6, new Article 4a):** providers of high-risk systems may **exceptionally process special categories of personal data** strictly necessary to detect and correct bias, under six cumulative safeguards (no alternative data, technical limits on re-use, security controls, no transfer to others, deletion once corrected, record of necessity). Other providers and deployers may do so under the same safeguards, without any obligation to do so.
- **Prohibitions (point 7, Article 5):** new points (ba) and (bb) and paragraphs 1a and 1b described above; manipulation that does not increase the exposure of depicted intimate parts or alter the nature of the depicted activity is not caught.
- **Classification (point 8, Article 6):** AI used solely for non-safety user assistance, optimisation, efficiency, automation, convenience or quality control is not a safety component; a product requiring third-party assessment only for non-safety risks (such as radio spectrum) does not trigger the high-risk route.
- **Requirements and documentation (points 9 to 13):** data-quality rules refer to Article 4a; SMEs and SMCs may give simplified technical documentation; quality-management proportionality; Article 25 value-chain duties: the original provider must **cooperate with the new provider** (documentation, known limitations and failure modes, targeted technical access); fundamental-rights impact assessment may cross-refer to the data protection impact assessment, and the AI Office builds a questionnaire template.
- **Notified bodies and standards (points 14 to 19):** (details below)  a **single application and unified assessment** for bodies designated under both the AI Act and Annex I Section A sectoral law; the Commission must request European standards "without undue delay" to support joint compliance; **high-risk AI within the Cyber Resilience Act's scope is deemed to comply with AI Act Article 15 cybersecurity requirements** where Article 12(1) of the Cyber Resilience Act is met (point 18, new Article 42(3)).
- **Details of points 11 to 19:** the Commission must create a **simplified technical-documentation form** for SMEs and SMCs, which notified bodies must accept (Article 11); the value-chain written-agreement duty (Article 25(4)) **does not apply to third parties offering tools, services, processes or components, other than general-purpose AI models, under a free and open-source licence**; for Annex I Section A products a manufacturer who may self-assess under sectoral law may do so only if it has also applied the harmonised standards or common specifications covering all AI Act Section 2 requirements, and the high-risk classification alone does not oblige it to choose third-party assessment (Article 43(3)).
- **Codes of practice (points 20, 21):** the Commission assesses whether codes on marking and labelling of AI-generated content, and on general-purpose AI obligations, are adequate. If the Commission finds a code on marking and labelling inadequate, it **may adopt an implementing act setting common rules** (Article 50(7), examination procedure under Article 98(2)). **Drafting inconsistency to flag:** recital 102 says the Commission's implementing-act empowerments in Articles 50(7), 56(6) and 72(3) are removed, but the operative text of Article 50(7) as published still contains it (Article 56(6) and 72(3) do drop it: 72(3) becomes guidance with a voluntary template). The operative text is what binds.
- **Sandboxes and real-world testing (points 22 to 26, 33):** each Member State's national sandbox operational by **2 August 2027**; the EDPS may run one for Union bodies; the **AI Office may run a Union-level sandbox**; real-world testing extended to Annex I Section A systems, and new Article 60a lets Member States allow it for Annex I Section B products.
- **SMEs and governance (points 26 to 30):** simplified quality management for all SMEs without partner enterprises; the AI Office must receive adequate resources (Article 64(3)); expert fees aligned; post-market monitoring plan guidance and template by **2 September 2027**.
- **Enforcement (points 31, 32, 34):** Articles 75, 75a, 75b, 75c, 75d as in QUICK FACTS; Article 75(1a) providers of high-risk systems under the AI Office report **serious incidents to the AI Office** (Article 73 timing applies: 15 days, 2 days for widespread infringement or critical-infrastructure disruption, 10 days for a death); Article 75(1d) the AI Office must notify and consult the national authority before a decision restricting, withdrawing or recalling a system on a national market; Article 75(2a) a national authority can ask the AI Office to assess a case, and the AI Office answers within **four months**; the AI Office **entrusts pre-market third-party tests and assessments of its high-risk systems to notified bodies** acting for the Commission, and may withdraw that delegation with immediate effect (Article 75(1e)); on-site inspections follow Article 75a(4): where national law requires court authorisation, the national judge checks only that coercive measures are **neither arbitrary nor excessive** and may not review the necessity of the inspection, whose legality is reviewable **only by the Court of Justice**; Article 77 strengthens cooperation with fundamental-rights authorities.
- **Delegations, penalties, transition (points 35 to 40):** delegated-act powers adjusted (Articles 2(13) and 30(2) added, conferred **for five years from 27 July 2026**, tacitly extended unless Parliament or Council object); Member States must take SMEs, start-ups and SMCs' economic viability into account when imposing penalties; Member States' penalty rules may include non-monetary measures; Article 99 gets a new fine tier for value-chain duties (Article 25(2) and (4)) and the SMC lower-of cap; Article 111 grace period clarified.
- **Annexes (points 41 to 43):** Annex I Section A point 1 deleted and machinery Regulation 2023/1230 added to Section B; two items deleted from Annex VIII Section B (simplified EU-database registration); Annex XIV added.

## Recital highlights (read in full, 47 recitals)
- **Why the dates moved (recitals 2, 24, 100):** delayed standards, common specifications and national authorities made the original 2 August 2026 start for high-risk rules unworkable; the text says protection of health, safety and fundamental rights is not reduced.
- **Grace period for existing systems (recital 98, Article 111(2)):** if at least one unit of a high-risk system type and model was lawfully placed on the market before the date, other units of the same type and model may continue without new obligations while the design stays unchanged; the first-unit date is decisive; any **significant design change** triggers full compliance.
- **What the new prohibition catches (recitals 11 to 16, 42 to 44):** only **realistic** depictions of identifiable people's intimate parts or sexually explicit activity, not cartoonish or impossible depictions; example safeguards providers may use include data cleaning, refusal training, safe prompt design, output controls, runtime guardrails, content classification and filtering, usage restrictions, abuse detection and notice-and-action; deployers are caught only when they use a system to generate such material; accidental generation is not covered; medical uses where the person cannot consent are not precluded; manipulation that does not increase exposure (changing a background, adding a caption, adjusting brightness) is excluded.
- **Notified bodies (recital 56):** bodies notified under Annex I Section A sectoral law may assess AI Act conformity for **18 months from 27 July 2026**; designation applications can be filed at any time.
- **Who supervises what (recitals 82 to 86):** the AI Office's exclusive competence reaches providers and their deployers within the same undertaking; other deployers stay under national supervision; systems used by Union institutions stay with the EDPS; for AI embedded in a very large platform or search engine, the first point of entry is the **DSA risk assessment, mitigation and audit duties (DSA Articles 34, 35, 37)**, without prejudice to ex post AI Act enforcement, with ne bis in idem and proportionality of overlapping fines expressly required; powers not specified for the AI Office, such as the Regulation 2019/1020 Article 16(3) measures, remain available to it, but it may not use a related general power to dodge the limits of a specified one.
- **AI literacy (recital 36):** stringent literacy obligations were judged unsuitable for all providers and deployers; support comes through Commission and Member State tools, the Board's recommendations and the Apply AI Alliance.
- **Machinery (recital 104):** machinery moves from Annex I Section A to Section B, a sectoral approach; the delegated acts amending Annex III of Regulation 2023/1230 must apply by 2 August 2028.
