# Guidance on Grant Reductions (EU 2021-2027)

## QUICK FACTS

- Grant reductions apply to serious non-compliance: substantial errors, irregularities, fraud, or serious breach of grant agreement obligations (Art 28.1 MGA).
- Reductions governed by Article 28 of the Model Grant Agreement (MGA), with calculation methodology in Article 22.3.4 MGA.
- Reduction rate matrix: ranges from 5% (gravity 1) to 100% (gravity 5), with fraud starting at minimum gravity 3 (25% minimum).
- Reductions only at beneficiary termination, final payment, or afterwards, never at interim payments.
- Applied as percentage of accepted EU contribution; for lump sum grants, calculated via special formula based on percentage paid minus percentage established in review.
- Beneficiary receives contradictory phase notice (pre-information letter or payment letter) and right to submit observations before final decision.
- Aggravating factors: prior warning from granting authority, bad faith, failure to cooperate, recurrence of breach; mitigating factors: beneficiary remedied situation, took precautionary measures, acted in good faith, cooperated with authority.
- When multiple breaches occur, only the highest reduction rate applies; other breaches counted as aggravating factors.
- Reductions may be combined cumulatively with cost rejections (both financial impacts sum).
- Appeal mechanism: if grant reduction leads to recovery, observations trigger confirmation letter; if no recovery, observations trigger payment review procedure that may grant additional payment if observations accepted.
- Cannot apply reductions for events recognised as force majeure (Article 35 MGA).


## When a grant is reduced (the legal triggers)

Under Article 28.1 of the Model Grant Agreement, reductions can be applied in three main categories:

1. **Substantial errors, irregularities or fraud**
   - Significant breaches that demonstrate non-compliance with grant conditions
   - Fraud automatically qualifies for minimum gravity 3 (25% reduction minimum)

2. **Serious breach of obligations under the grant agreement or during the award procedure**
   - Improper implementation of the action (several activities not carried out)
   - Non-compliance with call conditions
   - Submission of false information
   - Failure to provide required information
   - Breach of ethics or security rules (if applicable)
   - Failure to cooperate with checks, reviews, audits and investigations
   - Breach of the obligation to display the EU emblem
   - Incorrect handling of EU-classified information
   - Plagiarism

3. **Systematic or recurrent errors, irregularities, fraud or serious breach of obligations in other grants**
   - Extension of findings from other grants if they have material impact on the current grant (Article 25 MGA)

Reductions are only applied for serious cases. Minor issues like slight delays in deliverables or technical deviations without significant impact on the action or EU financial interests will normally not trigger a reduction.

Situations recognised as force majeure cannot constitute a breach of obligations and therefore cannot result in a reduction.


## Calculation method (with example)

### General Principle

The reduction is proportionate to the seriousness and duration of the breach. The granting authority applies the reduction rate to the **accepted EU contribution** of the beneficiary who committed the violation, then deducts the resulting amount from the accepted EU contribution to establish the revised total.

### Reduction Rate Matrix

The reduction rate depends on two dimensions: **type of breach** and **gravity/impact** (0 to 5, where 0 = no reduction):

| Type of Breach | Gravity 0 | Gravity 1 | Gravity 2 | Gravity 3 | Gravity 4 | Gravity 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fraud | N/A | N/A | N/A | 25% | 50% | 75% or 100% |
| Substantial errors or irregularities | 0% | 5% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% or 100% |
| Obligations during award procedure | 0% | 5% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% or 100% |
| Obligations during grant implementation | 0% | 5% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% or 100% |
| Non/improper implementation of the action | 0% | 5% | 10% | 25% | 50% | 75% or 100% |

### Determining Gravity

When deciding on gravity, the granting authority considers:

**Aggravating factors:**
- Prior warning from granting authority about the breach but beneficiary did not take remedial measures
- Beneficiary acted in bad faith or with intentionality
- Beneficiary failed to cooperate with the granting authority
- Reputational damage to the granting authority
- Recurrence of the breach
- Beneficiary committed multiple breaches in this or other grants

**Mitigating factors:**
- Beneficiary remedied the situation following a warning
- Beneficiary took precautionary measures (proactively or following a warning) to prevent similar future breaches
- Beneficiary acted in good faith
- Beneficiary cooperated throughout proceedings
- The project fully achieved its objectives (does not apply to fraud cases)

### Special Case: Lump Sum Grants

For lump sum grants (where work packages are locked and cannot be re-opened for cost rejection), non-improper implementation established at final payment or ex-post technical review is addressed through a special reduction formula:

**Financial impact = Beneficiary's lump sum share in EUR for work package × {percentage paid – percentage established in review}**

This formula is applied for each work package in scope and the results summed.

### Example

A beneficiary received an accepted EU contribution of EUR 100,000. An audit discovers improper implementation of the action (activities not carried out) that had a moderate impact on project objectives. The granting authority assesses this as gravity 2 (obligation during grant implementation). Under the matrix, a gravity 2 improper implementation breach carries a 10% reduction rate.

Calculation: EUR 100,000 × 10% = EUR 10,000 reduction
Revised accepted EU contribution: EUR 100,000 – EUR 10,000 = EUR 90,000

### Multiple Breaches

If a beneficiary commits multiple breaches in the same grant agreement, only the highest reduction rate determined for each breach is applied. Other breaches may be counted as aggravating factors when setting the applicable rate.


## Decision procedure and contradictory phase

### Timing

Grant reductions can be applied at beneficiary termination, at final payment, or after final payment. They cannot be applied during interim payments.

### Notification Process

The way reductions are communicated depends on whether they trigger a recovery:

1. **If grant reduction does NOT lead to a recovery**: Beneficiary is informed in a payment letter

2. **If grant reduction DOES lead to a recovery**: Beneficiary is informed in a pre-information letter

In both cases, the beneficiary receives opportunity to submit observations.

### Observation and Review Procedure

- For ongoing grants, beneficiaries are usually informed immediately after the breach is discovered, with a warning about potential grant reduction and request to take remedial measures
- Beneficiaries have a right to submit observations on all elements in the letter, including the grant reduction itself (Article 28.2 MGA)
- The granting authority follows up on observations:
  - If no recovery: through a payment review procedure which may grant additional payment if observations are accepted as well-founded
  - If recovery: through a confirmation letter indicating the final position on recovery after considering observations


## Beneficiary rights (review, appeal, arbitration)

### Right to Submit Observations

All beneficiaries have a right to submit observations during the contradictory phase, before the final decision on grant reduction. This right applies whether or not a recovery is triggered.

### Payment Review Procedure

If the grant reduction does not lead to a financial recovery, beneficiaries can request a payment review procedure. The granting authority will reconsider the observations submitted and may grant an additional payment if the observations are considered well-founded.

### Confirmation Process

If the grant reduction leads to a recovery, the granting authority issues a confirmation letter after considering beneficiary observations, setting out the final position on recovery.

### General Principles

All grant reductions are applied in accordance with general principles of proportionality and equal treatment of beneficiaries, ensuring that reductions are coherent and harmonised across EU services.

### Recourse

The beneficiary may pursue administrative review or legal remedies according to applicable EU law procedures, including the right to challenge the granting authority's decision through formal appeal mechanisms as defined in the grant agreement.


## Relationship with suspension and termination

Grant reduction is one of several "Chapter 5 measures" available to the granting authority for breach of contract. These measures may be applied individually or in combination:

### Other Chapter 5 Measures

- **Cost rejection**: Ineligible costs are rejected from the accepted EU contribution
- **Suspension of payments**: Grant payments are suspended during investigation or remedial period
- **Termination**: The entire grant agreement is terminated for serious or persistent breach
- **Administrative sanctions**: Exclusion from future EU award procedures or financial penalties may be imposed

### Cumulative Application

Grant reductions may be combined with other measures. When grant reduction takes place at the same time as cost rejection, the financial impact of both measures is cumulative (they add together).

### Proportionality

The principle of proportionality must be respected in all cases, whether measures are applied individually or in combination.
