# ETIAS: European Travel Information and Authorisation System

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2018/1240 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 12 September 2018 establishing a European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS), entered into force 9 October 2018
- What it is: a pre-travel authorisation, not a visa, required for nationals of visa-exempt third countries entering the Schengen area for short stays (up to 90 days in any 180-day period)
- Who needs it: nationals of roughly 60 visa-exempt third countries (including the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Brazil) travelling to 30 European countries -- an estimated 1.4 billion people are eligible to apply
- Fee: EUR 20 per application (revised up from the originally planned EUR 7). Applicants under 18 or over 70 are exempt from the fee
- Validity: three years, or until the travel document used to apply expires, whichever comes first. Multiple entries allowed within validity
- Launch: ETIAS is expected to start operations in the last quarter of 2026 (Q4 2026). The exact date will be confirmed by the EU several months in advance. A six-month transitional grace period is expected to follow launch, during which enforcement is phased in
- Sequencing: ETIAS launch is tied to completion of the Entry/Exit System (EES) -- EES must be operational first, since ETIAS relies on EES infrastructure and border-crossing records
- Application process: online or via app, most decisions issued within minutes; most applications are automatically approved. Applications flagged by automated checks go to the ETIAS Central Unit and, if needed, to the ETIAS National Unit of the Member State of first entry
- ETIAS Central Unit: established within Frontex (European Border and Coast Guard Agency), operational 24/7, over 200 staff, verifies flagged applications, runs the Travel to Europe app and assistance centre, and manages risk indicators and screening rules. Publishes an annual activity report to the European Parliament, Council and Commission by 31 March
- ETIAS National Units: one per Schengen Member State, take final decisions on applications forwarded by the Central Unit, including refusals
- Screening criteria: security risk, irregular migration risk, high epidemic risk, plus a cross-check against the ETIAS watchlist (persons linked to terrorism or serious crime)
- Interoperability: ETIAS checks against the Schengen Information System (SIS), Visa Information System (VIS), Entry/Exit System (EES), Eurodac, ECRIS-TCN (criminal records of third-country nationals), and Europol and Interpol data, via the shared Common Identity Repository and the interoperability framework
- Technical operator: eu-LISA (EU Agency for the Operational Management of Large-Scale IT Systems) develops and operates the ETIAS IT infrastructure; Frontex operates the ETIAS Central Unit
- Refusal, annulment and revocation: an authorisation can be refused at application stage, or annulled/revoked later if grounds for issuing it are no longer met (for example, a new hit on a watchlist or SIS alert). Refused/annulled/revoked applicants have a right of appeal in the Member State that took the decision
- Carriers (airlines, ferry and coach operators) must verify passengers hold a valid ETIAS authorisation before boarding, once ETIAS is enforced

## What ETIAS is

ETIAS is not a visa. It does not require a visit to a consulate, biometric enrolment or an interview. It is a light-touch, largely automated pre-screening system: travellers who are currently allowed to enter the Schengen area without a visa (for short stays) will, once ETIAS is enforced, need to complete an online application and pay a small fee before travelling. Most authorisations are expected to be granted within minutes, and are then valid for multiple trips over three years.

The purpose is security and migration-risk screening rather than revenue: ETIAS closes an information gap, because until now the EU had almost no advance data on visa-exempt travellers before they reached the external border. It complements the Entry/Exit System (EES), which records biometric entry and exit data at the border itself.

## Who is affected

ETIAS applies to nationals of third countries that do not need a Schengen visa for short stays -- roughly 60 countries, covering an estimated 1.4 billion people, including the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Japan, Brazil and others on the EU's visa-exempt list (Regulation (EU) 2018/1806, Annex II). It does not apply to EU/EEA/Swiss citizens, holders of a valid Schengen visa, or holders of residence permits/cards issued by a Schengen state.

## Legislative and operational sequencing

- Regulation (EU) 2018/1240 was adopted 12 September 2018, establishing the legal framework for ETIAS
- The system's launch depends on the prior rollout of the Entry/Exit System (EES), which records biometric entry/exit data for third-country nationals at the external border. ETIAS relies on EES's technical infrastructure and traveller records
- Following repeated delays to EES, ETIAS is now expected to start operations in Q4 2026, with a phased six-month grace period before mandatory enforcement (expected around April 2027)
- eu-LISA is responsible for building and operating the underlying IT system (the Central System), including its interoperability with SIS, VIS, EES, Eurodac and ECRIS-TCN

## Screening and decision process

1. **Application**: the traveller submits personal data, passport details, travel plans and answers to background questions (criminal record, past refusals, health, conflict-zone travel) via the ETIAS website or mobile app, and pays the EUR 20 fee (waived under 18 / over 70)
2. **Automated cross-check**: the application is automatically checked against SIS, VIS, EES, Eurodac, ECRIS-TCN, Europol and Interpol data, and the ETIAS watchlist
3. **No hit**: authorisation is issued automatically, typically within minutes
4. **Hit or ambiguity**: the application is forwarded to the ETIAS Central Unit (within Frontex) for manual verification, and if needed onward to the ETIAS National Unit of the Member State of intended first entry, which takes the final decision (issue, refuse)
5. **Refusal, annulment, revocation**: a refused, annulled or revoked application can be appealed in the Member State that made the decision, in accordance with that state's national law

## Governance

- **Frontex**: hosts and staffs the ETIAS Central Unit, which does the manual screening layer and publishes an annual activity report to the European Parliament, Council and Commission
- **eu-LISA**: builds and runs the technical Central System and its interoperability links to other large-scale EU justice-and-home-affairs databases
- **Member States**: each runs an ETIAS National Unit that takes final decisions on flagged applications and handles appeals
- **European Data Protection Supervisor / national data protection authorities**: oversight of personal data processing under ETIAS

## Why it matters for EU policy professionals

- Any organisation whose staff, clients or event attendees travel from the US, UK or other visa-exempt countries to the EU needs to plan for the EUR 20 fee, the pre-travel application step, and the three-year validity window once ETIAS is enforced
- ETIAS sits inside the EU's wider "smart borders" and interoperability architecture alongside EES, SIS, VIS, Eurodac and ECRIS-TCN -- understanding one component requires understanding how they interlock
- Carriers (airlines, coach and ferry operators) face a new pre-boarding verification obligation once ETIAS is enforced, with commercial and compliance implications
- The repeated delays (originally planned for 2021, now Q4 2026) are a useful case study in how technically interdependent EU IT-systems projects (EES must precede ETIAS) slip
