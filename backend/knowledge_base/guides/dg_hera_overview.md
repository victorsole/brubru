# DG HERA: Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: DG HERA (Directorate-General for Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority) is the European Commission department responsible for anticipating, preparing for, and rapidly responding to serious cross-border health emergencies. Its core remit covers medical countermeasures: vaccines, therapeutics, diagnostics, PPE, and CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) countermeasures.
- **Established**: 16 September 2021 as an internal Commission service by Commission Decision C(2021) 6712 final (published in OJ 2021/C 393 I/02). Elevated to a standalone Directorate-General under the second von der Leyen College (December 2024).
- **Director-General**: Florika Fink-Hooijer
- **Commissioner**: Hadja Lahbib (portfolio title: verify via EU Who-is-Who at ec.europa.eu/commissioners/2024-2029 or the HERA organisational page)
- **Governance bodies**: HERA Board (Member State representatives, relevant Commission services and EU agencies; strategic guidance); HERA Advisory Forum (industry, civil society, academic experts; scientific and technical advice on supply chains, manufacturing capacity, and epidemiological intelligence); Civil Society Forum (sub-group under the Advisory Forum); Joint Industrial Cooperation Forum (industry liaison, sub-group under the Advisory Forum)
- **Mandate**: development, procurement, stockpiling and equitable distribution of medical countermeasures; threat assessment and intelligence; support to Member States on preparedness planning; industrial readiness for pandemic and crisis response
- **Key initiatives**: Critical Medicines Alliance (launched April 2024, 300-plus organisations, Strategic Report 28 February 2025); EU FAB (always-on vaccine manufacturing capacity network); rescEU medical stockpile (in cooperation with DG ECHO); HERA Industry Hub; Medical Countermeasures Strategy
- **Underpinning regulations**: Regulation (EU) 2022/2371 on serious cross-border threats to health (CELEX 32022R2371); Regulation (EU) 2022/2372 establishing a crisis framework for medical countermeasures (CELEX 32022R2372); Critical Medicines Act (proposal COM(2025) 102, provisional inter-institutional agreement May 2026); EMA reinforced mandate under Regulation (EU) 2022/123 (CELEX 32022R0123)
- **Budget**: indicative EUR 5 billion across 2022-2027 under EU4Health, Horizon Europe, and the rescEU envelope
- **Joint Procurement Agreement**: 37 countries (EU Member States plus EEA and candidate countries) are signatories
- **Relationship to**: EMA (regulatory assessment and shortage monitoring), ECDC (epidemiological surveillance and risk assessment), DG SANTE (public health policy), DG ECHO (rescEU civil protection), national authorities (Health Security Committee coordination)
- **Main URL**: `health.ec.europa.eu/health-emergency-preparedness-and-response-hera_en` (live, verified June 2026)
- **Commission organisational page**: `commission.europa.eu/about/departments-and-executive-agencies/health-emergency-preparedness-and-response-authority_en` (live, verified June 2026)

## From Authority to Directorate-General: Institutional History

HERA's creation was a direct response to failures exposed by the COVID-19 pandemic. Before 2021, the EU lacked a dedicated body responsible for medical countermeasure procurement or industrial readiness for health crises. The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) handled epidemiological intelligence, and DG SANTE coordinated health policy, but no Commission service held the mandate to contract with manufacturers for vaccines or therapeutics ahead of a declared emergency, or to coordinate EU-level stockpiling of CBRN countermeasures.

On 16 September 2021, the Commission established HERA by Commission Decision C(2021) 6712 final (OJ 2021/C 393 I/02) as an internal service, administratively linked to DG SANTE. Its creation ran in parallel with the European Health Union legislative package: Regulation (EU) 2022/2371 (serious cross-border threats, in force January 2023) gave HERA a statutory basis for joint procurement and a formal role in health security governance, while Regulation (EU) 2022/2372 (crisis framework for medical countermeasures, in force November 2022) established specific emergency-phase powers.

The shift from "Authority" to "Directorate-General" came with the second von der Leyen College in December 2024. HERA became a standalone DG, separate from DG SANTE, with a dedicated Commissioner in its reporting line, placing health preparedness on the same institutional footing as other major Commission policy areas. Florika Fink-Hooijer continues as Director-General. This elevation reflects the strategic priority placed on preparedness: the Commission now treats health-crisis readiness not as an emergency annex to health policy, but as a standing government function.

For the full legislative framework that gives HERA its powers, see guide `eu_health_security_framework`.

## Mandate and the Three Pillars

HERA's work organises around three functional pillars.

### 1. Preparedness

Building the knowledge, industrial capacities, and stockpiles needed before a crisis materialises. This covers threat assessment and intelligence gathering (monitoring emerging biological, chemical, radiological and nuclear threats); R&D investment in medical countermeasure platforms (vaccines, antivirals, diagnostics, PPE); maintaining always-on manufacturing capacity through EU FAB; and HERA's role in the Health Security Committee (HSC), where preparedness plans are coordinated with Member States. The Medical Countermeasures Strategy (published 2021, updated in HERA's work programmes) defines the categories of countermeasure for which the EU seeks domestic production or pre-negotiated access.

### 2. Crisis Response

Activating emergency procurement mechanisms when the Commission formally recognises a public health emergency at EU level under Regulation (EU) 2022/2371, or when a Medical Countermeasure Emergency Framework is triggered under Regulation (EU) 2022/2372. In crisis mode, HERA can procure on behalf of Member States, manage the rescEU medical stockpile in coordination with DG ECHO, and activate pre-negotiated supply agreements. The Joint Procurement Agreement (37 countries) provides the legal and administrative infrastructure for emergency collective purchasing.

### 3. Industrial Readiness

Ensuring the EU pharmaceutical and medtech sector can scale up production rapidly. The EU FAB network, the HERA Industry Hub, and the Critical Medicines Alliance all serve this pillar. HERA works with industry to map production capacity, identify supply-chain vulnerabilities, and create financial incentives for companies to maintain reserve capacity. The Critical Medicines Act (provisional agreement May 2026) adds a legislative dimension to this work: Strategic Projects fast-tracking, procurement resilience requirements, and an EU-manufacturing preference in public tenders.

## Critical Medicines Alliance

The Critical Medicines Alliance was launched in April 2024 as a multi-stakeholder consultative forum co-convened by the Commission under HERA's leadership, to address Europe's structural dependence on non-EU suppliers for critical active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and finished medicines.

The Alliance brings together more than 300 organisations: pharmaceutical companies, generic and API manufacturers, patient organisations, healthcare professional bodies, Member State health authorities, and academic institutions. Unlike the formal governance bodies (HERA Board, HSC), membership in the Alliance is open and the forum operates as a collaborative knowledge and recommendation-generation platform.

The Alliance published its Strategic Report on 28 February 2025, setting out recommendations across four areas: incentivising EU investment in manufacturing capacity; demand-side measures favouring procurement resilience and EU-manufacturing preference; supply-chain diversification and strategic international partnerships; and improved shortage monitoring. The Strategic Report fed directly into the Commission's Critical Medicines Act proposal (COM(2025) 102, Strasbourg 11 March 2025).

The Alliance is distinct from the Critical Medicines Coordination Group established by the Critical Medicines Act: the Alliance is a multi-stakeholder consultative body open to industry and civil society, while the Coordination Group is a formal inter-institutional body composed of the Commission and Member State representatives only.

For the full procedure and legal content of the Critical Medicines Act, see guide `critical_medicines_act`.

## EU FAB: Always-On Vaccine Manufacturing Capacity

EU FAB (EU Vaccine Manufacturing Network) is HERA's programme to secure always-on manufacturing capacity for vaccines within the EU. Its purpose is to ensure the EU is not dependent solely on ad-hoc industry commitments during a pandemic, and can produce vaccines at scale within months of a new pathogen being identified.

Under EU FAB, the Commission concluded Service Level Agreements with a set of vaccine manufacturers across multiple EU Member States. Participating companies commit to reserving a defined proportion of their production capacity for EU pandemic needs, in exchange for a retainer fee paid by HERA. The programme was formally launched in 2022, following a call for expression of interest that attracted manufacturers across several technology platforms (mRNA, viral vector, protein-based). The target capacity covers sufficient doses for the EU population in a pandemic scenario.

EU FAB addresses a strategic gap exposed by COVID-19, when EU Member States competed with each other and with non-EU countries to secure scarce vaccine supplies. (Individual company names, contracted capacity figures, and progress reports are published on the HERA section of health.ec.europa.eu; verify directly against current HERA publications.)

## rescEU Medical Stockpile

HERA builds and manages EU-level strategic stockpiles of critical medical countermeasures under the rescEU strand of the Union Civil Protection Mechanism (UCPM), in cooperation with DG ECHO. The rescEU medical stockpile covers CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) medical items: antidotes, antibiotics for mass-casualty scenarios, PPE, radiation-treatment medicines, and related emergency supplies.

The stockpile operates as a flexible network of supply hubs distributed across Member States. Items can be rapidly mobilised and deployed to any EU Member State, or to third countries under the UCPM's external dimension, upon request to the Emergency Response Coordination Centre (ERCC).

HERA's role within the rescEU strand is to define the medical countermeasure composition of the stockpile and manage procurement; DG ECHO provides the civil protection framework, logistics and deployment mechanisms. The arrangement reflects the organisational separation between health preparedness (HERA mandate) and civil protection logistics (DG ECHO mandate), which are governed by different legal bases but must operate jointly in any major emergency.

## Joint Procurement and IPCEI Med

HERA manages EU joint procurement of medical countermeasures under Article 12 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2371. The legal vehicle is the Joint Procurement Agreement (JPA), signed by 37 countries (all 27 EU Member States plus EEA states and several candidate countries). When a joint procurement exercise is launched, the Commission negotiates framework contracts on behalf of the participating Member States, which can then place orders under those contracts.

Joint procurement is the successor to the ad-hoc vaccine advance purchase agreements used during COVID-19 and is now a standing mechanism. It enables the EU to aggregate demand, reduce price disparities between Member States, and avoid the supply disruptions that arise when Member States compete independently for scarce countermeasures.

IPCEI Med (Important Project of Common European Interest in Health) represents the heavy industrial-policy complement: it mobilises public and private investment in EU health value chains, including manufacturing infrastructure and R&D facilities, on the model of IPCEI on hydrogen and microelectronics. HERA participates in IPCEI Med governance alongside DG GROW and national authorities. IPCEI Med approval status and approved projects should be verified via the Commission's State Aid decisions register, as individual tranches are approved on a rolling basis.

## HERA Industry Hub

The HERA Industry Hub is the Commission's single-entry-point interface for health-industry engagement with HERA: procurement processes, R&D funding calls, EU FAB participation, and crisis-response contracting. It provides manufacturers and biotech companies with information on how to enter supplier frameworks, respond to HERA procurement calls, and access EU4Health and Horizon Europe grants relevant to medical countermeasures.

The Hub also organises HERA Industry Days, a recurring forum for dialogue between HERA leadership and the life-sciences sector. The next HERA Industry Days is scheduled for 23-24 November 2026.

(The Industry Hub does not have a dedicated stable sub-URL at the time of writing; access via the main HERA page at health.ec.europa.eu/health-emergency-preparedness-and-response-hera_en and the Overview navigation.)

## Relationship with EMA, ECDC, and National Authorities

The four institutional actors form an interlocking system with defined but distinct roles.

**HERA** holds the mandate for procurement, stockpiling, industrial readiness, and crisis-phase management. As a Commission DG, it acts on political authority and appropriations; it signs contracts and procures on behalf of Member States.

**EMA** (European Medicines Agency) holds the regulatory role under Regulation (EU) 2022/123: scientific assessment, marketing authorisations, the Emergency Task Force (ETF) for accelerated reviews, and the shortage-monitoring and mitigation mandate via the Medicines Shortages Steering Group (MSSG). EMA identifies shortage risks; HERA procures the countermeasures. See guide `ema_centralised_procedure_regulation`.

**ECDC** (European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control) provides epidemiological intelligence and risk assessments that feed HERA's threat-assessment function: surveillance data, epidemic modelling, risk communication, and EU Health Task Force deployment to outbreak sites. ECDC threat assessments are the operational trigger for HERA preparedness reviews.

**Health Security Committee (HSC)**: chaired by the Commission (HERA leads the secretariat), composed of senior national health-ministry officials; the political coordination layer for preparedness planning, joint procurement decisions, and formal recognition of a public-health emergency at EU level under Regulation (EU) 2022/2371.

**National authorities** retain primary competence for health system management under TFEU Article 168 (EU competence complements, rather than replaces, national health policy). Member States implement national preparedness plans, maintain their own stockpiles, and participate in joint procurement via the JPA. HERA's added value is pooling demand, budget and industrial capacity at EU level.

## Funding Sources

HERA's budget draws on three main EU instruments:

**EU4Health Programme (2021-2027)**: Regulation (EU) 2021/522 (CELEX 32021R0522), total envelope EUR 5.3 billion. EU4Health funds preparedness actions, joint procurement administration, and health-system resilience projects, with HERA as a key implementing body. See guide `eu4health_2027_priorities`.

**Horizon Europe**: R&D funding for medical countermeasure development (vaccines, antivirals, diagnostics). HERA aligns its R&D calls with Horizon Europe's Health cluster (Cluster 1) and with relevant partnerships such as the Global Health EDCTP3 partnership and CEPI. See guide `eu_global_health_resilience_initiative_2026`.

**rescEU envelope (UCPM)**: Funding for stockpile procurement under the Union Civil Protection Mechanism, managed jointly with DG ECHO. Appropriations are separate from EU4Health and Horizon Europe.

HERA's indicative operational budget across 2022-2027 is approximately EUR 5 billion, combining contributions from all three programmes.

## How Brubru Users Should Engage with HERA

**Pharmaceutical and biotech companies**: Monitor EU FAB calls for expressions of interest (always-on manufacturing capacity contracts); respond to HERA procurement calls via the Industry Hub; participate in Critical Medicines Alliance working groups and consultation processes; engage with the Joint Industrial Cooperation Forum, which sits under the HERA Advisory Forum.

**Medtech and diagnostics companies**: Track HERA procurement frameworks for diagnostics and medical devices; note the rescEU CBRN stockpile specification updates; engage via the Joint Industrial Cooperation Forum.

**Public-affairs and advocacy professionals**: The HERA Board (Member State representatives) is the key interlocutor for national-level positions on preparedness; the HSC is the political coordination forum. Stakeholder input on the Critical Medicines Act via Critical Medicines Alliance working groups remains the most direct lever for shaping HERA's operational priorities as the Act enters application. Track HERA Industry Days for strategic announcements.

**Research institutions**: EU4Health and Horizon Europe calls on medical countermeasures (vaccines, antivirals, rapid diagnostics) are HERA-relevant; the HERA Advisory Forum includes academic representation for scientific expertise; the Medical Countermeasures Strategy signals priority investment areas.

For supply-security legislation framing (procurement resilience, EU-preference, Strategic Projects), see guide `critical_medicines_act`. For the wider EU pharmaceutical regulatory landscape, see guide `eu_pharmaceutical_framework`. For the EU Health Security framework that provides HERA's statutory powers, see guide `eu_health_security_framework`.
