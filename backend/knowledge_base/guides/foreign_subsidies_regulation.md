# Foreign Subsidies Regulation (FSR)

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Regulation (EU) 2022/2560 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on foreign subsidies distorting the internal market
- CELEX: 32022R2560
- EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2560
- Adopted: 14 December 2022
- Entry into force: 12 January 2023
- Application date (substantive rules): 12 July 2023
- Application date (notification obligations): 12 October 2023
- Legal basis: Articles 114 and 207 TFEU (internal market + common commercial policy)
- Responsible DG: DG COMP (Directorate-General for Competition) + DG TRADE (for joint enforcement with third-country trade instruments)
- Responsible Commissioner: Teresa Ribera (EVP for Clean, Just and Competitive Transition + Competition)
- Lead EP committee: INTA (International Trade), opinion committees: IMCO, ECON
- Purpose: Address distortions caused by subsidies granted by non-EU governments to companies operating in the EU single market, closing regulatory gap where EU State aid rules do not apply
- Three enforcement tools: (a) M&A notification obligation, (b) public procurement notification obligation, (c) ex officio investigations
- M&A notification threshold: EU turnover of target >= EUR 500 million AND combined foreign financial contributions >= EUR 50 million in last 3 years
- Public procurement notification threshold: estimated contract value >= EUR 250 million AND bidder received foreign financial contributions >= EUR 4 million per non-EU country in last 3 years
- Implementing Regulation: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/1441 (CELEX 32023R1441) -- procedural rules for notifications and investigations
- Maximum review deadline M&A: 125 working days from notification (Phase 1: 25 days; Phase 2: extendable)
- Fines: up to 10% of aggregate worldwide turnover for procedural infringements
- Remedies toolkit: commitments (structural/behavioural), prohibition of concentration, prohibition of contract award, redressive measures including divestiture, access commitments, reduction of capacity, R&D investment requirements
- First conditional clearance under FSR (21 April 2026): **Lisbon high-speed railway line bid** (DG GROW press release IP/26/853) -- Commission cleared concentration subject to conditions related to commitments by third-country bidder
- Pending high-profile cases (confidential): several ex officio investigations in Chinese EV manufacturers, wind-turbine tenders, rail rolling stock, solar panels
- Cumulative notifications received (Commission data 2025): 200+ M&A notifications, 60+ public procurement notifications in first 2 years
- Clearance ratio: approximately 95% cleared without conditions; 3-4% conditional; 1-2% under in-depth Phase 2 review

## Why the FSR Exists

Prior to 2023, the EU State aid framework (Articles 107-109 TFEU) scrutinised subsidies granted by EU Member States but had no mechanism to address subsidies granted by non-EU governments. This created an asymmetry: EU companies faced strict State aid rules, while non-EU companies could benefit from foreign subsidies when investing, bidding or trading in the EU single market. Chinese SOEs, US Inflation Reduction Act beneficiaries, and Gulf sovereign wealth funds were of particular concern.

The FSR introduces a **parallel State aid-like regime** for foreign financial contributions, giving the Commission the power to investigate, impose remedies, or prohibit transactions if they distort the EU internal market.

## Three Enforcement Tools

### Tool 1: M&A Notification Obligation (Articles 19-29)

Mandatory ex ante notification to the Commission when:
- Acquisition target has EU turnover >= EUR 500 million, AND
- Parties received combined foreign financial contributions >= EUR 50 million in the last 3 years

Review timeline:
- Phase 1 (preliminary review): 25 working days
- Phase 2 (in-depth investigation): 90 working days (extendable)
- Standstill obligation: transaction cannot be closed until clearance

### Tool 2: Public Procurement Notification Obligation (Articles 27-33)

Mandatory ex ante notification when:
- Estimated contract value >= EUR 250 million, AND
- Bidder (or subcontractor holding >= 20% of the contract) received foreign financial contributions >= EUR 4 million per non-EU country in the last 3 years

Contracting authorities cannot award the contract until Commission clearance.

### Tool 3: Ex Officio Investigations (Articles 9-18)

Commission can investigate any market situation on its own initiative:
- Greenfield investments
- R&D subsidies
- Operating aid
- Tax advantages
- No ownership change required

Remedies include: commitments, divestiture, capacity reduction, R&D investment obligations, access commitments to infrastructure.

## Lisbon Railway Bid Case (21 April 2026)

**First published conditional clearance under the FSR** (DG GROW press release IP/26/853, 21 April 2026).

Key facts:
- Target: Portuguese high-speed railway line bid (Lisbon-Porto)
- Foreign financial contributions flagged: subsidies from non-EU State to the bidder's parent company
- Commission finding: foreign subsidies could have distorted the procurement by allowing an artificially low bid
- Conditions imposed (structural and behavioural): commitments on pricing, access to technology, subcontracting to EU firms, ring-fencing of subsidised operations
- Signal: Commission willing to clear subject to conditions rather than prohibit, but precedent sets tight scrutiny for railway/infrastructure contracts

This is the first publicly communicated conditional clearance of an FSR procurement case and will be cited as the reference methodology in future cases.

## Interaction With Other EU Instruments

| Instrument | Relationship with FSR |
|-----------|----------------------|
| EU State aid rules (Articles 107-109 TFEU) | Apply to EU Member State subsidies; FSR closes the non-EU gap |
| EU Merger Regulation (Reg 139/2004) | FSR applies in parallel to merger control; separate notifications |
| EU Public Procurement Directives (2014/24, 2014/25) | FSR adds foreign subsidies layer to procurement |
| International Procurement Instrument (IPI, Reg 2022/1031) | IPI addresses market access reciprocity; FSR addresses distortive subsidies |
| Anti-Subsidy Regulation (Reg 2016/1037) | Trade defence instrument for imports; FSR addresses internal market effects |

## Enforcement Trends

- Sectoral focus in ex officio actions: Chinese EVs, solar, wind, rail rolling stock, ports, critical raw materials, semiconductors
- Geographic focus: China (largest share), Gulf SWFs (UAE, Saudi, Qatar), Turkey, Russia sanctions-related
- Procedural approach: Commission increasingly asking bidders pre-notification whether foreign financial contributions thresholds met
- Phase 2 rate: approximately 5-8% of M&A notifications go to Phase 2
