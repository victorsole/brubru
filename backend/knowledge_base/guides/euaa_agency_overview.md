# EUAA: European Union Agency for Asylum (ex-EASO)

## QUICK FACTS
- Name: European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA)
- Seat: Valletta, Malta (MTC Block, Winemakers Wharf, Grand Harbour)
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2021/2303 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 December 2021 on the European Union Agency for Asylum
- History: transformed the European Asylum Support Office (EASO, established 2010) into the EUAA; the new mandate took effect 19 January 2022
- Governance: Executive Director (day-to-day management) + Management Board (one representative per Member State plus the Commission)
- Core functions: Country of Origin Information (COI, including the shared Medical Country of Origin Information network, MedCOI); Country Guidance notes; a training catalogue including the European Asylum Curriculum and a European Masters in asylum and migration; the Asylum Report and statistical/data analysis; operational and technical support deployed to Member States under migratory pressure; Dublin and relocation support; resettlement and complementary pathways support; monitoring of asylum procedures and reception conditions in Member States
- Oversight safeguard: Fundamental Rights Officer and an individual complaints mechanism for people affected by EUAA-supported operations
- Operational tool: standing pool of asylum-support experts (asylum support teams) that can be deployed at short notice
- Relationship to the 2024 Pact on Migration and Asylum: the EUAA's monitoring role over national asylum and reception systems is being scaled up as part of Pact implementation (phased rollout through 2026); verify current status against the EUAA website before citing specifics
- Predecessor note: EASO (2010-2021) had a support role only; the EUAA has a broader, harder-edged mandate including monitoring and standing capacity for deployment

The European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA) is the EU body that supports Member States in applying the Common European Asylum System (CEAS). It does not decide individual asylum claims; national authorities retain that power. Instead, the EUAA provides expertise, information, training, operational staff and, increasingly, monitoring of how Member States run their asylum and reception systems.

## From EASO to EUAA

The European Asylum Support Office (EASO) was set up in 2010 to help Member States cooperate on asylum practice, mainly through training and information exchange. In the aftermath of the 2015-2016 migration crisis, the Commission proposed strengthening the agency into a genuine asylum agency with operational capacity. Regulation (EU) 2021/2303 recast EASO as the European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA), with the new legal framework applying from 19 January 2022.

The upgrade gave the agency:
- A standing reserve of asylum support teams that can be deployed to a Member State facing disproportionate pressure, similar in concept to Frontex's rapid border intervention teams
- A stronger monitoring mandate over how Member States implement CEAS instruments in practice
- An enhanced knowledge and information role, consolidating Country of Origin Information and Country Guidance production
- A Fundamental Rights Officer and complaints mechanism, mirroring safeguards introduced for Frontex

## Core functions

### Country of Origin Information (COI) and Country Guidance
The EUAA produces and coordinates COI reports on countries and regions that generate asylum applications to the EU, drawing on Member State asylum authorities, international organisations and open sources. A dedicated network, MedCOI, focuses on medical treatment availability in countries of origin, feeding into cases involving health-based protection claims. Building on COI, the EUAA issues Country Guidance notes that give practical guidance to national caseworkers on how to assess claims from specific countries, aiming to reduce divergence in decision-making between Member States.

### Training
The EUAA maintains a training catalogue used by asylum caseworkers, interpreters and other practitioners across the EU. The European Asylum Curriculum is the agency's structured training programme, and the EUAA also supports a European Masters in asylum and migration, offered with partner universities, to build professional expertise in the field.

### Data, statistics and reporting
The Asylum Report is the EUAA's flagship annual publication, covering trends in applications, decisions, backlogs and reception across the EU. The agency also runs shorter-cycle data products (monthly and quarterly overviews) tracking application volumes and nationalities, used by Member States, EU institutions and researchers to anticipate pressure points.

### Operational and technical support
Where a Member State's asylum or reception system comes under significant pressure, the EUAA can deploy asylum support teams: caseworkers, interpreters, COI experts and reception specialists sent to reinforce national administrations. Deployments have supported Greece, Italy, Cyprus, Malta and Spain at various points, and the agency has also provided support connected to the large-scale displacement from Ukraine since 2022. Support is normally requested by the Member State or triggered by a Commission recommendation, and is distinct from the EUAA's monitoring role (see below).

### Dublin, relocation and resettlement support
The EUAA assists Member States with the practical operation of the Dublin rules on determining which state is responsible for an asylum claim, and supports intra-EU relocation of applicants and refugees where Member States agree to share responsibility. It also has a support role in resettlement and complementary pathways, helping Member States identify, process and welcome persons resettled from outside the EU.

### Monitoring
Under Regulation (EU) 2021/2303, the EUAA monitors the operational and technical application of CEAS instruments by Member States, including asylum procedures and reception conditions, and can propose measures where it identifies shortcomings. This monitoring function is being expanded as part of the 2024 Pact on Migration and Asylum, which links certain Pact obligations (such as the new border and asylum procedures) to EUAA-verified compliance. Confirm the latest state of play on the EUAA website, since the monitoring rollout is phased through 2026.

### Fundamental rights safeguards
The EUAA has a Fundamental Rights Officer, independent from operational management, and an individual complaints mechanism through which people affected by EUAA-supported activities (for example during a deployment) can raise concerns about fundamental rights breaches. This mirrors the equivalent safeguard structure at Frontex.

## Governance

- **Executive Director**: runs the agency day to day and is accountable to the Management Board
- **Management Board**: one member per Member State plus two Commission representatives (non-voting representatives from Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland also participate, reflecting those countries' association with the Dublin system), sets the agency's work programme and budget
- **Consultative Forum**: brings in civil society organisations, UNHCR and other stakeholders for structured dialogue with the agency

## How the EUAA fits with other EU migration bodies

- **eu-LISA** operates the large-scale IT systems (Eurodac, EES, VIS, ETIAS, SIS II) that Member States and the EUAA rely on for identification and data-matching; the EUAA is a *user* of Eurodac data, not its operator
- **Frontex** manages external border control and returns support; the EUAA manages asylum-system support. The two agencies cooperate on hotspots and joint deployments in frontline Member States
- **The Commission (DG HOME)** sets the CEAS legislative framework, including the 2024 Pact on Migration and Asylum; the EUAA implements and monitors the operational side of that framework
