# Directive on Administrative Cooperation in Tax (DAC1 to DAC9): Information Exchange and Reporting

## QUICK FACTS
- **Base act**: Council Directive 2011/16/EU on administrative cooperation in the field of taxation ("DAC"), adopted 15 February 2011, repealing the 1977 Mutual Assistance Directive. Legal basis **Article 115 TFEU** (approximation of laws affecting the internal market) -- Council unanimity, European Parliament consulted only (no co-decision).
- **Nine amendments to date (DAC1-DAC9)**, each adding a new automatic-exchange category or cooperation tool:
  | Amendment | Directive | Adopted | Subject | Applies from |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | DAC1 | 2011/16/EU | 15 Feb 2011 | Base framework: 5 categories (employment income, director fees, pensions, life insurance, immovable property) | 1 Jan 2013 |
  | DAC2 | (EU) 2014/107 | 9 Dec 2014 | Financial account information -- the EU's Common Reporting Standard (CRS) | 1 Jan 2016 |
  | DAC3 | (EU) 2015/2376 | 8 Dec 2015 | Cross-border tax rulings + advance pricing arrangements (APAs) | 1 Jan 2017 |
  | DAC4 | (EU) 2016/881 | 25 May 2016 | Country-by-country reporting (CbCR) for MNE groups, consolidated revenue >= EUR 750 million | FY2016, reported 2017 |
  | DAC5 | (EU) 2016/2258 | 6 Dec 2016 | Tax authority access to AML beneficial-ownership registers | 1 Jan 2018 |
  | DAC6 | (EU) 2018/822 | 25 May 2018 | Mandatory disclosure of reportable cross-border arrangements (MDR) by intermediaries, hallmarks A-E | 1 Jul 2020 (COVID-delayed reporting to 1 Jan 2021; retroactive to arrangements from 25 Jun 2018) |
  | DAC7 | (EU) 2021/514 | 22 Mar 2021 | Digital platform operator reporting (accommodation, transport, personal services, goods) + joint audits | 1 Jan 2023, first reports due 31 Jan 2024 |
  | DAC8 | (EU) 2023/2226 | 17 Oct 2023 | Crypto-asset reporting aligned with OECD CARF; extends to e-money tokens/CBDCs | 1 Jan 2026 (transposition by 31 Dec 2025), first reports due by 30 Sep 2027 |
  | DAC9 | (EU) 2025/872 | 14 Apr 2025 (OJ 6 May 2025, in force 7 May 2025) | Streamlines exchange of the Pillar Two GloBE Information Return (GIR/TTIR) between Member States | Applies to FY2024, transposition by 31 Dec 2025, first reporting by 30 Jun 2026 |
- **Lead DG**: DG TAXUD (Taxation and Customs Union). **Council configuration**: ECOFIN.
- **Touches every digital platform and crypto exchange operating in the EU**: Airbnb, Booking.com, Vinted, Bolt, Uber, Deliveroo (DAC7); every CASP authorised or registered under MiCA (DAC8).
- **Complementary AEOI infrastructure**: CESOP (Central Electronic System of Payment information, Directive (EU) 2020/284 + Regulation (EU) 2020/283, applicable from 1 January 2024) collects cross-border payment data from payment service providers to fight VAT fraud, feeding the same anti-fraud logic as DAC but running as a separate legal instrument.
- **Verify live**: DAC8's exact first-reporting window and DAC9's national transposition status vary by Member State tracker; check the Commission DAC page before citing a specific country's deadline.

Directive 2011/16/EU is the EU's backbone for automatic exchange of tax information between the 27 national tax administrations. It began as a modest five-category exchange regime in 2011 and has been amended nine times to keep pace with the OECD's global tax-transparency agenda (CRS, BEPS Action 12/13, CARF) and with new economic activity that national tax authorities could not otherwise see: gig-economy platform income, crypto-asset transactions, and now Pillar Two top-up tax data. Each amendment keeps the "DAC" shorthand plus a number, so practitioners refer to "DAC6 arrangements" or "DAC7 platforms" as shorthand for the specific reporting obligation, even though all nine amendments sit inside the same consolidated directive.

## Why DAC Matters Now

Three amendments dominate current compliance workloads:

**DAC6** (mandatory disclosure of cross-border tax arrangements) requires intermediaries (tax advisers, lawyers, accountants, banks) -- or the taxpayer if the intermediary is bound by legal professional privilege -- to report "reportable cross-border arrangements" that meet one of five hallmark categories (A: generic hallmarks tied to a main-benefit test; B: specific hallmarks tied to a main-benefit test; C: specific hallmarks on cross-border payments; D: hallmarks on automatic exchange of information/beneficial ownership circumvention; E: transfer-pricing hallmarks). Non-compliance penalties are set nationally and vary sharply (from a few thousand euro up to over EUR 800,000 in some Member States).

**DAC7** shifts the reporting burden onto digital platform operators rather than individual sellers. A "reportable seller" is any user (EU or non-EU resident) who receives consideration through the platform for: immovable property rental (including short-term/holiday lets), personal services, sale of goods, or vehicle rental. Platforms above a de minimis threshold (fewer than 30 goods-sales transactions and EUR 2,000 in a year are exempt for goods sellers) must collect seller identity, tax residence, and financial-account data and report it annually to one Member State tax authority, which then exchanges it with all relevant Member States. This directly targets marketplaces such as Airbnb, Booking.com, Vinted, Bolt, Uber and Deliveroo, and interacts with the Short-Term Rentals Regulation's own registration-data pipeline.

**DAC8** extends the same logic to crypto-asset service providers (CASPs). It transposes the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) into EU law and requires any CASP authorised or registered under MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) to collect and report EU-resident users' crypto-asset transactions, mirroring the CRS approach used for traditional financial accounts under DAC2. DAC8 also extends CRS-style reporting to e-money and central bank digital currencies. Member States must transpose by 31 December 2025; the directive applies from 1 January 2026, and the first reporting window (for CY2026 data) runs from 1 January to 30 September 2027.

**DAC9** is the newest and narrowest amendment: it does not create a new reporting population but streamlines how Member States exchange the GloBE Information Return (GIR, also called the Top-up Tax Information Return or TTIR) that in-scope multinational and large domestic groups must file under the Pillar Two Minimum Taxation Directive (Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523). DAC9 adopts a "dissemination approach": the Member State that receives the centrally filed GIR forwards the relevant sections to every other Member State with a taxing right over the group (the ultimate parent's jurisdiction gets the full return; jurisdictions with income-inclusion or under-taxed-profits rights get only the relevant jurisdictional sections). DAC9 applies to fiscal year 2024 data, with national transposition due by 31 December 2025 and first reporting due by 30 June 2026 -- six months ahead of most groups' first Top-up Tax Information Return filing deadline.

## DAC1-DAC5: The Foundational Layer

- **DAC1** (2011/16/EU): established mandatory automatic exchange (AEOI), exchange on request (EOIR) and spontaneous exchange (SEOI) as the three cooperation channels, plus five automatic-exchange categories: employment income, director fees, pension income, life insurance products, and ownership/income from immovable property.
- **DAC2** ((EU) 2014/107): the EU's implementation of the OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS) -- financial institutions report account holder balances and income to their home tax authority, exchanged automatically across the EU (and, via bilateral CRS agreements, globally).
- **DAC3** ((EU) 2015/2376): automatic exchange of cross-border advance tax rulings and advance pricing arrangements, a direct response to the LuxLeaks scandal.
- **DAC4** ((EU) 2016/881): country-by-country reporting (CbCR) for MNE groups with EUR 750 million+ consolidated revenue, aligned with OECD BEPS Action 13. Feeds into (but is legally distinct from) the separate **public** CbCR Directive (EU) 2021/2101, effective from June 2024.
- **DAC5** ((EU) 2016/2258): grants tax authorities access to the beneficial-ownership registers created under the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives.

## Enforcement, Penalties and Interaction with Other Files

DAC does not set EU-wide penalty amounts -- Member States transpose their own sanctions regimes for non-compliance, which creates significant divergence, especially for DAC6 (some Member States' penalties exceed EUR 800,000 for repeated non-disclosure) and DAC7 (platform operators can face de-registration/exclusion from a Member State's market for persistent non-compliance). Practitioners should always check the transposing national law, not the directive text, for the actual penalty exposure.

DAC sits alongside several adjacent EU tax-transparency files: **CESOP** (payment-data reporting from PSPs, applicable since 1 January 2024, aimed at VAT fraud rather than direct tax); the **Pillar Two Minimum Taxation Directive** (Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523), whose GIR/TTIR filings DAC9 is built to exchange; **ATAD** (Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, general anti-abuse and CFC rules) and the pending **Unshell/ATAD III** proposal on shell entities; **MiCA** (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114), which supplies the CASP authorisation/registration population that DAC8 draws its reporting obligation from; and **VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA)**, adopted by Council 11 March 2025, which introduces its own digital reporting requirements (DRR) for VAT on a separate track from DAC.

## Status

DAC6, DAC7 and CESOP are fully live and generating annual reporting cycles. DAC8 transposition is due across all 27 Member States by 31 December 2025 ahead of its 1 January 2026 application date; DAC9 transposition is due on the same 31 December 2025 date, with first Pillar Two information exchanges due by 30 June 2026. Watch DG TAXUD's DAC page and national transposition trackers for Member State-level implementation gaps, and expect a DAC10 discussion once the OECD finalises further CARF/CRS amendments or a Pillar One outcome requires its own exchange mechanism.
