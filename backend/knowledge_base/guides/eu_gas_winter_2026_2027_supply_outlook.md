# EU gas security of supply for winter 2026-2027: Commission and ENTSOG assessment (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Commission assessment, 8 October 2026 (DG Energy):** the **Gas Coordination Group** met and welcomed the presentation by the European Network of Transmission System Operators for Gas (**ENTSOG**) of its **2026-2027 Winter Supply Outlook**, published the same day. Message: gas storage is **below historical levels**, but the report confirms that the **EU gas system is sufficiently flexible to meet demand this winter**. Infrastructure can handle higher liquefied natural gas (LNG) imports and compensate for lower storage.
- **ENTSOG's caution:** keep adequate storage levels through the season to cope with late-winter cold spells and ease refilling next summer. LNG volumes depend mainly on winter temperatures; the decision to import LNG or withdraw from storage ultimately lies with the market.
- **Not 2021-2022 again, says the Commission:** the EU is better prepared thanks to more diversified supply, higher LNG import capacity and lower gas demand.
- **Russian LNG:** the Commission presented the implementation of the **REPowerEU Gas Regulation**, focusing on preparing for the **end of Russian long-term contracts for LNG imports from the start of next year (2027)**.
- **Earlier in 2026:** on **3 September 2026** the Gas Coordination Group saw no immediate risk to security of supply and the Commission saw no reason to intervene; it noted that the Middle East situation was unstable and that **Qatari LNG production was still shut down**. In **April 2026** ENTSOG's Summer Outlook showed storage could be refilled to at least **80% by 1 November** depending on LNG availability, and the Commission recalled Commissioner Jørgensen's letter inviting Member States to use the flexibility in the **Gas Storage Regulation** and consider lowering their filling target to **80%**.
- **Legal basis for the outlooks:** ENTSOG's annual Winter and Summer Supply Outlooks are required by Article 26(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1789 on the internal markets for renewable gas, natural gas and hydrogen (CELEX 32024R1789).
- **Press commentary is not the Commission's view.** Some press headlines on 9 October speak of a possible supply shortage; the Commission's own assessment above says the system is prepared. Report both as different sources, never as the Commission's position.
- **Political track:** the invitation letter for the European Council of 15 and 16 October 2026 asks leaders to guide the Commission on "targeted and temporary measures" to ease the impact of high energy prices (see `european_council_15_16_october_2026`).
