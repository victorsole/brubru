# EUIPO: European Union Intellectual Property Office

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: The **European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO)**, headquartered in **Alicante, Spain**. Self-financed EU decentralised agency, funded from applicant fees, not the general EU budget.
- **History and name**: Founded **1994** as the **Office for Harmonization in the Internal Market (OHIM)** under Council Reg (EC) No 40/94. Renamed **EUIPO** on **23 March 2016** (Reg (EU) 2015/2424, trade mark reform package). "OHIM" still appears in older documents and case law: same office, earlier name.
- **What it administers**: (1) the **EU trade mark (EUTM)** under the **EUTMR, Regulation (EU) 2017/1001** (CELEX 32017R1001); (2) **registered/unregistered EU designs** (renamed from "Community designs" by the **2024 design reform**, Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 + Directive (EU) 2024/2823, both 23 October 2024, Regulation applicable from 1 May 2025); (3) the **craft and industrial geographical indications (GI) register**, delegated under **Regulation (EU) 2024/1143** (applicable to craft/industrial products from 1 December 2025; see `gi_craft_industrial_2024_1143.md`). The Commission (DG AGRI) keeps exclusive competence over the agri-food/wine/spirit GI families in that same Regulation; EUIPO's role there is technical/consultative only.
- **Executive Director**: **João Negrão** (Portugal), in office since **1 October 2023**, five-year term.
- **Governance**: a **Management Board** and a **Budget Committee**, each with one representative per Member State plus two Commission representatives and one European Parliament representative. The Council of the EU appoints the Executive Director, Deputy Executive Director, and the President and Chairpersons of the Boards of Appeal.
- **Boards of Appeal**: **five Boards** plus the **Grand Board** (nine members, EUIPO's highest decision-making body for legally difficult or precedent-setting appeals). Further appeal runs to the General Court and Court of Justice of the EU.
- **Mediation Centre**: runs Alternative Dispute Resolution for trade mark and design disputes, under the Boards of Appeal.
- **Observatory + enforcement**: hosts the **European Observatory on Infringements of Intellectual Property Rights**; runs the **IP Enforcement Portal (IPEP)**, the **eSearch Case Law** database, and the free search tools **TMview** and **DesignView**.
- **SME Fund**: EUIPO delivers the **"Ideas Powered for Business" SME Fund** (fee-reimbursement vouchers, co-funded with the Commission): see `euipo_sme_fund_ideas_powered_for_business.md`. Not to be confused with EIC innovation/scale-up funding.
- **Unitary Supplementary Protection Certificate (SPC), status check**: the Commission has proposed making EUIPO the future competent office for a new unitary SPC. **NOT yet adopted law**: as of mid-2026 still in interinstitutional trilogue, no confirmed adoption date or final regulation numbers. Treat as a pending proposal, not an operative EUIPO competence.
- **Not the Commission, not the EPO**: EUIPO is a separate EU agency from the Commission (which sets overall IP policy) and from the **European Patent Office (EPO)**, a non-EU intergovernmental body that examines patents. EUIPO does not grant patents.

## What EUIPO Does

EUIPO is the EU's registration office for two of the three classic industrial property rights that can be protected EU-wide in a single procedure: trade marks and designs. It does not handle patents (that sits with national patent offices, the EPO, and, once operational, the Unified Patent Court and Unitary Patent system) or copyright (which is not registered at EU level at all).

**EU trade marks (EUTM)**: a single application, filed directly with EUIPO or via a national IP office, gives unitary protection across all EU Member States. Governed by the EUTMR (Regulation (EU) 2017/1001) plus the EU Trade Mark Implementing Regulation. EUIPO examines absolute grounds for refusal (distinctiveness, descriptiveness, public policy), publishes applications for a three-month opposition window, and registers marks that clear examination and opposition. For the substantive rules on what can be registered, opposition grounds and enforcement of an EUTM, see `eu_trade_mark_system_eutmr.md`.

**EU designs (formerly Community designs)**: registered EU designs protect the visual appearance of a product (shape, contours, colours, texture, materials, ornamentation) for up to 25 years (five-year renewable terms). Unregistered EU designs give automatic, shorter protection (three years from first disclosure) without registration, useful for fast-moving sectors such as fashion. The 2024 reform (Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 + Directive (EU) 2024/2823) modernised the definitions of "design" and "product" to explicitly cover digital and non-physical designs, movements/transitions/animations, virtual goods and graphic user interfaces, alongside a phased repair-clause liberalisation for spare parts.

**Craft and industrial GIs**: since the reform under Regulation (EU) 2024/1143, EUIPO operates the EU-wide registration procedure for geographical indications protecting craft and industrial products (for example ceramics, glassware, textiles, cutlery, stoneware) whose qualities or reputation are essentially attributable to their place of production. Procedure: Member State assessment first, then EUIPO scrutiny, publication in the Official Journal (OJ-C series), a three-month opposition window, and registration with EU-wide effect. See `gi_craft_industrial_2024_1143.md` for the full regime, including the parallel agri-food/wine/spirit GI pillars that remain under Commission (DG AGRI) management.

## Boards of Appeal and Litigation Route

A party dissatisfied with a first-instance EUIPO decision (examination refusal, opposition decision, cancellation/invalidity decision, or a craft/industrial GI decision) can appeal to one of the **five Boards of Appeal**. Legally difficult, precedent-setting or internally inconsistent cases can be referred up to the **Grand Board**, EUIPO's highest internal decision-making body. Beyond EUIPO, further appeal lies to the **General Court** of the Court of Justice of the European Union, and ultimately to the **Court of Justice** on points of law. The Boards of Appeal Rules of Procedure are revised on a biennial cycle; the current amended rules (entering into force 1 July 2026) add provisions specific to appeals in craft/industrial GI cases.

## Funding and Fee Structure

EUIPO is financially self-sufficient, covering its operating costs from the fees paid by applicants for trade marks, designs and (from December 2025) craft/industrial GIs. This structure means EUIPO does not draw on the general EU budget for its core registration activity, though it co-finances the SME Fund jointly with the European Commission and receives Commission oversight on broader EU IP policy.

## How This Differs From the European Commission

For Brubru users, the operative distinction: the **European Commission** (principally DG GROW for trade marks/designs/craft-industrial GIs, DG AGRI for agri-food/wine/spirit GIs) sets the overarching EU intellectual property policy framework (the EU IP Action Plan) and retains exclusive competence over the traditional agri-food GI system. **EUIPO** is the operational registration office: it examines, publishes, and registers EU trade marks and EU designs outright, and runs the craft/industrial GI register as a delegated task. EUIPO is not a Commission department; it is a separate, fee-funded EU agency based in Alicante with its own Executive Director, Management Board and Boards of Appeal.
