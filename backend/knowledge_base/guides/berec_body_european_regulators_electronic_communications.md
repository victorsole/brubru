# BEREC: Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications

## QUICK FACTS

- **Full name**: Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications (BEREC)
- **Legal basis**: Regulation (EU) 2018/1971 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 December 2018 establishing BEREC and the Agency for Support for BEREC (BEREC Office), amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120 and repealing Regulation (EC) No 1211/2009
- **CELEX**: 32018R1971 | **OJ**: L 321, 17.12.2018, p. 1-35 (same OJ issue as the European Electronic Communications Code, adopted the same day)
- **Legal base**: Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation)
- **Predecessor**: 2009 Regulation (EC) No 1211/2009, which in turn replaced the informal European Regulators Group (ERG); BEREC began operating January 2010
- **Two distinct components**: (1) **BEREC** itself, i.e. the Board of Regulators, a network body with no separate legal personality; (2) the **BEREC Office**, a fully-fledged EU decentralised agency that provides professional and administrative support
- **BEREC Office seat**: Riga, Latvia (Zigfrīda Annas Meierovica bulvāris 14, 2nd floor, LV-1050 Riga), decided 31 May 2010
- **Board of Regulators**: one representative per Member State's national regulatory authority (NRA), each with one vote; 27 voting members (one per EU Member State). The European Commission attends as a non-voting observer. EEA/EFTA and candidate-country NRAs may take part without voting rights under working arrangements.
- **Chair**: elected by the Board of Regulators for a one-year term, renewable once. 2026 Chair: Marko Mišmaš; incoming 2027 Chair: Alejandra de Iturriaga Gandini.
- **Voting threshold**: simple majority for routine decisions; two-thirds majority required to elect the Chair, adopt rules of procedure, and adopt certain guidelines
- **Working Groups**: organise BEREC's technical work by topic: e.g. Cybersecurity and Resilience, Digital Markets, Net Neutrality, Regulatory Accounting, Spectrum, End-User/Consumer Protection
- **Outputs**: mostly **guidelines, opinions, reports and common positions** (soft law that NRAs and the Commission must "take utmost account of" and justify any departure from); a narrow set of procedures under the EECC and the Roaming Regulation give BEREC opinions near-binding practical weight (e.g. cross-border SMP-remedy consistency mechanism, wholesale roaming dispute opinions, input to the single EU-wide voice-termination-rate methodology)
- **Implements**: European Electronic Communications Code (EECC, Directive (EU) 2018/1972); Open Internet/net-neutrality Regulation (EU) 2015/2120 (as amended); Roaming Regulation (EU) 2022/612, "Roam Like at Home" recast, applicable 1 July 2022 to 30 June 2032; intra-EU communications price caps (maximum EUR 0.19/minute for calls, EUR 0.06/SMS)
- **Cross-regulatory role**: founding member of the Digital Markets Act's Article 40 High-Level Group of Digital Regulators, alongside the European Data Protection Board/Supervisor, the European Competition Network (ECN), the Consumer Protection Cooperation Network (CPC) and the European Regulatory Group of Audiovisual Media Regulators (ERGA): 30 members total, six per network: advising the Commission on consistent DMA enforcement
- **Security cooperation**: works with ENISA and the NIS Cooperation Group on network resilience through its Cybersecurity and Resilience Working Group; contributed to the 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox; EECC Article 40-41 annual security-incident summary reports flow to the Commission and ENISA
- **Pending change**: the proposed Digital Networks Act (COM(2026) 16 final, procedure 2026/0013(COD), still in EP committee as of July 2026) would **repeal Regulation (EU) 2018/1971** and rename the BEREC Office as the **Office for Digital Networks (ODN)**, broadening its mandate to cover network resilience, spectrum and satellite matters. Not yet adopted.

## Context

BEREC exists because EU telecoms regulation is implemented by 27 national regulatory authorities (NRAs), not by the Commission directly. Divergent national application of the same EU rules on spectrum, access, market analysis and consumer rights would fragment the single telecoms market. BEREC's job is to keep that application consistent: issuing guidelines, delivering opinions, and giving NRAs a forum to coordinate before they act nationally.

The body traces back to the informal European Regulators Group (ERG, 2002), replaced by the first BEREC Regulation (EC) No 1211/2009 as part of the 2009 "Better Regulation" telecoms package. That Regulation was itself repealed and recast by Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, adopted on the same day as the European Electronic Communications Code (EECC) and published in the same Official Journal issue. The recast strengthened BEREC's independence guarantees, formalised the BEREC Office as a genuine EU agency (rather than a lightly-staffed secretariat), and gave BEREC substantial new tasks under the EECC: geographical survey guidelines, symmetric-access guidelines, security incident reporting, emergency-communications databases, and much more.

## Structure: BEREC vs the BEREC Office

These are legally two different bodies sharing one brand.

**BEREC (the Board of Regulators)**
- The decision-making network itself: heads or high-level representatives of the 27 NRAs, each casting one vote.
- Has no separate legal personality of its own: it is a regulatory network, not an agency.
- Meets several times a year in plenary; most substantive work happens in Working Groups and is then adopted by the Board.
- The Commission participates as an observer without voting rights and "may neither seek nor take instructions" that would compromise NRA independence: the reverse also holds: BEREC members act independently of national governments too.

**BEREC Office**
- A fully-fledged EU decentralised agency (the "Agency for Support for BEREC"), with its own legal personality, budget, Director and Management Board.
- Seated in Riga, Latvia since 2010.
- Provides professional, administrative and logistical support to the Board of Regulators and its Working Groups: drafting support, secretariat, IT (databases for numbering, general authorisation and emergency services), communications, and programming documents (annual/multiannual work programmes, budget).
- The Management Board oversees the Office's administration; a Director runs day-to-day operations.

## Functions and Instruments

BEREC's main statutory tasks (Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Article 4, and the EECC) fall into four broad categories:

1. **Consistency mechanism**: reviewing NRA draft measures on market definition/SMP designation and remedies under the EECC's Article 32-33 procedure, and delivering an opinion when the Commission raises "serious doubts": NRAs must take the "utmost account" of it.
2. **Guidelines**: BEREC issues guidelines on subjects the EECC specifically delegates to it, for example symmetric access to in-building wiring (Article 61(3)), geographical broadband surveys (Article 22), public-warning system equivalence assessment (Article 110), and net-neutrality/open-internet implementation.
3. **Reports and databases**: annual reports, thematic reports (e.g. its 2026 series of thematic papers on the Digital Networks Act proposal), and operational databases such as the numbering/authorisation registers and the E.164 database of Member State emergency-service numbers.
4. **Opinions on cross-border disputes**: under the Roaming Regulation and the EECC, BEREC delivers opinions in cross-border disputes between operators or between an operator and an NRA in a different Member State; the deciding NRA must take utmost account of the opinion.

None of these outputs are regulations or decisions in the TFEU Article 288 sense: they remain soft law. What makes them consequential is the "utmost account" duty combined with peer pressure inside the Board of Regulators: an NRA that departs from a BEREC guideline without strong justification faces reputational and legal-challenge risk.

## Cross-body cooperation

- **Digital Markets Act (DMA) High-Level Group**: established under DMA Article 40 to advise the Commission on consistent enforcement across overlapping regimes. Composed of five European networks/bodies: BEREC, the European Data Protection Board/Supervisor, the European Competition Network, the Consumer Protection Cooperation Network, and the European Regulatory Group of Audiovisual Media Regulators: with six representatives each (30 total). The Group has produced joint work on AI-related gatekeeper obligations.
- **ENISA / NIS Cooperation Group**: BEREC's Cybersecurity and Resilience Working Group cooperates with ENISA on network and 5G security, including the EU 5G Cybersecurity Toolbox and follow-up to the "Nevers Call" on submarine cable and network resilience.
- **European Commission**: attends all Board of Regulators meetings as a non-voting observer; the Commission can request BEREC opinions and must take utmost account of them in specific procedures (e.g. voice-termination rate methodology, transnational market definition).

## Public consultations

Like the Commission, BEREC runs public consultations before adopting major guidelines and its multiannual Strategy, typically via its consultation platform. Stakeholders (operators, consumer groups, civil society) can respond within the published window; responses are published unless confidentiality is requested.
