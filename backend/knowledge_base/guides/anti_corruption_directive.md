## Overview

The Anti-Corruption Directive is a landmark legislative proposal to strengthen the EU's anti-corruption framework. It replaces fragmented instruments (the 2003 Framework Decision on private sector corruption and the 1997 Convention on corruption involving EU/Member State officials) with a single comprehensive directive.

## Key Provisions

### Criminal Offences Harmonised
The directive establishes minimum rules on:
- Bribery in the public and private sector (active and passive)
- Misappropriation of funds
- Trading in influence
- Abuse of functions
- Obstruction of justice
- Illicit enrichment (where compatible with national constitutional traditions)

### Penalties
- Minimum maximum penalties for natural persons (imprisonment)
- Corporate liability for legal persons (fines, exclusion from public procurement, judicial winding-up)
- Aggravating circumstances (organised crime involvement, high-ranking officials, large amounts)

### Prevention Measures
- Anti-corruption authorities with adequate independence and resources
- Asset declarations for public officials
- Whistleblower protection (complementing Directive (EU) 2019/1937)
- Lobbying transparency rules
- Cooling-off periods for former officials

### EU Dimension
- Amends the PIF Directive (2017/1371) to align corruption offences affecting EU financial interests
- Strengthens OLAF and EPPO cooperation
- Corruption as an EU-level priority crime (Article 83(1) TFEU)
