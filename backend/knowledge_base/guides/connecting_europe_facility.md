# Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) 2021-2027: Transport, Energy, Digital

## QUICK FACTS

- **Full name**: Regulation (EU) 2021/1153 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 7 July 2021 establishing the Connecting Europe Facility and repealing Regulations (EU) No 1316/2013 and (EU) No 283/2014
- **CELEX**: 32021R1153
- **EUR-Lex**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32021R1153
- **OJ reference**: OJ L 249, 14.7.2021, pp. 38-81
- **Period**: 2021-2027 (applied retroactively from 1 January 2021)
- **Total budget**: **EUR 33.71 billion** across three pillars (Article 4(2))
- **Climate contribution target**: 60% of the overall financial envelope must contribute to climate objectives
- **Three pillars and their budgets**:
  - **CEF Transport: EUR 25.807 billion**: General Envelope EUR 12.830bn + Cohesion Envelope EUR 11.286bn (transferred from the Cohesion Fund) + Military Mobility EUR 1.691bn. Covered in full detail in `cef_transport_funding.md`, this master guide only summarises Transport and cross-links out.
  - **CEF Energy: EUR 5.838 billion**: cross-border energy infrastructure, with 15% of the envelope reserved for cross-border renewable energy projects (the Renewable Energy Financing Mechanism, REFM).
  - **CEF Digital: EUR 2.065 billion**: digital connectivity infrastructure of common European interest (5G corridors, submarine cables, backbone networks).
- **Implementing bodies**: **CINEA** (Climate, Infrastructure and Environment Executive Agency) runs CEF Transport and CEF Energy calls, evaluation, and grant management; **HADEA** (Health and Digital Executive Agency) runs CEF Digital.
- **Lead DGs**: DG MOVE (Transport policy), DG ENER (Energy policy), DG CNECT (Digital policy): the DGs set policy, while CINEA/HADEA administer the money.
- **CEF Energy source URL**: https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/programmes/connecting-europe-facility/energy-infrastructure-connecting-europe-facility-0_en
- **CEF Digital source URL**: https://hadea.ec.europa.eu/programmes/connecting-europe-facility_en
- **Master programme URL**: https://cinea.ec.europa.eu/programmes/connecting-europe-facility_en
- **Successor**: CEF is folded into consolidated MFF 2028-2034 planning (Commission proposal COM(2025) 570, 16 July 2025 restructures EU funding into fewer, larger envelopes, including a European Competitiveness Fund). Confirm the CEF-specific successor once sectoral MFF 2028-2034 regulations are tabled.

This guide is the **master CEF guide** covering the whole regulation and all three sectors. For deep detail on Transport (corridors, ERTMS, co-financing rates, Military Mobility envelope, Annex I pre-identified projects) see `cef_transport_funding.md`.

## What CEF is: the three-pillar structure

CEF is the EU's flagship infrastructure-investment instrument, funding cross-border and missing-link projects in transport, energy, and digital networks that the market alone will not deliver, because benefits spill over borders while a single Member State bears the cost. The current CEF ("CEF II") runs 2021-2027 under Regulation (EU) 2021/1153, which repealed and replaced the original 2014-2020 CEF Regulation (EU) No 1316/2013 and the Digital Single Market financing Regulation (EU) No 283/2014.

All three pillars share the same regulation, the same core eligibility logic (projects of common interest, cross-border relevance, co-financing rather than 100% grants in most cases), and the same audit/control framework, but they are administered by different agencies and driven by different sectoral DGs.

## CEF Transport (summary, see `cef_transport_funding.md` for full detail)

- **EUR 25.807 billion**, split into General Envelope (EUR 12.830bn, all Member States), Cohesion Envelope (EUR 11.286bn, lower-GDP Member States, co-financing up to 85%), and Military Mobility (EUR 1.691bn, dual-use civil-defence infrastructure).
- Funds TEN-T core and comprehensive network works, cross-border rail/road/inland waterway/port/airport links, ERTMS signalling deployment, and alternative fuels infrastructure.
- Administered by **CINEA**, policy-steered by **DG MOVE**.
- See `cef_transport_funding.md`, `ten_t_regulation_2024_1679.md`, `ertms_deployment_2030.md`, and `military_mobility_dual_use_logistics.md` for full detail.

## CEF Energy

- **EUR 5.838 billion** for cross-border energy infrastructure that advances the Energy Union and supports the European Green Deal's decarbonisation goals.
- **Two main funding routes**:
  - **Projects of Common Interest (PCIs)**: cross-border energy infrastructure projects between two or more EU Member States, identified on a biennial Union list under the TEN-E Regulation (EU) 2022/869. PCIs can receive CEF grants for studies and, in limited cases, works.
  - **Projects of Mutual Interest (PMIs)**: the equivalent category for infrastructure connecting an EU Member State with a third country (e.g. Energy Community contracting parties, Western Balkans, Eastern Partnership countries), reflecting the external dimension of EU energy security policy.
- **Renewable Energy Financing Mechanism (REFM)**: at least 15% of the CEF Energy envelope is reserved for cross-border renewable energy projects. REFM allows Member States to pool statistical transfers and co-finance renewable generation capacity (mainly offshore wind and solar) built in another Member State, counting the output toward the funding Member State's national renewable target. REFM was established by Regulation (EU) 2020/1294 and is separate in legal basis from the main CEF Regulation, but CINEA administers both under the same CEF Energy call architecture.
- **Priority corridors and thematic areas**: hydrogen infrastructure and hydrogen corridors (a growing share of PCI/PMI calls since the 2022 TEN-E recast extended eligibility to hydrogen and smart electricity grids), offshore grids (hybrid interconnectors linking offshore wind farms to multiple national grids, central to the North Seas Energy Cooperation and Baltic Energy Market Interconnection Plan), cross-border electricity interconnectors, smart grids, and CO2 transport infrastructure.
- **Governance**: TEN-E Regulation (EU) 2022/869 is the policy framework (defines PCI/PMI categories, corridors, and the biennial project list); CEF Regulation 2021/1153 is the funding instrument. Same relationship as TEN-T-to-CEF-Transport.
- **Administered by CINEA**, policy-steered by **DG ENER**.
- Cross-link: `energy_grids_package.md`, `eu_energy_policy.md`.

## CEF Digital

- **EUR 2.065 billion** for digital connectivity infrastructure of common European interest, smaller than Transport or Energy, but strategically significant for 5G rollout, submarine cable resilience, and reducing EU dependence on non-EU backbone providers.
- **What it funds**:
  - **5G corridors**: continuous 5G coverage along major transport paths (road and rail TEN-T corridors), enabling connected and automated mobility.
  - **5G for Smart Communities**: local/regional 5G deployment for public-interest use cases (smart agriculture, e-health, public safety) in areas the market would not otherwise serve.
  - **Submarine and terrestrial backbone cables**: deployment, upgrade, and emergency repair capacity for submarine cable systems connecting Member States and linking the EU to third countries and territories; this has become a higher political priority after 2023-2024 Baltic Sea cable-damage incidents raised EU concern about critical undersea infrastructure resilience.
  - **Backbone networks connecting to third countries**: very-high-capacity network links extending EU digital connectivity toward neighbourhood and partner regions (part of the EU's Global Gateway digital connectivity strand).
  - **Operational digital platforms**: cross-border digital service infrastructure supporting public administrations and interoperability (complementing, not duplicating, the Digital Europe Programme's cloud/AI/cybersecurity capacity-building remit).
- **Administered by HADEA**, policy-steered by **DG CNECT**.
- CEF Digital is deliberately narrower than the wider EU digital connectivity agenda: the **Digital Europe Programme** (also HADEA-administered, guide planned as `digital_europe_programme.md`) funds AI, cybersecurity, cloud/data, and digital-skills capacity, while CEF Digital funds physical connectivity infrastructure. The **Digital Networks Act** proposal addresses the regulatory framework for telecoms infrastructure investment, distinct from CEF Digital's grant funding.
- Cross-link: `digital_networks_act.md`.

## Implementing bodies: CINEA and HADEA

| Agency | Full name | CEF pillars managed | Other programmes managed | Serves which DGs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CINEA** | Climate, Infrastructure and Environment Executive Agency | CEF Transport, CEF Energy | LIFE, Horizon Europe Cluster 5 (Climate, Energy, Mobility), Innovation Fund, EMFAF, Just Transition Mechanism | DG MOVE, DG ENER, DG CLIMA, DG ENV, DG MARE |
| **HADEA** | Health and Digital Executive Agency | CEF Digital | EU4Health, Single Market Programme (Food strand), Horizon Europe Clusters 1 (Health) and 4 (Digital, Industry, Space), Digital Europe Programme | DG SANTE, DG CNECT, DG GROW, DG RTD |

Neither agency is a DG. CINEA and HADEA run calls, evaluate proposals, sign and manage grant agreements, and monitor delivery; the sectoral DGs (MOVE, ENER, CNECT) set the underlying policy and priorities that shape each Multi-Annual Work Programme. See `eu_commission_executive_agencies.md` for the full six-agency map.

## Synergy calls across pillars

CEF explicitly supports **synergy (cross-sectoral) calls** combining two or more pillars in a single project, for example rail electrification bundled with alternative fuels infrastructure (Transport + Energy), or 5G corridor deployment bundled with smart mobility digitalisation (Transport + Digital). Synergy calls typically carry a co-financing bonus (up to 10 percentage points above the standard rate for the lead sector) to incentivise cross-pillar bundling. CINEA and HADEA coordinate jointly on synergy call design and evaluation panels where a project spans both agencies' remits.
