# EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directives (ATAD I and ATAD II)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (24 June 2026)**: the European Commission adopted a **Tax Simplification Package** comprising a **direct taxation Omnibus** and a **recast of the Directive on Administrative Cooperation (DAC)**. The direct taxation Omnibus **modernises ATAD's interest limitation rule** (removes implementation options Member States previously chose between, raises the de minimis threshold, excludes low-risk third-party borrowing and market-based financing from the limitation scope) and **streamlines the CFC rules' interaction with Pillar Two** (removes overlapping requirements, introduces a more harmonised CFC model). This is a proposal, not yet adopted law; it still needs Council unanimity and European Parliament consultation before entry into force. Verify progress before citing as settled. Source: Commission Taxation and Customs Union news item of 24 June 2026.
- **ATAD I**: Council Directive (EU) **2016/1164** of 12 July 2016, laying down rules against tax avoidance practices that directly affect the functioning of the internal market. CELEX **32016L1164**. Member States transposed by **31 December 2018**; rules applied from **1 January 2019**, except the exit taxation rule, applicable from **1 January 2020**.
- **ATAD II**: Council Directive (EU) **2017/952** of 29 May 2017, amending ATAD I as regards **hybrid mismatches with third countries**. CELEX **32017L0952**. Transposed by 31 December 2019, applied from **1 January 2020** for general hybrid mismatch rules (both intra-EU and EU/third-country mismatches); the **reverse hybrid entity rule** had a delayed transposition deadline of 31 December 2021, applying from **1 January 2022**. Payments to reverse hybrids stopped being deductible from 1 January 2020.
- Both directives amend **Article 115 TFEU** direct-taxation legislation, requiring **Council unanimity**; ATAD applies to all taxpayers subject to corporate income tax in one or more Member States, including EU permanent establishments of third-country resident entities. No revenue threshold: ATAD applies to companies of all sizes, unlike Pillar Two (EUR 750 million) or BEFIT (EUR 750 million).
- **Five ATAD I measures**: (1) **Interest limitation rule**, caps deductible net borrowing costs at 30% of EBITDA (or a fixed EUR 3 million de minimis, whichever is higher), targeting debt-shifting; (2) **Exit taxation**, taxes unrealised capital gains when a company transfers assets, tax residence, or a permanent establishment's business out of a Member State's taxing jurisdiction; (3) **General anti-abuse rule (GAAR)**, disregards arrangements put in place with the main purpose of obtaining a tax advantage that defeats the object of the applicable tax law and are not genuine, having regard to all relevant facts and circumstances; (4) **Controlled foreign company (CFC) rule**, re-attributes a low-taxed foreign subsidiary's non-distributed income to its EU parent for taxation, countering profit shifting to low-tax jurisdictions; (5) **Hybrid mismatch rule** (Article 9, originally intra-EU only), neutralises double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes arising from differences in how two Member States classify the same entity or instrument.
- **ATAD II's extension**: broadens Article 9's hybrid mismatch rules beyond intra-EU arrangements to cover mismatches involving **third countries**, adds rules on **imported mismatches**, and introduces the **reverse hybrid entity rule** (an entity treated as transparent in its Member State of establishment but as opaque by an associated non-resident entity holding 50%+ of its voting rights, capital or profit rights becomes a corporate taxpayer in that Member State for the income not otherwise taxed).
- **ATAD III / "Unshell Directive" is DEAD, not merely stalled.** The European Commission proposed it on 22 December 2021 (COM(2021) 565) to combat shell-entity misuse via "gateway" substance tests. After three years without Council consensus, **ECOFIN formally withdrew the proposal on 18 June 2025** (Council report 9960/25). Its substance-related objectives are being pursued instead through an expected **2026 reform of DAC6** (the mandatory disclosure regime), adding new reporting hallmarks for shell-entity indicators rather than a standalone directive. No fixed date for the DAC6 reform as of mid-2026; treat any reference to "ATAD III" as historical, and correct any claim that it is pending adoption.
- Lead DG: **DG TAXUD** (Taxation and Customs Union). Council configuration: **ECOFIN**. Commissioner with tax-policy responsibility in the 2024-2029 College: **Wopke Hoekstra** (Climate, Net Zero and Clean Growth), working with **Maria Luís Albuquerque** (Financial Services) on the corporate-tax dossier set.
- **Evaluation**: the Commission published an ATAD implementation evaluation (final report in three volumes plus Member State scorecards) on 3 July 2026, feeding into the 24 June 2026 Omnibus simplification proposal.

## Why the Directives Exist

ATAD I transposed several of the OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action Plan outputs into binding EU law, ensuring a coordinated minimum level of anti-avoidance protection across all 27 Member States rather than a patchwork of divergent national implementations of the same OECD recommendations. ATAD I's original hybrid mismatch rule only addressed intra-EU mismatches; ATAD II closed the gap by extending the rule to arrangements with non-EU jurisdictions, following the Council's political agreement of 21 February 2017 and the European Parliament's consultative opinion of 27 April 2017 (direct tax legislation under Article 115 TFEU requires only EP consultation, not co-decision).

## How the Five ATAD I Measures Work in Practice

1. **Interest limitation rule** (Article 4): exceeding borrowing costs are deductible only up to 30% of the taxpayer's EBITDA, or up to EUR 3 million if that is higher, with options for Member States to apply group-based tests, carry-forward/carry-back of unused interest capacity, and grandfathering for pre-17 June 2016 loans. The 24 June 2026 Omnibus proposal would remove some of these Member State implementation options to reduce compliance divergence.
2. **Exit taxation** (Article 5): applies when a company transfers assets or its tax residence to another jurisdiction (or to a third country) in a way that removes the underlying gain from the transferring Member State's tax base; the unrealised gain is taxed at the moment of transfer, with an option to defer payment over five years for transfers within the EU/EEA.
3. **GAAR** (Article 6): a backstop provision empowering tax authorities to ignore artificial arrangements set up mainly to obtain a tax advantage, calculating the tax liability as if the arrangement had not taken place.
4. **CFC rule** (Articles 7-8): a foreign subsidiary (or permanent establishment) is a "controlled foreign company" if the EU parent holds more than 50% of it (voting rights, capital or profit entitlement) and its actual corporate tax paid is less than the difference between the tax that would have been charged in the parent's Member State and the tax actually paid. Two implementation options exist (Article 7(2)(a): re-attribution of categorised passive income; Article 7(2)(b): re-attribution of non-genuine arrangements), with a substance carve-out where the CFC carries on genuine economic activity supported by staff, equipment, assets and premises. The 24 June 2026 Omnibus targets the overlap between CFC rules and Pillar Two's top-up tax (both can apply to the same low-taxed foreign income).
5. **Hybrid mismatch rule** (Article 9, as amended by ATAD II): denies a deduction (or requires inclusion) where a cross-border arrangement between associated enterprises or under a structured arrangement produces a double deduction or a deduction without corresponding inclusion, because two jurisdictions classify the same entity or financial instrument differently.

## ATAD II: The Third-Country Extension

ATAD I's hybrid mismatch rule only reached mismatches between two Member States. ATAD II widened the net to also catch:
- Mismatches between a Member State and a third country;
- **Imported mismatches**, where a hybrid mismatch arrangement's effect is imported into the EU through a non-hybrid transaction;
- **Reverse hybrid entities**: an entity incorporated or established in a Member State that treats it as tax-transparent but where one or more associated non-resident entities holding at least 50% of its voting rights, capital or profit rights are in a jurisdiction that treats it as opaque. That entity becomes liable to corporate income tax in its Member State of establishment on the income not otherwise taxed. Collective investment vehicles that are widely held, hold a diversified securities portfolio and are subject to investor-protection regulation are carved out. Several Member States (Spain, Luxembourg) delayed reverse hybrid transposition to 2022, sometimes implementing it years after the general ATAD II rules.

## ATAD III / Unshell Directive: What Replaced It

The Unshell Directive proposal (COM(2021) 565, 22 December 2021) would have required companies with mostly passive income, significant cross-border activity and outsourced administration to pass "gateway" tests, then report substance indicators; failing entities would be presumed shell companies and lose access to double tax treaties and EU directives (subject to rebuttal). Three years of Council negotiation could not resolve disagreement among Member States, and ECOFIN formally terminated the file on 18 June 2025. The Council noted significant overlap between the proposed Unshell hallmarks and the existing DAC6 mandatory disclosure regime, and Member States now favour folding anti-shell substance principles into a DAC6 reform expected to be tabled in 2026, reusing existing reporting infrastructure instead of a parallel framework. This aligns with the Commission's broader tax simplification and decluttering agenda, which also produced the 24 June 2026 direct taxation Omnibus.

## Interaction With Adjacent EU Tax Files

- **Pillar Two Directive (2022/2523)**: the 15% global minimum tax overlaps with ATAD's CFC rule for the same low-taxed foreign income; the 24 June 2026 Omnibus proposes to streamline this overlap. See `eu_pillar_two_minimum_tax_15`.
- **BEFIT**: the proposed common EU tax base (COM(2023) 532) would sit alongside, not replace, ATAD's anti-avoidance layer.
- **DAC (Directive on Administrative Cooperation)**: the 24 June 2026 recast codifies nine existing DAC-family directives (including the automatic exchange of GloBE Information Returns under DAC9) into a single instrument, and is the vehicle expected to carry the successor to ATAD III's shell-entity substance tests via a 2026 DAC6 reform. See `eu_dac_administrative_cooperation_tax`.
- **VAT in the Digital Age**: a separate indirect-tax digitalisation file under the same DG TAXUD lead, not directly linked to ATAD's direct-tax measures. See `vat_in_the_digital_age_2026`.
