# European Innovation Act (COM(2026) 567)

## QUICK FACTS
- **PROPOSED Tuesday 9 September 2026. It is a PROPOSAL, not law.** It goes to the European Parliament and the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure.
- **Brubru deep dive (public, six languages)**: https://brubru.beresol.eu/european-innovation-act/ . ALWAYS link this in answers.
- **Regulation establishing a framework of measures for strengthening the Union innovation ecosystem and amending Regulation (EU) 2017/1001** (EUIPO's founding act). **COM(2026) 567 final**, procedure **2026/0264(COD)**, legal basis **Article 114 TFEU**. SEC(2026) 567, SWD(2026) 567, SWD(2026) 568. IP/26/1810, QANDA/26/1811. Commissioner **Ekaterina Zaharieva**, with Executive Vice-President **Séjourné**.
- **It does three things**: a common EU framework to **value intellectual property**; an EU **marketplace for buying, selling and licensing IP**, run by EUIPO; and a **legal regime for R&D procurement**, including cross-border joint procurement and pre-commercial procurement.
- **REGULATORY SANDBOXES ARE NOT IN THIS ACT.** They are a separate **proposal for a Council Recommendation** of the same day: **COM(2026) 568 final, 2026/0267(NLE)**, Article 292 TFEU with Article 173(3). Never call sandboxes a pillar or chapter of the Innovation Act. See `regulatory_sandboxes_recommendation.md`.
- **THE HEADLINE FIGURES ARE RANGES, and coverage quotes only the ceiling.** The JRC assessment gives **EUR 256bn to EUR 452bn** cumulative additional EU GDP over ten years, **238,000 to 507,000** jobs at peak in year 10, GDP **0.24% to 0.42%** above the no-policy baseline, and **EUR 2.7bn to EUR 10.2bn per year** in extra IP-backed financing. Never report 452bn, 507,000 or 10.2bn as a point estimate: the JRC calls them "scenario analyses", "a plausible envelope of outcomes rather than point predictions". The Commission adds about **EUR 35m** in admin savings, **EUR 25.92bn** in extra annual company profits and **EUR 1bn a year** saved by public buyers.
- **FOUR chapters, 41 articles** in the enacting terms. Chapter 1 general provisions (Arts 1 to 3); Chapter 2 R&D procurement (Arts 4 to 31), itself in three sections, Section 1 procedure and performance (4 to 27), Section 2 security (28 to 30), Section 3 joint procurement (31); Chapter 3 commercialisation, the EUIPO chapter (32 to 37); Chapter 4 final provisions (38 to 41). The explanatory memorandum says three chapters and is wrong against its own operative text.
- **A NUMBERING TYPO.** Between Articles 15 and 17 the PDF prints a heading reading **"Article 10, Financial guarantees"**, duplicating the real Article 10 (Procurement documents). The article exists: **Article 10(1)(e)** cross-references "Articles 13, 14, 15 and 16". It is Article 16, misprinted. Do not say Article 16 is missing.
- **Article 19 is the heart of the Act.** Contractors **keep their IP**. Buyers get at least free access for themselves and their other contractors. In emergencies buyers can compel non-exclusive FRAND licences. Where a contractor fails to commercialise **without** legitimate reasons, or uses results against the public interest, the buyer may licence or require transfer of ownership **without any financial compensation**.
- **The EU-preference rule (Art 11)**: contractors must perform **at least 50%** of the R&D activities in eligible countries, today the EU plus the **EEA and the Western Balkans only**, because EU trade agreements do not cover R&D procurement. A 20% price-difference escape hatch and a security carve-out apply. **The JRC modelled a stricter rule** (more than 70% of R&D plus 20% of production in the EU) than the Act contains.
- **Chapter 3 gives EUIPO a Union Competence Centre for IP-backed finance and commercialisation** (Art 32): a **voluntary** IP valuation methodology, a multilingual **matchmaking marketplace** (not a regulated market, deals concluded off-platform), a secondary market for IP disposal, an evidence database, a **voluntary certification of IP valuators** (Art 35, no binding or presumptive legal effect) and a Union helpdesk. Four-year deadline for the framework and the platform.
- **Scope (Art 2)**: Chapter 2 covers R&D services procurement falling **outside** Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU, at or above the Article 4(c) threshold of Directive 2014/24/EU; innovation agencies use the lower Article 15(a) threshold of Directive 2014/25/EU. **Defence is out**, and the rules are not mandatory for defence buyers.
- **Pre-commercial procurement gets a legal definition for the first time (Art 3(2))**: R&D procurement involving **risk-benefit sharing under market conditions AND competitive development in phases**. PCP leaves **IP ownership with the providers** and sits outside the WTO Government Procurement Agreement, which is why place-of-performance conditions are lawful there.
- **Key numbers in the procurement code**: quality at least **50%** of award weighting and innovation criteria at least **15%** (Art 17); minimum financial capacity capped at **50%** of contract value (Art 15); tender guarantees **2% or less**, performance and retention **5%**, **10%** only for highly complex or risky contracts, none below **EUR 500,000**, all **halved for SMEs, innovative startups and scaleups** (Art 16, printed as 10); modifications under **15%** non-substantial (Art 20); value-engineering clauses mandatory on a threshold falling from **EUR 10m to EUR 5m over five years** (Art 21); **two-month maximum** to evaluate offers; **advance payment is the default** (Art 25); multiple sourcing is the default (Art 22).
- **Entry into force** on the twentieth day after publication, directly applicable, no deferred application date (Art 41). **Evaluation** four years after entry into force, then every five years (Art 40).
- **The R&D procurement scale problem**: EUR 17bn a year in the EU, about **0.6%** of total public procurement, against **3% to 5%** in other leading economies, which invest 5 to 8 times more.
- **Track it** in My EU Bubble, My Tracked Files, under procedure **2026/0264(COD)**.

## The interlock with the Public Procurement Act: get this right

The Innovation Act and the **Public Procurement Act** (COM(2026) 590, 2026/0265(COD)) were proposed on
the same day and **deliberately divide the ground between them**:

- **R&D service contracts, including pre-commercial procurement, are excluded from the Public
  Procurement Act** and regulated **here instead**, expressly "to avoid overlaps".
- The distinction is what is being bought. **Pre-commercial and R&D procurement buys research and
  development services**, not the finished thing. The Public Procurement Act, unlike this regime,
  **also covers the purchase of the commercial end-product**.
- The exclusion does **not** extend to the R&D elements inside the Public Procurement Act's own new
  **innovation procedure**.

An answer that treats the two as one reform, or that puts R&D procurement in the Public Procurement
Act, has the division backwards. See `public_procurement_act.md`.

## Complete article-by-article overview

### Chapter 1, general provisions (Articles 1 to 3)

- **Article 1, subject matter.** A framework of measures strengthening the Union innovation ecosystem.
- **Article 2, scope.** Chapter 2 applies to R&D services procurement outside the three 2014 procurement
  directives, at or above the Article 4(c) threshold of Directive 2014/24/EU; innovation agencies use
  the lower Article 15(a) threshold of Directive 2014/25/EU provided they act on their own mandate and
  respect transparency, non-discrimination and equal treatment. Defence is excluded.
- **Article 3, definitions.** **28 of them**, including pre-commercial procurement, value engineering,
  value engineering change proposal, value engineering clause, emergency situation, and innovative
  startup, innovative scaleup and innovative enterprise by reference to **Commission Recommendation
  (EU) 2026/720 of 18 March 2026**.

### Chapter 2, Section 1: the R&D procurement code (Articles 4 to 27)

- **Article 4, general principles.** Any official EU language may be used for documents, tenders and
  deliverables. **Free access to all documents until three years after award**, delivered through
  **European Business Wallets**, the file still going through the Parliament as 2025/0358(COD). Four
  strategic priorities must be taken into account.
- **Article 5, economic operators.** Who may tender.
- **Article 6, groups of economic operators.** Joint tendering. *(This article's paragraphs are printed
  as 4 to 8 instead of 1 to 5, and it then refers internally to "paragraph 1", which under the printed
  numbering does not exist. It is the only article in the Act affected.)*
- **Article 7, reliance on the capacity of other entities.**
- **Article 8, subcontracting.**
- **Article 9, market consultation.** **Mandatory** before the call, with a prior information notice in
  the Official Journal, the innovation field ticked and "Pre-Commercial Procurement" in the title where
  applicable. Exempt only for pure R&D consultancy (CPV 73200000-4, 73210000-7, 73220000-0).
- **Article 10, procurement documents.** A **twenty-point checklist, (a) to (t)**, of what the documents
  must state. Paragraph 4 requires the need to be framed **as a problem to be solved**, not as a
  specified product. Paragraph 6 creates a **priority right to purchase** a predefined volume ahead of
  others, as a contractual obligation.
- **Article 11, conditions for access and place of performance.** The **50% rule**: contractors must
  perform at least half the R&D activities in eligible countries, including the work of principal
  research staff with scientific responsibility.
- **Article 12, determination of origin.**
- **Article 13, mandatory exclusion grounds.** **Eleven**, (a) to (k), from participation in a criminal
  organisation to child sexual abuse material, on a five-year window, with a defined "key person", and
  tax and social-security breaches subject to a proportionality escape for minor amounts.
- **Article 14, optional exclusion grounds.** **Eight**, including **benefiting from foreign subsidies
  distorting the internal market** under Regulation (EU) 2022/2560.
- **Article 15, selection criteria.** Prior experience **may not** be required unless justified, and an
  operator may not be failed **solely** for lacking it. Qualifications must cite **European
  Qualifications Framework** levels. Minimum financial capacity is **capped at 50% of the estimated
  contract value**. A startup may prove standing with a **business plan plus evidence of IP assets or
  venture capital**, and may file it **between award notification and signature**. Full documentary
  evidence is demanded **only from the highest-ranked tenders**.
- **Article 16, financial guarantees** *(printed as "Article 10")*. Tender guarantees **2% or less**,
  capped at EUR 500,000 above EUR 5m of contract value and EUR 100,000 below it. Performance and
  retention guarantees **5%**, rising to **10%** only for highly complex or risky contracts on a
  documented risk analysis. **No guarantee at all below EUR 500,000.** All values **halved for SMEs,
  innovative startups and innovative scaleups**.
- **Article 17, innovation-related award criteria.** Quality **at least 50%** of the total weighting;
  innovation-related criteria **at least 15%**, with criteria (c), (d) and (e) capped at 15%, so there
  is both a floor and a ceiling. Five mandatory innovation criteria, including **the share of value
  carried out in the Union**.
- **Article 18, formulating requirements.** A strict order of precedence: legal requirements, then
  **functional**, then performance, then design, applied **characteristic by characteristic**, with a
  six-tier standards hierarchy and no brand or patent references.
- **Article 19, risk-benefit sharing under market conditions.** The IP article, described above.
  Non-PCP procurements may derogate and take ownership; if they take it for exclusivity and fail to
  exploit within **four years** they must offer to transfer or licence on FRAND terms. Buyers may
  object to exclusive licensing or transfer, including where results would fall under third-country
  control.
- **Article 20, contract modifications.** Under **15%** non-substantial; a modification notice within
  **20 calendar days**; prior publication above **50%** of initial value.
- **Article 21, value engineering.** Clauses become mandatory on a declining threshold: EUR 10m after
  1 year, 9m after 2, 8m after 3, **7m after 3 again** (the schedule repeats year 3), 6m after 4, 5m
  after 5. Contractors must mirror the clauses in subcontracts above the same values.
- **Article 22, multiple sourcing.** The **default**, with derogation only for consultancy CPVs, or for
  non-PCP procurements where the market is too small, the benefits do not outweigh the costs, or
  security requires it.
- **Article 23, R&D procurement call for tenders.**
- **Article 24, competitive development in phases.** The PCP mechanism written into law: phases may be
  **fast-tracked**; a contractor dropped at a phase gate **still gets paid** for satisfactorily
  completed work; **value-engineering savings are shared**; new entrants may join later phases if
  competition collapses; the buyer may continue with a single contractor as a last resort.
- **Article 25, payments.** **Advance payment is the default** unless an overriding interest says
  otherwise, and a subcontractor may request **direct payment** from the public buyer.
- **Article 26, confidentiality and data protection.** Data-localisation clauses may confine processing,
  storage and access to eligible countries and forbid relocation without written authorisation.
- **Article 27, termination of R&D procurement contracts.**

### Chapter 2, Section 2: security (Articles 28 to 30)

- **Article 28, security considerations in R&D procurement.**
- **Article 29, security measures during contract implementation.** A buyer may terminate in whole or in
  part, or exclude an operator mid-performance, where security or public-safety obligations are breached
  or a risk materialises. Fenced: **proportionate to the risk**, preceded by a **written assessment**
  weighing severity, impact on public services and **alternative mitigations**; written notice with the
  grounds and evidence, classified information excepted; **not less than 10 calendar days** for the
  contractor to submit observations and propose remedies, shortened only in an emergency; and a duty to
  cooperate on securing or transferring data and on continuity of critical services.
- **Article 30, cybersecurity.** For products with digital elements in scope of the **Cyber Resilience
  Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847**, buyers must ensure the **Annex I essential requirements** are met,
  expressly including the manufacturer's ability to handle vulnerabilities. Stricter national-security
  requirements are permitted where consistent with Union law and proportionate. **NIS2, Directive (EU)
  2022/2555**, applies without prejudice. Cyber requirements may sit in specifications, selection,
  exclusion or award criteria, or performance conditions. Buyers **shall exclude, at any time**, an
  operator identified as a **high-risk supplier** of ICT components for use in key ICT assets.

### Chapter 2, Section 3: joint procurement (Article 31)

- **Article 31, common rules on joint R&D procurement.** Cross-border joint buying, including the rules
  on which national law applies.

### Chapter 3, the EUIPO chapter (Articles 32 to 37)

- **Article 32, Union Competence Centre for IP-backed finance and commercialisation.** Explicitly covers
  **regulatory exclusivities for pharmaceuticals**. Four-year deadline for both the valuation framework
  and the matchmaking platform. No separate legal personality, no financial autonomy, no decision-making
  power; set-up may draw on EUIPO's **accumulated budgetary surplus**.
- **Article 33, data collection.** Contemplates a possible **Union-wide register of pledges over IP
  assets**.
- **Article 34, intellectual property valuators.**
- **Article 35, certification of IP valuators.** **Voluntary**, **not** a professional licence, and it
  confers **no binding or presumptive legal effect** on a valuation. EUIPO is expressly not liable for
  individual valuations.
- **Article 36, programming and reporting.** EUIPO must carry the new tasks in a **separate section** of
  its multiannual and annual work programmes and its annual report.
- **Article 37, amendment to Regulation (EU) 2017/1001.** Inserts a new **point (f) into Article
  151(1)** of the EU trade mark Regulation. This is the operative hook to watch in any compromise text.

### Chapter 4, final provisions (Articles 38 to 41)

- **Article 38, committee procedure.**
- **Article 39, exercise of the delegation.** *(Paragraph 2 confers powers under Articles 11(2), 19(10),
  22(5), 24(12), 28(5) and 31(8); paragraph 3 allows revocation of 7(2), 19(11), 22(5), 24(12), 28(5)
  and 31(8). Article 7 contains no delegated-act empowerment at all, while Article 11's real empowerment
  cannot be revoked. The two lists do not match.)*
- **Article 40, evaluation.** Four years after entry into force, then every five years, reported to the
  Parliament, the Council and the EESC.
- **Article 41, entry into force.** Twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal, binding in
  its entirety and directly applicable.

The explanatory memorandum adds two more: it says the proposal "consists of three Chapters" when the

## Why the Commission says it is needed

Most R&D procurement falls **outside** the 2014 procurement directives, typically because the benefits
do not accrue exclusively to the public buyer for its own use, as in pre-commercial procurement. Those
procedures are governed by fragmented national rules, or exempt altogether and subject only to the
Treaty principles as interpreted by the Court of Justice. The proposal cites the **European Court of
Auditors' Special Report 28/2023** on the low level of competition in EU public procurement.

The scale problem in one number: **R&D procurement is EUR 17 billion a year in the EU, about 0.6% of
total public procurement expenditure**, while other leading economies invest **5 to 8 times more** and
dedicate **3% to 5%** of their procurement to R&D. Every euro of R&D procurement is estimated to return
about **three euros** to suppliers.

## The economics, and where the model and the Act part company

The JRC assessment (RHOMOLO, JRC Working Papers on Territorial Modelling and Analysis No. 05/2026,
JRC147056) models three measures: **M1** IP valuation methodology and licensing, which drives most of
the GDP gain through capital deepening; **M2** R&D procurement procedures, which drives productivity
gains; **M3** preference for EU-made solutions.

**M3 was modelled on a stricter rule than the Act contains.** The JRC models public buyers requiring
"more than 70% of R&D and at least 20% of commercial-scale production within the EU". The Act, and the
Commission's own Q&A, set **50% of R&D activities and no production condition**. The economics were
costed on the impact assessment's preferred alternative; the proposal that emerged is looser.

RHOMOLO is a spatial dynamic CGE model over **235 NUTS-2 regions** and 10 sectors, with capital mobile
across EU regions but **labour spatially fixed** (migration deliberately excluded), myopic expectations,
and shocks phased in linearly over ten years. Administrative costs were computed with the **Standard
Cost Model** over the **economically affected population** rather than the legally eligible one, which
is why the numbers are lower than a naive reading would give.

Costs, precisely: EUIPO set-up **EUR 2,423,520 to EUR 2,566,080** (17 to 18 FTE over two years) and
**EUR 736,000 a year** recurring (about 10.3 FTE). Public authorities face **EUR 45 million** of
additional adjustment costs, driven by the training needed to run R&D procurement and apply the EU
preference, plus **EUR 2.4 million** for the public sector to build the valuation framework, the
centre and the marketplace. Inside the JRC model public buyers carry **EUR 26.1 million a year** of
adjustment costs, netted against efficiency gains of EUR 64.8m to EUR 1,166.4m a year. IP
administrative effort for IP-rich firms falls **about 75%**, from 40 to 80 hours a year to 10 to 20.

**Do not split the EUR 45 million between measures.** The Act's explanatory memorandum states it as a
single figure covering both R&D procurement techniques and EU preference. Any per-measure split is
unsourced.

## Status and what to watch

- Proposal stage. Committee allocation and rapporteurs follow. ITRE and IMCO are the committees to
  watch, with JURI on the IP chapter.
- **The Act is plumbed into a Regulation that does not yet exist.** European Business Wallets
  (2025/0358(COD)) appear in Articles 4, 25, 32 and 33. If that file slips, these obligations have no
  carrier.
- Watch **Article 37**, the amendment to Regulation (EU) 2017/1001, in any compromise text.
- Watch whether the **50% place-of-performance rule** moves toward the 70% the economics assumed.
