# EU Civil Protection Mechanism and rescEU

## QUICK FACTS
- **Instrument**: Union Civil Protection Mechanism (UCPM), the EU's joint framework for disaster response, prevention and preparedness
- **Founding legal act**: **Decision No 1313/2013/EU** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 December 2013 on a Union Civil Protection Mechanism (CELEX 32013D1313)
- **First major amendment**: **Decision (EU) 2019/420** of 13 March 2019, which created **rescEU**, the EU-financed strategic reserve, after the 2017 wildfire season exposed gaps in Member State-only capacity
- **Current governing amendment**: **Regulation (EU) 2021/836** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 May 2021 (CELEX 32021R0836), applying retroactively from 1 January 2021. It fully EU-funds rescEU capacities, lets the Commission directly procure additional rescEU capacities in urgent cases, adds transport/logistics as a rescEU capacity (a COVID-19 lesson learnt), and strengthens the ERCC's operational, analytical, monitoring, information-management and communication role
- **Budget 2021-2027**: **EUR 1.26 billion** baseline, plus **up to EUR 2.06 billion** in top-up funding from the EU Recovery Instrument (EURI) for COVID-related civil-protection measures
- **Participation**: 27 EU Member States plus 10 participating states: Iceland, Norway, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Türkiye, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine
- **Three layers**: (1) national civil-protection capacities of participating states; (2) the **European Civil Protection Pool (ECPP)**, capacities pre-committed by Member States and co-financed by the EU; (3) **rescEU**, a strategic reserve **fully financed by the EU** (purchase, operation and maintenance), used as the last-resort layer when national and pooled capacities are insufficient or unavailable
- **rescEU capacities**: firefighting aircraft and helicopters (standby fleet based in Portugal, Spain, France, Italy, Croatia and Greece for planes; Slovakia, Czechia and Romania for helicopters, with the first Romanian helicopter delivered January 2026), aerial medical evacuation, an emerging rescEU Emergency Medical Team field-hospital capability, medical stockpiles, CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) detection and decontamination assets, emergency shelter units, transport aircraft, and power generators. Resources are hosted across 22 Member States and participating states
- **Coordination**: the **Emergency Response Coordination Centre (ERCC)**, run by DG ECHO in Brussels, is the UCPM's 24/7 operational hub. It monitors global hazards, receives assistance requests and matches offers to needs
- **Activation trigger**: a national government, another EU/participating state, a UN agency or another international organisation submits a request via the ERCC; the ERCC then circulates the request, mobilises Member State offers and, if needed, deploys rescEU assets
- **Cost-sharing**: requesting states typically receive host-nation support at no or reduced cost for ECPP-pooled assets (co-financed up to 75% of transport/operational costs by the EU); rescEU assets are deployed free of charge to the requesting state since they are already fully EU-funded
- **Mapping support**: activations are frequently paired with the **Copernicus Emergency Management Service (Copernicus EMS)**, which provides satellite-derived damage-extent and rapid-mapping products
- **Track record**: over 830 activations since the 2001 predecessor mechanism was established, including 64 activations in 2025 alone
- **Lead DG**: DG ECHO (Directorate-General for European Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid Operations)
- **Commissioner**: civil protection sits under Commissioner **Hadja Lahbib** (Preparedness, Crisis Management and Equality, von der Leyen II Commission, since 1 December 2024)
- **EP lead committee**: ENVI (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety) has legislative lead on UCPM revisions; Council configuration is the Justice and Home Affairs Council / General Affairs Council depending on the file

## What the UCPM does

The Union Civil Protection Mechanism pools and coordinates disaster response across Europe so that no single state has to face a major emergency alone. It covers natural disasters (wildfires, floods, earthquakes, storms), man-made disasters, and public health emergencies. Unlike humanitarian aid instruments that operate outside the EU under DG ECHO's separate mandate, the UCPM's primary focus is intra-European mutual assistance, though it can also support non-EU countries hit by disaster on request.

The Mechanism does three things:
1. **Prevention and preparedness**: risk assessment, training (the Union Civil Protection Knowledge Network), exercises and early-warning systems
2. **Response coordination**: matching a stricken country's request with offers of help from other participating states or the rescEU reserve, via the ERCC
3. **Financing**: EU co-financing of transport and operational costs for Member State assets deployed through the ECPP, and full EU financing of rescEU assets

## The three-layer structure

### Layer 1: National capacities
Each participating state maintains its own civil-protection capacities (fire services, medical teams, search-and-rescue units) and can offer them to other states through the Mechanism on a voluntary basis.

### Layer 2: European Civil Protection Pool (ECPP)
A pool of response capacities that Member States voluntarily pre-commit and keep on standby for EU-wide deployment. The EU co-finances the adaptation, certification, transport and operational costs of these pre-committed assets, typically up to 75%. Pool assets remain under national ownership and command but are available for cross-border deployment through the ERCC.

### Layer 3: rescEU
The **strategic reserve of last resort**, created in 2019 and substantially strengthened by Regulation (EU) 2021/836. rescEU capacities are **owned, leased or contracted by the Commission or by Member States on the EU's behalf, and are fully financed by the EU** (100% of purchase, operating and maintenance costs), removing the co-financing burden that limited earlier pooled capacity. Key rescEU modules:

- **Firefighting aircraft and helicopters**: the standby aerial firefighting fleet, expanded after repeated record wildfire seasons (Greece, Portugal, Spain, France, Italy, Croatia)
- **Medical evacuation and field hospitals**: aerial medical evacuation for critical-care patients and the developing rescEU Emergency Medical Team, described by the Commission as aiming to be the largest field hospital capability globally
- **Medical stockpiles**: supplies for health emergencies (masks, ventilators, vaccines during COVID-19; the model has since been generalised for future health crises)
- **CBRN capabilities**: detection, sampling, identification, monitoring and decontamination equipment for chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear incidents
- **Shelter and transport/logistics**: emergency shelter units, transport aircraft for personnel and cargo, and power generators (added as a distinct rescEU capacity by Regulation (EU) 2021/836 following COVID-19 supply-chain lessons)

## How activation works

1. A disaster occurs in a participating state (or a non-EU state requests help)
2. The affected country submits a request for assistance through the ERCC
3. The ERCC alerts all participating states and relevant international partners
4. Member and participating states offer available assets; the ERCC checks rescEU availability if national/pooled offers are insufficient
5. The affected country selects the offers it needs
6. The ERCC coordinates deployment logistics and co-finances transport costs; Copernicus EMS rapid-mapping products are typically activated in parallel to support damage assessment

## Recent activations (illustrative, non-exhaustive)

- Mediterranean wildfire seasons (Greece, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Croatia), recurring summer activations of the rescEU aerial firefighting fleet
- Ukraine: sustained UCPM/rescEU support since February 2022 (medical evacuations, shelter, generators, winterisation equipment)
- Central and Eastern European floods (Storm Boris, September 2024, and subsequent flood seasons)
- Earthquake response (e.g. Türkiye-Syria February 2023, drawing on urban search-and-rescue modules)
- Iberian heatwave and wildfire seasons (2025-2026)

Note: the UCPM/rescEU response layer is operationally distinct from, but often runs in parallel with, the **EU Solidarity Fund (EUSF)**, which reimburses post-disaster public expenditure rather than providing in-kind emergency assets. See `eu_solidarity_fund_eusf`.

## Stakeholder map

- **DG ECHO**: lead Directorate-General, runs the ERCC
- **Commissioner Hadja Lahbib**: Preparedness, Crisis Management and Equality portfolio
- **ENVI committee (European Parliament)**: legislative lead on UCPM revisions
- **Council (Justice and Home Affairs / General Affairs configurations)**: Member State coordination
- **National civil-protection authorities**: the operational counterparts that request and offer assistance
- **Copernicus Emergency Management Service (Copernicus EMS)**: satellite mapping partner, run jointly with DG DEFIS/EUSPA infrastructure
- **UN OCHA and international organisations**: can request UCPM assistance for non-EU disasters
