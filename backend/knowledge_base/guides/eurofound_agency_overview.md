# Eurofound: European Foundation for the Improvement of Living and Working Conditions

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Foundation for the Improvement of Living and Working Conditions (Eurofound)
- Seat: Dublin, Ireland (Loughlinstown, Wyattville Road)
- Founded: 1975 (Council Regulation (EEC) No 1365/75); recast founding act: **Regulation (EU) 2019/127** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 January 2019, which repealed and replaced the 1975 Regulation and updated the tripartite governance rules
- Status: EU decentralised (tripartite) agency, one of three tripartite agencies alongside Cedefop (vocational training, Thessaloniki) and EU-OSHA (occupational safety and health, Bilbao)
- Governance: Management Board (government, employer and trade union representatives from every Member State, plus the European Commission and one independent expert appointed by the European Parliament), an Executive Board, and an Executive Director
- Mandate: research and comparative data on living and working conditions, industrial relations and social dialogue, employment and labour markets, and social cohesion, feeding EU and national policymaking
- Flagship surveys: European Working Conditions Survey (EWCS, running since 1990-91), European Quality of Life Survey (EQLS), European Company Survey (ECS, since 2004-05, formerly ESWT)
- Flagship databases/monitors: European Restructuring Monitor (ERM, since 2002, tracks large-scale restructuring events and job gains/losses across the EU27 plus Norway), EU PolicyWatch (government and social-partner responses to COVID-19, the war in Ukraine, inflation and other shocks), Minimum Wage Database (statutory minimum wages plus collective-agreement minimum rates for low-paid sectors), Living, working and COVID-19 e-survey (now the ongoing "Living and working in the EU" e-survey series)
- Field network: Network of Eurofound Correspondents (NEC), one national correspondent team per Member State plus Norway, feeding the six strategic research areas of the Eurofound Programming Document
- Distinguish from: **Cedefop** (vocational education and training, Thessaloniki) and the **European Training Foundation (ETF)** (VET support for EU candidate and neighbourhood countries, non-EU focus, Turin): Eurofound covers living and working conditions broadly across the EU, not training specifically
- Annual output: minimum wage annual review reports, EWCS/EQLS/ECS topical reports, industrial relations country profiles, restructuring notifications, upward-convergence monitoring reports

Eurofound is the EU's principal tripartite research agency on jobs, work quality and living conditions. Its comparative surveys and administrative databases are the reference EU-wide dataset that the European Commission, the European Parliament and national governments use to benchmark labour-market and social outcomes, and they are widely cited in EU social-policy debates, including on the Minimum Wage Directive, the European Pillar of Social Rights and the EU social dialogue framework.

## What Eurofound does

Eurofound was set up in 1975 as one of the EU's first specialised bodies, tasked with contributing to the planning and design of better living and working conditions. Its founding act was recast in 2019 (Regulation (EU) 2019/127) to modernise its governance while preserving its defining feature: **tripartism**. Unlike most EU agencies, Eurofound's Management Board formally seats government, employer and trade union representatives from every Member State alongside the Commission, giving social partners a direct institutional voice in setting its research agenda.

Eurofound does not have regulatory or enforcement powers. It is a knowledge agency: it collects harmonised, comparable data across all 27 Member States (and, for some outputs, EU candidate or EFTA countries) through large-scale surveys, administrative monitoring and its correspondent network, then publishes analysis that feeds Commission impact assessments, European Semester country reports, European Parliament committee work (notably EMPL) and national policy debates.

## Governance structure

- **Management Board**: one government representative, one employer representative and one trade union representative per Member State, plus Commission representatives and one independent expert appointed by the European Parliament. This tripartite composition is the defining institutional feature shared only with Cedefop and EU-OSHA.
- **Executive Board**: a smaller steering body that prepares Management Board decisions.
- **Executive Director**: day-to-day management and legal representation of the agency.
- **Network of Eurofound Correspondents (NEC)**: national teams in every Member State plus Norway that monitor and report on working-life developments, contribute to comparative research, validate and update Eurofound databases, and support national outreach. The NEC is the operational backbone that keeps Eurofound's country-level data current between survey waves.

## Flagship surveys

**European Working Conditions Survey (EWCS)**
Running since 1990-91 (the seventh wave was fielded in 2024, based on roughly 35,000 face-to-face interviews), the EWCS is Eurofound's longest-running instrument. It asks workers detailed questions (around 200 in recent waves) about job quality, physical and psychosocial risks, work intensity, autonomy, work-life balance and job security, and is the main EU-wide time series on working conditions.

**European Quality of Life Survey (EQLS)**
Documents living conditions and the social situation of EU citizens more broadly than the workplace: subjective wellbeing, quality of society, public services, social exclusion and trust in institutions.

**European Company Survey (ECS)**
Carried out regularly since 2004-05 (originally the European Establishment Survey on Working Time and Work-Life Balance, ESWT), the ECS surveys management and employee representatives at establishment level on workplace practices, working time arrangements and social dialogue at company level.

**Living and working in the EU e-survey series**
An online, rapid-turnaround instrument (launched as "Living, working and COVID-19" during the pandemic) used to track fast-moving social and economic conditions between the large face-to-face survey waves, later extended to cover the cost-of-living crisis and other shocks.

## Flagship databases and monitors

**European Restructuring Monitor (ERM)**
Tracking large-scale restructuring events (announced job losses and job gains from mergers, relocations, bankruptcies, business expansions) across the EU27 plus Norway since 2002. Includes an ERM legal database (national and EU restructuring-related legislation) and an ERM support-instruments database (public and company measures to manage restructuring, e.g. short-time work schemes).

**EU PolicyWatch**
A rolling database of government and social-partner policy responses to major shocks: COVID-19, the war in Ukraine, the post-2022 inflation surge, and company practices to mitigate social and economic impacts. Useful for tracking how Member States and social partners actually implemented crisis measures rather than what was formally announced.

**Minimum Wage Database and annual reviews**
Two complementary strands: (1) statutory/national minimum wage data for the Member States that have one, updated annually (Eurofound publishes an annual "Minimum wages in [year]: annual review" research report tracking real-terms growth, often benchmarked against the EU Minimum Wage Directive's adequacy reference values); and (2) a database of minimum wage rates set through collective agreements in low-paid sectors, covering all 27 Member States (with a noted data gap for Poland after 2022).

**Upward convergence monitoring**
Analysis of whether Member State performance on employment, working conditions and social outcomes is converging (gaps narrowing) or diverging over time, drawing on NEC input and Eurofound's survey time series.

## How Brubru users engage with Eurofound data

- Cite EWCS/EQLS/ECS findings as the reference EU-wide evidence base in position papers on working conditions, job quality or workplace practices.
- Use the European Restructuring Monitor to evidence sector-specific job-loss or relocation trends when engaging on industrial policy, trade or state-aid files.
- Use EU PolicyWatch and the annual minimum wage reviews when tracking Member State implementation of the Minimum Wage Directive or crisis-response measures.
- Treat Eurofound outputs as independent tripartite-agency research, distinct from Commission impact assessments: useful as corroborating third-party evidence in consultation responses and MEP briefings.
- Do not confuse Eurofound with Cedefop (training/skills) or the ETF (non-EU vocational training support) when citing agency sources on labour-market policy.
