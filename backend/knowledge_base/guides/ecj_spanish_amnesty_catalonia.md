# Spanish Amnesty Law for Catalonia before the Court of Justice (Cases C-523/24 and C-666/24)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Topic**: Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU), Grand Chamber, judgments of **16 July 2026** on whether EU law precludes the **2024 Spanish amnesty law** adopted for the Catalan independence process.
- **Holding (both cases)**: **EU law does not preclude the Spanish amnesty law.** The adoption and application of an amnesty falls within the competence of the Member States; the Court's review is limited to (a) problems of a **systemic nature** capable of undermining the proper functioning of the national judicial system in fields covered by EU law, and (b) respect for the **effectiveness of the specific EU rules actually affected** (the Directive on combating terrorism and the preliminary ruling procedure).
- **Cases**:
  - **C-523/24 Sociedad Civil Catalana** — reference from the **Spanish Court of Auditors (Tribunal de Cuentas)**; about the Union's **financial interests** and **effective judicial protection**.
  - **C-666/24 ACVOT** (Associació Catalana de Víctimes d'Organitzacions Terroristes) — reference from the **Spanish National High Court (Audiencia Nacional)**; about the **Directive on combating terrorism**.
- **The amnesty law (LOA)**: adopted by the Spanish Parliament on **10 June 2024**; amnesties criminal, administrative and public-funds liability for acts connected with the **1 October 2017 independence referendum** and the wider Catalan independence process. The **Spanish Constitutional Court** declared it compatible with the Constitution (with **two exceptions**) on **26 June 2025** (ruling 137/2025).
- **Advocate General Opinions**: **Dean Spielmann**, delivered **13 November 2025** (CELEX **62024CC0523** and **62024CC0666**).
- **Judgments**: 16 July 2026 (CELEX **62024CJ0523** and **62024CJ0666**).
- **Press release**: No **112/26**, 16 July 2026.
- **Key EU-law anchors**: **Directive (EU) 2017/541** on combating terrorism (CELEX 32017L0541); **Article 325(1) TFEU** (protection of the Union's financial interests); **second subparagraph of Article 19(1) TEU** (effective judicial protection); **Article 267 TFEU** (preliminary ruling procedure).

## What the Court held (both cases)
The Court starts from a competence boundary: adopting and applying an amnesty is a matter for the Member States, not the Union. The Court therefore does **not** review the amnesty law in full. Its review is limited to:
1. Whether the law creates **problems of a systemic nature** capable of undermining the proper functioning of the national judicial system in the fields covered by EU law; and
2. Whether it respects the **effectiveness** of the specific EU rules actually affected: the Directive on combating terrorism and the preliminary ruling procedure.

Through that narrow lens the Court finds **no incompatibility**. It sets **one firm condition**: the amnesty law's requirement that national courts decide within a **maximum of two months** (and lift interim measures) **must not deprive the preliminary ruling procedure of its effectiveness**. If that two-month deadline applied even where a national court had made a request for a preliminary ruling to the Court of Justice and was still awaiting the answer, the relevant provisions of the amnesty law **would have to be set aside**. The Spanish Government maintained that the law can be interpreted so that proceedings may be suspended and interim measures maintained until the Court replies.

## Case C-523/24 Sociedad Civil Catalana (Court of Auditors)
The Spanish Court of Auditors rules on liability actions for loss to the public assets of the Autonomous Community of Catalonia (expenditure on the referendum and international promotion of independence, 2011-2017). Three questions:
- **Financial interests of the Union (Article 325 TFEU)**: the Court answers that the extinction of that liability does **not** undermine the Union's financial interests. An adverse effect on the Union budget cannot be inferred from an adverse effect on the national budget alone; in particular, a reduction in gross national income that might result from the secession of part of a national territory does not establish harm to the Union's financial interests.
- **Effective judicial protection (Article 19(1) TEU, second subparagraph)**: in a field within Member State competence, the Court's review is limited to systemic problems. The procedural provisions of the amnesty law (a two-month deadline, without examining defence submissions/exculpatory evidence, without hearing all parties) **cannot lead to systemic problems** and so are not precluded.
- **Preliminary ruling procedure**: the two-month deadline could deprive the preliminary ruling procedure of effectiveness if applied while the Court of Justice has not yet ruled on a pending reference (the firm condition above).

## Case C-666/24 ACVOT (National High Court)
- **Directive on combating terrorism (2017/541)**: the Directive contains **no provision on national mechanisms for extinguishing criminal liability such as amnesties**, and therefore does not set limits on them. The Spanish amnesty law does **not compromise the effectiveness** of the Directive: it merely provides, a posteriori, for the absence of prosecution for certain offences committed in the sole specific context of the Catalan independence movement (acts between 1 November 2011 and 13 November 2023), while **excluding acts that intentionally caused serious breaches of human rights**.
- **Legal certainty**: using abstract formulations / general categories rather than exhaustive lists respects legal certainty, provided the interpretation is reasonably foreseeable. The exclusion refers to legal concepts (intention, causality, seriousness of a human-rights breach) that the competent courts can apply.
- **Equal treatment and non-discrimination**: offences committed in the specific political-reconciliation context and those committed in other contexts are **not comparable situations**, so there is no breach.
- **Primacy of EU law and sincere cooperation**: since the Directive's effectiveness is not undermined, the law does not infringe the principle of primacy or the principle of sincere cooperation.

## Judgment versus Advocate General Opinion (comparative analysis)
Advocate General **Dean Spielmann** delivered both Opinions on 13 November 2025. An Advocate General advises but does not bind the Court.
- **C-666/24 (terrorism): closely aligned.** Spielmann proposed that Directive 2017/541 does not preclude the amnesty (provided no intentional serious human-rights breaches), and that legal certainty, equal treatment, primacy and sincere cooperation are respected. The Court reached the same conclusion.
- **C-523/24 (financial interests + judicial protection): the AG was more critical than the Court.**
  - **Agreement**: no harm to the Union's financial interests; and the two-month deadline cannot apply while a preliminary reference is pending.
  - **Divergence**: Spielmann considered that the **mandatory two-month deadline is incompatible** with the second subparagraph of Article 19(1) TEU where it prevents a court from carrying out the investigation needed to determine whether the assets are EU funds; and that hearing **only** the affected public bodies (not the parties who brought the action in the public interest) is likewise incompatible with effective judicial protection. The **Court, using its narrower systemic-review approach, did not follow him** on those points, holding the procedural rules cannot create systemic problems. On the preliminary-ruling protection, Court and AG agree.
