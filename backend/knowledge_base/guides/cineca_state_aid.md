# Commission Decision (EU) 2025/1963 — State Aid SA.39639 (Cineca)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Instrument:** Commission Decision (EU) 2025/1963, document C(2024) 7987
- **SA case number:** SA.39639 (2021/C) (ex 2021/NN)
- **Beneficiary:** Cineca, a non-profit inter-university consortium
- **Member State:** Italy
- **Decision date:** 21 November 2024
- **Legal basis:** Article 108(2) TFEU (formal investigation procedure); Article 107(1) TFEU (substantive test)
- **Outcome:** No-aid finding. The alleged measures do not constitute State aid. No recovery.
- **Amounts under investigation:** MIUR IT services: EUR 10-20 million per year (range across the period); non-commercial HPC: approximately EUR 11 million per year; university IT services: approximately EUR 159 million from State universities over 2015-2018
- **Appeal/litigation:** The decision was adopted by the Commission on 21 November 2024 and may be subject to review before the EU Courts (General Court and Court of Justice). No appeal information was reported in the decision text.

## Context

The Commission received a complaint from BeSmart S.r.l., an Italian private IT company, in October 2014. BeSmart alleged that Cineca, a publicly owned non-profit consortium of Italian universities and the Ministry of Education (MIUR), was receiving illegal State aid through: (1) direct public funding for IT services to the MIUR without competitive tender; (2) direct awards by universities without tender for university management software; and (3) cross-subsidisation of Cineca's commercial IT activities from public funds received for non-commercial purposes.

The Commission opened a formal investigation in March 2021, published in OJ C 177, 7.5.2021. After extensive exchanges with Italy, Cineca, and four interested third parties (including BeSmart and two competing IT companies), the Commission adopted the final no-aid decision on 21 November 2024.

---

## What Is Cineca?

Cineca was established in 1967 as an inter-university computing centre at the initiative of the Ministry of Education. By the period under investigation it had three main business areas:

1. **IT services to the MIUR**: managing 15 national information systems through which the Ministry supervises, funds, accredits and monitors the Italian university and research system (national student register, training-offer database, staff databases, research registers, funding allocation systems).
2. **University IT services (U-GOV)**: an integrated software suite for student enrolment, HR, accounting, course management, and document management, provided to more than 80 public universities and some non-State and foreign universities.
3. **High-Performance Computing (HPC)**: Italy's largest HPC centre, funded jointly by MIUR contributions (institutional/non-commercial HPC) and commercial revenues from private clients (ENI, EU Horizon projects).

Cineca is a non-profit entity subject to audit by the Italian Court of Auditors and Deloitte, and its accounts are independently verified.

---

## The Central Legal Issue: Was Cineca an Undertaking?

State aid under Article 107(1) TFEU can only be granted to an **undertaking**, meaning an entity engaged in an economic activity. EU case law (SAT/Eurocontrol, Cali e Figli, CompassDatenbank, TenderNed, HIS Decision) establishes that an activity is **not** economic if it is intrinsically linked to an essential function of the State by its nature, aim, and the rules to which it is subject.

The Commission applied this test to two strands:

### IT Services to Italian State Universities

The Commission found an intrinsic link on three grounds:
- **Legal obligation**: Italy's Constitution (Article 33) obliges the State to provide free tertiary education. State universities implement that obligation. Cineca's articles of association (all versions 2006-2019) bind it to provide IT services enabling universities to fulfil their public functions. Royal Decree No 1592/1933 required universities to form supporting consortia.
- **Common purpose**: Cineca and the universities share the same objective: enabling the State to discharge its constitutional duty of tertiary education.
- **Essential and inseparable nature**: Cineca's eight core functionalities (enrolment, training offers, post-graduate management, academic staff admission, scientific-output collection, accounting, HR/payroll, document management) cover almost 100 per cent of universities' IT needs. No other provider offered an equivalent complete integrated solution. Removing any single component would leave the whole system non-functional.

**Conclusion**: provision of IT services to Italian State universities does **not** constitute an economic activity (recital 337). Cineca is not an undertaking for this purpose.

Note: non-State (private) universities are excluded from this finding.

### IT Services to the MIUR

The same three-factor analysis was applied to the 15 MIUR-specific functions (national student register, Universitaly portal, research registers, staff planning tools, etc.). Cineca has mirrored the MIUR's internal structure and accumulated four decades of regulatory knowledge that no external provider could replicate in any operationally tolerable transition period.

**Conclusion**: provision of IT services to the MIUR is intrinsically linked to the State task of tertiary education and does **not** constitute an economic activity (recital 417). Cineca is not an undertaking for this purpose.

The Commission followed its own HIS Decision (SA.34402, 2019), which had made an identical finding for German HIS GmbH's university IT services.

### Imputability (University IT Services)

Even if the university IT payments were economic in nature, the Commission found that imputability to the Italian State was **not proven**. State universities have full constitutional autonomy (Law 168/1989). Multiple universities in practice engaged Cineca's competitors. Imputability would require transaction-specific evidence of State influence, which the complainant did not provide (recitals 293-303).

### No Advantage

As an alternative analysis, the Commission assessed whether Cineca received an advantage. It verified Italy's cost/revenue tables for 2006-2019:
- MIUR IT services: cumulative costs exceeded payments by EUR 66 million over the period. Surplus margins in individual years were 4.4 to 18.6 per cent, far below the 40-60 per cent market average for IT services.
- University IT services: the single comparable transaction offered by the complainant was found incomparable (different service packages, different time periods). No advantage was proven.

### No Cross-Subsidisation

The Commission conducted a detailed ex-post examination of Cineca's accounting data. Both the MIUR IT and the non-commercial HPC activities ran cumulative deficits or were ring-fenced in endowment funds; neither produced a surplus that could have funded commercial activities. The cost-allocation methodology (Activity-Based Costing from 2015/2016, validated by Italian courts) was found adequate. No cross-subsidisation was established (recital 479).

---

## The Finding

**Article 1 of the Decision**: "The measure which the Italian Republic has implemented for Cineca does not constitute aid within the meaning of Article 107(1) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union."

There is no recovery order.

---

## What It Means for Public Bodies and In-House Entities

This decision, together with the HIS Decision and the TenderNed judgment, establishes that:

1. An in-house entity providing bespoke, integrated IT services that are essential to a State's ability to discharge a constitutional function (education, public health administration, court management) is not an undertaking and cannot receive "State aid".
2. The shield applies even where competitors exist, provided no other provider offers a complete and equivalent package.
3. The entity must maintain strict accounting separation between non-economic and economic activities to prevent cross-subsidisation.
4. Compensation that covers actual costs without producing a significant commercial margin does not constitute an advantage.
5. For decentralised public bodies (universities, municipalities) with genuine autonomy, imputability is not automatic and must be shown case by case.
