# REFIT and the Fit for Future Platform: simplifying existing EU law

## QUICK FACTS

- **REFIT** (Regulatory Fitness and Performance Programme) is the European Commission's standing programme for reviewing the EXISTING stock of EU law to cut unnecessary cost and complexity while keeping the underlying policy benefits. It does not touch new legislation.
- Launched by the Communication "EU Regulatory Fitness", COM(2012) 746 final, 2 December 2012 (CELEX [52012DC0746](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT?uri=celex%3A52012DC0746)). Built on the 2007 Action Programme for Reducing Administrative Burden and the 2002 Better Regulation programme.
- REFIT is part of the Commission's **Better Regulation** agenda, alongside the **Regulatory Scrutiny Board** (which screens NEW proposals, not existing law) and the Have Your Say public consultation portal.
- Run by the Commission Secretariat-General (Better Regulation and Foresight unit), overseen politically by the Commissioner responsible for interinstitutional relations and foresight; every Commissioner is individually tasked with "systematically reviewing" the EU law in their own portfolio for simplification potential (2024-2029 mandate instruction).
- **Fit for Future Platform (F4F)**: high-level expert group set up by Commission Decision of 11 May 2020, operational 2021-2024, mandate expired 30 November 2024. Successor to the earlier REFIT Platform (2015, mandate expired 31 October 2019). Delivered 41 opinions with around 260 concrete simplification suggestions. No successor platform of the same kind has been established as of July 2026; the Commission's current toolkit for gathering ground-level input is "reality checks" and "stress testing" run directly by DGs, not a standing expert group.
- **'One in, one out' approach**: piloted in 2021, fully implemented from 2022. Every new Commission proposal that creates adjustment or administrative costs must offset them, as far as possible, by removing an equivalent cost elsewhere, ideally in the same policy area.
- **Burden-reduction target**: cut administrative burden by **at least 25%** for all businesses and **at least 35%** for SMEs by the end of the 2024-2029 Commission mandate, aiming for €37.5 billion in cumulative savings. Set out in the Competitiveness Compass (IP/25/339, January 2025) and the Communication on Simplification and Implementation (11 February 2025), following the Draghi report on EU competitiveness.
- Progress so far: 2025 omnibus proposals and other simplification initiatives delivered an estimated €15 billion in net annual savings plus €5.6 billion in one-off cost removal; the 2022-2024 one-in-one-out period delivered close to €14 billion in net savings.
- Monitoring tools: the annual **REFIT Scoreboard** (tracks every REFIT initiative through its lifecycle since 2015) and the **Annual Burden Survey** (published since 2017, reports on REFIT, F4F, and one-in-one-out results each year).
- Official REFIT page: [commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/evaluating-and-improving-existing-laws/refit-making-eu-law-simpler-more-efficient-and-future-proof_en](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/evaluating-and-improving-existing-laws/refit-making-eu-law-simpler-more-efficient-and-future-proof_en). Official Simplification page: [commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/better-regulation/simplification-implementation-and-enforcement/simplification_en](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/better-regulation/simplification-implementation-and-enforcement/simplification_en).

## What REFIT is

REFIT stands for the Regulatory Fitness and Performance Programme. It is the mechanism through which the European Commission systematically reviews the body of EU law that is already in force, checking whether it still delivers its intended benefits at the lowest possible cost and administrative burden for citizens, businesses and public administrations. REFIT does not ask "should this law exist at all"; it asks "can this law achieve the same objective more simply, more cheaply, or with less paperwork".

REFIT sits inside the Commission's wider Better Regulation agenda, which also covers impact assessment of NEW proposals (via the Regulatory Scrutiny Board, see `regulatory_scrutiny_board`) and stakeholder consultation (via the Have Your Say portal, see `have_your_say_consultations`). REFIT is the "existing law" half of Better Regulation; the RSB is the "new law" half.

Every Commission proposal to revise existing EU legislation is expected to consider simplification and burden reduction as part of the revision, not as an afterthought. Since 2017 this expectation has been formalised: all revisions of existing EU legislation are automatically brought under the REFIT umbrella.

Citizens and small and medium-sized enterprises, which represent 99% of all businesses in the EU, are identified by the Commission as the group that benefits most from REFIT, because they are disproportionately affected by compliance costs and regulatory complexity relative to their size.

## The Fit for Future Platform (F4F): what it was

The Fit for Future Platform was a high-level expert group that advised the Commission on simplification and modernisation of existing EU law. It was established by a Commission Decision adopted on 11 May 2020 and operated from 2021 until its mandate expired on 30 November 2024, with a final plenary meeting held on 17 October 2024.

F4F replaced an earlier body, the REFIT Platform, which had been set up in 2015 and whose mandate expired on 31 October 2019.

**Composition**: F4F was organised into two groups.
- The **Government Group**, bringing together one representative per EU Member State plus three members from the European Committee of the Regions (CoR), supported by the CoR's own **RegHub** network (regional hubs that consult regional and local authorities directly on implementation problems).
- The **Stakeholder Group**, bringing together representatives of business, civil society, social partners, and the European Economic and Social Committee (EESC), supported by the Commission's **SME Envoys Network**.

**Output**: over its four-year mandate F4F adopted 41 opinions containing around 260 specific, actionable suggestions for simplifying or modernising EU laws, covering priorities such as reducing reporting obligations, improving implementation and enforcement, digitalisation, and future-proofing legislation against new technologies and societal trends. The Platform worked from an annual work programme agreed with the Commission, which selected the specific EU laws to be reviewed each cycle (for example, 15 initiatives were selected for review in March 2021, its first full cycle).

**Current status (as of July 2026)**: F4F's mandate has not been renewed and no directly equivalent standing expert-group platform has replaced it. Instead, the 2024-2029 Commission has folded ground-level simplification input into two newer, less formal instruments run directly by the responsible Directorates-General: "reality checks" (structured consultations with the businesses, public authorities, and practitioners who apply a given law day to day, used to shape recent omnibus packages on sustainability reporting, fertilisers, cosmetics, industrial decarbonisation and defence) and "stress testing" of the existing legislative stock, where each Commissioner is individually tasked with systematically reviewing the laws in their own portfolio. Anyone researching current F4F-style stakeholder engagement should check the Commission's live simplification page for whether a new platform has since been convened, rather than assuming F4F is still active.

## The 'one in, one out' approach

Since 2021 (pilot) and fully from 2022, the Commission applies a 'one in, one out' discipline to its own legislative output. The principle: any new adjustment costs or administrative costs created by a Commission proposal must be offset, to the greatest extent possible, by removing an equivalent burden elsewhere, ideally within the same policy area. This is not a literal "one law repealed per one law adopted" mechanism; it is a cost-based offsetting exercise, quantified in euros of compliance cost per year, reported annually in the Annual Burden Survey.

The approach complements REFIT rather than replacing it: REFIT asks whether an existing law can be simplified in its own right; one-in-one-out asks whether the net regulatory cost burden across the EU acquis is being kept stable or falling as new law is added.

## Burden-reduction targets and the 2025-2026 Omnibus wave

The 2024-2029 Commission, responding to the Draghi report on EU competitiveness, set explicit numerical burden-reduction targets as part of its **Competitiveness Compass** (published 29 January 2025, IP/25/339) and the accompanying **Communication on Implementation and Simplification** (11 February 2025):

- Cut administrative burden by **at least 25%** for all EU businesses.
- Cut administrative burden by **at least 35%** for small and medium-sized enterprises specifically.
- Target: €37.5 billion in cumulative recurring administrative cost savings by the end of the 2024-2029 mandate.

To deliver on these targets, the Commission has been publishing a rolling series of **Omnibus proposals**: packages of legislative amendments that revise multiple existing EU legal acts at once, usually within a single policy area, to achieve efficiency and coherence in one negotiation rather than many. From January 2025 to June 2026 the Commission tabled twelve Omnibus packages:

1. **Omnibus I** (sustainability, 26 February 2025): simplifies the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD), and the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). CBAM simplification removes obligations for roughly 182,000 importers (about 90% of those currently covered).
2. **Omnibus II** (investment simplification, 14 May 2025): streamlines InvestEU and European Fund for Strategic Investments reporting.
3. **Omnibus III** (Common Agricultural Policy, 14 May 2025): reduces reporting and monitoring burden for farmers and national administrations.
4. **Omnibus IV**, see `eu_omnibus_iv_small_midcap` (small mid-caps, digitalisation, common specifications, 21 May 2025): extends SME-style relief to small mid-cap companies.
5. **Omnibus V** (defence readiness, 17 June 2025): simplifies rules for the European defence industry.
6. **Omnibus VI**, see `omnibus_vi_chemicals_simplification` (chemicals, 8 July 2025): cuts compliance costs for chemical manufacturers while maintaining health and environmental protection.
7. **Omnibus VII** (digital rulebook, 19 November 2025): simplifies AI, data, and cybersecurity rules.
8. **Omnibus VIII** (environmental legislation, 10 December 2025): simplifies industrial emissions, circular economy, environmental assessment and geospatial data rules.
9. **Omnibus IX** (automotive, 16 December 2025): simplifies technical requirements and testing procedures for motor vehicles.
10. **Omnibus X** (food and feed safety, 16 December 2025): simplifies plant protection, biocidal products, feed, official controls and animal health rules.
11. **Omnibus XI** (taxation, 24 June 2026): simplifies EU direct taxation compliance for cross-border businesses.
12. **Omnibus XII** (energy and tyre labelling, 24 June 2026): simplifies energy and tyre labelling requirements.

A further "Citizens omnibus" is scheduled for Q4 2026. Half of the proposals in the Commission's 2026 Work Programme are simplification proposals, reflecting how central this agenda has become to the 2024-2029 mandate.

Reported results to date: the 2025 wave of omnibus and other simplification initiatives delivered around €15 billion in net annual recurring savings plus €5.6 billion in one-off administrative cost removal. This builds on the roughly €14 billion in net savings delivered under one-in-one-out over 2022-2024.

## How REFIT differs from the Regulatory Scrutiny Board

These two mechanisms are frequently confused because both sit inside "Better Regulation", but they act at opposite ends of the legislative lifecycle:

| | REFIT / Fit for Future Platform | Regulatory Scrutiny Board (RSB) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Existing, already-adopted EU law | New Commission proposals and major evaluations still in preparation |
| Question asked | Can this law be simplified or made cheaper to comply with, without losing its benefits? | Is the impact assessment behind this NEW proposal (or evaluation) good enough quality to support a political decision? |
| Mechanism | Screening, fitness checks, stakeholder platforms, reality checks, omnibus proposals | Independent quality-control opinions (positive / positive with reservations / negative) on draft impact assessments |
| Output | Simplification recommendations, legislative amendments (omnibus packages) | Opinions that can, in effect, block a proposal from being tabled if the impact assessment is inadequate |

See `regulatory_scrutiny_board` for the RSB in detail.

## Why it matters to a policy professional

- **Predicting what gets amended next**: REFIT fitness checks, reality checks, and the rolling Omnibus wave are the best early signal of which existing EU laws are about to be reopened for amendment, often years before a formal legislative proposal appears. Tracking Commissioner-level "stress testing" announcements and reality-check calls for evidence gives advance notice.
- **Lobbying entry points**: reality checks and, historically, F4F opinions were direct channels for stakeholder input into simplification. With F4F's mandate lapsed, the live channel is the Have Your Say consultation attached to each specific fitness check, evaluation, or Omnibus proposal (see `have_your_say_consultations`).
- **Compliance planning**: because Omnibus packages amend multiple existing obligations at once (reporting deadlines, thresholds, documentation formats), businesses tracking a specific regulation (CSRD, CBAM, REACH, the AI Act, etc.) need to also track whichever Omnibus package touches it, since it may change compliance dates or exempt whole categories of company.
- **Distinguishing genuine deregulation from cost-shifting**: 'one in, one out' is a net-cost accounting exercise, not a repeal count. A law can be simplified on paper (fewer reporting fields) while a different, unrelated obligation is removed elsewhere to balance the books.
