# Eurojust: EU Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name**: Eurojust, the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation
- **Seat**: The Hague, Netherlands (purpose-built premises with secure coordination facilities and multilingual interpretation)
- **Legal basis**: Regulation (EU) 2018/1727 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 November 2018 on the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust), and replacing and repealing Council Decision 2002/187/JHA (CELEX 32018R1727). Eurojust itself was originally established by Council Decision 2002/187/JHA (2002); the 2018 Regulation modernised its governance and aligned it with the post-Lisbon EU agency model.
- **What it is not**: Eurojust is a **decentralised EU agency** for judicial cooperation, not a prosecuting body. It does not conduct investigations or prosecutions itself; it coordinates and supports national authorities. Contrast with the **EPPO** (European Public Prosecutor's Office), which does prosecute PIF (EU-budget-fraud) crimes directly, and with the Commission's **executive agencies** (CINEA, HADEA, EISMEA, ERCEA, REA, EACEA), which are funding-implementation bodies with no judicial role.
- **Governance structure**: the **College** (one National Member per Member State, seconded prosecutors/judges/police officers of equivalent competence, directs operational activity and can act as a body) + the **Executive Board** (governance oversight, chaired by the College President) + the **Administrative Director** (day-to-day administration, appointed by the College).
- **National dimension**: each Member State also runs a **national coordination system** with **national correspondents** (for Eurojust matters, terrorism matters, and the European Judicial Network) that link national authorities to their National Member at Eurojust.
- **Core function**: coordinates cross-border criminal investigations and prosecutions of serious organised crime affecting two or more Member States (or a Member State and a third country), primarily through **coordination meetings** (bringing together prosecutors/judges from the states involved) and **coordination centres** (real-time operational support during joint action days, e.g. simultaneous arrests/searches).
- **Networks hosted**: Eurojust hosts the secretariats of several EU practitioner networks: the **European Judicial Network (EJN)** in criminal matters, the **Joint Investigation Teams (JITs) Network**, the **Genocide Network** (on genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes), and networks on judicial cybercrime cooperation and on organised crime prosecutors.
- **Ukraine accountability**: Eurojust hosts the **Core International Crimes Evidence Database (CICED)**, preserving and analysing evidence of international crimes committed in Ukraine, and supports accountability efforts alongside the **International Criminal Court (ICC)** and the **International Centre for Prosecution of the Crime of Aggression against Ukraine (ICPA)**.
- **Funding role**: Eurojust co-funds Joint Investigation Teams (JITs) between Member States and, where agreed, third countries, covering costs such as translation, travel and logistics for joint operational activity.
- **Key partners**: works alongside **EPPO** (mutual support on PIF-crime cases and evidence), **Europol** (police-level intelligence versus Eurojust's judicial-coordination role), **OLAF** (anti-fraud investigations), **Frontex** (border-crime dimension), and third countries via **liaison prosecutors** stationed at Eurojust and cooperation agreements covering more than 70 jurisdictions.
- **Membership**: all 27 EU Member States participate via a National Member seconded to Eurojust; Denmark participates as an EU Member State but with limited-scope Title V opt-outs relevant to some JHA files (Eurojust participation itself continues under the pre-Lisbon arrangements preserved for Denmark).

## What Eurojust does

Eurojust exists to close the coordination gap that arises when serious crime crosses national borders but criminal justice systems remain strictly national. Its everyday work centres on:

1. **Coordination meetings**: bringing together prosecutors, investigating judges and police liaison officers from the Member States (and, where relevant, third countries) involved in a cross-border case, to align investigative and prosecutorial strategy, resolve jurisdiction conflicts, and speed up execution of mutual-recognition instruments such as the European Investigation Order and the European Arrest Warrant.
2. **Coordination centres**: Eurojust-hosted real-time command centres during "joint action days", where National Members and law-enforcement liaisons monitor simultaneous cross-border operations (arrests, searches, seizures) as they happen.
3. **Legal and analytical support**: advice on applicable EU judicial-cooperation instruments, case-management support through the Eurojust Case Management System, and cross-referencing of parallel investigations across Member States to detect links other authorities may have missed.
4. **Prevention of jurisdiction conflicts**: under Framework Decision 2009/948/JHA, Eurojust can be asked to mediate where two or more Member States claim jurisdiction over the same facts (ne bis in idem risk).
5. **Network secretariats**: administrative and operational backbone for the EJN, JITs Network, Genocide Network, and the cybercrime/organised-crime prosecutor networks, extending Eurojust's practical reach beyond its own casework.
6. **International crimes and Ukraine**: the CICED evidence database and cooperation with the ICC and ICPA on accountability for international crimes committed in the context of Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine.

## Governance in detail

**The College.** Composed of 27 National Members, one per Member State, each a prosecutor, judge or police officer of equivalent competence seconded by their national authorities. The College elects a President and Vice-Presidents, directs Eurojust's operational and organisational activities, and can act as a College in urgent or particularly complex cases (for example, authorising cross-border data exchange requests under strict conditions).

**The Executive Board.** Assists the College with governance and administrative decisions (budget, staffing, annual work programme) so that the College itself can focus on operational casework; chaired by the College President and includes the Commission as a non-voting member.

**The Administrative Director.** Responsible for day-to-day management of the agency (staff, budget execution, premises), accountable to the College and the Executive Board, appointed for a renewable term.

**National coordination system.** Each Member State designates national correspondents for Eurojust, for terrorism matters, and for the European Judicial Network, plus contact points that link national prosecutors and investigating judges to the National Member. This is the channel through which national authorities request Eurojust's support or open a case file.

## Eurojust versus adjacent bodies

| Body | Role | Relationship to Eurojust |
|---|---|---|
| EPPO (European Public Prosecutor's Office) | Investigates and prosecutes PIF crimes (fraud against the EU budget) directly | Works alongside Eurojust on evidence-sharing and coordination; EPPO can rely on Eurojust's network and case-management support for cross-border elements of PIF cases |
| Europol | EU police cooperation agency; intelligence analysis, no judicial powers | Operational/intelligence counterpart; Eurojust adds the judicial-coordination layer that turns intelligence into admissible cross-border casework |
| OLAF | Commission anti-fraud office; administrative investigations into fraud against the EU budget | Refers cases with a criminal dimension to Eurojust/EPPO for judicial follow-up |
| Frontex | EU border and coast guard agency | Cooperates with Eurojust on the criminal-justice dimension of cross-border smuggling and trafficking networks it detects |
| European Judicial Network (EJN) | Network of contact points facilitating direct judicial cooperation between Member States, for cases not requiring full Eurojust coordination | Secretariat hosted by Eurojust; EJN handles lighter-touch bilateral requests, Eurojust handles complex multilateral coordination |
| Commission executive agencies (CINEA, HADEA, EISMEA, ERCEA, REA, EACEA) | Implement EU funding programmes on behalf of policy DGs | Not comparable in function; listed here only to clarify Eurojust is a decentralised judicial-cooperation agency, not a funding-implementation body |

## Legal instruments Eurojust relies on

Eurojust's coordination work depends on the underlying mutual-recognition and cooperation instruments that Member States use to act on its coordination outcomes, notably:

- The **European Investigation Order** (Directive 2014/41/EU, CELEX 32014L0041) for cross-border evidence gathering.
- The **European Arrest Warrant** (Council Framework Decision 2002/584/JHA) for surrender of suspects and convicted persons.
- Framework Decision 2009/948/JHA on prevention and settlement of conflicts of jurisdiction.
- The **e-Evidence Regulation** (Regulation (EU) 2023/1543, CELEX 32023R1543) and **e-Evidence Directive** (Directive (EU) 2023/1544, CELEX 32023L1544) for direct-to-service-provider digital evidence requests, which increasingly run in parallel with Eurojust-coordinated investigations.

## How this flows through Brubru answers

When users ask "what does Eurojust do", "what is Eurojust's mandate", "who is my country's Eurojust National Member", or "Eurojust versus EPPO":

- Anchor on **Regulation (EU) 2018/1727** (CELEX 32018R1727) as the current legal basis, noting it replaced Council Decision 2002/187/JHA.
- Be precise that Eurojust **coordinates and supports**, it does not itself investigate or prosecute: that distinction is the most common source of user confusion versus EPPO.
- Point to the College/Executive Board/Administrative Director structure when asked about governance, and to the national coordination system when asked how a national authority engages Eurojust.
- Mention the EJN, JITs Network, Genocide Network and CICED when the question concerns the practitioner networks or the Ukraine accountability dimension.
- Cross-refer to the European Investigation Order and European Arrest Warrant guides for the operational instruments Eurojust coordination relies on, and to the EPPO guide for the prosecuting counterpart.
