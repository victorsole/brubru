# AGA - Annotated Grant Agreement (EU 2021-2027 MFF)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Issued by**: DG Budget (European Commission) under the EU Financial Regulation 2024/2509
- **What it explains**: The Model Grant Agreements (MGAs) for all EU funding programmes; helps applicants and beneficiaries understand their grant terms and obligations
- **Programmes covered**: Horizon Europe, Euratom, EIC, CEF, DIGITAL, LIFE, CERV, Erasmus+, ESF+, CREA, and 21 other EU funding programmes
- **Current version**: V2.0 (1 April 2025); hyperlinked with cross-references and green-marked new rules
- **Document length**: 431 pages in PDF
- **Purpose**: User guide for translating technical grant agreement language into practical implementation guidance; focuses on "how to interpret your grant" rather than legal references
- **Core article structure**: 44 core articles covering consortium setup, eligible costs, implementation rules, reporting, audits, intellectual property, and dispute resolution
- **Key themes**: Articles 6 (eligible costs), 9 (reporting/payment), 13 (audits), 16 (IPR), 17 (communication/open science), 21 (financial reporting), 25 (checks and audits)
- **Cost categories**: Personnel, travel, equipment, subcontracting, internally invoiced goods/services, financial support to third parties, unit costs
- **Liability and checks**: Articles 11 (liability), 25 (audits/investigations), 27 (cost rejection), 28 (grant reduction), 33 (damages), 34 (administrative sanctions)
- **Special annexes**: Annex 5 covers programme-specific rules on confidentiality, ethics, EU values, IPR, communication, access to research infrastructure, and procurement rules
- **Official source**: https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/aga_en.pdf

## Purpose and scope

The Annotated Grant Agreement (AGA) is the European Commission's plain-language guide to interpreting and implementing the Model Grant Agreements used across all EU funding programmes in the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF).

**When to consult the AGA vs the MGA itself**: The AGA is your first stop for understanding what the grant contract means. It explains each MGA article with practical examples, warning boxes, and clarifications on edge cases. The MGA is the legal contract itself, embedded in your Grant Agreement. If your specific grant has choices listed in the Data Sheet (e.g. "Unit costs" or "Lump sums"), the AGA will show you which options apply and how to calculate costs under each one.

The AGA deliberately avoids technical vocabulary and jargon. It aims to answer "what does this clause mean for my project?" rather than "what does EU law say?" This makes it essential reading before your first report or audit.

## Structure of the AGA

The AGA mirrors the MGA's structure exactly, so you can follow the same article numbers:

| **Section** | **Article** | **Topic** |
|---|---|---|
| General | 1-2 | Subject, definitions |
| Action | 3-4 | What you're doing, how long, when it starts |
| Grant | 5-6 | Duration, eligible and ineligible costs |
| Consortium | 7-10 | Roles (beneficiary, coordinator, affiliated entity, subcontractor, associated partner) |
| Implementation rules | 11-18 | How you must run the project (conflicts of interest, confidentiality, ethics, data protection, IPR, communication) |
| Grant administration | 19-26 | Information obligations, record-keeping, reporting, payments, guarantees, audits |
| Consequences | 27-35 | Cost rejection, grant reduction, suspension, termination, damages, force majeure |
| Final provisions | 36-44 | Communications, amendments, applicable law, entry into force |
| Annex 5 | Programme-specific | Confidentiality, ethics, EU values, IPR rules, communication requirements, special rules (ERC, access to research infrastructure, procurement, co-funded partnerships) |

## Key articles to know

### **Article 6 - Eligible and Ineligible Costs** (Core for financial reporting)

This is the longest article in the MGA. It defines what costs you can declare to the grant:

- **Direct costs**: Personnel (salaries, overheads), travel, equipment, subcontracting, purchases
- **Indirect costs**: Usually 25% flat rate of eligible direct costs (for Horizon Europe, CEF, LIFE, etc.); covers management, rent, utilities, administration
- **Unit costs**: Simplified daily rates or hourly rates pre-set by the Commission (e.g. EUR 15 per km for travel); no receipt required if you use the unit cost option
- **Lump sums**: Single payment for a work package, regardless of actual spend; no detailed cost accounting needed
- **Personnel costs**: Calculated as annual salary divided by 215 working days; capped at 8 hours per day; part-time or on leave reduces the daily rate pro rata
- **Seconded persons**: External staff charged to the grant; invoiced price must be reasonable and follow best value for money rules
- **Equipment**: Capital purchases (EUR 750+ typically) depreciated over the useful life; fully depreciated equipment with residual value can be charged at full price if declared in the proposal
- **Subcontracting**: Subcontractor invoice is the eligible cost; must be awarded on best value for money or lowest price basis; normally capped as a percentage of the budget

### **Article 9 - Other Participants** (Know the rules for subcontractors, associated partners, and third parties)

Different participant roles have different eligibility rules:

- **Affiliated entities**: Treated mostly like beneficiaries; must have a formal legal or capital link to a beneficiary; their costs are eligible if conditions are met
- **Subcontractors**: Implement action tasks (not just supply goods/services); cost is the invoice price; must be selected competitively; limited to a percentage of the budget (varies by programme)
- **Associated partners**: Do NOT receive funding; can implement important action tasks; fully named in the grant; must comply with grant rules through the responsible beneficiary
- **Third parties receiving financial support**: In specific cases (e.g. prizes, grants to SMEs), third parties may receive funding under conditions set in Annex 1

### **Article 13 - Confidentiality and Security**

You must protect confidential information exchanged during the project:

- Information marked "CONFIDENTIAL" or containing sensitive data is confidential unless the originator consents to disclosure
- The granting authority (European Commission and EU executive agencies) may disclose to the Court of Auditors, OLAF, and the European Court of Justice for audit and investigation purposes
- Specific rules for HE, DEP, EDF, CEF, EMFAF and other programmes are in Annex 5

### **Article 14 - Ethics and Values** (Mandatory compliance)

- Projects must comply with fundamental rights, values, and ethical principles
- Research on humans, animals, or sensitive data requires ethics review before signature
- Specific ethical rules for each programme are in Annex 5 (e.g. HE, ERC, RFCS)
- EU values (democracy, human rights, rule of law) must be upheld throughout

### **Article 15 - Data Protection**

- GDPR compliance is mandatory
- The beneficiary is the data controller; the granting authority is a data processor for reporting data
- Personal data (e.g. researcher names, salaries) must be protected and disclosed only for audit purposes

### **Article 16 - Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)**

- **Background IP**: Existing knowledge you bring to the project; you retain ownership; the granting authority has access rights for audit and use
- **Results**: New knowledge, software, publications created during the action; ownership belongs to the creator unless the Grant Agreement specifies joint ownership
- **Open access**: In most programmes, research publications must be open access (CC-BY licence) as soon as possible, usually within 6 months of publication
- **FAIR data**: Research data must be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable (exception: sensitive data, IPR, or confidential information)
- **Benefit clauses**: If results are exploited commercially, the beneficiary may generate revenue above the EU grant (no claw-back unless explicitly stated)

### **Article 17 - Communication, Dissemination and Visibility**

- You must acknowledge EU funding in all communications (website, publications, events, social media)
- Use the EU flag and the EU funding statement: "This [project / publication] has received funding from the European Union's [programme name] under grant agreement No [number]"
- Dissemination targets vary by programme (e.g. Scientific publications, policy briefs, social media, training events, tool releases)
- For Horizon Europe and some other programmes, open science is mandatory (open access publications, FAIR data)

### **Article 21 - Reporting**

Financial reporting must be submitted periodically (every 12 to 18 months typically) and at the end of the action:

- **Periodic reports**: Financial statement with actual costs per category, explanation of deviations, justification of subcontracting above threshold
- **Final report**: Due within 60 days of the end date; includes final financial statement, certificates on financial statement (if required), audit trail
- **Records**: Keep signed timesheets, invoices, receipts, bank statements, subcontractor contracts for 5 years after final payment
- **Exchange rates**: If your costs are in a currency other than the Euro, apply the monthly exchange rate (ECB or programme-specific rate) on the date costs were incurred

### **Article 22 - Payments and Recoveries**

- **Pre-financing**: Up to 50% of the maximum grant amount paid within 30 days of Grant Agreement signature
- **Interim payments**: Triggered by submission of periodic reports; calculated based on eligible costs claimed minus pre-financing and any recovery orders
- **Balance**: Final payment (or recovery) due within 90 days of the granting authority's decision on the final report; calculated as maximum grant minus all interim payments and any penalties
- **Recoveries**: The granting authority may order repayment of unspent pre-financing, ineligible costs, or costs rejected during audit

### **Article 25 - Checks, Reviews, Audits and Investigations**

The granting authority can verify your project at any time during and after the action:

- **Desk reviews**: Checking submitted reports for eligibility and compliance
- **On-the-spot checks**: Physical visits to your site to verify documents, equipment, and work
- **Ex-post audits**: After the action ends, audits can happen for up to 2 years (or longer for OLAF investigations)
- **OLAF access**: The European Anti-Fraud Office has independent access to your documents and premises
- **Court of Auditors access**: ECA can audit EU grants under the EU Financial Regulation
- **Non-cooperation**: Failure to cooperate with checks or investigations can trigger grant suspension or termination

### **Article 27 - Rejection of Costs and Contributions**

The granting authority may reject (declare ineligible) any costs that do not meet the eligibility criteria in Article 6:

- Costs incurred outside the action duration
- Personnel salaries not supported by timesheets
- Equipment not depreciated correctly
- Subcontracting done without competitive selection
- Ineligible activity (off-topic work)
- **Consequence**: The rejected amount is deducted from the next payment

### **Article 28 - Grant Reduction**

If ineligible costs are detected, the granting authority may reduce the grant by:

- The amount of the rejected cost PLUS
- A penalty percentage (0–100%, depending on severity and intentionality)

Examples:
- Unintentional error in personnel cost calculation: 10% penalty
- Intentional misrepresentation of work done: Up to 100% penalty (full cost rejected plus full grant reduction)
- Lack of competitive selection for subcontracting (above threshold): 5–25% penalty

### **Article 31 - Grant Agreement Suspension**

The granting authority may suspend (pause) your grant if:

- You fail to submit a required report on time
- You breach a significant provision of the Grant Agreement
- An investigation reveals serious irregularities
- You do not provide requested information or documents

Suspension stops your payments until you remedy the issue.

### **Article 32 - Grant Agreement or Beneficiary Termination**

The granting authority may terminate your grant (end it) if:

- You commit grave breaches (fraud, corruption, gross negligence)
- You are insolvent or subject to insolvency proceedings
- You lose eligibility (e.g. excluded from EU funding by OLAF/ECA findings)
- You do not fulfil your consortium role or are unable to deliver
- A beneficiary terminates itself (planned exit with granting authority approval)

Effects:
- Pre-financing not yet spent must be returned
- Eligible costs up to the termination date are covered
- Costs after termination are not eligible
- Final payment = eligible costs minus pre-financing minus any recoveries

### **Article 33 - Liability for Damages**

If the project causes harm (environmental damage, injury, financial loss to a third party), the beneficiary is liable:

- The grant does NOT cover damages claims
- Beneficiaries are jointly and severally liable (if multiple beneficiaries, each can be held responsible for the full amount)
- The grant may be reduced to offset damages

### **Article 34 - Administrative Sanctions and Other Measures**

The granting authority or OLAF may impose sanctions (beyond cost rejection and grant reduction):

- Financial penalties (up to 10–20% of maximum grant amount)
- Temporary exclusion from EU funding (1–5 years)
- Permanent exclusion (for fraud or corruption)
- Publication of the sanction (reputational consequence)

## How beneficiaries use it during the grant lifecycle

### **Pre-signature phase (Grant Agreement Preparation)**

- **Read your MGA and the AGA in parallel**: Understand what costs you can declare and what reporting is required before you start the action
- **Check your Data Sheet**: It lists which cost categories, forms of funding, and special rules apply to your grant
- **Prepare your budget and timesheets**: Use the eligible cost categories from Article 6 to set up your accounting
- **Review consortium roles**: Article 7 clarifies which beneficiaries are responsible for what; appoint the coordinator

### **During project implementation**

- **Keep records**: Article 20 requires timesheets (for personnel), invoices (for purchases), subcontracting contracts (before award), travel receipts (if you don't use unit costs)
- **Monitor eligible costs**: Ensure costs match the categories in Article 6; track any changes that might affect eligibility
- **Manage conflicts of interest**: Article 12 requires disclosure and mitigation; keep a register
- **Communicate**: Article 17 requires you to acknowledge EU funding; plan dissemination activities
- **Update your participant data**: Article 19 requires you to keep your Participant Register data (LEAR, address) up to date

### **Before submitting a periodic report**

- **Review Article 21 requirements**: Your report must include a financial statement, narrative of work done, and explanation of any deviations from the plan
- **Check Article 6 again**: Only declare costs that match the eligible cost categories and time commitment rules
- **Prepare documentation**: If you are audited, you need to prove every cost with a receipt, timesheets, or contract (Article 25)
- **Verify exchange rates**: If you have foreign currency costs, apply Article 21 exchange rate rules

### **After audit findings**

- **Read Article 27 carefully**: If costs are rejected, you need to return the amount
- **Understand Article 28 penalties**: Depending on the severity, a penalty may apply in addition to the cost rejection
- **Request a review**: You have the right to ask the granting authority to reconsider (Article 37)
