# State of the Union 2026: the Parliament debate

## QUICK FACTS
- **What**: the European Parliament debate that followed President **Ursula von der Leyen**'s State of the Union address, Strasbourg, **Wednesday 16 September 2026**, about **10:26 to 13:08 CEST**. The sitting ran about 90 minutes behind the speaking list. For the address itself see `soteu_2026_address`.
- **Source status**: Brubru's own recording of the Parliament's **English interpretation** channel, machine transcribed. The EP verbatim report was **not out on 16 September**. Quotes here are the interpreter's English, not the speaker's original language, and may differ from the verbatim. Her reply in the debate is **NOT in the published speech text** (SPEECH/26/1868).
- **Counts (by Brubru's count)**: **98 items**: **78 MEP speeches** (8 group leaders, 54 one-minute, 8 catch-the-eye, 8 closing round), **18 blue-card items in 9 exchanges**, 1 Council statement, 1 Commission reply. Stance of the 78 towards the address (editorial judgement): **supportive 23, mixed 16, critical 18, hostile 21**.
- **Groups**: EPP (European People's Party) and Renew Europe supportive; S&D (Socialists and Democrats) mixed; Greens/EFA and The Left critical; PfE (**Patriots for Europe**), ECR (European Conservatives and Reformists) and ESN (**Europe of Sovereign Nations**) hostile.
- **Top complaint: cost of living**: fuel, heating and living costs, incl. ETS2, in **19 of 78** speeches from six groups. The published text proposes no measure on it and her reply did not address it. **Law today**: ETS2 was created by Directive (EU) 2023/959 amending the ETS Directive; Brubru's records show its start postponed from 2027 to 2028; the Social Climate Fund (Regulation (EU) 2023/955) is meant to cushion households.
- **Her one reply (12:53 to 12:58)**: asked that **EU Inc** not become "a directive" with 27 national variants (it is proposed as a **regulation**, COM(2026) 321, now in Parliament); said priorities need **new own resources**; defended the wildfire response with figures Brubru has not verified; paid tribute to Daphne Caruana Galizia. **Not answered**: Article 42(7) for Cyprus, sanctions on Israel, fuel prices, PfE claims about Hungary.
- **Ceuta**: named in **10** speeches from four groups (EPP, S&D, PfE, Renew). PfE and ECR speakers demanded returns and deportations; S&D asked for solidarity with Spain. The Pact on Migration and Asylum, incl. its Crisis Regulation, has applied since 12 June 2026.
- **Canada**: mentioned in **19** speeches; broad support, with The Left and ECR objecting. The Treaties contain **no associate-membership status**.
- **Greens/EFA**: said they will **ask for a committee of inquiry** into Green Deal implementation. That is a stated intention, **not a filed request**: Article 226 TFEU needs a request by a quarter of MEPs (180 of 720).
- **Council**: Irish Minister of State **Thomas Byrne** said he is confident of an **MFF agreement by the end of 2026**; the MFF needs Council unanimity and Parliament's consent.
- **Naming rule**: this guide names only group leaders announced by the chair, von der Leyen and the Council Presidency. Other speakers are identified by group only.
- Related guides: `soteu_2026_address`, `28th_regime_innovation_act`, `mff_2028_2034`, `eu_own_resources_reform_2028_2034`, `eu_migration_asylum_pact`, `eu_canada_relations`, `european_defence_union`, `social_climate_fund`.

## The President's reply

- **One reply, not two.** No reply followed the group leaders: the sitting went straight to the Council. Her only reply ran from **12:53:04 to 12:58:08**, after the catch-the-eye round.
- **Getting the Council to deliver**: answering a Renew speaker, she called for public pressure on files such as **EU Inc**. As interpreted, she warned against turning "this very straightforward regulation" into a directive with "27 different national variations", adding "I'm not speaking here of the parliament" [12:54:34 to 12:54:56], and said "we do not turn this speedboat, that is so necessary, to a heavy tanker" [12:54:58 to 12:55:02]. She also said founders face 27 legal systems and 60 company forms, that EU Inc would allow a company to be set up online within 48 hours for less than 100 euros, cited an IMF estimate of more than 3% GDP over 10 years, and a message from 100 tech founders and investors. None of this is in the published text; the IMF figure and the letter are her statements, not checked by Brubru.
- **Budget**: she set out "the budgetary equation": keep national contributions stable, invest in priorities and repay NextGenerationEU, so "we need new, own resources" [12:55:42 to 12:55:55]. The published text only says a modern budget needs new own resources.
- **Wildfires**: she said the Emergency Response Coordination Centre oversaw 40 aircraft, 18 ground teams and almost 800 firefighters pre-positioned in 14 countries. Her figures; not verified by Brubru.
- **Media freedom**: a tribute to the Maltese journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia, murdered in 2017: her legacy inspired the Media Freedom Act, the Commission stays vigilant through its rule of law reports, and "There can be no impunity for Daphne's murder" [12:57:42]. The transcript's wording on the date and on an earlier visit is garbled: check the verbatim before quoting those parts.
- **Close**: "I heard critics, yes, that's democracy, but much more strong support" [12:57:56], with a call on pro-Europeans to work together.
- **Not answered**: the yes-or-no question on Article 42(7) for Cyprus or Greece; calls for sanctions on Israel; fuel prices and the cost of living; the PfE claims about Hungary; the ECR question on NATO and the United States in a European Security Council; the EPP call on the southern flank and Morocco.

## The Council Presidency

Thomas Byrne, Irish Minister of State for European Affairs and Defence, 11:03:31 to 11:10:17. Part of his statement was in French and is covered only as the English channel carried it.

- Welcomed Prime Minister Mark Carney and said the proposal significantly advances EU-Canada relations.
- Listed where the address converges with the Irish Presidency programme: Ukraine, security and defence, the One Europe, One Market roadmap "without delay" [11:05:43], and enlargement to "full members and maybe associate members" [11:05:53], with no shortcuts.
- "The Kids Act is a great idea" [11:06:45]; he pledged the Presidency's cooperation. Agreed on the risks of AI; welcomed the rule-of-law proposals; restated support for a two-state solution and financial support to Palestinians.
- "Securing the MFF is a top priority of the Irish presidency" [11:07:55]. He will chair a General Affairs Council discussion on it next week and said he is confident "we can reach agreement by the end of 2026" [11:08:29 to 11:08:34].
- Closed with a tribute to John Hume.

- **EPP**: behind the President's direction. Weber called for a European defence pillar with joint command, drones and satellites, warning against 27 national "bonsai armies" (as interpreted) [10:28:52, early recording, time approximate to about 40 seconds], a strong MFF and more trade deals. One-minute speakers pressed for security against hybrid threats including the southern flank at Ceuta, an operational Europol, and a digital levy on large technology companies as an own resource. In a blue-card answer an EPP speaker, as interpreted, did not commit to a cordon sanitaire against extreme groups; the passage is partly garbled.
- **S&D**: supportive of the pro-European majority but pushing from the left. García Pérez asked for an adaptation plan with investment, simplification without deregulation, a tax on crisis profits, stronger housing and quality-jobs laws funded by own resources, an arms embargo on Israel and confiscation of frozen Russian assets. One-minute speakers raised DSA and DMA enforcement, a digital tax, solidarity with Spain over Ceuta, the European Health Union and gender equality.
- **PfE**: nine of ten speeches hostile. Bardella described two Europes, one that writes the rules and one that pays [10:38:35 to 10:38:58], blamed climate rules including ETS2 for fuel and heating costs, said the mass crossing at Ceuta was "a sovereignty crisis" [10:40:19], opposed distributing migrants under the Pact and said "When the money is European, the jobs need to be in Europe" [10:41:32].
- **ECR**: more varied. Jaki reviewed her promises since 2020 and told her to "stop censoring the internet" [10:45:50 to 10:45:56]. Other ECR speakers called the deference to Canada's Prime Minister embarrassing, asked why the UK was not first in line for associate membership, called a European Security Council with the UK and Canada "institutional inflation" [11:50:16], warned it must not sideline NATO or the United States, and one called for her resignation.
- **Renew**: the first-round speech called for "pro-European radicalism" [10:46:30]: apply the Migration Pact, implement the Green Deal, a European law on climate resilience, ratification of CETA by all Member States and a joint response to tariffs. A Renew one-minute question on making Member States deliver is the one she answered first.
- **Greens/EFA**: Reintke called the summer a climate emergency and said "my group is going to ask for an inquiry committee into the shortcoming of executing what we had already decided in the European Union in the Green Deal" [10:55:49 to 10:55:54], with a fossil-fuel phase-out timeline, a windfall profits tax and an adaptation plan funded in the MFF. A Greens/EFA speaker welcomed Canada in a humorous speech: "Canada will never be the 51st state" [12:05:24].
- **The Left**: all six critical. Aubry said the address ignored the cost of living, called for taxing the rich and capping prices of essentials, and asked of the European Security Council and associate membership for Canada: "who has given you a democratic mandate to do that?" [11:00:32]. The closing speech questioned the State of the Union format itself.
- **ESN**: all five hostile. Aust opened: "The State of the Union can be described by a loss of borders and losing competitiveness" [11:01:33]. Speakers called for scrapping the ETS and ETS2, one for ending funding for Ukraine, another for lifting sanctions on Russia.
- **Non-attached**: two speeches from different national contexts, one critical of the budget, one personally hostile; no common position.

## Main themes and the law today

Checked against Brubru's guides as they stood on 16 September 2026. The debate changes none of this: speeches in plenary create no legal obligation.

| Theme raised | In the debate | Law today |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of living, fuel and ETS2** | 19 of 78 speeches, six groups plus one catch-the-eye speaker; PfE, ECR and ESN blamed ETS2 | ETS2 (buildings and road transport fuels) was added to Directive 2003/87/EC by Directive (EU) 2023/959. Brubru's `european_climate_law` and `social_climate_fund` guides record its start postponed from 2027 to 2028. The Social Climate Fund, Regulation (EU) 2023/955, funded from ETS2 revenue, supports vulnerable households from 2026. Scrapping ETS2 would need a legislative amendment by Parliament and Council. Speakers' price figures are their claims. |
| **Migration and Ceuta** | 23 speeches on migration; Ceuta named in 10, from EPP, S&D, PfE and Renew | The Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since 12 June 2026, including the Crisis Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1359) with tools against the instrumentalisation of migrants; the Return Regulation is still under negotiation. Frontex's January to August 2026 figures exclude the end-of-July Ceuta movement, and Frontex says most of those who crossed returned to Morocco unregistered (`frontex_agency_overview`). The Emergency Response Framework she announced has no text. Crossing totals given in the debate are speakers' figures. See `eu_migration_asylum_pact`. |
| **Canada associate membership** | Canada mentioned in 19 speeches | The EU Treaties contain no associate-membership status; the nearest tool is an association agreement under Article 217 TFEU. CETA has been provisionally applied since 2017 and is not fully in force. Nothing was proposed in law. See `eu_canada_relations`. |
| **Article 42(7) TEU for Cyprus** | A catch-the-eye speaker asked for a yes-or-no answer; another described Turkish and Russian incursions near Cyprus (groups unknown); she did not answer | Article 42(7) TEU obliges Member States to give aid and assistance to a Member State that is the victim of armed aggression on its territory. It binds the Member States, not the Commission, and it has been invoked once, by France in 2015. Whether incidents below armed aggression trigger it is exactly the gap her proposed Emergency Security Protocol would address; that protocol has no text. Cyprus is not in the published speech. See `european_defence_union`. |
| **Sanctions on Israel** | 5 speeches (S&D leader, The Left leader, a Greens speaker, two catch-the-eye speakers) plus 1 blue-card item | The EU-Israel Association Agreement has been in force since 1 June 2000. EU restrictive measures need unanimity in the Council. The published speech says Member States "have been unable to build the necessary majorities for a unified response" to the measures she proposed a year earlier. Brubru's `eu_sanctions_implementation_framework` records no EU sanctions on Israeli settlements as of 8 September 2026. |
| **MFF and own resources** | 17 speeches on the budget; she answered with the "budgetary equation" | The 2028-2034 MFF is a Commission proposal (2025/0570(APP)): Council unanimity under Article 312 TFEU and Parliament's consent. New own resources need a Council decision by unanimity, consultation of Parliament and ratification by all Member States (Article 311 TFEU). A digital levy is not among the Commission's proposed resources. See `mff_2028_2034`, `eu_own_resources_reform_2028_2034`. |
| **Greens' committee of inquiry** | Announced by the Greens/EFA leader | A committee of inquiry under Article 226 TFEU investigates alleged contraventions or maladministration in implementing EU law. It is set up at the request of **a quarter of MEPs** (180 of 720) and cannot examine facts that are before a court. On 16 September this was a stated intention; no request had been filed. Past example: PEGA, on spyware (2022 to 2023). |
| **EU Inc: directive or regulation** | 3 speeches (EPP, S&D, Renew); the only gap she answered | EU Inc is proposed as a **regulation** under Article 114 TFEU (COM(2026) 321, 2026/0074(COD)), directly applicable, not transposed. JURI leads in Parliament; EMPL, the opinion-giving committee, held a vote on 10 September 2026. Her warning against a directive, "not speaking here of the parliament", reads as aimed at the Council; Brubru has no Council text proposing a directive. See `28th_regime_innovation_act` and https://brubru.beresol.eu/eu-inc/index.html |

## Claims made in the debate

Speakers' figures and allegations are theirs, not findings. Handle them this way:
- **Attribute, never assert.** Say "a PfE speaker alleged" or "the ECR closing speech claimed", with the group, and add what the record shows where Brubru has it.
- **Hungary**: two PfE speeches, including the closing one, claimed the President had worked against Hungary's previous government and admitted it. The published speech contains no such acknowledgement; it praises the 12 April 2026 election outcome. Present it only as a claim made in the debate (see `hungary_election_2026_magyar`).
- **Migrants and crime**: a PfE speech and the ECR closing speech made sweeping generalisations linking migrants to crime. These are allegations, not evidence; do not repeat them as fact.
- **Ceuta numbers**: speakers gave crossing and return figures. Frontex expects no definitive total for the Ceuta movement.
- **Price, staffing and spending figures** (petrol prices, ETS2 cost per litre, Commission staff or jet spending, insolvencies) are speakers' claims and were not checked.
