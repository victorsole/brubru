## What Are Territorial Supply Constraints?

Territorial Supply Constraints (TSCs) are unilateral or contractual practices by branded goods manufacturers that **partition the EU single market along national borders**. Typical forms:

- **Refusal to supply**: manufacturer refuses to sell to a retailer willing to source cross-border
- **Differentiated product ranges**: manufacturer offers different SKUs, packaging, or formulations per Member State, preventing cross-border sourcing
- **Dual pricing**: manufacturer charges higher prices to retailers who intend to resell across borders
- **Gross-to-net restrictions**: manufacturer imposes rebates, loyalty schemes, or year-end bonuses conditional on national-only sourcing
- **Supply allocation quotas**: manufacturer limits volumes per country, preventing large retailers from centralising procurement

## Economic Impact

Belgian, Dutch, French, Italian, and Spanish competition and consumer authorities have all raised TSC concerns:

- **Belgian consumers** pay up to 9% more for branded FMCG goods than neighbours
- **Retail price parity studies** show systematic differences of 20-30% for identical products across the EU
- **Small and mid-sized retailers** are disproportionately affected -- they cannot replicate cross-border sourcing that large chains achieve via group companies
- **Consumer welfare loss**: estimated at EUR 14 billion/year by 2018 Commission analysis; higher after inflation

## Legal Framework

### Article 34 TFEU (Free Movement of Goods)
Prohibits measures having equivalent effect to quantitative restrictions. However, Article 34 applies to **Member State measures**, not unilateral conduct by private companies. Private TSCs therefore fall under Articles 101/102 TFEU.

### Article 101 TFEU (Anti-Competitive Agreements)
Captures TSCs where there is agreement, concerted practice, or decision between undertakings:
- Manufacturer's agreements with wholesalers imposing national-only supply clauses
- Coordinated refusals to supply across a distribution network
- Vertical Block Exemption (VBER, Reg (EU) 2022/720) may apply if market share <30% and no hardcore restrictions

### Article 102 TFEU (Abuse of Dominance)
Applies where a dominant manufacturer refuses to supply without objective justification:
- Essential facilities doctrine (limited)
- Margin squeeze
- Anti-competitive discrimination

### Vertical Block Exemption (VBER) Hardcore List (Article 4)
**Hardcore restrictions that void VBER protection:**
- Restrictions on passive sales to end customers
- Fixed or minimum resale prices (RPM)
- Cross-territory restrictions in selective distribution

TSCs often fall within Article 4(b) (passive sales restrictions) -- manufacturers cannot legally prevent retailers from responding to unsolicited orders from other Member States.

## Policy Options (from EPRS "Tackling" briefing)

1. **Tighter enforcement of Article 101** via the Vertical Guidelines revision
2. **TSC-specific regulation** -- new EU legislative instrument targeting territorial supply practices
3. **Consumer / retailer rights directive** -- right to source cross-border with remedies
4. **Market monitoring mechanism** -- Commission + NCAs + consumer organisations
5. **Mandatory price transparency** -- manufacturer pan-EU wholesale price lists
6. **Parallel trade facilitation** -- streamlined processes for parallel importers
7. **Dual quality directive expansion** -- existing 2019/2161 (UCP-D) on dual quality food, extend to all branded goods

## Draghi and Letta Reports

Both strategic reports identified single-market fragmentation costs:
- **Letta Report** (April 2024): "EU's fragmented consumer markets prevent the pass-through of scale efficiencies; a genuinely integrated retail market could save EUR 50-100 billion annually"
- **Draghi Report** (September 2024): consumer goods sector competitiveness damaged by intra-EU price discrimination

## Current Commission Action

- **Single Market Strategy** (expected 2026): COM(2026) package on single market implementation is likely to include TSC measures
- **DG COMP**: ongoing cases against several FMCG manufacturers (details confidential)
- **DG GROW**: Single Market Enforcement Task-Force (SMET) coordinating with NCAs and consumer agencies
- **European Consumer Centres (ECC-Net)**: collecting TSC complaints
