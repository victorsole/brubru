# AI Regulatory Sandboxes: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 (rules for the AI Act's sandboxes)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 of 10 November 2026 laying down the common rules for the AI regulatory sandboxes that Article 57 of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) requires national authorities to set up. Adopted under Article 58(1) of the AI Act. CELEX 32026R2487, ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/2487/oj.
- **Dates: published in the Official Journal on Wednesday 11 November 2026; in force on the twentieth day after publication, which is 1 December 2026** (Article 10). In force? NOT yet as of 11 November 2026. No later application date: it applies from entry into force.
- **Who it binds: the national competent authorities that run sandboxes** (and the European Data Protection Supervisor for EU bodies). It does NOT add obligations for AI providers in general; it sets the terms for those who choose to apply. Participants stay responsible for complying with EU law (Article 2(6)).
- **Free for small firms (Article 2(3)):** access for SMEs, including start-ups, is prioritised and free of charge for regulatory guidance, advice and supervision. Authorities may recover only "exceptional costs" for other services agreed in advance, itemised on the invoice. Public authorities benefit from the same regime.
- **Larger firms (Article 2(4)):** authorities may charge the full cost of the services. **Providers with no registered office or branch in the EU (Article 2(5))** pay a participation fee and apply through an authorised representative.
- **Who can apply (Article 3):** any provider or prospective provider, in ANY Member State, not only where it is established. The AI system must be not yet on the market, or about to be substantially modified, or about to become high-risk through a change of use. Selection criteria: need for the sandbox, regulatory learning, public interest, maturity, innovation. **The authority answers within three months** of a complete application or of a call's deadline; the applicant can appeal.
- **Sandbox plan (Article 5):** agreed between the provider and the authority: timeline, objectives, which AI Act requirements are tested, personal data and the data protection authority, safeguards, real-world testing where relevant.
- **What you leave with (Article 6):** a **written proof** of the activities carried out and an **exit report**. Market surveillance authorities and notified bodies in every Member State must recognise both as documentation. The exit report is NOT a declaration of conformity.
- **Extension and suspension (Article 8):** extension by agreement up to 30 days before the planned end; the authority can suspend participation for significant risks to health, safety or fundamental rights that cannot be mitigated.
- **Reporting (Article 9):** authorities send annual reports to the AI Office and the AI Board and publish them or a summary.
- **What was NOT read:** the recitals in full. The deadline by which each Member State must have a sandbox running is in the AI Act itself, not in this act.
