# IPA III: Instrument for Pre-accession Assistance 2021-2027

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal basis:** Regulation (EU) 2021/1529 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 September 2021 establishing the Instrument for Pre-accession Assistance (IPA III), CELEX: 32021R1529, OJ L 330, 20.9.2021. Supplemented by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2128 (thematic priorities/programming framework) and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2236 (implementation rules).
- **Budget:** EUR 14.162 billion in current prices for 2021-2027 (EUR 13.804bn operational + EUR 357m administrative appropriations); total financial programming reaches roughly EUR 14.99bn once contributions are added.
- **Five thematic windows** (with indicative shares of the operational budget, per the Commission's own IPA III overview page):
  1. Rule of law, fundamental rights and democracy -- 15.13% / approx. EUR 2.089bn
  2. Good governance, EU acquis alignment, good neighbourly relations and strategic communication -- 16.59% / approx. EUR 2.291bn
  3. Green agenda and sustainable connectivity -- 42.25% / approx. EUR 5.860bn (the largest window by far)
  4. Competitiveness and inclusive growth -- 22.31% / approx. EUR 3.080bn
  5. Territorial and cross-border cooperation -- 3.51% / approx. EUR 485m
- **Beneficiaries:** Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, and Türkiye (candidate since 1999, negotiations formally suspended since 2018 -- Türkiye's IPA III access is correspondingly limited and reoriented toward civil society, people-to-people contacts and migration management rather than institution-building support). Moldova, Ukraine and Georgia access pre-accession-adjacent support mainly through regional and cross-border windows rather than as core IPA III beneficiaries.
- **Performance-based allocation, no fixed per-country envelopes (the key IPA II to IPA III shift):** IPA II (2014-2020) set indicative multiannual country allocations up front, largely on country size and negotiation progress, so each beneficiary could predict roughly what it would receive over seven years. IPA III abandons that model: there is a single common budget for all beneficiaries, and access to funds is driven by performance criteria (project/programme maturity, expected impact, progress on rule of law, fundamental rights and governance). The Commission still balances this against an overall "fair share" of assistance and predictability across the seven-year period, so performance is a modifier on top of a baseline expectation of fairness, not a pure competition.
- **"Reward for progress" principle:** faster, better-performing reformers can draw down more assistance and unlock top-ups; laggards on fundamentals risk reduced or reprioritised allocations (see the Serbia funding-freeze episode, April 2026, in `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement`).
- **Fundamentals-first conditionality:** rule of law, judiciary, fundamental rights, democratic institutions and public administration reform ("the fundamentals") are assessed first and gate progress in every other negotiating cluster; IPA III window 1 funding and RGF disbursements are both explicitly tied to fundamentals performance.
- **Managed by DG ENEST** (Directorate-General for Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood) -- created in the December 2024 Von der Leyen II reorganisation when the former single DG NEAR was split, with the Mediterranean/Gulf portfolio moving to the new DG MENA. Commissioner: Marta Kos (Enlargement, Slovenia).
- **Sits alongside, not inside, the Reform and Growth Facility (RGF):** a separate EUR 6 billion instrument for the Western Balkans (2024-2027, Regulation (EU) 2024/1449, in force 25 May 2024, EUR 2bn grants + EUR 4bn concessional loans), reinforcing IPA III rather than replacing it -- disbursements are milestone-conditional on the same fundamentals-first logic.
- **Complements NDICI-Global Europe:** EUR 79.5 billion instrument (Regulation (EU) 2021/947, CELEX: 32021R0947) covering the Eastern Partnership and wider external action; IPA III and NDICI share thematic architecture but IPA III is enlargement-specific.
- **IPARD III** is the agricultural/rural-development component of IPA III (see `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement` for grant-management detail).
- **Verified sources:** enlargement.ec.europa.eu/funding-technical-assistance/overview-instrument-pre-accession-assistance_en (budget + window percentages); EUR-Lex CELEX 32021R1529 (regulation text, title, budget figure).

## What IPA III is, and how it differs from IPA II

IPA III is the EU's principal financial instrument for supporting candidate countries (Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Türkiye) and potential candidates (Kosovo) on their path to membership, covering the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework period. It succeeds IPA II (2014-2020) and IPA I (2007-2013).

The structural break with IPA II is allocation logic. IPA II fixed indicative country envelopes at the outset of the period, calculated mainly from country size and negotiating progress at the time, giving each beneficiary a predictable multiannual ceiling. IPA III drops the pre-fixed envelope model in favour of a single common pot allocated on performance: project and programme maturity, expected impact, and demonstrated progress on rule of law, fundamental rights and governance determine how much of the pot a beneficiary can actually draw down in a given cycle. The regulation still requires the Commission to keep an eye on overall fairness and predictability across the seven years, so performance operates as a modifier on a baseline fair-share expectation rather than a winner-takes-most contest, but the direction of travel from IPA II to IPA III is unambiguous: reward reform delivery, do not simply reward being on the list.

This is the single most important thing to explain to a client or MEP asking "how much IPA money does country X get": there is no ex-ante country ceiling to quote. The honest answer is "as much as the country's reform delivery and project pipeline can absorb, within the Commission's overall fairness balancing," which is a genuinely different message from IPA II's fixed multiannual tables.

## The five thematic windows

IPA III assistance is programmed against five thematic windows (the Commission's operational language; earlier IPA III drafting used a six-cluster structure before consolidation into today's five windows for the indicative financial framework):

1. **Rule of law, fundamental rights and democracy** (approx. 15.13% / EUR 2.089bn). Judiciary independence and efficiency, anti-corruption, organised crime, fundamental rights, media freedom, civil society space. This window is the fundamentals gate: weak delivery here constrains disbursement across the whole programme.
2. **Good governance, EU acquis alignment, good neighbourly relations and strategic communication** (approx. 16.59% / EUR 2.291bn). Public administration reform, public financial management, statistical capacity, alignment with the EU acquis chapter by chapter, bilateral good-neighbourly relations (a live issue for several Western Balkans pairs), and strategic communication to counter disinformation about the accession process itself.
3. **Green agenda and sustainable connectivity** (approx. 42.25% / EUR 5.860bn -- by far the largest window). Environment, climate mitigation and adaptation, energy (including the clean-energy transition for coal-dependent Western Balkans economies), transport and connectivity infrastructure. IPA III overall targets 26.2% climate mainstreaming and 3.7% biodiversity mainstreaming across the whole instrument, and this window is where most of that spend lands.
4. **Competitiveness and inclusive growth** (approx. 22.31% / EUR 3.080bn). Private-sector development, education, employment, social policy, agriculture and rural development (IPARD III sits here), digital economy.
5. **Territorial and cross-border cooperation** (approx. 3.51% / EUR 485m). Interreg IPA cross-border programmes between Member States and beneficiaries, and beneficiary-to-beneficiary cooperation; co-financed at up to 85% by the EU.

Window 3's dominance (42% of the whole envelope) reflects the EU's twin bet that (a) energy and transport connectivity is the fastest way to visibly tie Western Balkans economies into the single market, and (b) decarbonising coal-heavy Western Balkans power systems is itself an accession-relevant acquis chapter (energy, environment, climate).

## Conditionality and the fundamentals-first methodology

IPA III is built to enforce the EU's "fundamentals-first" accession methodology, in place since the 2020 revised enlargement methodology: rule of law, judiciary reform, fundamental rights, democratic institutions and public administration reform (the "fundamentals cluster," Cluster 1 in accession negotiations) must show credible progress before other negotiating clusters advance, and delays or backsliding on fundamentals can reopen or slow already-closed clusters.

This gates IPA III money in two ways. First, window 1 funding is itself conditioned on demonstrated fundamentals progress, not simply promised reforms. Second, and increasingly consequential in practice, the parallel Reform and Growth Facility (RGF) makes fundamentals a formal disbursement precondition for its EUR 6bn envelope: each RGF tranche release for Albania, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia and (via amended implementing decision) Bosnia and Herzegovina is tied to a country-specific Reform Agenda whose milestones span rule of law, anti-corruption, single-market alignment, and green and digital transition. A country failing fundamentals benchmarks risks both a slower IPA III window 1 drawdown and a paused or reduced RGF tranche -- the two instruments now reinforce the same conditionality lever from different budget lines.

## Programming and management: DG ENEST

IPA III programming and management sits with DG ENEST (Directorate-General for Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood), created in the December 2024 Von der Leyen II Commission reorganisation when the former single DG NEAR (Neighbourhood and Enlargement Relations) was split in two: DG ENEST took the enlargement track (Western Balkans, Türkiye) and the Eastern Partnership (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Moldova, Ukraine); the Mediterranean, Middle East and Gulf portfolio moved to the newly created DG MENA. Commissioner Marta Kos (Slovenia) holds the Enlargement portfolio; Director-General Gert Jan Koopman leads DG ENEST.

Programming follows a multiannual indicative programming framework set out via Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2128, translated into country and multi-country action programmes adopted through Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2236 procedures. Financing Agreements signed under IPA II remain valid and are administered under DG ENEST continuity, alongside TAIEX (Technical Assistance and Information Exchange) and Twinning instruments used for shorter-cycle institution-building support that complements the larger IPA III programmes.

## Relationship to the Reform and Growth Facility and NDICI

| Instrument | Budget | Scope | Relationship to IPA III |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reform and Growth Facility (RGF) | EUR 6bn (2024-2027), Reg. (EU) 2024/1449 | Western Balkans single-market convergence, four pillars (single market integration, economic integration, green/digital transition, fundamentals) | Separate, newer instrument that reinforces IPA III; milestone-based disbursement on top of, not instead of, IPA III window funding |
| Moldova Growth Plan (RGF for Moldova) | EUR 1.9bn (2025-2027), Reg. (EU) 2025/535 | Moldova-specific reform-linked disbursement | Parallel RGF-model instrument outside IPA III (Moldova is not a core IPA III beneficiary) |
| NDICI-Global Europe | EUR 79.5bn, Reg. (EU) 2021/947 | Neighbourhood, development and international cooperation worldwide | Shares programming architecture and thematic language with IPA III but is the instrument for non-enlargement partners (Eastern Partnership, wider external action) |
| Ukraine Facility | EUR 50bn (2024-2027), Reg. (EU) 2024/792 | Ukraine-specific reconstruction and reform support | Outside IPA III; Ukraine's accession-linked support runs through this dedicated facility, not IPA III |

The practical takeaway for advocacy and client work: IPA III is the base pre-accession layer for the seven core enlargement beneficiaries; the RGF (and its Moldova sibling) is a newer, faster-disbursing, milestone-driven top-up layered on top of IPA III for the Western Balkans specifically; NDICI and the Ukraine Facility sit outside the IPA III perimeter entirely and should not be conflated with it in funding queries.

## Brubru tracking angles

- **Disbursement tranches:** track RGF tranche releases (they are announced country by country via DG ENEST/neighbourhood-enlargement.ec.europa.eu press releases) as the fastest visible signal of fundamentals-conditionality being applied in real time -- more informative than the underlying IPA III annual programming documents, which move slower and are less granular.
- **Window 1 vs window 3 balance:** a beneficiary's public communications often emphasise green/connectivity wins (window 3, the largest window) while its rule-of-law delivery (window 1) lags -- useful for cutting through spin in country progress reports.
- **Türkiye's asymmetric access:** any client query mentioning "Turkey IPA funding" needs the caveat that full institution-building access has been suspended since 2018; only civil society, people-to-people and migration-management lines remain active.
- **Serbia funding-freeze episode** (April 2026): concrete case study of the conditionality lever being pulled -- track for precedent on how the Commission actually applies performance-based reallocation in practice, not just in regulation text.
- **MFF 2028-2034 successor instrument:** IPA III's follow-on design is under negotiation as part of the next Multiannual Financial Framework; watch for whether performance-based allocation is retained, tightened, or partially reversed back toward fixed envelopes.
