# European Defence Fund (EDF)

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Defence Fund (EDF)
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2021/697 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2021, OJ L 170, 12.5.2021 (CELEX 32021R0697)
- Repeals: Regulation (EU) 2018/1092 (the pilot instrument that ran EDIDP)
- Budget: EUR 7.953 billion in current prices for 2021-2027 (often cited as "nearly EUR 7.3 billion" when expressed in 2018 constant prices, the MFF convention). Cut down from an original Commission proposal of EUR 13 billion during the 2020 MFF negotiations.
- Two funding windows: Research (roughly EUR 2.65-2.7 billion, up to 100% EU co-financing) and Capability Development (roughly EUR 5.3 billion, up to 80% EU co-financing, supplementing Member States' own contributions)
- Managing authority: European Commission, DG DEFIS (Directorate-General for Defence Industry and Space), implementing via annual work programmes
- Eligibility: consortia of at least 3 legal entities established in 3 different EU Member States or Norway (the only associated third country); no entity controlled by a non-associated third country without an approved guarantee; recipients and subcontractors must have their executive management structure in the EU
- Bonus mechanisms: extra funding rate for SME and mid-cap participation, for cross-border SME involvement, and for projects aligned with PESCO (Permanent Structured Cooperation) priorities
- Predecessors: Preparatory Action on Defence Research (PADR, 2017-2019) and European Defence Industrial Development Programme (EDIDP, 2019-2020) -- combined pilot budget EUR 590 million
- EU Defence Innovation Scheme (EUDIS): the EDF sub-scheme dedicated to start-ups, SMEs and non-traditional defence players, with cascade funding to lower the entry barrier
- 2026 Work Programme: adopted by the Commission on 17 December 2025, EUR 1 billion for R&D, 31 call topics across 7 thematic calls, 3 non-thematic calls, 1 hypersonic-glide-vehicle action and 2 EU Alliance for defence medical countermeasures actions; calls opened January 2026 on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal, deadlines running through September/October 2026
- Cumulative deployment: by March 2025, 5 annual EDF work programmes adopted, roughly EUR 5.4 billion committed (EUR 6 billion including the PADR/EDIDP precursors)
- Sits inside the wider Readiness 2030 / ReArm Europe package (EUR 800 billion mobilisation) alongside SAFE (EUR 150 billion loans), EDIP (EUR 1.5 billion grants for joint procurement) and national fiscal escape-clause space
- Commissioner: Andrius Kubilius (Defence and Space)
- Flagship EDF-funded projects: Future Combat Air System (FCAS) components, European Patrol Corvette, next-generation ground combat systems
- Verify EDF envelope + EDIP/EDIRPA status live -- figures move as new work programmes and MFF 2028-2034 negotiations progress.

## Overview

The European Defence Fund is the EU's flagship instrument for co-financing collaborative defence research and development. It sits upstream of procurement: EDF money funds the R&D and prototyping phase that produces the technologies and demonstrators later bought through EDIP, national programmes or NATO-coordinated acquisition. Before the EDF, EU-level defence R&D funding barely existed -- defence was treated as a national prerogative exempted from EU competence. The 2017-2020 pilot programmes (PADR for research, EDIDP for development) proved the legal and political model; the EDF, established by Regulation (EU) 2021/697, scaled it into a full seven-year Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) line for the first time.

The EDF's stated aim is to foster the competitiveness, efficiency and innovation capacity of the European Defence Technological and Industrial Base (EDTIB), reduce duplication of national defence R&D spending, and increase interoperability between Member States' armed forces. It is explicitly designed to counter the fragmentation that leaves the EU with 178 separate weapon system types against roughly 30 in the United States.

## Budget and the Two Windows

| Window | Approximate budget | EU co-financing rate | What it funds |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|----------------|
| Research | EUR 2.65-2.7 billion | Up to 100% | Fundamental and applied research into future defence technologies |
| Capability Development | EUR 5.3 billion | Up to 80% (supplementing national contributions) | Prototyping, testing, qualification and certification of new defence products and technologies |

The total EDF envelope is usually quoted as EUR 7.953 billion in current prices (the figure written into the Regulation) or "nearly EUR 7.3 billion" in 2018 constant prices, the accounting convention the Commission also uses for MFF headline figures -- both numbers describe the same envelope and are not in conflict. The original 2018 Commission proposal was EUR 13 billion; it was cut roughly in half during the COVID-shadowed 2020 MFF negotiations.

## Eligibility

To receive EDF funding, a consortium must include **at least three legal entities established in three different Member States or Norway** (the only country associated to the EDF alongside the 27 Member States). Key conditions:

- Recipients and subcontractors must be EU-based, with their executive management structure located in the Union
- Entities controlled by a non-associated third country or by a third-country national cannot receive EDF funding unless they obtain Commission-approved guarantees demonstrating the entity will not be subject to control or restriction by that third country
- Non-associated third-country entities may participate under strict conditions safeguarding EU security and defence interests, but cannot access classified information or receive EU funding directly
- Bonus funding rates apply for SME participation, mid-cap participation, cross-border SME involvement, and alignment with PESCO capability priorities

## Programme Structure and How to Apply

The EDF is implemented through **annual work programmes** adopted by the Commission (DG DEFIS), each organised along roughly 30 thematic and horizontal action categories covering land systems, air combat, naval defence, cyber, space, and disruptive technologies. Calls for proposals are published on the **EU Funding & Tenders Portal** (the "SEDIA" portal), the same submission system used across EU research programmes including Horizon Europe.

The 2026 Work Programme, adopted by the College on 17 December 2025, earmarks EUR 1 billion for collaborative R&D: 31 call topics across seven thematic calls, three non-thematic calls, one action targeting threats from hypersonic glide vehicles, and two actions supporting the EU Alliance for defence medical countermeasures. Around EUR 60 million is reserved for disruptive technologies and a further EUR 60 million for non-thematic SME calls. Calls opened in January 2026 with submission deadlines running through September/October 2026.

### EU Defence Innovation Scheme (EUDIS)

EUDIS is the EDF's dedicated channel for start-ups, SMEs and non-traditional defence suppliers -- companies that would otherwise struggle to navigate a defence-specific consortium requirement. It uses cascade funding (smaller sub-grants distributed through intermediary projects) and lighter administrative requirements to widen participation beyond established defence primes.

## Predecessors: PADR and EDIDP

The EDF was not built from scratch. Two pilot programmes tested the model during the 2014-2020 MFF:

- **PADR** (Preparatory Action on Defence Research, 2017-2019): the first-ever EU budget line for defence research, modest in scale but politically significant as proof that Article 173 and 182 TFEU could support defence-related EU spending
- **EDIDP** (European Defence Industrial Development Programme, 2019-2020): extended EU co-financing to the development phase (prototyping), established under Regulation (EU) 2018/1092

Combined, PADR and EDIDP committed around EUR 590 million -- a fraction of the EDF's EUR 7.953 billion, but the legal and institutional template both instruments established (consortium rules, DG DEFIS management, work-programme structure) carried directly into the EDF Regulation.

## EDF in the Wider Defence Funding Landscape

The EDF is one of several EU defence instruments that have multiplied since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. It is distinct from, but feeds into, the following:

| Instrument | Focus | Budget | Relationship to EDF |
|------------|-------|--------|----------------------|
| EDF | Collaborative R&D and capability development | EUR 7.953B (2021-2027) | The base programme this guide covers |
| EDIP | Joint procurement of mature defence equipment | EUR 1.5B (2025-2027) | Downstream of EDF -- procures what EDF helped develop |
| EDIRPA | Predecessor to EDIP, urgent joint procurement | EUR 310M (2023-2025) | Superseded by EDIP |
| ASAP | Ammunition production scale-up | EUR 500M | Parallel, production-focused instrument |
| SAFE | Loans to Member States for national defence investment | EUR 150B (loans, not grants) | Parallel financing channel under Readiness 2030, Article 122 TFEU |
| AGILE | Rapid deployment of disruptive defence tech from New Defence players | EUR 115M (2027) | Bridges EDF research outputs into rapid fielding |

For the loan-based SAFE instrument and the broader Readiness 2030 / ReArm Europe package, see **safe_rearm_europe**. For the full joint-procurement landscape (EDIP, EDIRPA, ASAP, the 35% collaborative-procurement target) see **eu_defence_procurement**.

## Institutional Actors

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| DG DEFIS | Lead Commission department, drafts and manages annual EDF work programmes |
| Commissioner Kubilius | Defence and Space portfolio, political sponsor of the EDF |
| European Defence Agency (EDA) | Advisory role on capability priorities, coordinates with the Capability Development Plan (CDP) |
| SEDE subcommittee (EP) | Parliamentary oversight of defence spending and EDF implementation |
| ITRE committee (EP) | Industrial and research dimension of EDF-funded projects |
| PESCO participating Member States | Capability projects that receive EDF bonus funding when aligned |
| NATO | Capability targets referenced in EDF priority-setting, avoiding transatlantic duplication |
