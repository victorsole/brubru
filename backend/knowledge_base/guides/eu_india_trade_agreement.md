- **This is a procedural stage, not entry into force.** Nothing is signed, nothing applies, no tariff has moved. The Commission has asked the Council for authorisation. Anyone told that the India deal "is in force" in September 2026 has been told something false.
- **Negotiations concluded**: **27 January 2026 in New Delhi**. The Commission describes it as the largest trade agreement ever concluded by either side, and the most ambitious trade opening India has ever granted a partner.
- **Headline market access**: tariffs eliminated or reduced on **96% of EU goods exports to India** (the conclusion press release of 27 January 2026 gave the figure as 96.6%), saving around **EUR 4 billion per year** in duties on European products.
- **Scale**: the EU and India already trade over **EUR 180 billion** in goods and services per year, supporting close to **800,000 EU jobs**. Commissioner Šefčovič describes the combined market as 2 billion people and around a quarter of global GDP.
- **Two separate negotiations are still open and must not be conflated with the FTA**: the **Investment Protection Agreement** and the **Geographical Indications Agreement** are both still **being negotiated**. Only the FTA has been concluded.
- **Lead**: DG Trade and Economic Security. **Commissioner**: Maroš Šefčovič (Trade and Economic Security). **EP committee**: INTA (International Trade). **Legal basis**: Article 207 TFEU for the substance, Article 218 TFEU for the procedure.

## What the agreement is

The EU-India Free Trade Agreement is a comprehensive trade agreement covering goods, services, investment access, public procurement disciplines, intellectual property, sustainable development and small business facilitation. Negotiations were relaunched on 8 May 2021, when EU and Indian leaders agreed to pursue a balanced, ambitious, comprehensive and mutually beneficial agreement, and to open two separate negotiations in parallel on investment protection and on geographical indications.

The pace changed after the **College of Commissioners visited India on 28 February 2025**, when President von der Leyen and Prime Minister Modi jointly agreed to accelerate. Negotiations were concluded on **27 January 2026**.

## Where the file actually stands

This is the distinction that matters most, because the vocabulary is easy to blur.

| Stage | Status as at 14 September 2026 |
|---|---|
| Negotiations concluded | **Done**, 27 January 2026 |
| Legal revision and translation into all official languages | Carried out after conclusion |
| Commission proposals to the Council for signature and conclusion | **Done, 11 September 2026** |
| Council decision authorising signature | **Pending** |
| Signature by the EU and India | **Pending** |
| European Parliament consent | **Pending** |
| Council decision on conclusion | **Pending** |
| Indian internal ratification | Under way in parallel |
| Entry into force | **Not reached** |

"Presented to the Council for signature" means the Commission has formally asked Member States, sitting in the Council, to authorise the EU to sign. It creates no rights and no obligations for traders. Tariff schedules bite only on entry into force.

## The institutional path still ahead

1. **Council decision on signature.** The Council acts on the Commission proposal COM(2026) 483. Trade agreements under the Common Commercial Policy are decided in the Foreign Affairs Council in its Trade configuration.
2. **Signature.** Once authorised, the EU and India sign the agreement.
3. **European Parliament consent.** The Parliament must give consent before conclusion. **INTA** is the lead committee. Consent is a single yes or no vote on the whole text: the Parliament cannot amend a concluded international agreement, which makes the INTA scrutiny phase the real point of influence for stakeholders.
4. **Council decision on conclusion.** On the Commission proposal COM(2026) 482, after Parliament has consented.
5. **Indian ratification.** The Indian authorities are running their own internal procedures in parallel.
6. **Entry into force.** Only once both sides have completed their steps.

A caution on national ratification: the Commission's 11 September announcement sets out the Council, Parliament consent and conclusion sequence and does not state that ratification by all Member State parliaments is required. Whether any part of the package is a mixed agreement needing national ratification depends on the final legal architecture of the texts, which could not be verified from the sources available on 14 September 2026. Do not assert either way without reading the Council decisions.

## What the agreement contains

### Industrial tariffs
India grants the EU tariff reductions it has not given any other trading partner. Tariffs on cars fall gradually from 110% to as low as 10%, and are fully abolished on car parts after five to ten years. Tariffs of up to 44% on machinery, 22% on chemicals and 11% on pharmaceuticals are mostly eliminated. The Commission expects EU goods exports to India to double by 2032.

### Agri-food
The agreement removes or reduces tariffs that average over 36% on EU agri-food exports. Indian tariffs on wine fall from 150% to 75% at entry into force and eventually to levels as low as 20%. Olive oil goes from 45% to zero over five years. Processed agricultural products such as bread and confectionery see tariffs of up to 50% eliminated.

Sensitive European sectors are protected: **beef, chicken meat, rice and sugar are excluded from liberalisation**. All Indian imports must continue to meet EU health and food safety rules.

### Services and investment access
EU companies get privileged access to the Indian services market, including financial services and maritime transport. The Commission describes India's financial services commitments as its most ambitious in any trade agreement, going beyond what India has granted other partners.

### Intellectual property
A high level of protection and enforcement covering copyright, trade marks, designs, trade secrets and plant variety rights, building on existing international treaties and bringing the two systems closer together.

### Sustainability
A dedicated trade and sustainable development chapter covering environmental protection, climate change, workers' rights, women's empowerment, a dialogue platform and implementation provisions. Alongside it, the EU and India agreed to sign a Memorandum of Understanding establishing an EU-India platform for cooperation on climate action, with **EUR 500 million** of EU support over two years envisaged for India's emission reduction and industrial transformation, subject to the EU's budgetary rules.

### Small businesses
A dedicated chapter for small and medium enterprises, including contact points on each side to help smaller firms use the agreement's provisions.

## The two parallel tracks

| Track | Status as at 14 September 2026 |
|---|---|
| **Free Trade Agreement** | Concluded, proposals with the Council |
| **Investment Protection Agreement** | Still being negotiated |
| **Geographical Indications Agreement** | Still being negotiated |

The GI agreement matters commercially for EU producers, because it is the instrument that would curb imitations of protected European food and drink names on the Indian market. It is not part of the FTA and is not covered by the September 2026 proposals.

## Trade picture

- The EU is **India's third-largest trading partner**, with trade in goods worth **EUR 118 billion in 2025**, or 11.1% of India's total trade.
- India is the **EU's ninth-largest trading partner**, accounting for 2.3% of the EU's total goods trade, behind the United States (17.6%), China (14.7%) and the United Kingdom (9.8%).
- EU-India goods trade has grown **83.7% in the last decade**.
- Trade in services was **EUR 67 billion in 2024**, of which EU exports were EUR 29 billion.
- EU foreign direct investment stock in India reached **EUR 132.8 billion in 2024**, up from EUR 105.1 billion in 2021. India's stock in the EU was EUR 13.7 billion.
- Around **6,000 European companies** are present in India.
- EU imports from India are mainly chemicals, machinery and appliances, mineral products, textiles and base metals. EU exports to India are mainly machinery and appliances, transport equipment, optical and medical instruments, and chemicals.

The Commission's own assessment notes that India's trade regime remains relatively restrictive, with technical barriers to trade, sanitary and phytosanitary measures and deviations from international standards affecting goods, services, procurement and investment. That is the baseline the agreement is meant to change.

## Standing bilateral machinery

- **EU-India High-Level Dialogue on Trade and Investment**, established in 2021 by a decision of the July 2020 EU-India Summit.
- **EU-India Trade Sub-Commission**, established under the 1994 Cooperation and Partnership Agreement, with specialised working groups.
- **EU-India Trade and Technology Council**, whose **third meeting was held in Brussels on 15 July 2026**.
