Guide for EU policy professionals on the European Court of Auditors: institutional mandate, audit methodology, discharge procedure, and relevance for advocacy strategy.

## Treaty Basis and Institutional Status

The European Court of Auditors was established in 1975 by the Treaty of Brussels and upgraded to full EU institution status by the Maastricht Treaty in 1992. Its founding provisions are codified in Articles 285-287 TFEU.

**Article 285 TFEU** establishes the ECA as the EU's external auditor and defines its composition as one Member per Member State. The ECA exercises its functions in full independence.

**Article 286 TFEU** sets out the appointment procedure: Members are appointed by the Council, acting by qualified majority, after consulting the European Parliament, from among persons who belong or have belonged to external audit bodies in their respective Member States or who are especially qualified for the audit role. Terms are six years and renewable. Members are fully independent from any external instruction.

**Article 287 TFEU** defines the mandate: the ECA shall examine the accounts of all EU revenue and expenditure, examine whether revenue has been received and expenditure incurred in a lawful and regular manner, and check whether financial management has been sound. The Treaty expressly mandates the Statement of Assurance (DAS -- *déclaration d'assurance*) covering the reliability of the accounts and the legality and regularity of the underlying transactions.

**Institutional status:** The ECA is not a court in the judicial sense. It does not adjudicate or sanction. It is the EU's supreme audit institution, with full independence from the Commission, Parliament, and Council. Its findings are authoritative but non-binding -- the sanctioning function rests with the EP (discharge), OLAF (administrative investigation) and EPPO (criminal prosecution).

### Composition and Appointment

The College of 27 Members reflects the one-per-Member-State principle, though Members act in full independence and are not representatives of national governments. Each Member State nominates a candidate; the Council appoints by qualified majority after consulting the European Parliament. Six-year terms are renewable.

The President is elected by the Members from among themselves for a renewable three-year term. The President chairs Court meetings, oversees institutional management, and represents the ECA externally in relations with Parliament, the Council, and the Commission.

## The Five Audit Chambers and CEAD

The ECA's operational work is organised into five thematic audit chambers, each headed by a Dean elected by the chamber Members.

| Chamber | Name | Policy scope |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **I** | Sustainable use of natural resources | Agriculture (CAP), fisheries, environment, climate, rural development |
| **II** | Investment for cohesion, growth and inclusion | Structural and cohesion funds (ERDF, ESF+, JTF, Cohesion Fund), employment, education, research and innovation |
| **III** | External action, security and justice | External aid (NDICI-Global Europe), humanitarian aid (ECHO), enlargement, CFSP/CSDP, migration, justice and home affairs |
| **IV** | Regulation of markets and competitive economy | Internal market, competition, banking supervision (SSM/SRM), customs, taxation, trade |
| **V** | Financing and administering the Union | EU budget, own resources, administrative expenditure, CAP Pillar I direct payments, MFF management |

**CEAD (Coordination, Evaluation, Assurance and Development)** is a cross-chamber body rather than a thematic audit chamber. CEAD handles:
- audit quality control and methodology (aligning ECA practice with ISSAI standards)
- strategy and institutional planning
- evaluation and performance monitoring of the ECA's own work programme
- inter-institutional coordination (with CONT, COCOBU, Council)
- relations with national supreme audit institutions and international audit bodies

Together, Chambers I-V and CEAD constitute the full working organisation of the College.

### Chamber Deans

Each audit chamber is headed by a Dean elected by chamber Members for a two-year term. The Dean chairs chamber meetings, coordinates audit work programmes, and presents chamber outputs to the full Court for adoption. Adoption is collegiate: the full 27-member Court votes on all audit reports and opinions.

## Joint Audit Directorate for Cohesion (DAC)

The DAC is a cross-chamber operational structure dedicated to the audit of EU shared management funds, primarily cohesion policy under Common Provisions Regulation (EU) 2021/1060. It operates under the oversight of Chambers I and II.

| Sector | Geographic coverage |
|--------|-------------------|
| DAC.1 | Italy, Malta, Slovenia, Croatia, Finland, Denmark, Sweden |
| DAC.2 | France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Ireland |
| DAC.3 | Germany, Austria, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary |
| DAC.4 | Spain, Portugal, Greece, Cyprus |
| DAC.5 | Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia |
| DAC.A | Horizontal: methodology, coordination, IT tools, Commission liaison |

The DAC produces shared management error rate estimates that feed into the DAS and inform the Commission's audit assurance chain (Managing Authorities, Audit Authorities, Commission DG acceptance of accounts). For detailed DAC methodology -- Arachne risk scoring, materiality thresholds, flat-rate correction levels, and the annual assurance deadline of 15 February -- see `cohesion_policy_audit.md`.

## Audit Types

### Financial Audit

Financial audit examines whether EU accounts present a true and fair view of the EU's financial position and results of operations. The ECA audits the consolidated accounts prepared by DG BUDG / the Commission's Accounting Officer. The primary output is the annual opinion on account reliability included in the DAS.

The ECA follows International Standards on Supreme Audit Institutions (ISSAI) adapted for the EU context. The consolidated accounts have been signed off as reliable (unqualified opinion on accounts) every year since 2007.

### Compliance Audit

Compliance audit assesses whether revenue and expenditure transactions comply with applicable EU law and financial regulations. This is the source of the "error rate" estimates reported annually and the element most prominently cited in political debate. The ECA uses statistical sampling, Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS), and stratified random sampling to project error rates to the full population of expenditure.

The DAS compliance opinion is the politically sensitive element: the overall estimated error rate for EU payments has declined significantly since the early 2000s but has never reached an unqualified (clean) compliance opinion across all budget areas, reflecting the structural complexity of 80% of the EU budget being implemented via shared management with 27 Member States.

### Performance Audit (Value for Money)

Performance audit examines whether EU programmes and policies achieve their objectives economically, efficiently and effectively (the "3 Es"):

| "E" | Audit question |
|-----|---------------|
| **Economy** | Were inputs acquired at the lowest reasonable cost? |
| **Efficiency** | Was the best output obtained from the inputs? |
| **Effectiveness** | Were the stated objectives achieved? |

Performance audit is the primary vehicle for Special Reports. It is discretionary (the ECA selects topics on the basis of its annual work programme) and covers specific policies, programmes, management systems, and institutional arrangements. Special reports do not result in a binary pass/fail opinion; they produce findings and recommendations addressed to the Commission, Member States, or both.

## Statement of Assurance (DAS): Methodology and Significance

The DAS is issued annually under Article 287(1) TFEU as the centrepiece of the ECA Annual Report. It consists of two distinct opinions:

1. **Opinion on accounts:** Have the consolidated EU accounts been drawn up in accordance with applicable accounting rules and do they give a true and fair view of the EU's financial position?
2. **Opinion on legality and regularity:** Do the underlying revenue and expenditure transactions comply with the relevant EU law and financial rules?

### Materiality Framework

The ECA applies a **2% materiality threshold** for the DAS compliance opinion. A quantified estimated error rate at or above 2% constitutes material error and triggers a qualified opinion on that budget area.

| Estimated error rate | DAS opinion on that area |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Below 2% | Unqualified (clean) -- reasonable assurance |
| 2% or above | Qualified -- material error identified |
| Pervasive material error | Adverse (rare in practice) |

The Commission's own error rate estimates (published in DG annual activity reports) use the same 2% benchmark. The ECA's independent estimates serve as a cross-check on the Commission's self-assessment.

### Historic DAS Trend

For many years the overall DAS compliance opinion on payments was adverse or heavily qualified. The estimated error rate has declined substantially since 2010, driven by improved management and control systems in shared management. Cohesion policy consistently shows higher error rates (typically 4-7%) than other budget areas due to the complexity of verifying eligibility rules across thousands of operations in 27 Member States. Direct management expenditure (research grants, external aid directly administered by the Commission) typically shows lower error rates (2-3%). Administrative expenditure (staff costs of institutions) is consistently below 1%.

## Product Types

### Annual Report on the EU Budget

Published each autumn (typically October/November), the Annual Report is the ECA's central publication. It comprises:
- the DAS (reliability of accounts plus compliance opinions by budget heading)
- analysis of each major budget heading: agriculture, cohesion, research and innovation, external action, administration
- error rate estimates per heading and for the overall budget
- follow-up on prior year recommendations (implementation rate by the Commission)
- specific findings on EU revenue (own resources)

The Annual Report is published in the Official Journal of the EU (OJ C series) and presented to the EP CONT committee and to the Council COCOBU formation.

### Annual Reports on Agencies and Joint Undertakings

Each EU decentralised agency, executive agency, and joint undertaking receives its own annual ECA report covering accounts and compliance. With approximately 40 bodies subject to annual ECA audit, these reports are issued as a stream through the autumn and winter cycle (typically November to February). Each feeds into a separate discharge procedure specific to that body, also handled by EP CONT.

### Special Reports

Special Reports are the ECA's flagship performance audit product. Approximately 18 are published per year, each addressing a specific EU programme, policy area, or management question. Structure: context and background, audit scope and approach, observations, conclusions, and numbered recommendations with Commission formal response appended.

Special Reports are increasingly cited in EP legislative debates, Council working party discussions, stakeholder advocacy, and academic analysis. They represent first-rank independent intelligence on EU programme effectiveness and implementation gaps. Each Special Report is freely available in all official EU languages.

**Finding Special Reports:** https://www.eca.europa.eu/en/search-publications -- filter by "Special Report" plus topic area plus year.

### Opinions

The ECA issues Opinions on legislative proposals when requested by an EU institution, or on its own initiative, when proposals have significant implications for financial management, accountability, or the ECA's audit mandate. Opinions are non-binding but carry considerable institutional weight in legislative processes. The ECA has issued Opinions on the Financial Regulation, on own resources proposals, on the structure of the MFF, and on specific programme regulations with complex audit provisions.

### Reviews

Rapid case reviews and landscape reviews cover specific audit questions without the full statistical sampling methodology of a Special Report. Used for emerging topics, preparatory scoping, or rapid responses to institutional demands from Parliament or the Council.

### Audit Compendia

Thematic compilations of ECA findings across several years of special reports on a related policy area (for example, a compendium of all cohesion policy audit findings across an entire programming period). Useful for advocacy professionals tracking accumulated evidence on a policy dossier.

### ECA Journal

The ECA Journal is a periodical thematic publication, not an official audit report. Each edition focuses on a specific topic and features contributions from ECA auditors, Members, and external experts including academics, national auditors, and policymakers. The journal explicitly states that content represents the views of authors and not the official position of the ECA. Distributed free of charge through the ECA website and social media channels.

## European Parliament Discharge Cycle

The discharge procedure under Article 319 TFEU is the primary accountability mechanism for EU spending. The ECA is its central technical pillar, providing the independent audit evidence on which Parliament's discharge decision is based.

### Annual Discharge Calendar

| Stage | Lead actor | Typical timing |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| ECA Annual Report adopted | ECA Court | October (year N+1) |
| Annual Report published in OJ | Publications Office | October/November |
| Annual Report presented to CONT | ECA President + Members | November |
| CONT selects discharge rapporteur | EP CONT | November |
| Questionnaire to Commission | CONT | December to January |
| Hearings with ECA Members and Commission | CONT | February to March |
| CONT votes discharge recommendation | EP CONT | March/April |
| EP plenary discharge vote | EP | May (year N+2) |
| Follow-up on conditions/postponement | Commission, Member States | Ongoing |

The EP may grant discharge, postpone discharge (requesting further information), or in exceptional cases refuse discharge. Refusal does not invalidate the budget exercise but carries significant political consequences; it has occurred twice in EU history (1998 for the 1996 budget, triggering the resignation of the Santer Commission). Postponement is more common and is used as a pressure instrument on the Commission to implement audit recommendations.

### CONT Committee Collaboration

The EP Committee on Budgetary Control (CONT) is the ECA's primary interlocutor in Parliament. CONT:
- receives and scrutinises the Annual Report and all Special Reports touching on budget implementation
- holds structured hearings with ECA Members (chamber deans) on specific audit findings
- follows up on ECA recommendations through discharge resolutions and own-initiative reports
- coordinates with the Council's Budget Committee (COCOBU) on discharge-related matters
- conducts its own on-the-spot visits to Member States and programmes

ECA Members responsible for chambers producing relevant findings are regularly invited to CONT hearings. The discharge rapporteur (an MEP from the CONT committee) leads parliamentary questioning of the Commission on audit findings and prepares the discharge recommendation for plenary vote.

### Discharge for Agencies

Each EU agency and joint undertaking has its own discharge vote, typically handled by CONT with the relevant sectoral committee as associated committee. The discharge rapporteur for agencies is usually the same CONT member across multiple bodies in the same policy cluster.

## Fraud Referrals: OLAF and EPPO

The ECA's mandate is external audit, not investigation or prosecution. When auditors encounter evidence of suspected fraud or serious irregularities, they refer the matter to:

**OLAF (European Anti-Fraud Office):** conducts administrative investigations into EU fraud, corruption, and misuse of funds. OLAF examines ECA referrals, conducts its own investigations (with powers to inspect premises and interview witnesses), and makes administrative recommendations to the Commission (for recovery or disciplinary action) or to national authorities (for criminal prosecution). OLAF has no prosecutorial powers.

**EPPO (European Public Prosecutor's Office):** since June 2021, EPPO is the primary recipient of criminal-law referrals affecting the EU's financial interests in its 22 participating Member States. When ECA audit evidence suggests criminal fraud rather than administrative error, referral goes to EPPO. See `eppo_european_public_prosecutor.md` for EPPO's jurisdiction, competence, and the OLAF-EPPO working relationship.

The boundary between administrative irregularity (administrative referral to OLAF) and criminal fraud (referral to EPPO) is not always clear at the point of ECA discovery; both channels may receive parallel referrals for the same facts. The Commission's own anti-fraud services and national audit authorities play a complementary role in the referral chain.

## International Cooperation

### Contact Committee of EU Supreme Audit Institutions

The Contact Committee brings together the presidents of all EU Member State supreme audit institutions (SAIs) and the ECA President for peer exchange on audit approaches, methodology, and cross-border EU spending. Given that approximately 80% of the EU budget is implemented via shared management, SAI-ECA coordination is operationally critical: national SAIs audit their countries' management and control systems while the ECA performs the independent cross-EU assessment for the DAS.

The Contact Committee publishes joint reports on cross-cutting topics (for example, the audit of the Recovery and Resilience Facility, or of cohesion policy across the programming period).

### INTOSAI

The International Organisation of Supreme Audit Institutions develops the ISSAI standards (International Standards of Supreme Audit Institutions) that form the methodological backbone of all ECA audits. The ECA participates actively in INTOSAI standard-setting bodies and contributes to the development of standards specifically relevant to supranational audit (a context without precise ISSAI precedent at the national level).

### EUROSAI

EUROSAI is the European regional organisation within INTOSAI, bringing together approximately 50 European SAIs (including non-EU member states). It facilitates capacity building, peer review, and cooperative audit projects. The ECA serves as the secretariat for the EUROSAI working group on the audit of EU funds.

## Brubru Tracking Angles

- **Annual Report alerts:** the October/November Annual Report is a primary advocacy intelligence input; error rate estimates by heading directly inform risk profiles for clients receiving EU funds in cohesion, agriculture, or research
- **Special Report monitoring:** approximately 18 per year across all policy areas; each is a ready-made evidence base on programme effectiveness; use the publication portal to filter by sector and year
- **ECA Opinions on legislation:** check for ECA Opinions before advising clients on accountability provisions in new spending regulations or financial rule revisions; ECA views carry weight in CONT and COCOBU deliberations
- **CONT hearings calendar:** track CONT committee agenda for ECA Member appearances; chamber deans appear when specific programmes are under parliamentary scrutiny
- **DAS error rate trends:** cohesion (typically 4-7%), agriculture (2-4%), research (2-3%) are baseline risk benchmarks for compliance advice
- **MFF 2028-2034 nexus:** ECA Opinions and Special Reports on post-2027 budget design feed directly into MFF negotiations; see `mff_2028_2034.md` for the legislative state of play
- **RRF/NRRP audits:** ECA has published dedicated Special Reports on Recovery and Resilience Facility implementation; directly relevant for clients receiving NRRP funds; see `eu_recovery_resilience_facility.md`
- **Discharge as advocacy leverage:** if a client's sector is under ECA scrutiny in an Annual Report or Special Report, the discharge resolution is the mechanism by which Parliament formally demands Commission action; advocacy timed to the CONT-rapporteur preparation window (November to March) achieves maximum leverage
