# European Competitiveness Fund: the proposed flagship of MFF 2028-2034

## QUICK FACTS
- **Status: PROPOSAL, not adopted.** The European Competitiveness Fund (ECF) was proposed by the European Commission on **16 July 2025** as the cornerstone of MFF Heading 2 in the 2028-2034 multiannual budget package. It is still being negotiated by the Council and the European Parliament; no figure below is final until the co-legislators agree and the regulation enters into force.
- **Legal basis (proposal stage):** Regulation proposal COM(2025)555, corrigendum COM(2025)555/1, impact assessment SWD(2025)555 and summary SWD(2025)556. Part of the wider MFF package alongside COM(2025)570 (MFF Regulation) and COM(2025)571 (Interinstitutional Agreement), all dated 16 July 2025.
- **Proposed envelope (as tabled by the Commission, HEDGE, under negotiation):** the Commission's own DG GROW page (`single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/access-finance/european-competitiveness-fund_en`) states an ECF allocation of **EUR 234 billion**, which combined with the proposed FP10/Horizon Europe successor (**EUR 175 billion**) totals **EUR 409 billion**, described as roughly **21% of the future EU budget**. Brubru's `mff_2028_2034` guide, tracking the wider Heading 2 envelope (ECF + FP10 + defence/space + education/mobility etc.), cites **EUR 410 billion (23.3% of the total MFF)** for Heading 2 as a whole, the small variance reflects rounding and differing scopes (ECF-plus-FP10 narrow figure vs full-Heading-2 figure) across Commission communications, not a change in the underlying proposal. Treat both as proposal-stage and provisional.
- **Proposed policy windows (per the DG GROW page, four windows as currently presented):** Clean Transition & Industrial Decarbonisation (~EUR 26.2 billion); Health & Biotech / Agriculture & Bioeconomy (~EUR 20.4 billion); Digital Leadership (~EUR 51.5 billion); Resilience & Security / Defence / Space (~EUR 125.2 billion). These four sum to roughly EUR 223 billion, with the remainder of the EUR 234 billion ECF envelope covering horizontal instruments (InvestEU-style guarantees, advisory services). Companion guide `fp10_ecf_competitiveness` describes an earlier/parallel Commission framing of "policy windows" (AI/digital, space, clean tech, biotech, defence, health, agriculture) drawn from the ECF regulation text and impact assessment, window naming has evolved between the July 2025 legislative text and later Commission communications materials; verify the live window taxonomy against the current regulation text before citing a definitive count.
- **What it consolidates:** the ECF merges roughly 13-14 existing funding instruments into one framework, including EU4Health (Reg. 2021/522), Digital Europe Programme (Reg. 2021/694), European Defence Fund (Reg. 2021/697), LIFE Programme (Reg. 2021/783), and parts of the EU Space Programme (Reg. 2021/696, Reg. 2023/588). It builds directly on the model and track record of **InvestEU** (cited by the Commission as having mobilised close to EUR 300 billion in investment since inception, each EUR of guarantee supporting roughly EUR 15.20 of total investment) and continues the logic of **STEP** (the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform, EUR 29 billion mobilised under the current 2021-2027 MFF), which the Commission describes as the pilot proving the case for a single, simplified competitiveness instrument.
- **Governance model (proposed):** the ECF adopts InvestEU's governance blueprint, a single fund with thematic "policy windows," common eligibility and simplification rules, an advisory hub, and a lending/guarantee capacity layered on top of direct grants. A new **Competitiveness Coordination Tool** is proposed to provide strategic steering across MFF headings so that ECF investment aligns with wider EU competitiveness priorities (Draghi-style economic security and strategic-autonomy goals).
- **Political origin:** the ECF design directly operationalises the **Draghi Report on EU Competitiveness** (September 2024, identifying an estimated EUR 750-800 billion/year EU-wide investment gap) and the **Letta Report on the Future of the Single Market** (April 2024, arguing single-market completion is itself a competitiveness multiplier). Both reports are cited explicitly by the Commission and by EPRS as the intellectual scaffolding for consolidating fragmented competitiveness spending into one fund.
- **Where it sits in the MFF 2028-2034 negotiation (as of July 2026):** Council reached a **partial general approach on the ECF** at the General Affairs Council of 16 June 2026 (alongside partial approaches on national/regional partnership plans and Global Europe), a building block, not a final Council position. The European Council held a policy debate on the wider MFF "negotiating box" on 18-19 June 2026; conclusions on the ECF envelope were pending at time of writing. EP's BUDG committee adopted its negotiating mandate on 16 April 2026 (plenary confirmation 29 April 2026), calling for 1.27% of GNI overall and flagging governance/accountability questions on the ECF's proposed lending facility. **No Council common position and no EP-Council agreement exist yet on the ECF.**
- **Relationship to sibling files:** ECF sits inside Heading 2 alongside FP10 (research pillar, autonomous but "tightly connected," see `fp10_ecf_competitiveness`); it is expected to absorb/succeed **InvestEU** and continue **STEP**; it is the financing vehicle referenced by the "One Europe, One Market" roadmap (`single_market_one_europe_roadmap`) and by the wider Commission funding-programme landscape (`eu_commission_funding_programmes_map`).

## Overview: not yet law

Every figure, window name and governance detail in this guide describes a **Commission proposal tabled on 16 July 2025**, now in inter-institutional negotiation. The MFF Regulation (of which the ECF Regulation is a component) requires **unanimity in Council** and **EP consent** under Article 312 TFEU, a demanding bar that historically produces significant change between first proposal and final adoption. Treat any envelope, window allocation or governance rule here as the Commission's opening position, not a settled outcome. Target application date is **1 January 2028**, contingent on the Council reaching unanimous agreement (aim: before end of 2026) and the EP granting consent in time for the 2027 legislative adoption window.

## Origin: Draghi + Letta into a 16 July 2025 proposal

The ECF is the clearest institutional translation of two 2024 diagnostic reports into a concrete spending instrument:

- **Draghi Report on EU Competitiveness** (September 2024): identified an estimated **EUR 750-800 billion per year** additional investment need for the EU to keep pace with the US and China on frontier technologies, energy transition and defence industrial capacity. Draghi argued that fragmented, small-scale national and EU instruments could not deliver investment at the scale needed, and called for consolidated EU-level financing with fewer, larger, simpler instruments.
- **Letta Report on the Future of the Single Market** (April 2024): argued that completing the single market (capital, services, energy, telecoms) is itself a competitiveness lever, and that EU-level financing instruments should reinforce single-market integration rather than fragment along national lines.

The Commission's 16 July 2025 MFF package answered both reports by proposing to fold thirteen-plus separate competitiveness-adjacent instruments into a single fund (the ECF) sitting inside a simplified four-heading budget architecture, alongside a doubled research budget (FP10) and a new Competitiveness Coordination Tool for cross-heading steering. EPRS's own 11 May 2026 study (`EPRS_STU(2026)785X`) explicitly maps the ECF's proposed EUR 410 billion Heading 2 envelope against the Draghi investment gap, concluding that even the enlarged fund covers only a fraction of the identified annual shortfall, a recurring point in EP committee debate.

## What it consolidates

The ECF proposal merges roughly 13-14 existing 2021-2027 instruments into one framework with common rules. Confirmed absorptions per the Commission's legislative text and impact assessment:

| Current programme | Regulation | Fate under ECF proposal |
|---|---|---|
| EU4Health | Reg. (EU) 2021/522 | Absorbed into ECF health/biotech window |
| Digital Europe Programme | Reg. (EU) 2021/694 | Absorbed into ECF digital window |
| European Defence Fund (EDF) | Reg. (EU) 2021/697 | Absorbed into ECF defence/security window |
| LIFE Programme | Reg. (EU) 2021/783 | Absorbed into ECF clean-transition window |
| EU Space Programme (partial) | Reg. (EU) 2021/696, Reg. (EU) 2023/588 | Partially absorbed into ECF space window |
| InvestEU | Reg. (EU) 2021/523 | Governance model and guarantee mechanism continued as ECF's horizontal instrument (confirm final legal treatment, successor vs parallel programme, once the regulation text stabilises) |
| STEP (Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform) | Established under the current MFF as a cross-cutting mechanism | Described by the Commission as the pilot proving the case for the ECF; STEP itself (EUR 29 billion mobilised, turned two in March 2026) does not survive as a separate structure once the ECF is adopted |

FP10 (the Horizon Europe successor, EUR 175 billion proposed) sits alongside the ECF inside Heading 2 but is designed to remain an **autonomous** programme, not absorbed into it, see `fp10_ecf_competitiveness` for the full FP10-ECF governance debate, including the university-coalition (CESAER, EUA, EU-LIFE, LERU, YERUN, Coimbra Group, The Guild) position that FP10 must stay excellence-driven and not be subordinated to ECF's industrial-deployment logic.

**DG GROW communications framing (four windows, EUR figures per `single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu`):**
1. Clean Transition & Industrial Decarbonisation, approximately EUR 26.2 billion
2. Health & Biotech / Agriculture & Bioeconomy, approximately EUR 20.4 billion
3. Digital Leadership, approximately EUR 51.5 billion
4. Resilience & Security / Defence / Space, approximately EUR 125.2 billion

**Regulation-text/impact-assessment framing (as captured in `fp10_ecf_competitiveness`, closer to COM(2025)555 itself):** AI and digital technologies; space; clean technologies and energy transition; biotechnology and health; defence; health, agriculture and bioeconomy.

Both framings agree the largest single allocation is the resilience/security/defence/space cluster, consistent with the MFF's broader defence-integration push (see `safe_rearm_europe`). Do not present either list as final: window boundaries, names and allocations are a live negotiating variable, and Council/EP amendments are likely to reshape them before adoption.

## Governance

The Commission proposes the ECF on the InvestEU governance template:

- **Single fund, common rules.** One set of eligibility, simplification and reporting rules replaces the fragmented rulebooks of the 13-14 absorbed instruments, the Commission's stated rationale is reducing administrative burden for beneficiaries (SMEs, start-ups, larger companies, universities, research entities, public bodies) navigating what is currently a patchwork of separate programmes.
- **Policy windows, not per-programme calls.** Funding is organised thematically rather than by legacy programme name, intended to allow flexible reallocation as strategic priorities shift.
- **Layered instruments.** Direct grants sit alongside a guarantee/lending capacity modelled on InvestEU (each EUR of EU guarantee cited as supporting roughly EUR 15.20 of total investment under the current InvestEU track record) and an advisory-services layer.
- **Competitiveness Coordination Tool.** A new proposed cross-heading steering mechanism intended to align ECF spending with wider EU competitiveness objectives spanning multiple MFF headings, details of its legal form and decision rights are still being clarified in the legislative text; EP BUDG has flagged democratic-accountability questions about how much discretion this tool and the associated lending facility would place outside normal budgetary scrutiny.
- **Competitiveness Seal** (cross-referenced from FP10): high-quality projects assessed under ECF evaluation but not funded due to budget constraints could receive a quality label enabling access to national or regional co-funding, mirroring the existing EIC Seal of Excellence.

## Position in the MFF 2028-2034 negotiation

The ECF cannot be adopted separately from the wider MFF package, it is a component regulation inside the Article 312 TFEU special legislative procedure (Council unanimity, EP consent only, no EP amendment power).

- **16 July 2025:** Commission adopts the ECF proposal (COM(2025)555) as part of the full MFF 2028-2034 package.
- **19 December 2025:** Council adopts a partial mandate on the single-market and customs programme regulation (adjacent file, not the ECF itself).
- **26 February 2026:** Competitiveness Council ministers hold a dedicated discussion on the ECF.
- **16 April 2026 / 29 April 2026:** EP BUDG adopts its negotiating mandate (16 April), confirmed in plenary (29 April), 1.27% of GNI ceiling overall, defence and competitiveness flagged as priority headings alongside cohesion and agriculture safeguards; EP raises accountability concerns about the ECF lending facility.
- **16 June 2026:** General Affairs Council agrees a **partial general approach** on three MFF building blocks, including the ECF, the first formal Council-level building block on the fund, still short of a full Council common position.
- **18-19 June 2026:** European Council holds a policy debate on the revised MFF "negotiating box," weighing the Competitiveness Fund against the new own-resources package and the wider China-competitiveness challenge; conclusions pending at time of writing.
- **Target:** Council aims for unanimous agreement on the full MFF (including the ECF) before the end of 2026, to allow adoption of the underlying legislation in 2027 and application from 1 January 2028.

## Risks

- **Unanimity risk:** any single Member State can block the MFF Regulation (and therefore the ECF) in Council. Net contributor vs net beneficiary dynamics, and disputes over the parallel Heading 1 CAP/cohesion cuts, could delay or reshape the ECF envelope as a side effect of unrelated Heading 1 disputes.
- **Envelope volatility:** the EUR 234 billion / EUR 409-410 billion figures are opening Commission positions. EP has already flagged the overall MFF ceiling (1.27% vs Commission's 1.26% of GNI) as contested; the ECF envelope is a plausible target for reallocation in either direction as trilogue progresses.
- **Governance/accountability gap:** EP BUDG has raised concerns that the ECF's lending facility (guarantee-based, off traditional grant-budget lines) and the proposed Competitiveness Coordination Tool could reduce parliamentary and public visibility over how competitiveness spending is steered, relative to today's programme-by-programme scrutiny.
- **Window-taxonomy instability:** the four-window DG GROW communications framing and the ECF regulation-text framing (six themes) have not yet converged publicly; final window structure, names and allocations remain a live drafting variable.
- **FP10 ring-fencing dispute:** research-community stakeholders (EUA, LERU, CESAER, etc.) continue to push for guaranteed ring-fencing of the FP10 EUR 175 billion research budget from ECF industrial-deployment logic; how this is resolved will materially affect how much of the combined EUR 409-410 billion envelope functions as competitiveness-fund proper versus protected research funding.
- **STEP transition risk:** STEP (the current-MFF pilot the ECF is meant to formalise and scale) ends with the current 2021-2027 MFF; any delay in ECF adoption beyond 1 January 2028 would create a financing gap for STEP-type strategic-technology projects between the two MFF periods.
