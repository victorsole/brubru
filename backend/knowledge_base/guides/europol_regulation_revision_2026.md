## What is Europol today

Europol is the EU agency for law-enforcement cooperation, headquartered in The Hague. Under Regulation (EU) 2022/991, its mandate covers:

- Analysing EU crime trends and supporting Member-State-initiated investigations
- Receiving personal data directly from private parties
- Processing large, complex datasets (with specific safeguards)
- Exchanging data with third countries (operational and strategic agreements)
- Conducting research and innovation activities

Europol does NOT have autonomous arrest, prosecution, or coercive powers — those remain with Member State authorities.

## What the revision is expected to address

The EPRS briefing identifies three lines of stakeholder concern that the Q2 2026 revision will need to address:

1. **Governance** — efficiency of decision-making structures (Management Board, Executive Director, JSB joint supervision)
2. **Administration** — operational scaling pressures as the agency's mandate has expanded faster than its administrative architecture
3. **Data protection framework** — compatibility with EU primary law (Charter Articles 7, 8) and secondary law (Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 on EU institutions' data protection)

The European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) has raised concerns about the 2022 expansion of Europol's data-processing powers, and there are pending CJEU proceedings on this issue.

## Why this matters now

The expansion of Europol's mandate has outpaced the institutional checks designed for a smaller agency. The Commission's December 2025 evaluation flagged a need to recalibrate: more powers + same governance = stress on data-protection compliance and democratic oversight.

The Q2 2026 proposal will become a high-salience LIBE file: GDPR-style transversal scrutiny applied to a law-enforcement agency, with the EDPS and the EDPB likely to weigh in formally.
