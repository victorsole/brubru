# MiCA (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, Markets in Crypto-Assets)

## QUICK ANSWERS

**What is MiCA?** The EU's first comprehensive framework for crypto-assets that are not already regulated as financial instruments. It covers the issuance, offer to the public and admission to trading of crypto-assets, and the licensing and supervision of crypto-asset service providers (CASPs).

**When does it apply?** It entered into force on 29 June 2023. The stablecoin rules (Titles III and IV, asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens) apply from 30 June 2024; the rest, including the CASP regime, from 30 December 2024.

**What are the three types of crypto-asset under MiCA?**
- **E-money tokens (EMTs):** reference one official currency (like a euro or dollar stablecoin). Issuable only by authorised credit institutions or electronic money institutions; redeemable at par at any time; no interest.
- **Asset-referenced tokens (ARTs):** reference another value, right or basket (including multiple currencies). The catch-all stablecoin category, with the strictest issuer regime.
- **Other crypto-assets, including utility tokens:** everything else (Title II).
Algorithmic stablecoins are treated as ARTs or EMTs if they aim to stabilise value.

**What is a crypto-asset white paper?** A mandatory disclosure document (issuer, project, offer, rights, technology, risks, environmental impact of the consensus mechanism) that offerors must publish. For Title II tokens it is notified to the competent authority but not pre-approved; for ARTs it is approved as part of authorisation. Information must be fair, clear and not misleading.

**Who needs a licence?** Crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) must be authorised, with a registered office and place of effective management in the Union. The ten crypto-asset services are: custody and administration; operation of a trading platform; exchange of crypto-assets for funds; exchange for other crypto-assets; execution of orders; placing; reception and transmission of orders; advice; portfolio management; and transfer services. One authorisation is valid Union-wide (passporting).

**Who supervises what?** National competent authorities supervise Title II issuers, ART issuers and CASPs; EMT issuers are supervised under the e-money framework. EBA directly supervises issuers of significant ARTs and significant EMTs. ESMA keeps a public register of white papers, ART and EMT issuers and CASPs.

**What makes a token significant?** Thresholds such as a large customer base, high market capitalisation or large transaction volumes. Significant ARTs and EMTs face stricter requirements (higher own funds, interoperability, liquidity management) and EBA supervision. An ART used widely as a means of exchange (over 1 million transactions and EUR 200 000 000 per day in a single currency area) must reduce activity.

**What is excluded?** Crypto-assets that are financial instruments, deposits, funds, securitisation positions, insurance or pension products; NFTs (unique and non-fungible); central bank digital money; fully decentralised services with no intermediary; and non-transferable digital assets (closed-loop loyalty points).

**Is there a transition period?** Yes. CASPs operating lawfully under national law before 30 December 2024 may continue until 1 July 2026 or until authorised (Member States may shorten this grandfathering window). ART issuers operating before 30 June 2024 may continue if they apply for authorisation before 30 July 2024.

## OVERVIEW

MiCA is the EU's bespoke, harmonised rulebook for crypto-assets outside existing financial-services law, built on Article 114 TFEU and the principle of "same activities, same risks, same rules". It addresses the previous fragmentation (different or no national rules) that left holders exposed and hindered the single market. It protects retail holders, market integrity, financial stability and monetary sovereignty, while requiring disclosure of the environmental impact of consensus mechanisms.

It is structured in nine Titles: subject matter, scope and definitions (I); crypto-assets other than ARTs or EMTs and the white paper (II); asset-referenced tokens (III); e-money tokens (IV); authorisation and operating conditions for crypto-asset service providers (V); prevention and prohibition of market abuse (VI); competent authorities, EBA and ESMA (VII); delegated acts (VIII); and transitional and final provisions (IX).

## KEY NUMBERS

- 149 articles, 9 Titles, 119 recitals.
- Application: stablecoins (Titles III/IV) from 30 June 2024; rest from 30 December 2024.
- 3 crypto-asset types; 10 crypto-asset services.
- White-paper exemptions: fewer than 150 persons per Member State; qualified investors only; total under EUR 1 000 000 over 12 months. ART authorisation not required below EUR 5 000 000.
- Retail right of withdrawal: 14 days.
- Qualifying holding: 10 % of capital or voting rights.
- "Used widely as a means of exchange" threshold: over 1 million transactions and EUR 200 000 000 per day.
- CASP grandfathering window: to 1 July 2026.
