# Like Company v Google (C-250/25): Advocate General's opinion on AI chatbots and press publishers' rights, 3 November 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **Is it a judgment? NO. It is the opinion of Advocate General Maciej Szpunar, delivered 3 November 2026. It does NOT bind the Court. The judges have begun deliberating; the judgment comes "at a later date", with no date given.** Never say "the Court ruled".
- **The case:** C-250/25 Like Company, a Hungarian web-portal operator, against Google Ireland over the Google Gemini chatbot, referred by the Budapest Regional Court; alleged use between 13 June 2023 and 31 May 2024. The Court's press release calls it "the first generative AI case to reach the Court of Justice". Four questions: two on training, two on chatbot answers.
- **The law:** the press publishers' right (Article 15(1) of Directive (EU) 2019/790) and the text and data mining exception (Article 4 of that Directive).
- **Training, two questions:** he advises declaring them **inadmissible** (no evidence these articles were used for training; training is not "online use" of a press publication). For completeness he suggests training on protected works is, "as a rule, freely permitted" under the text and data mining exception. That part is an aside, not his answer to a question he finds admissible.
- **Chatbot answers, where the live retrieval (RAG or "grounding") matters:** the right **may** be infringed "in certain circumstances". The reproduction right turns on **two factors**: (1) whether the chatbot accessed the publication unlawfully, in particular by circumventing technical measures such as a paywall; (2) whether the answer merely reports facts, or reuses the form of expression recognisably, to the point that the user need not open the original.
- **Facts stay free:** the right protects the form of expression and the publisher's investment, NOT the news itself.
- **Making available to the public:** infringed, as a rule, where the form of expression is **systematically** recognisable in answers to related queries.
- **Liability:** the provider is liable where such uses are inherent to its business model, or where it failed to take measures to counter them credibly and effectively.
- **What was NOT read:** the opinion's full text. On 3 November (about 11:00 CET) InfoCuria showed no result for the case and EUR-Lex had no English text, so this guide rests on **press release 155/26 only**. Give no paragraph numbers and no quotation beyond the above.
- **Not settled:** how often a real chatbot crosses these lines; what the Court will do; how national courts apply it. It does NOT say chatbots are unlawful, and does NOT say they are safe.
