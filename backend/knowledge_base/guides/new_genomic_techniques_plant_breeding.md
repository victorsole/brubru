# New Genomic Techniques (NGT) for Plant Breeding

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU regulatory framework for **new genomic techniques (NGTs)** in plant breeding -- targeted mutagenesis, cisgenesis, and related precision breeding methods. Establishes a tiered regime (NGT Category 1 and NGT Category 2) distinct from the 2001 GMO Directive
- **Commission proposal**: COM(2023) 411 of 5 July 2023 -- "Proposal for a Regulation on plants obtained by certain new genomic techniques and their food and feed"
- **Procedure**: 2023/0226(COD) -- ordinary legislative procedure
- Lead DG: **DG SANTE** (Health and Food Safety, Plant Health and Reproductive Material) with DG AGRI cooperation
- Lead EP committee: **ENVI** (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety); AGRI opinion
- EP rapporteur (file COM(2023) 411): Jessica Polfjärd (EPP, Sweden) -- 2024 mandate
- Council configuration: **AGRIFISH** (Agriculture and Fisheries Council); ENVI Council on environmental aspects
- Responsible Commissioner: Christophe Hansen (Agriculture and Food) and Olivér Várhelyi (Health and Animal Welfare)
- Legal basis: Article 43(2) TFEU (Common Agricultural Policy) and Article 114 TFEU (internal market)

## **LATEST (21 April 2026)**: Council adopted the new NGT rules

The **Council of the EU adopted the new NGT Regulation on 21 April 2026**, marking the formal end of Council deliberations after the 4 December 2025 provisional agreement with the European Parliament.

**Next steps:**
- Formal adoption by the European Parliament still required (plenary signing). Adoption-publication-entry-into-force timeline expected over the following months.
- Regulation enters into force **20 days after publication in the OJ**.
- Most provisions apply after a **24-month transition period** to allow implementing rules. The new framework is expected to apply from **mid-2028**.
- Within one year of entry into force, the Commission will publish a **study on the impact of patenting** on innovation, seed availability to farmers, and EU plant-breeding competitiveness. The Commission will then either flag follow-up measures or table a legislative proposal addressing the study's findings.

## Two-tier classification of NGT plants

The Regulation establishes two tiers based on the type and extent of genetic modification:

### Category 1: NGT plants equivalent to conventional plants
- Plants that could have arisen naturally or via classical breeding (limited number of small targeted genetic modifications)
- **Outside the scope of the 2001 GMO legislation** for risk assessment purposes
- Streamlined notification + verification procedure
- Listed in a public EU database
- **Banned from organic production** (consumer/organic-sector concern)
- Member States cannot restrict their cultivation -- internal market harmonisation

### Category 2: All other NGT plants
- More extensive modifications, or modifications that could not have arisen naturally
- **Subject to the existing GMO legislation, adapted by the new NGT rules** to reflect the risk profile of NGTs
- Risk assessment, authorisation, traceability, labelling all apply
- **Member States may take national measures** to ensure category-2 plants are not unintentionally present in non-NGT products
- **Member States may restrict or prohibit cultivation of category-2 plants on their territory** (cultivation opt-out)
- Listed in the public NGT database

## Why this matters

NGTs are the regulatory frontier between conventional plant breeding (outside EU GMO law) and classical genetic modification (regulated under the 2001 GMO Directive 2001/18/EC). The 25 July 2018 Court of Justice ruling **Case C-528/16 (Confédération paysanne)** held that organisms obtained via mutagenesis fall under the GMO Directive -- a position that froze EU NGT innovation for half a decade and pushed research and seed companies to non-EU jurisdictions (US, Argentina, UK, Japan).

The new NGT Regulation is the EU's reset:
- **Greater safety**: tiered risk approach matched to actual genetic-change profile
- **More transparency and improved labelling**: NGT plants must be listed in the public database; consumers can identify NGT-derived products via labelling rules adapted to the category
- **Enhanced innovation**: streamlined Category-1 procedure unlocks SME and academic plant-science investment that had been moving abroad

## Three-bucket regulatory map

How EU legislation now applies to plant breeding:

| Method | Regulatory regime |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Conventional breeding** | Outside the scope of EU GMO legislation |
| **Genetic modification (transgenesis)** | Regulated under the 2001 GMO legislation (Directive 2001/18/EC + Regulation (EC) 1829/2003 on GM food and feed) |
| **New genomic techniques** | Regulated under the new NGT rules, in combination with the GMO legislation if applicable (Category 2) |

## Key tensions in the negotiations

The 4 December 2025 provisional agreement was reached after long-running tensions on:
- **Patenting**: civil-society and farmer organisations argued NGT plants should be unpatentable to avoid market concentration. The compromise: a Commission patenting study within 12 months of entry into force, with a possible follow-up proposal.
- **Coexistence with organic farming**: organic-sector federations pushed for a strict ban from organic production. Adopted: Category-1 plants banned from organic production (despite their conventional-equivalent status), retaining the organic-sector right to "GMO-free" branding under Regulation (EU) 2018/848.
- **Cultivation opt-out**: Member States retained the right to restrict or prohibit Category-2 plants on their territory, mirroring the GMO Directive 2015/412 amendment that gave MS a similar opt-out for transgenic crops.
- **Labelling**: Category-1 plants are NOT mandatorily labelled at the consumer level, but the public database makes them traceable through the seed/feed chain. Category-2 plants follow the standard GMO labelling regime.
- **Coexistence rules**: practical thresholds for unintended presence of Category-2 NGT in non-NGT products were left to implementing acts (within the 24-month transition).

## Stakeholders

- **EuropaBio** (industry): supportive of streamlined Category-1 regime; views the Regulation as essential for EU competitiveness vs US, China, Japan, Argentina
- **IFOAM Organics Europe**: supported the organic-production ban; remains critical of the unlabelled Category-1 retail status
- **Euroseeds** (seed industry trade association): supportive
- **Friends of the Earth Europe + Greenpeace**: opposed; argue Category-1 streamlined procedure undermines precaution
- **Confédération paysanne (FR)**, **COAG (ES)**, **Coldiretti (IT)**: split, with concerns on patenting + cultivation opt-out enforcement
- **EFSA** (European Food Safety Authority): scientific opinion of November 2020 + 2023 supplementary opinion underpinned the Category-1 risk-equivalence approach
- **JRC**: scientific evidence base on NGT detection methods (NGT-1 plants are by definition not detectable from the product alone -- detection relies on database verification + paper trail)
