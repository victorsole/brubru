# Child sexual abuse Directive recast, 2024/0035(COD): provisional agreement, LIBE votes on 4 November 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **Is it law? NO, as of 3 November 2026.** A provisional agreement exists between Parliament and Council negotiators; the Council confirmed it by letter (GEDA/A/(2026)006274). **LIBE has a single roll-call vote on the provisional agreement, text as a whole, under Rule 75(4), on 4 November 2026** (voting list dated 26 October 2026; rapporteur Jeroen Lenaers). **This guide was written before that vote and gives no result.** Plenary and Council steps would follow; no date read. Commission proposal COM(2024) 60. The Legislative Observatory read "Awaiting Parliament's position in 1st reading" on 3 November; Parliament's first-reading vote was 17 June 2025 (T10-0116/2025), referred back for negotiations.
- **NOT the same file as the online detection Regulation 2022/0155(COD)** (often called chat control or CSAM Regulation). This one is criminal law: offences, penalties, victims. It replaces Directive 2011/93/EU (Article 40).
- **Numbers from the agreed text (annex to the Council letter, 84 pages; article numbers may change before publication). Each is a MINIMUM for the MAXIMUM penalty a Member State must provide, NOT a sentence:**
  - possession or acquisition, and knowingly accessing, child sexual abuse material: at least 2 years (Article 5(2), (3));
  - distribution, offering, and production: at least 3 years (Article 5(4), (5), (6));
  - **sexual extortion** (threatening a child with disclosure): at least 4 years if the child is below the age of sexual consent, at least 2 years above it (Article 9);
  - paying for or promising payment for access to a livestream of abuse: at least 2 years (Article 10).
- **Limitation periods (Article 21(2)):** at least **15, 20 or 32 years** from the date the victim reaches the age of majority, depending on the offence. Do not attach a number to an offence unless asked and checked.
- **AI (recitals):** "image" covers any visual representation regardless of how it was made, including artificial intelligence and deepfake technologies; instruction manuals on abuse may include prompts given to AI systems.
- **Timing if adopted:** in force on the twentieth day after publication (Article 41); most obligations to be transposed **3 years after entry into force** (Article 39), with dates left blank in the text. **No date of entry into force exists.**
- **What was NOT read:** all 84 pages; only Articles 5, 9, 10, 21, 39, 40, 41 and the recitals cited. The Council's letter is a scan with no extractable text: the reference and its description come from the eMeeting listing. The final legal text after lawyer-linguists is not available.
