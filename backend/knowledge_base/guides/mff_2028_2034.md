# Multiannual Financial Framework 2028-2034

## QUICK FACTS
Proposal: COM(2025) 570 final (MFF Regulation), COM(2025) 571 final (Interinstitutional Agreement)
Date: 16 July 2025
Total commitments: EUR 1.763 trillion (2025 prices), 1.26% of EU GNI (of which 0.11% for NGEU repayment)
Structure: 4 headings (down from 7+2 sub-headings), 16 programmes (down from 52)
NGEU repayment: EUR 149.3 billion included in total
Climate spending target: minimum 35%
Current MFF (2021-2027): EUR 1.246.6 billion (1.15% of EU GNI)
Procedure: 2025/0570(APP) -- consent procedure (EP approves or rejects, no amendments)
EP rapporteurs: Co-rapporteurs on MFF and own resources (BUDG committee)
Council: Unanimity required (Article 312 TFEU)
Status: Commission proposal stage; EP and Council negotiations ongoing (March 2026)
EP study: CASP_STU(2026)776029 -- "Navigating the European Commission's MFF Proposal for 2028-2034" (REGI committee, cohesion policy focus)

## Overview

The Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) is the EU's long-term budget plan, setting maximum spending ceilings for 7-year periods. On 16 July 2025, the Commission proposed the 2028-2034 MFF at nearly EUR 1.8 trillion in commitments (2025 prices), representing 1.26% of EU GNI. This is an increase from 1.15% under the current 2021-2027 MFF, but includes EUR 149.3 billion for Next Generation EU (NGEU) loan repayments, meaning net new spending is more modest.

The architecture has been radically simplified: 52 programmes consolidated into 16, and 7 headings reduced to 4. The Commission also proposes a new lending facility of up to EUR 400 billion for the Competitiveness Fund. Treaty basis: Article 312 TFEU (unanimity in Council, consent of EP).

## Budget Headings

### Heading 1: National and Regional Partnership Plans -- EUR 865 billion (49.1%)

The largest heading consolidates previously separate funds into integrated national plans:

| Component | Allocation | Change vs 2021-2027 |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Cohesion Policy (ERDF, ESF+, CF) | Merged into partnership plans | -11% for shared management programmes |
| Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) income support | Included | -10% (proposed, heavily contested) |
| Fisheries (EMFAF) | Included | Part of partnership plans |
| Migration and border management | EUR 30.6 billion | Significant increase (current: EUR 7.2B AMIF + EUR 5.2B BMVI) |
| Rural development and tourism | Included | Merged with cohesion |

Key innovation: Member States submit a single "National and Regional Partnership Plan" covering cohesion, agriculture, rural development, fisheries, and migration -- replacing separate Operational Programmes and CAP Strategic Plans.

Controversial: The proposed 30% reduction in the projected CAP budget triggered strong opposition from farming representatives. Merging cohesion policy into partnership plans raises concerns about ring-fencing regional development funds.

### Heading 2: Competitiveness Fund -- EUR 410 billion (23.3%)

Consolidates 13 existing budget lines into a single fund for EU competitiveness:

| Investment Area | Allocation | Notes |
|----------------|-----------|-------|
| Research and innovation (Horizon Europe successor) | Major increase | +63% vs current Horizon Europe |
| Resilience and security, defence industry, and space | EUR 115.7-131 billion | Unprecedented defence integration |
| Education and mobility (Erasmus+ merged with European Solidarity Corps) | EUR 36.2 billion | +30% combined increase |
| Digital transition | Included | Part of competitiveness envelope |
| Transport (CEF) | Included | Including CEF-Energy fivefold increase |
| Health, culture, citizens | Included | Merged programmes |

Key innovation: A lending capacity of up to EUR 400 billion through a new budgetary mechanism, in addition to the commitment appropriations. Defence spending is fully integrated into the EU budget for the first time at this scale.

The European Peace Facility remains off-budget at EUR 27.1 billion.

### Heading 3: Global Europe -- EUR 190 billion (10.8%)

| Component | Allocation | Share |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Global Europe Fund (GEF) | EUR 176.8 billion | 93.1% |
| Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) | EUR 3.0 billion | 1.6% |
| Other (actions, prerogatives) | EUR 3.1 billion | 1.6% |
| Margin | EUR 7.1 billion | 3.7% |

Key innovation: The GEF merges 7 current external instruments (NDICI-Global Europe, IPA III, etc.) into a single structure organised around 5 geographic areas: Europe, Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia and the Pacific, the Americas and the Caribbean, and the Middle East/North Africa/Central Asia.

Ukraine: A dedicated "Ukraine reserve" for reconstruction, provided as a loan guaranteed by the EU's budget headroom (above MFF ceilings).

### Heading 4: Administration

EU institutional administration costs (Commission, Parliament, Council, Court of Justice, Court of Auditors, etc.).

## Comparison: 2021-2027 vs 2028-2034

| Metric | 2021-2027 MFF | 2028-2034 MFF |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Total commitments (2025 prices) | EUR 1,246.6 billion | EUR 1,763 billion |
| Share of EU GNI | 1.15% | 1.26% (incl. 0.11% NGEU) |
| Net of NGEU repayment | N/A | EUR 1,613.8 billion (1.15% GNI) |
| Number of headings | 7 + 2 sub-headings | 4 |
| Number of programmes | 52 | 16 |
| Special instruments | 8 | 3 |
| Climate target | 30% | 35% minimum |

Note: When NGEU repayments (EUR 149.3 billion) are excluded, the effective spending level remains at 1.15% of GNI -- essentially unchanged. The 2% deflator used for the 2021-2027 MFF was below actual inflation, causing a loss of 0.11% of GNI in purchasing power.

## New Own Resources

To finance the expanded budget without increasing Member State GNI contributions, the Commission proposes 5 new own resources generating an estimated EUR 58.2 billion per year:

| Own Resource | Annual Revenue | Mechanism |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|
| Emissions Trading System (ETS 1) | EUR 9.6 billion | 30% of ETS 1 revenue to EU budget |
| Tobacco excise duty (TEDOR) | EUR 11.2 billion | 15% call rate on manufactured tobacco |
| E-waste own resource | EUR 15.0 billion | New levy on electronic waste |
| Corporate resource (CORE) | EUR 6.8 billion | Annual lump sum from companies with >EUR 100M net turnover |
| Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) | EUR 1.4 billion | 75% of CBAM revenue to EU budget |

Additional EUR 14.3 billion/year from adjustments to existing own resources (reduction of collection cost retention from 20% to 10% for traditional own resources; increased call rate for plastic packaging waste own resource to EUR 1/kg from 2028).

## European Parliament Position

EP first reactions (July 2025): Co-rapporteurs on MFF and own resources stressed the proposal "lacked ambition" and was "not up to the EU's challenges." Key concerns:
- Effective spending at same level as current MFF (1.15% GNI net of NGEU)
- CAP cuts will face strong resistance
- Cohesion policy ring-fencing unclear
- Defence integration welcome but governance questions remain
- Lending facility (EUR 400B) raises democratic accountability concerns

The REGI committee commissioned study CASP_STU(2026)776029 ("Navigating the European Commission's MFF Proposal for 2028-2034") analysing risks and opportunities for cohesion policy and regional development.

BUDG committee leads negotiations. PETI committee adopted an opinion on the MFF (2025/0571(APP)) in March 2026.

## Political Context

- The MFF proposal is part of the broader 2024-2029 Commission mandate priorities (competitiveness, defence, climate)
- The Draghi Report on Competitiveness (September 2024) and Letta Single Market Report influenced the Competitiveness Fund design
- The Niinisto Report on European preparedness shaped the defence integration
- CAP reform is the most politically sensitive element -- farm lobby mobilisation expected
- Cohesion policy countries (Central/Eastern Europe) concerned about funding levels
- Net contributor vs net beneficiary dynamics will shape Council negotiations
- Unanimity requirement in Council means any single Member State can block adoption
