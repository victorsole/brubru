## QUICK ANSWERS

**What did the EU decide?** It imposed definitive anti-dumping duties on single-mode optical fibre cables imported from China, after finding that Chinese exporting producers were dumping the product (selling below the constructed normal value) and causing material injury to the Union industry.

**What are the duty rates?** FTT group (FiberHome Telecommunication Technologies and related companies): 44.0% (TARIC additional code C696); ZTT group (Jiangsu Zhongtian Technology and related companies): 19.7% (TARIC additional code C697); other cooperating companies listed in Annex I: 31.2%; all other companies: 44.0% (TARIC additional code C999).

**What is dumped? What product?** Single-mode optical fibre cables, made up of one or more individually sheathed fibres with protective casing, whether or not containing electric conductors (CN ex 8544 70 00). Excluded: cables in which all fibres are fitted with connectors at the extremities, and submarine cables.

**Why was the normal value "constructed"?** China's domestic prices and costs were found to be affected by significant State-induced distortions, so they could not be used. Under Article 2(6a) of the basic Regulation the Commission constructed the normal value using undistorted costs from a representative country, Argentina, pricing each factor of production (aluminium, aramid yarn, optical fibre, sheath materials, steel, labour, electricity) at Argentine values.

**Why do the duties equal the dumping margins?** In anti-dumping the lesser-duty rule applies: the duty is the lower of the dumping margin and the injury elimination level. Here the injury elimination levels were higher (FTT 61.3%, ZTT 42.0%, other 52.7%, all 61.3%) than the dumping margins (44.0%, 19.7%, 31.2%, 44.0%), so the duties were set at the dumping margins.

**What was the injury?** Material injury to the Union industry: the sampled Chinese exporters undercut Union prices by 30.0% and 33.2% (overall undercutting margin 31.5%), with price depression and loss of sales. A causal link was established and other factors did not break it.

**Is there a parallel anti-subsidy case?** Yes. The Commission initiated a separate anti-subsidy (countervailing) investigation on the same product on 21 December 2020, which led to a later countervailing-duty regulation. Anti-dumping offsets unfair pricing; anti-subsidy offsets government subsidies.

**What is the difference between anti-dumping and anti-subsidy (countervailing) duties?** Anti-dumping duties offset selling below normal value (dumping). Countervailing duties offset government subsidies. Both are trade-defence instruments under separate basic Regulations (anti-dumping: Reg 2016/1036; anti-subsidy: Reg 2016/1037). The lesser-duty rule applies to anti-dumping; it no longer applies to anti-subsidy.

## OVERVIEW

This is the EU's anti-dumping action on Chinese optical fibre cables, the backbone cable of telecoms and internet networks. It is a textbook application of the Article 2(6a) "significant distortions" methodology (constructing the normal value from a representative country, here Argentina) and of the lesser-duty rule (duty capped at whatever removes the injury, which here meant the dumping margins). It ran in parallel with a separate anti-subsidy investigation on the same product.

## KEY NUMBERS

- Definitive anti-dumping duties: FTT/FiberHome group 44.0%; ZTT group 19.7%; other cooperating 31.2%; all others 44.0%.
- Dumping margins equal the duties (lesser-duty rule); injury elimination levels were higher (FTT 61.3%, ZTT 42.0%, other 52.7%, all 61.3%).
- Overall price undercutting margin: 31.5% (30.0% and 33.2% for the two sampled exporters).
- Investigation period: 1 July 2019 to 30 June 2020.
- Representative country for normal value: Argentina.
