# Ownership and Control Restrictions in EU Calls (2021-2027)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Applies to**: Horizon Europe (HE), Digital Europe Programme (DEP), European Defence Fund (EDF), Connecting Europe Facility Digital (CEF-Digital), Instrument on Supporting Ammunition Production (ASAP), and from September 2024 any EU programme in award procedures affecting security or public order (Regulation 2024/2509 Article 136)
- **Core rule**: Entities controlled by ineligible non-EU countries, entities, or nationals cannot participate unless they provide an approved guarantee
- **Definition of control**: Possibility to exercise decisive influence (de jure or de facto), directly or indirectly through intermediate entities, regardless of whether influence is actually exercised
- **Strategic autonomy rationale**: Protects EU security interests, strategic interests, and defence interests; prevents non-EU control of sensitive assets and technology
- **Third-country threshold**: Non-EU shareholder/partner/owner at ANY layer in the chain of control (including intermediate layers) with 5% or more shares must be identified
- **Dual-use protection**: Covers research, technology, and supply chains in areas critical to EU defence and security
- **Ownership Control Declaration (OCD)**: Mandatory for all affected participants; must be submitted at proposal stage
- **Central Validation Service (CVS)**: Performs detailed ownership control assessment (OCA) for HE, DEP, EDF, CEF-Digital; assessment valid for 36 months
- **Guarantee option**: Ineligible entities may still participate if they obtain an approved guarantee from national competent authorities
- **Timing requirement**: Conditions must be fulfilled at proposal submission; changes during evaluation are NOT taken into account unless agreed by granting authority
- **Change notification**: Any ownership/control changes during project implementation must be immediately notified to granting authority; failure may result in grant reduction

---

## Why These Restrictions Exist

### Strategic Autonomy Framework

The EU uses ownership and control restrictions to protect its strategic autonomy and shield critical capabilities from foreign influence. These restrictions target:

- **Research and innovation assets** in sensitive areas (quantum, semiconductors, artificial intelligence, dual-use technologies)
- **Defence and security interests** of the EU and its Member States
- **Critical digital infrastructure** and communication systems
- **Strategic supply chains** and technology access

The guidance (Version 2.0, 1 January 2026) explicitly notes that these restrictions are "necessary to protect EU interests" including strategic, security, and defence interests.

### Dual-Use and Critical Technology Control

Programmes such as Horizon Europe and the European Defence Fund apply these restrictions particularly to topics involving:

- Sensitive defence technologies
- Critical raw materials (see EIC Accelerator Challenge 2.4)
- Quantum technologies and advanced computing
- Semiconductor manufacturing and design
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning for strategic applications
- Biotechnology with dual-use potential

The guarantee procedure (Section 4.4) explicitly requires that intellectual property "are not transferred/exported outside the eligible countries" and "cannot be accessed by the controlling ineligible non-EU country/entities/nationals without the approval of the eligible country in which the participant is established."

The following EU programmes **mandatorily include** ownership control restrictions in specific calls:

| Programme | Regulation | Ineligible entities scope |
|-----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Horizon Europe** | Regulation (EU) 2021/695, Art. 22(5) | Non-EU country, non-EU entity, non-EU national |

| **European Defence Fund** | Regulation (EU) 2021/697, Art. 9(3-4) | Non-EU country, non-EU entity, non-EU national |
| **ASAP** (ammunition) | Regulation (EU) 2023/1525, Art. 10(1-2) | Non-EU country, non-EU entity, non-EU national |
| **CEF-Digital** | Regulation (EU) 2021/1153, Art. 11(4) | Non-EU country, non-EU entity, non-EU national |

### Article 136 Financial Regulation (September 2024 extension)

Since 1 September 2024, **any EU programme** may impose ownership control restrictions "in award procedures that affect security or public order, to protect security or public order interests, in particular concerning strategic assets and interests of the EU or its Member States, including the protection of the integrity of digital infrastructure, communication and information systems and related supply chains." (Regulation 2024/2509, Article 136)

### How to Know If a Call Is Restricted

Always **check the call conditions** (call text) to see whether:

- The call explicitly states that ownership control restrictions apply
- Only EU-controlled entities may participate
- Non-EU-controlled entities may participate only with a guarantee
- All participants or only certain cost categories are subject to checks

The call conditions specify the exact scope and which participants are subject to EU verification versus beneficiary self-checks.

## How Ownership and Control Are Defined

### Control Definition

**Control** is defined as "the possibility to exercise decisive influence on the participant, directly or indirectly, through one or more intermediate entities, 'de jure' or 'de facto'."

The key principle: **The fact that no influence is actually exercised is not relevant; as long as the possibility exists, control is assessed.**

### Non-EU Control Determination

A participant is considered **non-EU controlled** when:

- A shareholder, partner, member, or owner who is a non-EU country, non-EU entity, or non-EU national
- At any level in the chain of control (including intermediate layers)
- Has the possibility (de jure or de facto) to exercise decisive influence, particularly regarding strategic business decisions

Strategic business decisions include:

- Appointment and removal of senior management
- Decisions regarding budget
- Adoption of investment and business plans
- Market-specific decisions
- Approval of major essential investments
- IP rights, technology transfer, or licensing decisions
- Extension or acceptance of financial instruments (guarantees, loans)
- Asset acquisitions

### Chain of Control and Intermediate Layers

The ownership analysis examines **all ownership layers until the ultimate owners**, with complete mapping to identify non-EU influence at any layer.

**Example from the guidance:**

Company Y applies to participate. Company B (EU-established) is controlled by company B1 (also EU-established), which owns 60% of B's shares. However, B1 is controlled by non-EU national B11. Therefore, company B is non-EU controlled, because control passes through the intermediate layer to a non-EU national.

In the same example, company Y has:
- 80% of shares directly held by two EU companies and an EU national
- But at intermediate layers, 20% is controlled by company A (non-EU) and 40% is controlled by company B (ultimately non-EU via B11)
- Result: 60% ultimate non-EU control; company Y is non-EU controlled.

## Types of Control Assessed

### Sole Control

One shareholder/partner/member/owner alone can exercise decisive influence, typically through:

- Holding the majority of voting rights required for approval of strategic decisions
- Veto rights on strategic decisions (negative control) without needing cooperation with other shareholders
- Casting vote (if deemed effective and relevant)
- High likelihood of achieving a majority at shareholder meetings, based on historical voting patterns

### Joint Control

Two or more shareholders/partners/members/owners have the possibility to exercise decisive influence, typically through:

- Veto rights given to two or more shareholders on strategic decisions
- Equal voting rights (e.g. 50:50)
- Unanimity requirement for strategic decisions
- Equal appointment rights to decision-making bodies without effective casting vote for one shareholder

### Shifting Majorities and Minority Influence

- **Shifting majorities** (changing coalitions of minority shareholders) do NOT typically constitute joint control
- However, if ownership mapping shows **significant elements of non-EU influence** (e.g. place of establishment or nationality of the majority of shareholders), the participant may be considered non-EU controlled even if no stable control structure exists

### De Jure Versus De Facto Control

- **De jure** influence is analysed through legal documents: statutes, articles of association, shareholder agreements, governance rules, specific rights
- **De facto** influence is judged from the decision-taking reality: commonalities of interest, voting habits, actual board composition, and historical decision patterns

## Elements Considered in Control Assessment

### 1. Ownership Structure and Specific Rights

The ownership structure is analysed to establish which countries, entities, or natural persons have decisive influence, with special attention to:

- **Direct natural-person ownership**: If all owners are EU Member State nationals with no commercial or other controlling links, further assessment may not be necessary
- **Widespread shareholding**: If shareholding is widely dispersed and non-EU shareholders are the largest (even if their stake appears insignificant), detailed assessment is mandatory
- **Ultimate owners**: Must be identified if they hold 5% or more of shares/voting rights (or even less if requested)

#### Special Cases for Ownership Structure

**Listed Companies**
- Emphasis is placed on bodies entrusted with strategic decisions, shareholder meeting quorums and majorities, management body composition, and appointment possibilities for largest shareholders
- Veto rights or golden shares are scrutinised

**Unidentifiable Ultimate Owners**
- If not all ultimate owners can be identified (e.g. listed companies with significant floats), the participant must demonstrate that under applicable national law, unknown shareholders cannot exercise decisive influence alone or in concert, and must confirm no shareholders agreement for coalition exists

**Specific Rights to Scrutinise**
- Right to veto transfer of shares
- Pre-emption rights (right of first refusal on share sales)
- Specific rights to sell, purchase additional shares, or conditions imposed for investment
- Any right that results in non-EU country/entity/national control

### 2. Corporate Governance

Corporate governance is examined to establish whether strategic decisions may be influenced (actively or passively) by non-EU owners.

Assessment includes:

- Identification of governing bodies (General Assembly, Board of Directors, Supervisory Board, Management Board) responsible for strategic decisions
- Decision-making procedures: quorums, majorities, voting rules, prerogatives of other bodies
- Appointment of governing body members: nomination rules, approval requirements, shareholding links
- Voting rights per body member and per shareholder
- Requirements for pre-consent or approval by specific shareholders

#### Special Governance Cases

**Supermajority or Qualified Majority Requirements**
- If statutes require a supermajority (e.g. 2/3 or 75%) for strategic decisions, shareholders with simple majority may lack the power to impose decisions but may hold blocking rights and thus control

**Minority Shareholders with Specific Rights**
- Control may occur if a minority shareholder has the power to appoint more than half of governing body members or the right to manage activities and determine business strategy (e.g. general partner in limited partnership)

**Nominee, Investment Fund, Trust, or Other Institutional Arrangements**
- Analysis focuses on agreements/arrangements committing the nominee, fund, or trust; decision-making powers; and investor control mechanisms

**Associations, Unions, Alliances, Collective Structures**
- For entities without shareholders but with members, analysis focuses on voting rights per member, members' country of establishment (or nationality if natural persons), and decision-taking modalities
- Often members have equal voting rights (1 member = 1 vote) regardless of contribution, but voting may depend on criteria such as number of employees or contribution level

### 3. Commercial Links Conferring Control

Commercial links are analysed in combination with other factors to establish whether the participant or its owners are **dependent on commercial cooperation with a non-EU country/entity/national** in a way that grants strategic influence, such as:

- Long-term supply or buy agreements that permit non-EU partners to influence commercial strategy
- Joint ventures or other commercial cooperation arrangements with non-EU entities
- Dependency on non-EU customers/suppliers for critical inputs or markets

Supporting documents required:
- Information on non-EU customers/suppliers on which the participant depends
- Cooperation agreements/arrangements demonstrating control-conferring dependency

### 4. Financial Links Conferring Control

Financial links are analysed to establish whether the participant or its owners are **financially dependent on a non-EU country/entity/national** in a way that allows influence over strategic decisions.

Modes of financing assessed include:

- Capital increase
- Loans
- Guarantees
- Debt waivers
- Bail-outs
- Grants

If financial links involve shareholders, **financial dependence is measured in proportion to shareholding** (i.e. how much more than the value of the shares was financed).

Supporting documents required:
- Information on non-EU sources providing significant financing (type and degree of control)
- Financial agreements/arrangements with non-EU providers

### 5. Other Sources of Control

Any other means, processes, or links ultimately conferring control to a non-EU country/entity/national may be assessed if needed. Examples may include:

- Board seats held by non-EU nationals (in specific legal forms such as foundations with no shareholders)
- Technology or IP licensing agreements with controlling implications
- Membership in non-EU-controlled associations or consortia

## Documents Required

### Ownership Control Declaration (OCD)

The **Ownership Control Declaration** is a mandatory form signed by a person authorised to represent the legal entity. It must be submitted by all affected participants as part of the proposal.

For restricted calls, each participant must fill in the OCD; the coordinator merges all declarations into one document and submits with the proposal. Proposals missing the OCD may be declared inadmissible.

**Exception**: Public bodies validated in the Participant Register are automatically considered as controlled by their country; they do NOT need to submit an OCD. (Draft 'declared' status is NOT sufficient for this exemption.)

### Supporting Documents for Ownership Control Assessment

During Grant Agreement Preparation, if the proposal is selected, participants declaring themselves as 'EU controlled' are asked to upload supporting documents via a secure link. Required documents typically include:

#### Ownership and Direct/Indirect Shareholders
- Shareholder/member/partner registries or book of shareholders with exact distribution of shares/voting rights
- Commercial registry extracts
- Articles of association, incorporation documents, or memorandum of association
- Bank/financial institution certifications on shareholding

#### Specific Rights Attached to Shares
- Rights attached to each share class
- Number of votes per share
- Veto rights or blocking rights
- Consent or pre-approval rights on decisions
- Multiple voting shares (golden shares) documentation

#### Governance and Decision-Making
- Description of decision-making bodies and their composition
- Rules for election, appointment, nomination, or tenure of decision-making body members
- Decision-making procedures, quorum requirements, voting rules
- Members' agreements, partnership agreements, or investment agreements
- Minutes or reports of shareholders' meetings showing actual decision patterns

#### Nationality and Identity Proof
- Copy of ID card or passport of ultimate owner(s) holding 5% or more of capital or voting rights
- Proof of place of establishment for legal entities

#### Commercial and Financial Links
- Information on non-EU customers/suppliers on which the participant depends and cooperation agreements
- Information on non-EU financing sources and financial agreements

#### Other
- Any additional documents clarifying the control situation
- Updated declarations if ownership has changed since proposal submission

**Important**: Do NOT send documents before being formally requested; use only the secure upload link provided in the notification.

### Affiliation File (if required by specific call)

Some calls may require an **Affiliation File** describing relationships with associated partners, third parties giving in-kind contributions, subcontractors, or recipients of financial support to third parties. This file must also respect ownership control restrictions if those participants are subject to checks.

## Process: Declaration, Verification, and Exceptions

### Step 1: Ownership Control Declaration at Proposal Submission

All participants subject to checks must:
- Complete the OCD form with accurate ownership and control information
- Have the form signed by an authorised representative
- Submit to the coordinator, who merges all declarations into one document

### Step 2: Evaluation and Proposal Selection

- Ownership control status does NOT impact proposal evaluation
- Only after a proposal is selected for funding does the Central Validation Service (CVS) contact the participant

### Step 3: Detailed Ownership Control Assessment (OCA)

**For Horizon Europe, Digital Europe, EDF, and CEF-Digital:**

The Central Validation Service (CVS) performs a detailed OCA during Grant Agreement Preparation (GAP):

- Participants declaring as 'non-EU controlled' are normally NOT subjected to detailed assessment (status accepted as declared)
- Participants declaring as 'EU controlled' must upload supporting documents
- CVS contacts participants via secure notification with a document list
- Assessment is based on submitted documentation and may include clarification requests
- CVS may use other available information to verify details

**CVS cannot provide general advice or analyse call-specific eligibility; it collects documents and assesses control only. All eligibility decisions are made by the service in charge of the grant.**

**For other programmes:**

The EU service in charge of the grant performs the assessment directly.

#### Assessment Outcome and Validity

- **Outcome**: 'EU-controlled' or 'non-EU controlled' (validated ownership control status)
- **Validity period**: 36 months from the OCA completion date
- **Cannot be changed during GAP**: Unless explicitly agreed by the granting authority

#### Refusal to Cooperate

If a participant or its owners refuse to provide necessary information/documents:

- CVS may not finalise the assessment
- An unfinalised assessment automatically makes the participant ineligible
- No grants can be signed before assessment is complete

### Step 4: Eligibility Check by Granting Authority

Once the CVS completes the OCA, the service in charge of the grant checks whether the validated status impacts eligibility:

- If validated as 'EU controlled': no further action; participant is eligible
- If validated as 'non-EU controlled': the service checks if that non-EU country is ineligible under the call
  - If ineligible AND no guarantee is allowed: participant must be replaced or removed
  - If ineligible BUT guarantee is allowed: participant may submit a guarantee (Section 4.4)

The participant is **informed of the assessment details and allowed to submit observations** before a final eligibility decision.

### Step 5: Guarantee Procedure (if applicable)

See detailed section below (Section: Guarantees).

### Step 6: New Proposals in Restricted Calls

If a participant submits a new proposal in another restricted call:

- A **new OCD must always be submitted**
- If declared as 'non-EU controlled': status is automatically counted as non-EU controlled (no reassessment)
- If declared as 'EU controlled' AND 36-month validity has expired: a new OCA is conducted
- If declared as 'EU controlled' AND still within 36 months: normally no new assessment (unless the granting authority suspects changes or you have declared a change)

**Previously submitted documents** need not be resubmitted if unchanged; list them in a word document with new documents.

## Guarantees

### Purpose and Scope of Guarantees

A guarantee is a **derogation** (exception) that allows an otherwise ineligible participant (non-EU controlled by an ineligible country) to participate in a restricted call, provided that:

1. The guarantee is explicitly allowed by call conditions
2. The participant obtains approval from competent national authorities
3. The EU service accepts the guarantee as adequate

Guarantees are **programme-, call-, and project-specific**. A guarantee approved in one Horizon Europe project does NOT apply to another call or programme.

### What a Guarantee Must Demonstrate

The guarantee must show that:

1. **No contravention of protected interests**: The participant's involvement will not contravene the interests protected by the call (e.g. EU security, strategic autonomy, strategic interests, security and defence interests, or security/public order)

2. **No contravention of programme objectives**: The involvement will not contravene the objectives of the EU programme

3. **Concrete measures are in place** to ensure:

   a. The ineligible non-EU country/entities/nationals' control is NOT exercised in a way that:
      - Restrains, restricts, or compromises the participant's capacity to carry out the action and deliver results
      - Imposes restrictions on infrastructure, facilities, assets, resources, IP, or know-how needed for the action

   b. **Prevention of access to sensitive information**: Access by the controlling non-EU country/entities/nationals to sensitive information relating to the action is prevented, including:
      - Technical, legal, and organisational measures to prevent transfer or access (including unsolicited transfers)
      - Prevention of access to personal and non-personal data, processed data, and meta-data in the EU
      - Compliance with EU law and applicable national law
      - Adequate security clearance for employees/persons involved (where appropriate)

   c. **Intellectual property and results protection**:
      - IP and results remain with the participant during and after the action
      - IP and results are NOT subject to control or restriction by the controlling non-EU country/entities/nationals
      - IP and results are NOT transferred/exported outside eligible countries
      - Ineligible non-EU country/entities/nationals cannot access IP and results without approval of the eligible country where the participant is established AND in accordance with programme objectives

### Examples of Concrete Measures

The guidance provides examples of acceptable concrete measures:

- Specific management structures separate from normal management
- Protection mechanisms against the ineligible non-EU country/entity/national
- Mechanisms to prevent ineligible non-EU management access to sensitive information
- Protection against loss of sensitive data
- Registration/authorisation to operate in protected areas
- Security clearance of management, employees, and facilities
- Reporting duties to national authorities
- Audit, verification, and control rights for national authorities
- Mechanisms to control or approve ineligible non-EU country investments and foreign direct investment

### Preparation by the Participant

The participant must:

1. Prepare a guarantee statement addressing all conditions listed above
2. Explain concrete measures in sufficient detail
3. Have the guarantee signed by an authorised representative
4. Submit to the competent national authority for approval

**Advance preparation**: If ineligible non-EU control is known at proposal submission, it is strongly advised to prepare the guarantee as part of the proposal (even though the formal guarantee request comes later during GAP).

### National Authority Approval

The guarantee must be approved by **competent authorities of the country where the non-EU-controlled participant is established**.

**For Horizon Europe**: The Commission contacts the Member State (or eligible third country) directly; participants do NOT need to do this themselves.

**For other programmes**: It is normally the participant's responsibility to contact the national authority.

**Approval template**: A recommended (but not mandatory) approval template exists. The national authority may:
- Return the signed template to the participant, who then submits it to the granting authority, OR
- Communicate approval directly to the Commission by official means (email, Permanent Representation note, etc.)

The national authority is invited to:
- Confirm agreement to the participant's participation
- Check compliance with national rules
- Check compliance with national security, defence, and public order interests
- Confirm that measures to avoid issues appear appropriate

**Timing note**: If the template is not used, the communication must include sufficient detail and information for the EU to assess eligibility and finalise grant preparation.

### EU Assessment of the Guarantee

Once submitted, the service in charge of the grant:

1. Assesses the guarantee for **completeness**
2. Checks **compliance with eligibility conditions** and EU security, defence, and public order interests
3. Assesses whether **measures taken seem appropriate**

If the guarantee is incomplete or inadequate:
- The participant may be contacted for changes or updates
- The guarantee procedure can be reopened

**Strict interpretation**: Guarantees are a derogation and are interpreted strictly. The EU services may reject guarantees that:

- Include disclaimers
- Rely on anticipated changes to ownership control structure
- Lack sufficient detail on protective measures

**Implementation and monitoring**: The measures set out in the guarantee must be:
- Implemented and maintained throughout the entire action (and beyond, if needed)
- Subject to reporting requirements
- Monitored by the granting authority

If measures are not implemented or maintained, the guarantee may become invalid and the participant may be declared ineligible, with consequences for grant recovery.

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. Underestimating Indirect Non-EU Influence

**Mistake**: Assuming only direct shareholding matters.

**Reality**: The assessment traces ownership through ALL intermediate layers to ultimate owners. A 51% EU shareholder controlled by a non-EU national means the entire chain is non-EU controlled.

**Mitigated by**: Tracing complete ownership structure in advance; consulting with national authorities early.

### 2. Overlooking Veto Rights and Blocking Rights

**Mistake**: Assuming majority control is the only form of control.

**Reality**: Veto rights and blocking rights confer control, even if the shareholder holds less than 50%.

**Mitigated by**: Carefully reviewing articles of association, shareholder agreements, and governance rules for veto/blocking mechanisms.

### 3. Failing to Document Commercial/Financial Dependencies

**Mistake**: Submitting an OCD stating 'EU controlled' but failing to disclose major non-EU suppliers, customers, or debt holders.

**Reality**: Commercial and financial links can confer de facto control if the participant is dependent in a way that influences strategic decisions.

**Mitigated by**: Fully documenting non-EU commercial partners, suppliers, customers, and financing sources; assessing whether any confer strategic influence.

### 4. Mishandling Nominee Shareholders and Investment Funds

**Mistake**: Treating a nominee or investment fund as the ultimate owner without identifying the beneficial owners behind the fund.

**Reality**: The assessment must pierce the nominee/fund structure to identify ultimate owners and their countries.

**Mitigated by**: Obtaining fund agreements, trust documents, and beneficial ownership declarations.

### 5. Ignoring Natural Person Nationality Changes or Dual Nationality

**Mistake**: Assuming a shareholder's nationality based on residence or business activity.

**Reality**: Assessment is based **only on proof of nationality**. Natural persons with dual nationality (EU and non-EU) are considered EU nationals. Place of residence, economic activity, and centre of interests are NOT relevant.

**Mitigated by**: Obtaining passport/ID copies proving nationality; clarifying dual nationality status.

### 6. Inadequate Guarantee Detail

**Mistake**: Submitting a guarantee that states "we will protect sensitive information" without concrete measures.

**Reality**: Guarantees are interpreted strictly. Vague assurances or commitments to future changes are rejected.

**Mitigated by**: Preparing detailed guarantees with specific measures (separate management structures, security clearances, reporting mechanisms, legal/technical safeguards).

### 7. Failing to Report Control Changes During Project Implementation

**Mistake**: Experiencing an acquisition or change in shareholding mid-project but not notifying the granting authority.

**Reality**: Any changes in ownership/control during the action must be immediately notified. Failure to report may result in grant reduction or recovery.

**Mitigated by**: Establishing internal procedures to flag ownership changes; maintaining regular contact with the granting authority.

### 8. Assuming Public Body Exemption Without Validation

**Mistake**: Claiming exemption as a public body without a validated PIC in the Participant Register.

**Reality**: Only entities with formal validation as 'public body' (NOT draft 'declared' status) are exempt from submitting an OCD.

**Mitigated by**: Obtaining validated PIC status before proposal submission; confirming public body validation in the register.
