## Overview

The revision of the Tobacco Taxation Directive is one of the most politically sensitive tax files in the current legislative cycle. Council Directive 2011/64/EU, which sets minimum excise duty rates for cigarettes, cigars, cigarillos, and fine-cut tobacco, has not been substantively updated since 2011. In that time, the tobacco market has been transformed by the emergence of novel products -- heated tobacco products (HTPs), e-cigarettes, and nicotine pouches -- none of which are covered by the existing directive.

The Commission's recast proposal aims to modernise the framework by bringing these new products into scope, raising minimum excise duty rates for traditional tobacco to reflect inflation and health policy objectives, and creating a more coherent tax structure that discourages smoking while avoiding unintended market distortions.

Because the legal basis is Article 113 TFEU (indirect tax harmonisation), the directive requires unanimity in Council and the consultation procedure in the European Parliament. This means the EP's opinion is advisory -- the Council can adopt the directive without accepting EP amendments. However, the EP's position carries significant political weight, and the ECON committee's draft report will shape the public debate.

## Current Framework: Directive 2011/64/EU

The existing directive establishes:

### Cigarettes

| Element | Current Rule |
|---------|-------------|
| Minimum excise duty | EUR 1.80 per pack of 20, AND at least 60% of the weighted average retail selling price (WAP) |
| Structure | Mixed system: specific component (fixed amount per unit) + ad valorem component (percentage of retail price) |
| Minimum overall tax | At least 90 EUR per 1,000 cigarettes (specific + ad valorem excise combined) |
| Transitional periods | Extended to several newer Member States (Bulgaria, Romania, Croatia, Poland) |

### Other Tobacco Products

| Product | Minimum Rate |
|---------|-------------|
| Cigars and cigarillos | 5% of retail price OR EUR 12 per 1,000 items |
| Fine-cut tobacco (roll-your-own) | EUR 60 per kg OR 46% of WAP |
| Other smoking tobacco (pipe) | EUR 22 per kg OR 20% of WAP |

### Gaps in Current Framework

The 2011 directive does not cover:
- **Heated tobacco products (HTPs)**: IQOS, glo, Ploom -- taxed under national rules with wildly varying rates
- **E-cigarettes/vaping liquids**: No EU-level excise framework; some Member States tax them, others do not
- **Nicotine pouches**: Entirely unregulated at EU level (not even classified as tobacco products in most Member States)

This regulatory gap has created significant market distortions, with consumers and manufacturers exploiting cross-border tax differentials. Some Member States tax HTPs at cigarette-equivalent rates, while others apply negligible duties, creating incentives for cross-border shopping and illicit trade.

## Proposed Changes

The Commission's recast proposal introduces several fundamental changes:

### New Product Categories

For the first time, the directive would establish EU-level minimum excise duties for:

1. **Heated tobacco products (HTPs)**: Classified as a distinct category with minimum rates set at a percentage of the cigarette rate, reflecting their reduced but not eliminated health risks.

2. **E-cigarettes and vaping liquids**: A new excise duty per millilitre of e-liquid, with differentiated rates for nicotine-containing and nicotine-free liquids.

3. **Nicotine pouches**: Brought into scope as a novel nicotine product, with minimum rates per unit or per gram of nicotine content.

### Updated Minimum Rates for Traditional Tobacco

| Product | Current Minimum | Proposed Minimum |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| Cigarettes (per 1,000) | EUR 90 | EUR 117 (phased increase) |
| Cigarettes (% of WAP) | 60% | 63% |
| Fine-cut tobacco (per kg) | EUR 60 | EUR 93 |
| Cigars/cigarillos (per 1,000) | EUR 12 | EUR 25 |

### Inflation Adjustment Mechanism

The proposal introduces an automatic inflation adjustment mechanism, indexing minimum rates to the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP). This prevents the erosion of real tax rates over time and avoids the need for legislative revision every decade.

### Convergence Timeline

Member States with current rates below the new minima would have transitional periods (typically 4-8 years) to reach full compliance, with interim milestones.

## ECON Draft Report

The ECON committee is preparing its draft report under rapporteur Tomas Kubin (PfE (Patriots for Europe) / ANO 2011, Czechia). The amendment deadline is 13 April 2026, making this a live file for advocacy. Kubin published his draft report on 5 March 2026, proposing a more gradual increase of the minimum rates: limiting indexation to core inflation with a 12% maximum three-yearly adjustment ceiling, increasing the weight of the price-level index in the minimum-rate formula from one third to one half, and lowering the proposed minimum rates for heated tobacco products and nicotine pouches relative to the Commission proposal, with longer transitional periods. (Do not confuse Kubin with Ondrej Krutilek (ECR / ODS, Czechia), a separate Czech MEP active on tobacco files in the SANT committee.)

Key issues in the ECON debate:

### Tax Differentials for Reduced-Risk Products
A central controversy is whether HTPs and e-cigarettes should be taxed at significantly lower rates than traditional cigarettes to reflect their lower health risk profile (as argued by the tobacco industry and some public health economists), or at equivalent rates to prevent them from being used as a gateway to nicotine addiction (as argued by many public health organisations).

### Impact on Low-Income Consumers
Higher tobacco taxes are regressive -- they disproportionately affect lower-income consumers. Several MEPs from Central and Eastern European Member States are expected to propose longer transition periods or lower minimum rates for their national markets, where purchasing power is lower.

### Illicit Trade
The tobacco industry argues that excessive tax increases drive consumers toward illicit products, undermining both health objectives and revenue collection. The Commission's impact assessment acknowledges this risk but concludes that the proposed increases are moderate enough to avoid significant shifts to the black market.

### Revenue Earmarking
Some MEPs favour earmarking a portion of tobacco excise revenue for public health programmes (smoking cessation, cancer research). This is politically attractive but faces resistance from finance ministries that prefer general revenue.

### Nicotine Pouches
The classification and taxation of nicotine pouches is particularly contentious. Sweden, where snus (a related product) is legally sold under a treaty derogation, is closely watching the treatment of this category.

## Health Policy Link

The Tobacco Taxation Directive operates in tandem with the Tobacco Products Directive (2014/40/EU), which regulates product standards, packaging, labelling, and advertising. Together, they form the EU's comprehensive approach to tobacco control:

| Instrument | Scope | Legal Basis |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Tobacco Products Directive 2014/40/EU | Product standards, packaging, health warnings, advertising | Article 114 TFEU (OLP) |
| Tobacco Taxation Directive 2011/64/EU (recast) | Excise duty rates, tax structure | Article 113 TFEU (CNS, unanimity) |
| EU Beating Cancer Plan (2021) | Overarching health policy framework | Non-legislative |
| Council Recommendation on smoke-free environments | Public spaces, workplaces | Article 168 TFEU |
| Illicit trade protocol (FCTC) | Anti-smuggling, track and trace | International treaty |

The World Health Organisation's Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC), to which all EU Member States are party, identifies taxation as the single most effective policy instrument for reducing tobacco consumption. Article 6 FCTC recommends that tobacco taxes constitute at least 75% of the retail price.

## Revenue Impact

Tobacco excise duties generate significant revenue for EU Member States:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total EU tobacco excise revenue (2024) | EUR 103 billion |
| Highest revenue (Germany) | EUR 14.5 billion |
| Highest tax share of retail price (UK pre-Brexit) | ~82% |
| EU average tax share of retail price | ~72% |
| Estimated additional revenue from proposed increases | EUR 8-12 billion/year (steady state) |

### Own Resources Dimension

Tobacco excise has been discussed as a potential EU own resource in the context of the MFF 2028-2034 negotiations. The "TEDOR" own resource proposal (Tax on Earnings from Digital and Other Resources) would allocate a small percentage of national tobacco excise revenue to the EU budget. This remains politically sensitive, as Member States are reluctant to share tax revenue.

The unanimity requirement in Council makes this file particularly challenging. Any single Member State can block adoption. Historically, tax directives have taken years to negotiate in Council, with extensive use of transitional periods and derogations to build consensus.

## Key Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Position |
|-------------|----------|
| PMI (Philip Morris International) | Favours lower HTP rates to incentivise switching from cigarettes |
| BAT (British American Tobacco) | Supports tax differentiation for "reduced-risk" products |
