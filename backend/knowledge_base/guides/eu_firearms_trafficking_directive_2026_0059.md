## What the proposal does

Building on the **EU Action Plan on Firearms Trafficking 2020-2025** (COM(2020) 608) and the **Roadmap on Firearms Trafficking from the Western Balkans** (2018-2024), the 2026 proposal **harmonises criminal offences and minimum penalties** across the EU for firearms trafficking and a defined set of related offences.

Key elements (per Commission proposal COM(2026) 102):

### 1. Common definitions of firearms-trafficking offences
- **Illicit manufacturing** of firearms, parts, components, ammunition (including 3D-printed firearms and "ghost guns")
- **Illicit possession** with intent to distribute
- **Trafficking** across MS borders or to/from third countries
- **Trade-mark removal / serial-number obliteration** as a stand-alone offence
- **Conversion of blank-firing weapons** into live-firing firearms — closes a gap that the 2017 Implementing Regulation only partially addressed
- **Internet sales without authorisation** — explicit criminal offence for online marketplaces and individual sellers

### 2. Minimum-maximum penalty floor
- Article 83(1) TFEU empowers the EU to set **minimum rules** on minimum-maximum penalties
- The proposal sets the bar at: imprisonment of at least **4 years** for trafficking in a basic case; **8 years** for aggravated cases (organised crime, large quantities, military-grade weapons, trafficking to conflict zones)
- Member State penalties may go higher; the floor harmonises the EU's lower bound

### 3. Cross-border investigation and prosecution tools
- Mandatory MS participation in **EMPACT Firearms** (EU Policy Cycle for organised and serious international crime)
- Enhanced **Europol** coordination — Firearms Analysis Project (currently AP Firearms)
- **Eurojust** coordination on cross-border prosecutions
- Possible inclusion in **EPPO's mandate** as an ancillary competence (under review separately)

### 4. Victim and witness protection
- Specific protection regime for whistleblowers reporting firearms trafficking
- Cross-link with the **EU Anti-Trafficking Directive 2011/36/EU** for trafficking-in-human-beings interactions

### 5. Marking, deactivation, tracing
- Mandatory full digital traceability for firearms placed on the EU market — building on the **EU Firearms Directive 2021/555** Article 4 marking requirements
- **iARMS Database** (Interpol) interoperability obligations on MS competent authorities

## Why the proposal lands now

The political driver is the long-running concern that **Western Balkans-routed firearms** + **legally-acquired weapons converted from blank-firing or alarm guns** + **3D-printed component sales online** form a continuous risk pipeline feeding terrorism (e.g. November 2015 Paris attacks, March 2016 Brussels attacks), organised crime (Italian and Albanian mafias, Dutch and Swedish street violence), and lone-actor attacks. The 2024-2025 European cycle of organised-crime violence in **Sweden, the Netherlands, and Belgium** elevated firearms trafficking to a political priority — directly named in the **2024-2029 Strategic Agenda** of the European Council under "Internal Security".

|---|---|---|
| EU Firearms Directive | Dir (EU) 2021/555 | Civil-law acquisition + possession framework (this proposal complements with criminal law) |
