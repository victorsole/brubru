# EFSA: European Food Safety Authority

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) -- the EU's independent scientific risk-assessment agency for food and feed safety
- **Founding act:** Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 of 28 January 2002 (the **General Food Law**), CELEX 32002R0178 -- laid down general principles of EU food law, created the RASFF alert framework, and established EFSA, in the wake of the 1990s BSE crisis
- **Seat:** **Parma, Italy** -- designated by the Council as EFSA's permanent seat in December 2003; founded 2002, operational from 2003
- **Core mission:** independent scientific risk assessment and risk communication for the Commission, Parliament and Member States -- EFSA does **not** manage risk (no authorisations, limits or bans); the Commission (via comitology, e.g. SCoPAFF) and Member States do that
- **Transparency Regulation:** Regulation (EU) 2019/1381 (20 June 2019), CELEX 32019R1381, applicable from **27 March 2021** -- amended the General Food Law + five sectoral regimes (GMOs, additives, enzymes/flavourings, plant protection products, novel food): proactive study disclosure, mandatory EU study register, mandatory pre-submission consultation, EFSA own-initiative studies, reformed governance
- **Scientific panels (2024-2029 mandate): 11** -- FEEDAP (feed additives), AHAW (animal health/welfare), BIOHAZ (biological hazards), CONTAM (contaminants), FAF (food additives/flavourings), FCM (food contact materials), FEZ (food enzymes), GMO, NDA (nutrition/novel foods/allergens), PLH (plant health), PPR (pesticides). The 2021-2024 mandate had 10 panels (FCM+FEZ merged as CEP); split back for 2024-2029
- **Scientific Committee:** separate cross-cutting body developing harmonised risk-assessment methodology (emerging risks, weight-of-evidence), distinct from the 11 panels
- **Management Board (reformed by the Transparency Regulation):** 27 Member State reps + 2 EP reps + 2 Commission reps + 4 civil society/food-chain reps (consumer, environmental, farmer, industry) = 35 voting members, plus non-voting EFTA/EEA (Norway, Iceland) and EFSA Surveillance Authority reps, 38 total
- **Executive Director:** **Bernhard Url** (Austria), in post since 2014; **Nikolaus Kriz** nominated June 2025 as successor, taking office **September 2026**
- **Other governance bodies:** the **Advisory Forum** (national risk-assessment body reps, advises the Executive Director), **National Focal Points** (one per Member State), and the **Article 36 network of competent organisations** (Member State bodies EFSA can task and co-fund under Article 36 of the General Food Law)
- **EFSA Journal:** peer-reviewed, open-access journal publishing every scientific opinion, guidance document and statement -- the citable primary source for EFSA output
- **What EFSA does NOT do:** authorise products, set Maximum Residue Levels, approve pesticides, ban substances, run the EU Pesticides Database/GMO register, or manage RASFF alerts -- Commission (DG SANTE) risk-management functions drawing on EFSA's opinions
- **Underpins:** pesticide approvals/MRLs, novel food and edible-insect authorisations, GMO/New Genomic Techniques authorisations, additive/flavouring/enzyme approvals, animal health/welfare rules, plant health import controls, official-controls risk targeting

EFSA is the EU's central scientific reference point for anything that touches the food chain -- from pesticide residues and food additives to GMOs, animal disease and novel foods. It does not decide policy or authorise anything itself; it produces the independent risk assessments that the Commission and Member States then use to decide whether to approve, restrict or ban a substance or product. Understanding which EFSA panel is assessing a dossier, and where that assessment sits in the wider Commission decision chain, is essential for any stakeholder tracking a food-chain authorisation file.

## Legal Basis and Mandate

- **Regulation (EC) No 178/2002** (the General Food Law) is the foundational act of EU food law. It lays down the general principles and requirements of food law (traceability, the precautionary principle, food business operator responsibility), establishes the European Food Safety Authority, and lays the framework for the Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed (RASFF), which the Commission (DG SANTE) operates to circulate alerts on unsafe food and feed across Member States.
- Legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (internal market) and Article 168 TFEU (public health) considerations underpin the food-safety acquis EFSA supports.
- **Regulation (EU) 2019/1381** (the "Transparency Regulation") amended the General Food Law and five sectoral regulations/directives governing EFSA-relevant authorisation procedures (GMOs, food additives, food enzymes and flavourings, plant protection products, and novel food). It became applicable on 27 March 2021 and introduced:
  - **Proactive disclosure**: all studies and data submitted by applicants in support of an authorisation request are published, with limited confidentiality exceptions
  - **EU register of studies**: applicants and study sponsors must notify EFSA of commissioned studies before they are carried out, closing the loophole where unfavourable studies could simply not be submitted
  - **Pre-submission consultation**: EFSA advises prospective applicants on the content of a dossier before it is formally filed
  - **EFSA's own-initiative studies**: EFSA gained the power to commission additional studies itself where it identifies gaps or contradictions in the evidence
  - **Reformed governance**: the Management Board composition changed to broaden representation beyond Member State and Commission appointees (see QUICK FACTS)
  - **Strengthened risk communication**: a General Plan on Risk Communication coordinated between EFSA and the Commission
- EFSA's independence from risk management is a structural design choice of the General Food Law: EFSA assesses risk; the Commission and Member States manage it (deciding whether and how to act on that assessment, weighing non-scientific factors such as socio-economic impact or public acceptability).

## What EFSA Actually Does

### 1. Scientific risk assessment (the core function)
EFSA's 11 scientific panels and its Scientific Committee adopt scientific opinions, guidance documents and statements in response to requests from the Commission, the Parliament, Member States, or (for certain product authorisations) directly from industry applicants under EU sectoral law. Each panel covers a defined regulatory area:

| Panel | Acronym | Remit |
|-------|---------|-------|
| Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed | FEEDAP | Feed additive authorisations |
| Animal Health and Welfare | AHAW | Disease risk, welfare standards |
| Biological Hazards | BIOHAZ | Microbiological food safety |
| Contaminants in the Food Chain | CONTAM | Heavy metals, mycotoxins, process contaminants |
| Food Additives and Flavourings | FAF | Additive and flavouring safety |
| Food Contact Materials | FCM | Packaging and contact-material migration risk |
| Food Enzymes | FEZ | Enzyme safety for food processing |
| Genetically Modified Organisms | GMO | GMO and New Genomic Techniques risk assessment |
| Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens | NDA | Novel foods, health claims, allergens, dietary reference values |
| Plant Health | PLH | Pest and pathogen risk to plant health |
| Plant Protection Products and their Residues | PPR | Pesticide active substances and residues |

### 2. Peer review and dossier evaluation
For centrally regulated products (pesticide active substances, novel foods, food additives, GMOs), EFSA typically peer-reviews a dossier or a Rapporteur Member State's draft assessment, opens a public consultation window, and issues a conclusion that feeds directly into the Commission's implementing-act process (e.g. Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed -- SCoPAFF).

### 3. Self-tasked and Commission-requested scientific outputs
Beyond dossier-driven work, EFSA produces guidance documents (methodology for dossier submission), scientific statements responding to emerging issues, and horizon-scanning/emerging-risk identification work coordinated by the Scientific Committee.

### 4. Data collection and knowledge consolidation
EFSA compiles and analyses EU-wide monitoring data submitted by Member States (e.g. pesticide residue monitoring, zoonoses and foodborne outbreak data) to support both its own risk assessments and Commission risk-management decisions.

### 5. Risk communication
Under the Transparency Regulation, EFSA and the Commission coordinate a General Plan on Risk Communication, aiming for consistent, science-based public messaging on food-chain risks -- a response to past controversies (notably around glyphosate) where EFSA's scientific conclusions and public/political debate diverged sharply.

### 6. Cooperation with Member States
Through the Advisory Forum, National Focal Points and the Article 36 network of competent organisations, EFSA coordinates with national food safety agencies, sometimes co-funding joint scientific projects to build EU-wide risk-assessment capacity and avoid duplicated national assessments.

## Governance and Structure

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Management Board | 27 Member State reps + 2 EP reps + 2 Commission reps + 4 civil society/food-chain reps (35 voting), plus non-voting EFTA/EEA and EFSA Surveillance Authority reps (38 total); sets strategic direction, adopts the work programme and budget |
| Executive Director | Legal representative and day-to-day manager; currently Bernhard Url, succeeded by Nikolaus Kriz from September 2026 |
| Scientific Committee | Cross-cutting methodology and emerging-risk body, separate from the 11 panels |
| 11 Scientific Panels | Adopt opinions in their respective product/hazard area (see table above) |
| Advisory Forum | National risk-assessment body representatives, advises the Executive Director, coordinates with Member States |
| National Focal Points | One liaison contact per Member State |
| Article 36 network | Member State "competent organisations" EFSA can task and co-fund for joint scientific work |

## EFSA in the Wider EU Food Safety Architecture

| Body | Role | Relationship to EFSA |
|------|------|----------------------|
| EFSA | Independent scientific risk assessment | -- |
| European Commission (DG SANTE) | Risk management: proposes and adopts authorisations, MRLs, bans, runs RASFF and SCoPAFF | Decision-maker acting on EFSA's scientific opinions |
| SCoPAFF (Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed) | Member State comitology committee voting on Commission implementing acts | Votes on measures informed by EFSA conclusions |
| Rapporteur Member States | Prepare draft assessment reports for pesticide/certain dossiers | EFSA peer-reviews their draft assessments |
| National food safety authorities | Member State-level risk assessment and enforcement | Coordinate with EFSA via the Advisory Forum, Focal Points and Article 36 network |
| European Medicines Agency (EMA) | Risk assessment for human and veterinary medicines | Separate remit; cooperates with EFSA on antimicrobial resistance and veterinary/feed overlap issues |
| European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) | Risk assessment for chemicals (REACH, biocides) | Separate remit; cooperates with EFSA on pesticide active substances that are also chemicals under REACH |

## Why This Matters for Advocacy and Compliance

- **Track the right panel, not just the Commission file:** for any authorisation-dependent product (pesticide, novel food, GMO/NGT, additive, feed additive), the scientifically decisive stage is the relevant EFSA panel's peer review and public consultation, not just the later SCoPAFF vote. Stakeholders wanting to influence the evidence base should engage during the EFSA assessment, not only after the Commission publishes an implementing act.
- **Pre-submission consultation is a genuine engagement window:** since the Transparency Regulation, applicants can consult EFSA before filing, which changes the practical entry point for industry seeking a smoother dossier process.
- **Study register closes the "hide the bad study" gap:** any company submitting or commissioning a study relevant to an EU food/feed dossier must notify EFSA in advance, materially changing evidentiary strategy compared with the pre-2021 regime.
- **Independence from risk management is EFSA's core institutional identity:** citing an EFSA scientific opinion is not the same as citing a Commission decision. A stakeholder disagreeing with a risk-management outcome (e.g. an emergency authorisation or a political non-renewal) should be precise about whether the disagreement is with the science (EFSA) or the political/legal decision (Commission and Member States) -- conflating the two, as happened in the glyphosate debate, damages credibility.
- **11-panel structure changed in 2024:** anyone tracking food-contact-material or enzyme dossiers should note the FCM/FEZ split from the former CEP panel took effect with the 2024-2029 mandate; older documents referencing "the CEP Panel" describe the 2021-2024 structure.
- **EFSA Journal is the primary citable source:** for any EU-specific food safety claim in a position paper, briefing or compliance report, an EFSA Journal opinion is a stronger citation than secondary press coverage.
