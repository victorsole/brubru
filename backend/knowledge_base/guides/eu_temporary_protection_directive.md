# EU Temporary Protection Directive — Ukraine Displaced Persons

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Friday 26 June 2026 — COMMISSION PROPOSES A FURTHER ONE-YEAR EXTENSION)**: The European Commission adopted a proposal for a **Council Implementing Decision to extend temporary protection for people fleeing Ukraine by one more year, to 4 March 2028** (current expiry: 4 March 2027). The proposal is accompanied by (1) a **Communication** "a predictable and common European way forward for Ukrainians in the EU", (2) a proposal for a **Council Recommendation on a coordinated transition out of temporary protection** (towards national residence status, voluntary return, or other legal statuses), and (3) a **Voluntary Return and Recovery Programme pilot**. The proposal also signals that temporary protection is, as a rule, not to be granted to newly arriving persons not authorised by the Ukrainian authorities to leave Ukraine in view of military obligations. Lead: DG HOME (Commissioner for Internal Affairs and Migration). **Hedge: this is a PROPOSAL — the Council must adopt it (unanimity in practice via Implementing Decision) for the extension to take effect; the 4 March 2028 date is not yet legally binding.** Source: DG HOME news, `home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-proposes-extend-temporary-protection-people-fleeing-ukraine-additional-year-2026-06-26_en`.
- **Legal framework — Temporary Protection Directive**: **Council Directive 2001/55/EC** of 20 July 2001 on minimum standards for giving temporary protection in the event of a mass influx of displaced persons (CELEX **32001L0055**). It had never been activated before 2022. Temporary protection is a **collective, immediate protection status** that does not require individual asylum assessment.
- **Activation for Ukraine**: **Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382** of 4 March 2022 (CELEX **32022D0382**) introduced temporary protection for persons displaced from Ukraine as a consequence of the Russian military invasion of 24 February 2022. It was the first-ever activation of Directive 2001/55/EC.
- **Extension chain**: the status was extended in successive cycles to **4 March 2025**, then to **4 March 2026**, then (June 2025 cycle, Member States endorsing on 13 June 2025) to **4 March 2027**. The 26 June 2026 proposal would carry it to **4 March 2028**.
- **What the status grants**: a harmonised set of rights across Member States — a residence permit for the duration of protection, access to the labour market, housing, social welfare/means of subsistence, medical care, and access to education for minors. It is distinct from (and does not pre-empt) the right to apply for asylum.
- **Scale**: temporary protection has covered roughly **4 million** beneficiaries across the EU at its peak (the largest displacement into the EU since the Second World War); figures fluctuate as people return, move, or transition to other statuses.
- **Strategic direction (2025-2026)**: EU policy is shifting from open-ended extension towards a **structured exit / transition** — phasing beneficiaries into national residence permits, study/work statuses, or supported voluntary return, while keeping protection in place as long as the security situation in Ukraine prevents safe, durable return.

## How temporary protection differs from asylum
- **Temporary protection** = a fast, collective, time-limited status triggered by a Council decision in a mass-influx situation; no individual refugee-status determination required; rights are harmonised by Directive 2001/55/EC.
- **Asylum / international protection** = an individual assessment under the Common European Asylum System (now the EU Pact on Migration and Asylum, see `eu_migration_asylum_pact`). Beneficiaries of temporary protection retain the right to lodge an asylum application at any time.

## Key dates
- 20 July 2001 — Directive 2001/55/EC adopted (dormant until 2022)
- 4 March 2022 — first activation for Ukraine (Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382)
- 13 June 2025 — Member States endorse extension to 4 March 2027
- 26 June 2026 — Commission proposes extension to 4 March 2028 + transition package
- 4 March 2027 — current legal expiry (pending the proposed extension)
