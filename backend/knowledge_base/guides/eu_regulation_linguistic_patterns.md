# Linguistic Patterns of EU Regulations

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Linguistic patterns and drafting conventions for EU regulations
- Legal basis: Article 288 TFEU (binding in entirety, directly applicable in all Member States)
- Source corpus: 5 EP+Council regulations -- (EU) 2017/746 (IVD), (EU) 2024/1348 (asylum), (EU) 2024/1183 (eIDAS 2), (EU) No 536/2014 (clinical trials), (EU) 2020/1503 (crowdfunding)
- Modal verb rules: "shall" = binding obligation (enacting terms only, 300-450 per act); "should" = policy intent (recitals only, 150-200 per act); "may" = discretion (30-70 per act); "must" = virtually absent (0-2 per act)
- Typical structure: 75-110 recitals, 50-113 articles across 6-19 chapters, 18-74 definitions
- Closing formula: "This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States."
- Amendment technique: Alphanumeric insertion convention (Article 5a, paragraph 2a, point (c)(ia))
- Key cross-reference phrases: "in accordance with" (80-100 per act), "pursuant to" (40-80), "as referred to in" (30-60)
- Policy domains covered: Internal market, financial services, asylum/migration, health/pharma, digital/technology

Training reference for understanding, drafting, and amending EU regulations (acts with direct effect under Article 288 TFEU). Based on analysis of five EP+Council regulations across different policy domains:

- **Regulation (EU) 2017/746** -- In vitro diagnostic medical devices (health/internal market)
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/1348** -- Common procedure for international protection (asylum/migration)
- **Regulation (EU) 2024/1183** -- European Digital Identity Framework (digital/eIDAS 2, amending act)
- **Regulation (EU) No 536/2014** -- Clinical trials on medicinal products (health/pharma)
- **Regulation (EU) 2020/1503** -- European crowdfunding service providers (financial services)

## 1. Canonical Structure of an EU Regulation

Every EP+Council regulation follows a fixed structural hierarchy. Deviation from this structure is extremely rare.

### 1.1 Title Block

Full formal title always follows this pattern:

> REGULATION (EU) [Year]/[Number] OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of [Date in full] on [subject matter] and [amending/repealing prior act(s)]

Optionally followed by: "(Text with EEA relevance)" for acts extending to the European Economic Area.

Older regulations (pre-2015) include "No" before the number: "Regulation (EU) No 536/2014". Newer ones omit it: "Regulation (EU) 2024/1348".

### 1.2 Enacting Authority

Always in uppercase:

> THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL OF THE EUROPEAN UNION,

### 1.3 Preamble (Visas)

A fixed sequence of "Having regard to..." clauses citing the legal basis. The order is mandated by the Joint Practical Guide:

1. **Treaty basis**: "Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and in particular Article [X] thereof,"
2. **Commission proposal**: "Having regard to the proposal from the European Commission,"
3. **National parliaments**: "After transmission of the draft legislative act to the national parliaments,"
4. **Consultative bodies**: "Having regard to the opinion of the European Economic and Social Committee" / "After consulting the Committee of the Regions," (with OJ references)
5. **Other consultations** (if applicable): "After consulting the European Central Bank,"
6. **Legislative procedure**: "Acting in accordance with the ordinary legislative procedure,"

Each visa ends with a comma. Footnotes contain OJ references for opinions and EP/Council positions.

### 1.4 Recitals

Introduced by the single word:

> Whereas:

Numbered sequentially in parentheses: (1), (2), (3) ... Typical range: 75-110 recitals for a substantive regulation.

### 1.5 Enacting Formula

> HAVE ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:

### 1.6 Enacting Terms (Body)

Organised hierarchically:

| Level | Format | Example |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Chapter | Roman numerals, uppercase title | CHAPTER III -- AUTHORISATION PROCEDURE |
| Section | Arabic numerals within chapter | Section 2 -- Examination Procedure |
| Article | Arabic numerals, continuous | Article 28 |
| Paragraph | Arabic numerals within article | 1. / 2. / 3. |
| Point | Lowercase letters in parentheses | (a) / (b) / (c) |
| Sub-point | Lowercase Roman numerals | (i) / (ii) / (iii) |
| Subparagraph | Unnumbered text block after a paragraph | (no marker) |

Typical range: 50-113 articles across 6-19 chapters.

### 1.7 Final Clause

> This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.

This formula is unique to regulations (not used in directives) and reflects Article 288 TFEU.

### 1.8 Signature Block

> Done at [Brussels/Strasbourg/Luxembourg], [date in full].

Followed by signatories of both co-legislators (EP President and Council President).

### 1.9 Annexes

Referenced in the body ("as set out in Annex I"), numbered with Roman numerals. Contain technical specifications, correlation tables, templates, forms.

## 2. Modal Verb System

EU regulations enforce a strict modal verb discipline that distinguishes binding law from policy intent.

### 2.1 "Shall" -- Binding Obligation (Enacting Terms Only)

"Shall" is the exclusive verb for creating legal obligations. It appears 300-450 times in a typical regulation. It is NEVER used in recitals.

**Positive obligations:**
- "Member States **shall ensure** that..." (obligation on states)
- "The manufacturer **shall draw up** and keep up to date the technical documentation." (obligation on private actors)
- "The determining authority **shall examine** and take decisions on applications." (institutional duty)
- "ESMA **shall establish** a register of all crowdfunding service providers." (agency duty)

**Conditional obligations:**
- "Where a crowdfunding service provider offers individual portfolio management of loans, an investor **shall** give the mandate..." (triggered by a condition)

### 2.2 "Should" -- Policy Intent (Recitals Only)

"Should" is used exclusively in recitals to express the legislator's reasoning and intent. Typical count: 150-200 occurrences. It NEVER appears in operative articles.

- "A fundamental revision of that Directive **should** be needed..." (recital)
- "This Regulation **should** be without prejudice to..." (recital)
- "Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 **should** therefore be amended accordingly," (bridge recital)

### 2.3 "May" -- Permission and Discretion (Enacting Terms)

"May" grants a power without creating an obligation. Typical count: 30-70 occurrences.

- "Member States **may** require that such health institutions submit..." (state discretion)
- "The Commission **may** adopt implementing acts to..." (optional empowerment)
- "That consent **may** be withdrawn at any time..." (individual right expressed as permission)

**"May only" -- Conditional Permission:**
- "A clinical trial **may** be conducted **only** if..." (default prohibition lifted by conditions)
- "A clinical trial on minors **may** be conducted **only** where, in addition to the conditions set out in Article 28, all of the following conditions are met..." (heightened conditions)

### 2.4 "Shall not" -- Prohibition

- "Crowdfunding service providers **shall not** have any participation in any crowdfunding offer on their crowdfunding platforms." (absolute prohibition)
- "This Regulation **shall not** affect the application of Directive 2013/59/Euratom." (scope limitation)
- "Applicants subject to the border procedure **shall not** be authorised to enter the territory." (procedural prohibition)

### 2.5 "Must" -- Virtually Absent

"Must" appears 0-2 times in a typical regulation. EU legislative drafting systematically avoids it in favour of "shall". When it does appear, it is in narrow technical contexts ("the security of the duplicated datasets **must** be at the same level").

### Summary Table

| Verb | Where Used | Function | Frequency |
|------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| shall | Enacting terms only | Binding obligation | 300-450 per regulation |
| should | Recitals only | Policy intent/reasoning | 150-200 per regulation |
| may | Enacting terms | Discretion/permission | 30-70 per regulation |
| shall not | Enacting terms | Prohibition | 15-30 per regulation |
| must | Extremely rare | Avoided entirely | 0-2 per regulation |

## 3. Recital Patterns

### 3.1 Function of Recitals

Recitals are NOT binding law but they are legally relevant as interpretive aids. The Court of Justice uses them to determine legislative intent. They serve these functions:

| Function | Typical Position | Example |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| Policy context | Recitals 1-5 | Citing Commission communications, European Council conclusions |
| Problem identification | Recitals 3-8 | "Experience shows that a harmonised approach has only been partly achieved" |
| Objective statement | Recitals 5-10 | "In order to foster cross-border provision..." |
| Prior law critique | Recitals 3-6 | "Directive 2001/20/EC aims to simplify... However, experience shows..." |
| Specific provision justification | Middle recitals | One or more recitals explaining each key article |
| Delegated/implementing act justification | Near-end recitals | "In order to ensure uniform conditions..." |
| Fundamental rights compliance | Near-end recitals | "This Regulation respects the fundamental rights recognised by the Charter" |
| Subsidiarity and proportionality | Penultimate recitals | "Since the objectives... cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States..." |
| Territorial exclusions | Final recitals | "In accordance with Protocol No 22..." (Denmark opt-out) |
| Bridge recital | Last recital | "[The prior act] should therefore be [amended/repealed] accordingly," |

### 3.2 Connecting Phrases in Recitals

| Phrase | Frequency | Function |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| "In order to" | Very high (20-30 per act) | Teleological/purpose justification |
| "To ensure" | High (15-25) | Protective/guarantee rationale |
| "should be" | Very high (40+) | Soft prescriptive recommendation |
| "It is necessary to" / "It is essential to" | Medium (5-10) | Necessity argument |
| "It is appropriate to" | Medium (3-8) | Proportionality justification |
| "However" | Medium (5-10) | Qualification or counter-argument |
| "Therefore" / "should therefore" | Medium (7-16) | Logical consequence |
| "In particular" | Medium (5-15) | Specification |
| "Without prejudice to" | Low-medium (2-5) | Saving other provisions |
| "Where" | High (15-25) | Conditional framing |
| "Since" | Low (3-6) | Causal reasoning (subsidiarity recitals) |
| "In view of" / "Given" / "Having regard to" | Low (2-5) | Premise statement |
| "Moreover" / "Furthermore" / "In addition" | Low (2-5) | Additive reasoning |
| "This Regulation should be without prejudice to" | Low (1-3) | Self-limiting scope |

### 3.3 Subsidiarity Recital (Mandatory)

Every regulation includes a near-final recital with this formula:

> "Since the objectives of this Regulation... cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States but can rather, by reason of their scale and effects, be better achieved at Union level, the Union may adopt measures, in accordance with the principle of subsidiarity as set out in Article 5 of the Treaty on European Union. In accordance with the principle of proportionality as set out in that Article, this Regulation does not go beyond what is necessary in order to achieve those objectives."

## 4. Definitions Article

### 4.1 Opening Formula

Almost always Article 2 (occasionally Article 3 if Article 2 is "Scope"). Opens with:

> "For the purposes of this Regulation, the following definitions apply:"

### 4.2 Definition Syntax

Each definition follows an invariant pattern:

> (N) '[defined term]' means [definiens];

Examples:
- (14) 'sponsor' means an individual, company, institution or organisation which takes responsibility for the initiation, for the management and for setting up the financing of the clinical trial;
- (28) 'economic operator' means a manufacturer, an authorised representative, an importer or a distributor;

### 4.3 Definition Techniques

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Genus-differentia** (most common) | "'manufacturer' means a natural or legal person who manufactures... and markets that device" |
| **Enumerated conditions** | "'crowdfunding service' means... which consists of any of the following activities: (i)...; (ii)..." |
| **Cross-reference** | "'biometric data' means biometric data as defined in Article 2, point (s), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1358" |
| **Negative/exclusionary** | "'non-sophisticated investor' means an investor who is not a sophisticated investor" |
| **Umbrella/composite** | "'international protection' means refugee status or subsidiary protection status" |
| **Import from other act** | "the definitions of 'medicinal product'... set out in points (2)... of Article 1 of Directive 2001/83/EC apply" |

### 4.4 Scale

Typical range: 18-74 definitions. Technical/product regulations (medical devices, pharma) tend towards higher numbers (50-74). Financial/procedural regulations tend towards lower numbers (18-20).

## 5. Cross-Referencing System

### 5.1 Internal Cross-References

| Pattern | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| "as referred to in Article X" | Links to a concept defined/established elsewhere |
| "in accordance with Article X(Y)" | Procedural compliance reference |
| "pursuant to Article X" | Legal authority basis |
| "set out in Annex I" | Reference to technical annexes |
| "as laid down in paragraph 4" | Reference within the same article |
| "the first subparagraph of this paragraph" | Precise sub-article reference |
| "Article 42(1), point (c), (f) or (j)" | Multi-point reference |

### 5.2 External Cross-References

**Full citation (first mention):**
> Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 June 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data (OJ L 119, 4.5.2016, p. 1).

**Abbreviated (subsequent mentions):**
> Regulation (EU) 2016/679

**OJ citation format:**
> (OJ [Series] [Number], [Date], p. [Page])

### 5.3 Linking Phrases (Ranked by Frequency)

| Phrase | Function | Frequency |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| "in accordance with" | Procedural compliance | Very high (80-100 per act) |
| "pursuant to" | Legal authority/basis | High (40-80) |
| "as referred to in" | Cross-referencing concepts | High (30-60) |
| "without prejudice to" | Preserving other provisions | Medium (10-20) |
| "for the purposes of" | Scope limitation | Medium (5-15) |
| "within the meaning of" | Adopting a definition from another act | Medium (5-10) |
| "subject to" | Conditional applicability | Medium (5-10) |
| "by way of derogation from" | Exception to a general rule | Low-medium (3-8) |
| "notwithstanding" | Override of another provision | Low (3-6) |
| "mutatis mutandis" | Applied with necessary changes | Low (1-5) |

## 6. Temporal and Procedural Language

### 6.1 Entry into Force (Standard Formula)

> "This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union."

This is the default formula. Variations include immediate entry into force or a specific date.

### 6.2 Date of Application (Delayed Application)

Many regulations separate entry into force from date of application:

> "It shall apply from [specific date]."

This creates a preparation window (typically 1-2 years) between entry into force and actual obligation.

### 6.3 Staggered Application

Complex regulations may have different application dates for different provisions:

> "Articles 31 to 46 and Article 96 shall apply from 26 November 2017."
> "For class D devices, Article 24(4) shall apply from 26 May 2023. For class B and class C devices, Article 24(4) shall apply from 26 May 2025."

### 6.4 Deadline Expressions

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| "within X days" | "within 45 days from the validation date" |
| "not later than X days" | "not later than 15 days after they become aware of the incident" |
| "no later than [duration]" | "no later than two months from the date on which the application is lodged" |
| "at the latest" | "at the latest within 40 days of the matter having been brought before it" |
| "for a period of" | "for a period of at least 10 years" |
| "by [specific date]" | "by 10 November 2021" |
| "as soon as possible" | Used rarely, for non-critical notifications |
| "without undue delay" | Standard EU formula for "promptly" (no fixed number of days) |

### 6.5 Transitional Provisions

Characteristic patterns for managing the transition from old law to new:

> "Certificates issued... prior to [date] shall remain valid until the end of the period indicated on the certificate."
> "Devices lawfully placed on the market... may continue to be made available on the market or put into service until [date]."
> "Applications for international protection lodged before [date] shall be governed by Directive 2013/32/EU."

### 6.6 Review Clauses

> "By [date] and every [X] years thereafter, the Commission shall present a report to the European Parliament and to the Council on the application of this Regulation."

### 6.7 Repeal with Correlation

> "[Prior act] is repealed with effect from the date referred to in Article [X]."
> "References to the repealed [Directive/Regulation] shall be construed as references to this Regulation and shall be read in accordance with the correlation table set out in the Annex."

### 6.8 Binding Clause

Unique to regulations (never appears in directives):

> "This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States."

## 7. Delegation of Powers

### 7.1 Delegated Acts (Article 290 TFEU)

Used to **supplement or amend non-essential elements** of the regulation. Commission empowerment follows a standard pattern:

**Empowerment in the body:**
> "The Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts in accordance with Article [X] amending [specific provisions]."

**Safeguards (always in a dedicated article):**

| Safeguard | Standard Language |
|-----------|------------------|
| Time limit | "conferred on the Commission for a period of five years from [date]" |
| Tacit renewal | "tacitly extended for periods of an identical duration, unless the European Parliament or the Council opposes" |
| Revocability | "may be revoked at any time by the European Parliament or by the Council" |
| Expert consultation | "the Commission shall consult experts designated by each Member State" |
| Simultaneous notification | "the Commission shall notify it simultaneously to the European Parliament and to the Council" |
| Objection period | "shall enter into force only if no objection has been expressed... within a period of [two/three] months" |
| Separate acts | "a separate delegated act in respect of each power delegated" |

### 7.2 Implementing Acts (Article 291 TFEU)

Used to ensure **uniform conditions for implementation**. Governed by comitology rules (Regulation (EU) No 182/2011).

**Empowerment in the body:**
> "The Commission may, by means of implementing acts, establish [technical standards/specifications/procedures]."
> "Those implementing acts shall be adopted in accordance with the examination procedure referred to in Article [X]."

**Comitology procedures:**

| Procedure | When Used |
|-----------|-----------|
| Advisory procedure (Art. 4 of Reg. 182/2011) | Procedural/administrative matters with no direct safety impact |
| Examination procedure (Art. 5 of Reg. 182/2011) | Standard procedure for substantive acts |
| Urgency procedure (Art. 8 of Reg. 182/2011) | Immediately applicable acts in justified urgent cases |

### 7.3 Regulatory Technical Standards (Financial Services Specific)

Financial regulations delegate to EU agencies (ESMA, EBA) to draft standards:

> "ESMA shall submit those draft regulatory technical standards to the Commission by [date]."
> "The Commission is empowered to adopt the regulatory technical standards referred to in [paragraph] in accordance with Articles 10 to 14 of Regulation (EU) No 1095/2010."

## 8. Amendment Technique (for Amending Regulations)

When a regulation amends a prior regulation, it uses a closed set of formulaic instructions:

### 8.1 Amendment Instructions Vocabulary

| Instruction | Formula |
|-------------|---------|
| Full replacement | "Article X **is replaced by the following:**" |
| Amendment container | "Article X **is amended as follows:**" |
| Paragraph replacement | "paragraph X **is replaced by the following:**" |
| Point replacement | "point (X) **is replaced by the following:**" |
| Insertion of new article | "**the following article is inserted:** Article Xa" |
| Insertion of new paragraph | "**the following paragraph is inserted:** Xa." |
| Addition at end | "**the following points are added:**" |
| Deletion | "Article X **is deleted;**" / "paragraph X **is deleted;**" |
| Structural conversion | "**the single paragraph becomes paragraph 1;**" |
| Annex amendment | "Annexes I to IV **are amended, respectively, in accordance with** Annexes I to IV to this Regulation" |

### 8.2 Alphanumeric Insertion Convention

When new articles, paragraphs, or points are inserted between existing ones, they use letter suffixes to preserve the parent numbering:

- Articles: Article 5a, Article 5b, Article 5c...
- Paragraphs: 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b...
- Points: (5a), (23a), (23b)...

### 8.3 "Added" vs "Inserted"

- **"Inserted"**: placed between existing items (requires alphanumeric suffix)
- **"Added"**: appended at the end (uses next sequential number)

## 9. Characteristic Legal Jargon

### 9.1 Universal EU Legislative Terms

These terms appear across all regulations regardless of policy domain:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Member States | The 27 EU countries as legal entities |
| competent authority/authorities | National authority designated to enforce the regulation |
| economic operator(s) | Umbrella term for private actors in a supply chain |
| implementing acts | Commission acts for uniform implementation conditions |
| delegated acts | Commission acts supplementing/amending non-essential elements |
| examination procedure | Standard comitology procedure for implementing acts |
| conformity assessment | Process to verify compliance with legal requirements |
| harmonised standards | Standards adopted by European standardisation bodies |
| common specifications | Technical specifications adopted by the Commission where harmonised standards are insufficient |
| notified body/bodies | Third-party assessment bodies designated by Member States |
| CE marking | Marking indicating conformity with EU harmonisation legislation |
| placing on the market | First making available of a product on the EU market |
| making available on the market | Any supply for distribution, consumption, or use |
| market surveillance | Monitoring of products/services already on the market |
| post-market surveillance | Manufacturer's systematic monitoring after placing on market |
| vigilance | System for reporting and investigating serious incidents |
| durable medium | Any instrument allowing information to be stored and accessed for future reference |
| professional secrecy | Confidentiality obligation on public authorities |

### 9.2 Standard Legal Phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| "without prejudice to" | Does not affect the application of (preserves other provisions) |
| "in accordance with" | Following the rules/procedures of |
| "pursuant to" | Under the authority of / based on |
| "for the purposes of" | Within the scope of / as used in |
| "within the meaning of" | As defined by (another act) |
| "subject to" | Conditional upon |
| "by way of derogation from" | As an exception to |
| "notwithstanding" | Despite / overriding |
| "mutatis mutandis" | With the necessary changes |
| "inter alia" | Among other things |
| "ex nunc" | From this point forward |
| "without undue delay" | Promptly (without specifying exact days) |
| "duly justified" / "duly motivated" | With proper reasoning given |
| "on an individual basis" | Case-by-case |
| "shall be deemed to" | Legal fiction -- treated as if |
| "shall be presumed" | Rebuttable legal presumption |
| "binding in its entirety and directly applicable" | Standard formula for regulations |

### 9.3 Rights and Obligations Language

Rights in regulations are typically expressed NOT as subjective rights of the individual but as **obligations on authorities or regulated entities**:

- Not: "The investor has the right to a reflection period"
- But: "The crowdfunding service provider **shall provide for** a pre-contractual reflection period"

When rights are expressed directly:
- "Applicants **shall have the right** to remain on the territory" (asylum)
- "Natural or legal persons **may** claim compensation" (product liability)
- "That consent **may** be withdrawn at any time" (clinical trials)

Unconditional rights use reinforcing phrases: "without any resulting detriment", "without having to provide any justification", "at any time", "without giving a reason and without incurring a penalty".

## 10. Policy Domain Variations

While the structural and linguistic framework is consistent, different policy domains have characteristic vocabulary:

### 10.1 Internal Market / Product Safety
- Conformity assessment, CE marking, notified bodies, technical documentation, UDI, risk management system, quality management system, EU declaration of conformity, free movement, New Legislative Framework, post-market surveillance, field safety corrective action

### 10.2 Financial Services
- Competent authority, authorisation, sophisticated/non-sophisticated investor, key investment information sheet, prudential safeguards, regulatory technical standards (RTS), implementing technical standards (ITS), ESMA, EBA, passport mechanism, default rate, special purpose vehicle, due diligence

### 10.3 Asylum and Migration
- International protection, refugee status, subsidiary protection, determining authority, non-refoulement, safe third country, safe country of origin, subsequent application, border procedure, accelerated examination, effective remedy, best interests of the child, ex nunc examination

### 10.4 Health / Pharmaceuticals
- Clinical trial, sponsor, investigator, informed consent, adverse event, serious adverse event, suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction (SUSAR), benefit-risk balance, good clinical practice (GCP), good manufacturing practice (GMP), marketing authorisation, ethics committee, Eudravigilance, incapacitated subject

### 10.5 Digital / Technology
- Electronic identification, trust service, qualified trust service, European Digital Identity Wallet, electronic attestation of attributes, relying party, assurance level (low/substantial/high), interoperability, authentic source, electronic ledger, selective disclosure, strong user authentication, zero-knowledge proof

## 11. Gender-Inclusive and Accessibility Language

Modern EU regulations (post-2020) consistently use:
- "his or her" / "himself or herself" / "he or she" (gender-inclusive pronouns)
- "persons with disabilities" / "on an equal basis with other users" (UN CRPD language)
- "in a way adapted to their age and mental maturity" (child-appropriate communication)
- "comprehensive, concise, clear, relevant, and understandable to a layperson" (plain language standard)

## 12. Practical Guidance for Brubru Users

### 12.1 When Drafting Amendments to Regulations

1. Use "shall" for obligations, "may" for permissions, "shall not" for prohibitions. Never use "must" or "should" in operative text.
2. Cross-reference using "as referred to in Article X(Y)" or "in accordance with Article X".
3. When inserting new provisions between existing ones, use alphanumeric suffixes (Article 5a, paragraph 2a, point (c)(ia)).
4. Definitions follow the pattern: "'[term]' means [definiens];".
5. Preserve the existing structural hierarchy (chapter > section > article > paragraph > point > sub-point).

### 12.2 When Writing Public Consultations Referencing Regulations

1. Cite the regulation formally on first mention with its full title, then use the short form.
2. Reference specific articles and paragraphs to anchor arguments.
3. Use recitals to support interpretive arguments about legislative intent.
4. Invoke the subsidiarity and proportionality recitals when arguing for or against EU-level action.

### 12.3 When Proposing New Legislation

1. Follow the preamble sequence exactly (Treaty basis, Commission proposal, national parliaments, consultative bodies, procedure).
2. Include a definitions article early in the text (typically Article 2).
3. Include recitals justifying every major operative provision.
4. Include mandatory recitals for: fundamental rights (Charter), subsidiarity, proportionality, and data protection (EDPS consultation if applicable).
5. Use the standard delegation patterns for empowering the Commission (delegated acts for non-essential amendments, implementing acts for uniform conditions).
6. Include transitional provisions, a review clause, and the standard entry-into-force formula.

### 12.4 When Analysing Existing Regulations

1. The recitals reveal legislative intent -- use them to understand why provisions exist.
2. "Without prejudice to" clauses identify related legislation that remains applicable.
3. "By way of derogation from" clauses identify exceptions to general rules.
4. Delegated act provisions indicate which parts the Commission can change without the full legislative procedure.
5. Review clause dates indicate when the regulation will next be evaluated.
