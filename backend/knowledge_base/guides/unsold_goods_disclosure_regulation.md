# Unsold Goods Disclosure Regulation - Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2

- **Common name:** Unsold Goods Disclosure Regulation. It is the transparency rulebook for the ESPR's regime on unsold consumer products. Do not confuse it with the destruction BAN, which is a separate Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296 adopted the same day.

- **Application:** applies from 2 March 2027 (deferred to give operators time to implement the format). The underlying disclosure obligation in ESPR Article 24(1) itself runs from the first full financial year after the ESPR entered into force, namely 18 July 2024.

- **Structure:** 7 Articles, plus Annex I (disclosure format), Annex II (consumer-product categories reported at four-digit CN level) and Annex III (verification principles and procedure).

## Overview

This Regulation is the EU's answer to a practice long criticised in fashion, footwear and electronics: destroying perfectly usable unsold stock. It does not ban destruction (that is the sibling Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296). Instead it makes destruction visible. For the first time, large companies must publicly, annually, and in a single standard EU format, disclose how much unsold consumer stock they discard, why, and what happens to it.

The obligation comes from Article 24(1) of the ESPR. This Implementing Regulation, adopted under Article 24(3), supplies the operational detail the ESPR left to the Commission: the exact data points, the disclosure format, how product categories are delimited, how long records are kept, and how national authorities verify the figures.

## Who must disclose, and when

- **Large enterprises** must disclose now. **Medium-sized enterprises** are brought in from 19 July 2030. **Micro and small enterprises are exempt** (ESPR Article 24(1)).
- Disclosure is **annual**, covering the previous financial year, and must be made **within 12 months** of the end of that financial year, on the company's website in the Annex I format.
- **Donated products are not covered**: donating stock is not discarding it.
- **CSRD link option (Article 2(2)):** a company already publishing sustainability reporting in its management report under Directive 2013/34/EU (Articles 19a or 29a) that includes the Annex I information there may, instead of publishing on its website directly, provide a website link to that report with a clear indication of where the unsold-goods information sits.

## What must be disclosed (Annex I)

For each product category, per financial year:
- the **number of units** discarded and the **total weight** in kilograms (estimates allowed, flagged with a plus-or-minus sign);
- the **reasons for discarding**, referring where applicable to the legitimate reasons listed in delegated acts under ESPR Article 25(5) (product safety, hygiene, protection of intellectual property rights, or destruction required by law);
- any applicable **derogations**;
- the **proportion delivered to each waste treatment operation**: preparing for reuse, recycling, other recovery including energy recovery, and disposal, with "unknown" used where the treatment cannot be established;
- the **measures taken and the measures planned** to prevent the destruction of unsold consumer products.

Note the Regulation's own definition: **"destruction" is the sum of recycling, other recovery and disposal.** Preparing for reuse is not counted as destruction. The legal entity is identified by its EUID (the European unique identifier under Directive (EU) 2017/1132) or another officially recognised identifier.

## Product categories (Combined Nomenclature)

Categories are delimited using the Combined Nomenclature (CN) from Council Regulation (EEC) No 2658/87: the **first two digits** are generally sufficient. The products listed in **Annex II** must be reported at the more granular **four-digit CN level**. Annex II covers the categories most associated with the destruction of unsold goods, including apparel and clothing accessories, leather and fur articles, textiles and household linen, footwear-adjacent goods, electronics and telecoms (smartphones, computers, monitors, displays), household appliances (refrigerators, washing machines, dishwashers, vacuum cleaners), batteries, furniture and mattresses, toys and games, and sanitary articles. Components and products not primarily intended for consumers are outside the obligation.

## Verification (Annex III)

- **Five-year records (Article 4):** operators keep the documentation demonstrating the delivery and reception of discarded products, including statements from waste treatment operators, for five years after disclosure.
- **Risk-based verification (Article 5, Annex III):** competent national authorities target checks using criteria such as no disclosure or unusually low numbers versus peers or prior years, a past record of non-compliance, a high percentage of "unknown" waste treatment, the size and nature of the operator's activities, and intelligence from other sources.
- **10% tolerance:** where the gap between the disclosed figure and the figure calculated from the documentation is less than 10% of the disclosed number, the operator is treated as compliant.
- Cross-border non-compliance is shared with the competent authorities of other affected Member States.

## Where it sits

- **The framework:** the ESPR (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781), Articles 24 and 25, creates the disclosure obligation and the destruction regime.
- **This act:** the disclosure format and verification detail.
- **The sibling act:** the destruction BAN, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/296, which prohibits the destruction of certain unsold products (apparel and footwear) and sets the derogations.
- **The wider architecture:** the digital product passport regime and the sectoral product laws (batteries, construction products, toys, detergents, textiles) that share the same ESPR foundation.
