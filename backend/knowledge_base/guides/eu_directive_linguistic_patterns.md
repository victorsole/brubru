# Linguistic Patterns of EU Directives

Training reference for understanding, drafting, and amending EU directives (acts requiring national transposition under Article 288 TFEU). Based on analysis of five EP+Council directives across different policy domains:

- **Directive (EU) 2019/1** -- ECN+ Directive, empowering competition authorities (antitrust/internal market)
- **Directive (EU) 2023/977** -- Exchange of information between law enforcement authorities (JHA/police cooperation)
- **Directive (EU) 2020/1828** -- Representative actions for consumer protection (consumer law)
- **Directive (EU) 2016/800** -- Procedural safeguards for children in criminal proceedings (fundamental rights/criminal justice)
- **Directive (EU) 2024/2823** -- Legal protection of designs, recast (intellectual property/internal market)

---

## 1. How Directives Differ from Regulations

Under Article 288 TFEU, a directive is "binding, as to the result to be achieved, upon each Member State to which it is addressed, but shall leave to the national authorities the choice of form and methods." This fundamental legal characteristic permeates every linguistic choice in the text.

| Feature | Directive | Regulation |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Enacting formula | "HAVE ADOPTED THIS DIRECTIVE:" | "HAVE ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:" |
| Closing clause | "This Directive is addressed to the Member States." | "This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States." |
| Transposition article | Present -- deadline + reference method + communication obligation | Absent |
| Core obligation pattern | "Member States shall ensure that..." | Direct obligations on actors ("The authority shall...") |
| Rights creation | Indirect: via Member State obligations | Direct: rights conferred on individuals |
| National flexibility | "in accordance with national law", "Member States may..." | Prescribes directly, less national discretion |
| Effect | Requires national implementing legislation | Directly applicable, no transposition needed |

## 2. Canonical Structure

### 2.1 Title Block

> DIRECTIVE (EU) [Year]/[Number] OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of [Date] on [subject matter] and [amending/repealing prior act(s)]

Optionally followed by "(recast)" for recast directives and/or "(Text with EEA relevance)".

### 2.2 Preamble (Visas)

The visa sequence is identical to regulations:

1. **Treaty basis**: "Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and in particular Article [X] thereof,"
2. **Commission proposal**: "Having regard to the proposal from the European Commission,"
3. **National parliaments**: "After transmission of the draft legislative act to the national parliaments,"
4. **Consultative bodies**: EESC opinion, CoR opinion (with OJ references)
5. **Other consultations** (if applicable): ECB, Committee of the Regions
6. **Legislative procedure**: "Acting in accordance with the ordinary legislative procedure,"

### 2.3 Enacting Formula

> HAVE ADOPTED THIS DIRECTIVE:

### 2.4 Enacting Terms

Same hierarchy as regulations (Chapter > Section > Article > Paragraph > Point > Sub-point), but directives typically include these **directive-specific final articles** that regulations lack:

- **Transposition article** (always present)
- **Review/report article** (Commission evaluates transposition and implementation)
- **Entry into force article** (same as regulations)
- **Addressee article** (always present, always the final article)

### 2.5 Addressee Clause (Final Article)

Always the last article, always one sentence:

> "This Directive is addressed to the Member States."

Or, for directives with variable geometry (JHA/Schengen):

> "This Directive is addressed to the Member States in accordance with the Treaties."

This has no equivalent in regulations.

### 2.6 Annexes

May include:
- **Correlation table** (especially in recast directives) mapping old provisions to new
- **Transposition deadline table** (in recasts, preserving old transposition deadlines)
- **Lists of Union acts** within the directive's scope (e.g., Annex I of the Representative Actions Directive)
- Technical specifications, forms, templates

## 3. The Transposition Article

The transposition article is the **single most distinctive feature** of a directive. It has no equivalent in regulations.

### 3.1 Standard Formula

> "1. Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive by [DATE]. They shall immediately inform the Commission thereof."
>
> "When Member States adopt those measures, they shall contain a reference to this Directive or shall be accompanied by such a reference on the occasion of their official publication. The methods of making such reference shall be laid down by Member States."
>
> "2. Member States shall communicate to the Commission the text of the main measures of national law which they adopt in the field covered by this Directive."

### 3.2 Components

| Component | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| "shall bring into force the laws, regulations and administrative provisions" | The tripartite formula covering all forms of national measures |
| "necessary to comply with this Directive" | Result obligation, not verbatim transposition |
| "by [DATE]" | Transposition deadline (typically 2-3 years after adoption) |
| "They shall immediately inform the Commission thereof" | Notification obligation |
| "shall contain a reference to this Directive" | "Communautaire reference" -- traceability from national law to the directive |
| "The methods of making such reference shall be laid down by Member States" | Discretion on how to cite the directive |
| "shall communicate to the Commission the text" | Allows Commission to verify correct transposition |

### 3.3 Variations

**Split deadlines** (phased implementation):
> "By way of derogation from the first subparagraph, Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with Article 13 by [LATER DATE]."

**Two-date structure** (adoption deadline + application date):
> "Member States shall adopt and publish, by [DATE 1], the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive."
> "They shall apply those measures from [DATE 2]."

**Recast-selective transposition** (only new/amended provisions):
> "Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations or administrative provisions necessary to comply with Articles [list of specific articles] by [DATE]."

## 4. The "Member States Shall Ensure" Pattern

This is the **defining linguistic construction** of directives. It creates binding obligations while preserving Member State implementation freedom.

### 4.1 Quantitative Dominance

Across all 5 analysed directives, "Member States shall ensure that..." constitutes **77-92% of all "Member States shall" obligations** in the substantive articles.

| Directive | "MS shall ensure" | Total "MS shall" | Percentage |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|------------|
| ECN+ (2019/1) | 68 | 74 | 92% |
| Law enforcement info (2023/977) | 34 | 44 | 77% |
| Representative actions (2020/1828) | 28 | 48 | 58% |
| Children's rights (2016/800) | 30+ | ~40 | ~75% |
| Design protection (2024/2823) | Rare | -- | Low* |

*The Design Protection Directive is an exception: it uses substantive-declarative provisions ("A design right shall not subsist in...") instead of "Member States shall ensure" because it harmonises substantive law directly rather than prescribing outcomes.

### 4.2 How It Works

The construction operates on two levels simultaneously:

1. **Mandatory outcome**: "shall" makes the obligation binding; "ensure" requires the Member State to guarantee a result.
2. **Implementation freedom**: by saying "ensure that [X happens]" rather than "do X", the directive specifies WHAT must be achieved, not HOW.

### 4.3 Examples by Function

**Institutional obligation:**
> "Member States shall ensure that national administrative competition authorities are able to conduct all necessary unannounced inspections of undertakings..." (ECN+)

**Temporal obligation:**
> "Member States shall ensure that their Single Point of Contact provides the information requested... within the following time limits..." (Law enforcement)

**Rights-creating obligation:**
> "Member States shall ensure that children have the right to be accompanied by the holder of parental responsibility during court hearings." (Children's rights)

**Procedural obligation:**
> "Member States shall ensure that representative actions as provided for by this Directive can be brought by qualified entities." (Representative actions)

### 4.4 Companion Patterns

| Pattern | Function | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| "Member States shall ensure that..." | Core obligation (result) | See above |
| "Member States shall lay down..." | Delegate rulemaking to MS | "shall lay down rules on how... consumers... express their wish to be represented" |
| "Member States shall provide that..." | Create legal framework | "shall provide that the competent authorities are able to..." |
| "Member States shall designate..." | Institutional setup | "shall designate the competent authorities responsible" |
| "Member States may..." | Optional provisions | "may provide for an efficient administrative procedure" |
| "Member States may provide that..." | National option | "may provide for additional measures" |

### 4.5 Contrast with Regulations

A regulation would say: "The competent authorities shall conduct inspections..."
A directive says: "Member States shall ensure that competent authorities are able to conduct inspections..."

The directive addresses **Member States** and requires them to **ensure** a result. The regulation addresses **actors directly** and imposes obligations on them.

## 5. Modal Verb System

The modal verb system in directives is identical to regulations in principle, but the distribution differs because the primary addressee is Member States:

### 5.1 In Recitals

| Verb | Usage | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| should | Exclusive | "Member States should be able to extend the rights..." |
| shall | Never | Strictly reserved for enacting terms |

### 5.2 In Enacting Terms

| Verb | Usage | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| shall | Binding obligation | "Member States shall ensure that..." |
| may | Permission/discretion | "Member States may provide that..." |
| shall not | Prohibition | "A Member State shall not require a judicial authorisation..." |
| must | Virtually absent | Avoided in EU drafting (0-2 occurrences) |

### 5.3 Directive-Specific Modal Patterns

**"Member States should be able to"** (in recitals, signalling minimum harmonisation):
> "Member States should be able to extend the rights laid down in this Directive in order to provide a higher level of protection."

This phrase appears 5-15 times in typical directive recitals and is a strong signal of minimum harmonisation. It has no equivalent in regulation recitals.

## 6. Minimum Harmonisation vs Full Harmonisation

Directives exist on a spectrum from minimum to full (maximum) harmonisation. The linguistic markers are distinctive.

### 6.1 Minimum Harmonisation Markers

| Marker | Example | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| "at least" / "at a minimum" | "Member States shall at a minimum ensure that..." | Sets a floor |
| "without prejudice to" | "without prejudice to the ability of Member States to maintain more extensive guarantees" | Preserves national freedom |
| "shall not preclude" | "This Directive shall not preclude Member States from maintaining..." | Explicitly allows going further |
| "more stringent" / "more extensive" | "more extensive guarantees of independence" | Acknowledges MS can exceed the minimum |
| "Member States may" | "Member States may provide for additional measures" | National option |
| "in accordance with national law" | "in accordance with the conditions provided for in national law" | Defers to national traditions |
| Non-regression clause | "Nothing in this Directive shall be construed as limiting or derogating from..." | Preserves higher existing protections |
| "higher level of protection" | "the law of any Member State which provides a higher level of protection" | Floor, not ceiling |

### 6.2 Full (Maximum) Harmonisation Markers

| Marker | Example | Function |
|--------|---------|----------|
| "Member States shall not maintain or introduce provisions diverging from" | (negative -- this phrase is ABSENT from minimum directives) | Ceiling |
| Absence of "at least" and "may provide" | Direct substantive rules without flexibility | Prescriptive |
| Substantive-declarative style | "A design shall be considered new if..." | Rules stated directly |

### 6.3 The Non-Regression Clause

Criminal justice and fundamental rights directives include a mandatory non-regression clause:

> "Nothing in this Directive shall be construed as limiting or derogating from any of the rights and procedural safeguards that are ensured under the Charter, the ECHR, or other relevant provisions of international law, in particular the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, or the law of any Member State which provides a higher level of protection."

This establishes a **four-tier protection floor**: (1) EU Charter, (2) ECHR, (3) international law, (4) more protective national law.

## 7. Rights Creation in Directives

Directives create individual rights **indirectly**, through obligations on Member States. Three patterns are used:

### 7.1 Mediated Rights (Dominant Pattern)

> "Member States shall ensure that children **have the right to** a medical examination."

The right is named but wrapped inside a Member State obligation. The right does not exist by virtue of the directive alone; it exists because the directive obliges Member States to create it in national law.

### 7.2 Obligations Creating Implicit Rights

> "Member States shall ensure that children who are detained **are held separately from adults**."

No right is named. An obligation is placed on the state, and the individual right is the correlative of that obligation.

### 7.3 Consumer/Investor Protection Through Provider Obligations

> "The crowdfunding service provider **shall provide for** a pre-contractual reflection period."

Rights for individuals are expressed as obligations on regulated entities, with Member States required to create the enforcement framework.

### 7.4 Contrast with Regulations

Regulations can and do create rights directly:
> "Data subjects **shall have the right** to obtain from the controller..." (GDPR, a regulation)

Directives almost never use this direct construction because they lack direct effect until transposed.

## 8. Derogation and Exception Language

Directives, especially in criminal justice and fundamental rights, include carefully layered derogation mechanisms:

### 8.1 Standard Derogation Formula

> "Member States **may derogate from** [provision] where [condition], provided that [safeguard]."

### 8.2 Layered Derogation (Criminal Justice)

> "In exceptional circumstances, and only at the pre-trial stage, Member States may temporarily derogate from the application of the rights provided for in paragraph 3 to the extent justified in the light of the particular circumstances of the case, on the basis of one of the following compelling reasons: (a) where there is an urgent need to avert serious adverse consequences for the life, liberty or physical integrity of a person; (b) where immediate action by the investigating authorities is imperative to prevent substantial jeopardy to criminal proceedings."

Note the accumulated conditions: "exceptional circumstances" + "only at the pre-trial stage" + "temporarily" + "to the extent justified" + "compelling reasons" + enumerated grounds.

### 8.3 Best Interests Override

> "it being understood that the child's best interests shall always be a primary consideration"

This phrase overrides derogations in children's rights directives.

### 8.4 Proportionality Qualification

> "where this is proportionate in the circumstances of the case, taking into account, inter alia, whether a lawyer is present"

Built into the right itself, creating a flexible rather than absolute standard.

## 9. JHA/Schengen-Specific Features

Directives in the Area of Freedom, Security and Justice (AFSJ) contain distinctive features not found in other policy domains:

### 9.1 Variable Geometry Recitals

A bloc of consecutive recitals addressing opt-outs:

- **Protocol No 21** (Ireland): "Ireland is [taking part in / not taking part in] this Directive..."
- **Protocol No 22** (Denmark): "Denmark is not taking part in the adoption of this Directive and is not bound by it or subject to its application."
- **Schengen-associated states** (Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Liechtenstein): "This Directive constitutes a development of the provisions of the Schengen acquis within the meaning of [specific agreement]..."

### 9.2 Framework Decision Replacement

Post-Lisbon directives replacing pre-Lisbon Third Pillar instruments use:

> "Framework Decision [number] is repealed from [date]."
> "References to the repealed Framework Decision shall be construed as references to this Directive."

### 9.3 Schengen Convention Amendment

> "From [date], the parts of Articles 39 and 46 of the Convention implementing the Schengen Agreement that have not been replaced by Framework Decision [number] are replaced by this Directive in so far as those Articles relate to [scope]."

### 9.4 Legal Basis

JHA directives use specific Treaty bases:
- Article 82(2) TFEU -- criminal procedure minimum rules
- Article 83 TFEU -- criminal law harmonisation
- Article 87(2) TFEU -- police cooperation

These trigger Protocols 21 and 22, unlike Article 114 TFEU (internal market) directives.

## 10. Recast-Specific Features

When a directive is recast (consolidated with amendments), it includes these additional elements:

### 10.1 Title Marker

> "(recast)" appended to the title

### 10.2 Canonical Recast Recital

> "A number of amendments are to be made to [old Directive]. In the interests of clarity, that Directive should be recast."

This two-sentence recital (always recital (1)) is invariant across all EU recasts.

### 10.3 Transposition Limitation Recital

> "The obligation to transpose this Directive into national law should be confined to those provisions which represent a substantive amendment as compared with [old Directive]. The obligation to transpose the provisions which are unchanged arises under that earlier Directive."

### 10.4 Selective Transposition Article

The transposition article lists only the articles requiring new transposition, not the entire directive:

> "Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations or administrative provisions necessary to comply with Articles [list of specific amended/new articles] by [DATE]."

### 10.5 Deferred Repeal

> "[Old Directive] is repealed with effect from [transposition deadline], without prejudice to the obligations of the Member States relating to the time-limit for the transposition into national law of the Directive set out in Annex I."

### 10.6 Split Temporal Application

> "This Directive shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication."
> "However, Articles [list of unchanged provisions] shall apply from [transposition deadline]."

### 10.7 Mandatory Annexes

- **Annex I**: Transposition deadline of the repealed directive
- **Annex II**: Correlation table mapping old provisions to new

## 11. Cross-Referencing Patterns

### 11.1 Internal References

Identical to regulations: "as referred to in Article X(Y)", "in accordance with Article X", "set out in Annex I".

### 11.2 External References

Same citation format as regulations:

> Directive 2013/48/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 22 October 2013 on the right of access to a lawyer (OJ L 294, 6.11.2013, p. 1).

### 11.3 Directive-Specific Linking Phrases

| Phrase | Function |
|--------|----------|
| "in accordance with national law" | Defers implementation detail to MS |
| "as transposed into national law" | Acknowledges the transposition layer |
| "in the field covered by this Directive" | Scope delimiter for communication obligations |
| "necessary to comply with this Directive" | Transposition adequacy standard |

---

## 12. Definitions Article

Structure is identical to regulations:

> "For the purposes of this Directive, the following definitions apply:"

Followed by numbered definitions using the "means" copula:

> (1) 'term' means [definiens];

Typical range: 3-22 definitions. Criminal justice directives tend to have fewer (3-8) because they defer core concepts to national law. Competition and financial services directives have more (15-22).

## 14. Explanatory Documents Recital

Directives (not regulations) include a recital referencing the Joint Political Declaration of 28 September 2011:

> "In accordance with the Joint Political Declaration of 28 September 2011 of Member States and the Commission on explanatory documents, Member States have undertaken to accompany, in justified cases, the notification of their transposition measures with one or more documents explaining the relationship between the components of a directive and the corresponding parts of national transposition instruments."

This is unique to directives because only directives require transposition.

## 15. Practical Guidance for Brubru Users

### 15.1 When Drafting Amendments to Directives

1. Use "Member States shall ensure that..." for result obligations, not direct commands.
2. Use "Member States may..." for national options.
3. Include "in accordance with national law" when deferring implementation details.
4. For minimum harmonisation, include "at least" or "at a minimum" floor-setting language.
5. Preserve the "shall/should" split: "shall" only in enacting terms, "should" only in recitals.
6. When adding new provisions, ensure corresponding recitals justify them.

### 15.2 When Writing Public Consultations on Directives

1. Reference the transposition deadline and any Member State implementation gaps.
2. Cite recitals to support arguments about legislative intent.
3. For minimum harmonisation directives, argue for higher or lower national standards by reference to the non-regression clause.
4. Distinguish between the directive's requirements and the Member State's chosen transposition method.

### 15.3 When Proposing New Directives

1. Choose between minimum and full harmonisation and signal it consistently through "at least" markers or their absence.
2. Include mandatory recitals: subsidiarity, proportionality, fundamental rights (Charter), explanatory documents.
3. For JHA directives, include Protocol 21/22 recitals and the Schengen acquis recitals.
4. Always include a transposition article with the standard tripartite formula.
5. Always end with the addressee clause: "This Directive is addressed to the Member States."
6. Consider whether a non-regression clause is needed (mandatory for criminal justice/fundamental rights).

### 15.4 When Analysing Existing Directives

1. Check whether it is minimum or full harmonisation by looking for "at least", "Member States may", and "without prejudice to more favourable provisions".
2. The transposition deadline indicates when Member States must have implemented it.
3. The review clause indicates when the Commission will evaluate effectiveness.
4. For recast directives, use the correlation table (Annex) to map old provisions to new.
5. Check Protocol recitals for variable geometry (which Member States are bound).

### 15.5 Key Difference from Regulations for Amendators

When amending a directive (vs a regulation), remember:
- You are setting the **result** Member States must achieve, not prescribing the exact national rules.
- Use "Member States shall ensure that..." rather than direct commands on individuals.
- Include flexibility markers ("in accordance with national law", "Member States may provide that...") where national procedural traditions differ.
- The amendment will not take effect until Member States transpose it -- include realistic transposition deadlines.
