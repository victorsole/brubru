# EMA Medicine Shortages: SPOC, ESMP and the Critical Medicines Link

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the operational machinery the European Medicines Agency (EMA) uses to monitor, prevent and mitigate medicine shortages across the EU/EEA, built on the permanent mandate given to EMA by **Regulation (EU) 2022/123** (the "reinforced EMA role" regulation), CELEX **32022R0123**.
- **Legal basis**: Regulation (EU) 2022/123 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 January 2022 on a reinforced role for the European Medicines Agency in crisis preparedness and management for medicinal products and medical devices, OJ L 20, 31.1.2022. It amended Regulation (EC) 726/2004 (the centralised-procedure regulation) and gave EMA, for the first time, a permanent (not just crisis-triggered) shortage-monitoring mandate.
- **Medicines Shortages Steering Group (MSSG)**: the decision/coordination body established under Regulation 2022/123. Composition: one representative per Member State, one European Commission representative, one EMA representative, co-chaired by EMA and a Member State representative. Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway sit as observers, alongside the Patients' and Consumers' Working Party and the Healthcare Professionals' Working Party. MSSG coordinates urgent EU-wide action on medicine supply, quality, safety and efficacy issues, establishes critical medicines lists for major events, monitors supply and demand, and issues recommendations to the Commission and Member States. It does not have binding decision powers of its own; implementation rests with the Commission and Member States.
- **SPOC Working Party**: the Medicine Shortages Single Point of Contact (SPOC) Working Party, formally established May 2022 under Regulation 2022/123, after MSSG agreed its rules of procedure. Membership: one designated single point of contact per national competent authority (NCA) in each Member State. Chaired by EMA's Head of Supply and Availability of Medicines and Devices Service; vice-chair rotates with the Member State holding the Council presidency. Mandate: monitor events that could disrupt EU medicine supply, exchange information between EMA and NCAs, feed critical-medicines-list development, track supply levels, and report shortages and availability issues up to the MSSG. There is a parallel Medical Device Shortages SPOC Working Party for the medical-devices side of the 2022/123 mandate.
- **European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP)**: the IT platform (esmp.ema.europa.eu) through which marketing-authorisation holders (MAHs) and NCAs report shortage and supply/demand data to EMA. It centralises and automates data collection, replacing fragmented national reporting. Rollout: routine shortage-reporting functionality launched November 2024; became mandatory for MAHs and NCAs of centrally authorised products from **2 February 2025**; fully operational with all functionalities (routine reporting, close monitoring at MSSG request, crisis monitoring) from January 2025. Designed for interoperability with national and industry systems per the Commission's Interoperability Framework. Verify current operational scope live, as functionality has been phased in stages.
- **Union List of Critical Medicines**: the list of critical active substances/medicines that MSSG and the SPOC Working Party use to prioritise monitoring. First published December 2023, updated December 2024, containing 276 critical active substances (see `critical_medicines_act.md` for the full figure and how it links to the Critical Medicines Act's scope).
- **Marketing-authorisation-holder obligations**: MAHs must notify NCAs and EMA (via the ESMP for centrally authorised products) of anticipated or actual shortages, generally with defined advance notice periods, and must provide supply/demand data on request during close-monitoring or crisis scenarios. Direct Healthcare Professional Communications (DHPCs) are used to alert prescribers and pharmacists when a shortage has a clinical-management impact (for example switching patients to an alternative product or dosing regimen).
- **Public shortages catalogue**: EMA publishes a public catalogue of shortages of centrally authorised medicines (ongoing and resolved), searchable on the EMA website, distinct from the ESMP (which is the reporting backend used by industry and NCAs, not a public-facing catalogue).
- **Distinguish carefully**: the SPOC Working Party (NCA information-exchange network) and the MSSG (EU-level decision/coordination body) are two different governance layers created by the SAME regulation (2022/123); the ESMP is the IT platform that feeds both with data; the Union List of Critical Medicines is the prioritisation tool both bodies use; the Critical Medicines Act (COM(2025) 102, procedure 2025/0102(COD), see `critical_medicines_act.md`) is a separate, later, industrial-policy proposal that builds on this shortage-monitoring architecture but addresses manufacturing investment and procurement, not day-to-day shortage reporting.
- **Responsible DG / body**: European Medicines Agency (EMA), with DG SANTE and DG HERA involved on the policy side. EMA headquarters: Amsterdam. EMA Executive Director: Emer Cooke.
- **Official sources**: EMA overview `ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory/post-authorisation/medicine-shortages`; SPOC Working Party page `ema.europa.eu/en/committees/working-parties-other-groups/medicines-shortages-single-point-contact-spoc-working-party`; MSSG page `ema.europa.eu/en/about-us/who-we-are/executive-steering-group-shortages-medicinal-products`; ESMP overview `ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/medicine-shortages-availability-issues/european-shortages-monitoring-platform-esmp`; ESMP platform `esmp.ema.europa.eu`; Regulation (EU) 2022/123 on EUR-Lex, CELEX 32022R0123.
- **Why it matters for industry**: this is the compliance layer that sits underneath every shortage-related obligation an MAH has in the EU. It is also the operational precedent and evidence base the Critical Medicines Act and Critical Medicines Alliance build on: shortage data collected via SPOC/MSSG/ESMP feeds the vulnerability analyses behind the Act's Strategic Projects and "EU preference" procurement rules.

## Overview

Regulation (EU) 2022/123 was adopted in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, when the EU discovered it had no standing mechanism to monitor medicine and medical-device shortages at Union level; national systems and ad hoc Commission coordination could not keep pace with a genuinely cross-border supply shock. The regulation gave EMA a permanent, not merely crisis-triggered, mandate to prevent, monitor and mitigate shortages of medicines and medical devices "of major importance" during recognised major events, and, more broadly, to build the standing infrastructure (governance bodies and an IT platform) needed to do this on an ongoing basis.

That standing infrastructure has three parts. First, the **Medicines Shortages Steering Group (MSSG)**, an EU-level body of Member State, Commission and EMA representatives that decides when a "major event" designation applies, coordinates the EU response and issues recommendations, including establishing lists of critical medicines for the event in question. Second, the **SPOC Working Party**, a network of one designated contact point per national competent authority, which does the granular information-sharing work: exchanging early-warning signals about supply disruptions, feeding the critical-medicines-list process, and escalating issues up to MSSG. Third, the **European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP)**, the IT backbone that both bodies depend on: marketing-authorisation holders and NCAs report shortage and supply/demand data into the platform, replacing the patchwork of national reporting formats that made EU-wide situational awareness slow and incomplete before 2022/123.

The rollout of the ESMP was staged rather than immediate: routine reporting functionality went live in November 2024, and use became mandatory for marketing-authorisation holders and NCAs of centrally authorised products from 2 February 2025, with the platform reaching full operational scope (routine reporting, close monitoring at MSSG's request, and crisis monitoring) shortly after. This means the shortage-monitoring architecture created on paper in 2022 only became fully data-driven in practice from early 2025.

The Union List of Critical Medicines, first published December 2023 and updated December 2024 (276 critical active substances), is the prioritisation tool both MSSG and the SPOC Working Party use to decide which shortages merit close monitoring. That same list is also the scoping mechanism for the later, separate Critical Medicines Act proposal (COM(2025) 102), which addresses the industrial-policy side of the same problem: manufacturing investment, procurement rules and supply-chain diversification, rather than shortage detection and reporting. The Critical Medicines Alliance, the Commission's 2024 stakeholder-consultation forum, sits between the two: its February 2025 Strategic Report drew on the shortage patterns visible through SPOC/MSSG/ESMP monitoring to recommend the measures that became the Critical Medicines Act. See `critical_medicines_act.md` and `critical_medicines_alliance.md` for the full picture of how EU critical-medicines policy has evolved from monitoring (2022/123) through consultation (the Alliance, 2024) to industrial-policy legislation (the Act, 2025-2026).

## Governance in detail

**MSSG** meets regularly and, in a "major event", more frequently, to formally trigger the extended monitoring, reporting and mitigation measures Regulation 2022/123 makes available. It draws on the SPOC Working Party for member-state-level intelligence and can consult the Emergency Task Force (EMA's separate expert body for public-health-emergency scientific advice) when a shortage has a safety, efficacy or quality dimension requiring urgent scientific input.

**SPOC Working Party** meets on a routine cadence (monthly, based on published meeting summaries and agendas) even outside a formally declared major event, functioning as the standing information-exchange layer for shortages and availability issues affecting both human and veterinary medicines. A parallel Medical Device Shortages SPOC Working Party covers the medical-devices side of the same 2022/123 mandate, reflecting that the regulation's scope explicitly extends beyond medicines to medical devices.

**Marketing-authorisation holders** are the primary data source feeding this system: they are obliged to notify NCAs and, for centrally authorised products, EMA via the ESMP, of anticipated or actual shortages, and to keep supply/demand data current during close monitoring or crisis phases. Where a shortage requires a clinical response, for example switching patients to an alternative product, adjusting dosing or prioritising limited stock, Direct Healthcare Professional Communications (DHPCs) are the tool used to reach prescribers and pharmacists directly, separate from the regulatory reporting obligation itself.
