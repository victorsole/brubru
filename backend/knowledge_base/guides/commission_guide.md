# Understanding the European Commission

How the Commission works and how to engage with it.

## Structure

### College of Commissioners
- 27 Commissioners (one per member state)
- President: Ursula von der Leyen
- Meets weekly, typically Wednesdays
- Adopts proposals collectively

### Cabinets
Each Commissioner has a private office (cabinet):
- Head of Cabinet: Key power broker
- Members of Cabinet: Policy advisors
- Often first point of contact for stakeholders

## Legislative Process

### Proposal Development

1. **Political Guidelines**: Commission President's priorities
2. **Work Programme**: Annual list of planned initiatives
3. **Roadmap/Inception Impact Assessment**: Early public notice
4. **Public Consultation**: Stakeholder input (usually 12 weeks)
5. **Impact Assessment**: Economic, social, environmental analysis
6. **Inter-service Consultation**: All DGs review draft
7. **College Adoption**: Formal proposal

### Key Documents

**Liste de Point Prévu**
Provisional agenda of upcoming Commission meetings. Shows what's being discussed in coming weeks.

**Work Programme**
Annual document listing all planned legislative and non-legislative initiatives. Published each autumn.

**Have Your Say Portal**
All public consultations, feedback opportunities, and roadmaps in one place.

## Engaging with the Commission

### Entry Points

**Public Consultations**
- Open to all
- Published on Have Your Say
- Usually 12-week window
- All contributions published

**Expert Groups**
- Commission-convened advisory bodies
- Can be permanent or ad hoc
- Membership often by application
- Transparency Register lists all

**Stakeholder Events**
- DG-organised conferences and workshops
- Often sector-specific
- Check individual DG event pages

**Transparency Register Meetings**
- Registered lobbyists can request meetings
- Commissioners and Cabinet meetings published
- DG officials also accessible

### Timing

**Best Times to Engage**
- Before Work Programme finalised (summer)
- During public consultation period
- Before inter-service consultation
- After proposal but before Council/EP position

**Less Effective Times**
- After proposal adopted (focus shifts to co-legislators)
- During August break
- In final weeks before major deadlines

### What Works

**Do**:
- Provide concrete evidence and data
- Explain practical implementation issues
- Offer specific textual suggestions
- Engage early in the process
- Build relationships over time
- Coordinate with like-minded stakeholders

**Don't**:
- Send generic position papers
- Engage only when you want something
- Ignore Commission officials below Director level
- Miss consultation deadlines
- Approach only through political channels

## 2019-2024 Priorities (Von der Leyen I)

1. **European Green Deal** - Climate neutrality by 2050
2. **Europe Fit for Digital Age** - Digital services, AI, data
3. **Economy That Works for People** - Jobs, social fairness
4. **Stronger Europe in the World** - Geopolitical Commission
5. **Promoting European Way of Life** - Migration, security
6. **New Push for European Democracy** - Rule of law, participation

Plus: Recovery Plan for Europe (post-COVID)

## Commission vs Other Institutions

| Aspect | Commission | Parliament | Council |
|--------|------------|------------|---------|
| Role | Proposes | Co-legislates | Co-legislates |
| Access | Moderate | Open | Closed |
| Key phase | Pre-proposal | Amendment | Negotiation |
| Engagement | Consultations, meetings | Hearings, amendments | National positions |
| Timeline | Months before proposal | During reading | During negotiation |
