# Organising EU Policy Events in Brussels

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Practical event planning for EU policy professionals in Brussels
- Scope: Venue selection, speaker management, audience building, logistics, follow-up
- Key venue zone: Walking distance from the European Parliament (Schuman/Luxembourg area)
- Best day: Wednesday (highest MEP availability during committee weeks)
- Ideal lead time: 12+ weeks for a quality event; 6 weeks minimum realistic timeline
- Avoid: Strasbourg plenary weeks, European Council summits, August, Christmas-New Year
- Event formats: Roundtable (influence), panel discussion (awareness), reception (networking), press presentation (launch), expert workshop (technical input)
- Budget tip: EP premises are free if an MEP hosts the event
- Practical tips count: 7 common pitfalls, 5-point logistics checklist categories, 6-step promotion timeline

Practical guide for planning successful events in the EU bubble.

## Before You Start

### Ask the Right Questions

1. **Why this event?** Could a phone call to an MEP achieve the same outcome with less effort?
2. **Who is the real client?** Your boss, funder, or external partner may have different priorities
3. **What's the outcome?** Define success before planning

### Format Follows Function

| Goal | Best Format |
|------|-------------|
| Influence legislation | Small roundtable with key MEPs |
| Build awareness | Panel discussion (50-100 people) |
| Network building | Reception or dinner |
| Launch report | Press-focused presentation |
| Technical input | Expert workshop |

Don't organise a 150-person panel when a strategic roundtable would work better.

## Calendar & Timing

### The EP Calendar Rules Brussels

**DO book during:**
- Committee weeks (especially Wednesdays)
- Brussels mini-plenaries (less travel chaos)

**DON'T book during:**
- Strasbourg plenary weeks (MEPs away)
- European Council summits (everything stops)
- August (dead month)
- Between Christmas and New Year

### Wednesday is the Sweet Spot
Committee meetings happen Monday-Thursday. Wednesdays often have the best MEP availability.

## Location

### Stay in the Bubble

Keep events within walking distance of the EP. In 20 years, only exceptional reasons justify going outside this zone.

**Popular Venues:**
- EP premises (if MEP hosts)
- Residence Palace (Press Club)
- Committee of the Regions
- Representation offices (member state Perm Reps)
- Hotels near Schuman/Luxembourg

### Practical Considerations
- Easy metro/walking access
- Professional AV equipment
- Catering options
- Room flexibility
- Accessibility

## Speakers

### Securing Good Speakers

They're more likely to accept if:
- Topic is relevant to their work
- Other speakers are high-quality
- Setup looks professional
- Timing fits their schedule
- They're given clear expectations

If you're getting refusals, revisit these elements.

### Getting MEPs

- Approach through APA first
- Explain political relevance
- Offer flexibility on timing
- Provide clear brief
- Keep their commitment short (15-20 min max)

### Speaker Management

- Confirm in writing
- Send logistics pack 1 week before
- Brief them on other speakers
- Coordinate speaking points to avoid overlap
- Have backups identified

## Audience Building

### Your Mailing List is Key

Open rates determine registration numbers. Attendance will always be lower than expected.

**Build Lists Through:**
- Previous events
- Stakeholder networks
- Partner organisations
- LinkedIn connections
- Professional associations

### Promotion Strategy

| Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Save the date email | 8 weeks | Reserve calendars |
| Formal invitation | 6 weeks | Full details |
| LinkedIn post | 4 weeks | Broader reach |
| Reminder email | 3 weeks | Catch late deciders |
| Partner amplification | Ongoing | Extended reach |
| Final reminder | 1 week | Last push |

### Work Your Network

Identify "ambassadors" - people who will promote organically and bring others.

## Event Design

### Keep It Human-Centred

- Short speeches (max 10 minutes)
- Clear presentations (less text, more visually engaging)
- Time for Q&A
- Networking breaks
- Good catering
- Comfortable room temperature
- Fresh air if possible

If attendees leave drained and disengaged, the event failed regardless of content.

### Professional, Not Party

Policy events don't have to be boring, but shouldn't feel like a rave. Aim for:
- Familiar, professional structure
- One or two fresh elements to surprise
- Clear value proposition for attendees

## Common Pitfalls

- **Over-programming**: Leave time for networking
- **Too many speakers**: Quality over quantity
- **Poor moderation**: Brief moderator thoroughly
- **Technical failures**: Always test beforehand
- **Bad timing**: Check EP calendar religiously
- **Ignoring basics**: Cold rooms and bad coffee kill events
- **No follow-up**: Event is just the start

## After the Event

### Immediate (within 48 hours)
- Thank speakers personally
- Send follow-up email to attendees
- Share photos/key takeaways
- Post on social media

### Follow-Through
- Send promised materials
- Schedule follow-up meetings
- Document outcomes and lessons
- Update contact database
- Plan next engagement

## Budget Considerations

Major costs typically include:
- Venue hire
- Catering (often biggest expense)
- AV/technical
- Photography
- Materials/printing
- Staff time

Ways to reduce costs:
- EP premises (free if MEP hosts)
- Partner co-sponsorship
- Representation office venues
- In-house expertise instead of external
