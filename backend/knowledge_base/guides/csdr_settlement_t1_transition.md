# CSDR and the T+1 Settlement Transition (October 2027)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Instrument:** Central Securities Depositories Regulation (CSDR), Regulation (EU) No 909/2014 of 23 July 2014, OJ L 257, 28.8.2014, p. 1 (CELEX 32014R0909); improves securities settlement in the EU and regulates central securities depositories (CSDs)
- **Legal base:** Article 114 TFEU (internal market)
- **Supervisor:** ESMA (European Securities and Markets Authority), working with national competent authorities and the European System of Central Banks (ECB / Eurosystem via TARGET2-Securities)
- **CSDR Refit:** Regulation (EU) 2023/2845, published in the OJ 27 December 2023, entered into force 17 January 2024; amended five areas of CSDR: settlement discipline (pre-conditions for mandatory buy-ins), the passporting regime, banking-type ancillary services, oversight of third-country CSDs, and supervisory cooperation. It also mandated ESMA to report on the appropriateness of shortening the EU settlement cycle.
- **Settlement discipline -- cash penalties:** in force since **1 February 2022** under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1229 (the regulatory technical standards on settlement discipline), after two postponements (originally due 2020, deferred to 2021, then to 2022 because of COVID-19 implementation burden). CSDs impose daily cash penalties (0.5 to 1 basis point depending on instrument type) on participants that cause settlement fails.
- **Mandatory buy-ins:** downgraded by the CSDR Refit to a **last-resort measure**. The Commission may only activate mandatory buy-ins for specific instruments/transaction types if the cash-penalty regime proves insufficiently effective and settlement-fail rates threaten financial stability. Buy-ins are not automatically live.
- **T+1 transition -- the headline change:** the EU is shortening its standard securities settlement cycle from **T+2 to T+1** (settlement one business day after trade) for transferable securities (shares, bonds) traded on EU trading venues. Securities financing transactions (SFTs) are excluded.
- **Application date: 11 October 2027**, coordinated with the United Kingdom and Switzerland for a joint move (following the US, Canada and Mexico switch to T+1 in May 2024).
- **Legislative chain:** ESMA report (18 November 2024) recommended T+1 no later than 11 October 2027 -> Commission legislative proposal (12 February 2025) to amend CSDR -> political agreement between the European Parliament and the Council (18-19 June 2025) -> final text adopted and published as **Regulation (EU) 2025/2650** in the OJ on 14 October 2025 (CELEX 32025R2650) -> applies from 11 October 2027.
- **Governance structure for the migration:** a two-layer model. The **T+1 Coordination Committee**, chaired by ESMA Chair Verena Ross, brings together ESMA, the European Commission and the ECB with industry leadership at the top level. The **EU T+1 Industry Committee**, chaired by Giovanni Sabatini, coordinates operational workstreams (post-trade automation, allocations and confirmations, FX, corporate actions, securities lending recalls) and published a High-Level Roadmap on 30 June 2025.
- **Indicative timeline:** roughly one year to agree standards and processes, one year for implementation, one year for testing, to land an orderly T+1 go-live on 11 October 2027.
- **Wider context:** the transition sits inside the Commission's Savings and Investments Union agenda (deeper, more integrated EU capital markets) and interacts with the EMIR 3.0 clearing reforms and MiFID II/MiFIR post-trade transparency rules.

---

## Why T+1 Matters

Settlement is the "plumbing" of capital markets: the process by which a security and the corresponding cash actually change hands after a trade is agreed. A shorter settlement cycle reduces counterparty and systemic risk (less time between trade and settlement means less exposure to a counterparty defaulting before the transaction completes), lowers the collateral and margin CCPs need to hold against unsettled trades, and frees up capital that would otherwise sit as a buffer against settlement risk.

The catalyst was the US, Canadian and Mexican move to T+1 in May 2024. Once the EU's biggest trading partners settled in one day while the EU stayed on two, cross-border and cross-currency trades became structurally harder: an EU investor buying US shares would need to fund and settle a day earlier than their EU-side leg allowed, creating friction, FX timing mismatches and higher failure rates on cross-border transactions. The CSDR Refit (Regulation (EU) 2023/2845) responded by instructing ESMA to formally assess whether and how the EU should follow.

## The Legislative Chain

1. **CSDR Refit (Regulation (EU) 2023/2845)**, in force 17 January 2024, tasked ESMA with reporting on the costs, benefits and appropriate timeline for shortening the EU settlement cycle, alongside its other reforms (softening mandatory buy-ins to a last-resort tool, easing the CSD passporting regime, and tightening third-country CSD oversight).
2. **ESMA report (18 November 2024)** recommended the EU move to T+1 no later than 11 October 2027, based on industry consultation and a cost-benefit assessment.
3. **Commission legislative proposal (12 February 2025)** introduced a targeted amendment to CSDR mandating the shortened cycle and fixing 11 October 2027 as the application date.
4. **Political agreement (18-19 June 2025)** between the European Parliament and the Council on the text.
5. **Regulation (EU) 2025/2650**, the final amending act, was adopted by the co-legislators and published in the Official Journal on 14 October 2025 (CELEX 32025R2650). It enters into force twenty days after publication and **applies from 11 October 2027**.

## What Changes for Market Participants

- **Settlement window compresses by half:** an EU share or bond bought on a Tuesday must settle by close of business Wednesday, rather than Thursday under the current T+2 regime.
- **Trade affirmation and allocation windows shrink.** Asset managers, custodians and brokers must confirm and allocate trades same-day, which is the single biggest operational challenge for buy-side firms still relying on manual or next-day matching processes.
- **FX funding timing tightens**, particularly for non-EU investors who must now source EUR (or other settlement currency) a day earlier.
- **Securities lending recalls** need to be issued and returned faster, since the market for borrowing securities to cover short positions or lending programmes has one less day to operate.
- **Corporate actions processing** (dividends, rights issues, redemptions) must be re-timed against the shorter cycle.
- **CSDs, CCPs and trading venues** must upgrade matching, netting and settlement infrastructure to operate reliably within the compressed window; TARGET2-Securities (T2S) processes are a key dependency.

## Interaction with the Wider Reform Agenda

The T+1 transition is one strand of the Commission's post-trade modernisation push, alongside the CSDR Refit's mandatory-buy-in downgrade and third-country CSD oversight changes, the EMIR 3.0 active-account and clearing reforms, and the broader Savings and Investments Union objective of deepening and integrating EU capital markets. MiFID II/MiFIR transparency and reporting obligations continue to apply in parallel and are not suspended or altered by the T+1 switch itself.

## Who to Watch

- **ESMA** -- technical standards, coordination of the T+1 Coordination Committee, public information hub for Industry Committee outputs (ESMA formally disclaims responsibility for verifying the Industry Committee's own materials).
- **European Commission (DG FISMA)** -- co-legislator sponsor of Regulation (EU) 2025/2650, member of the T+1 Coordination Committee.
- **ECB / Eurosystem** -- T2S settlement infrastructure, member of the T+1 Coordination Committee.
- **EU T+1 Industry Committee** (chaired by Giovanni Sabatini) -- operational workstreams: post-trade automation, allocations and confirmations, FX, corporate actions, securities lending.
