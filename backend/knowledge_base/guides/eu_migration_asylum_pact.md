## Overview

The EU Pact on Migration and Asylum is the most significant overhaul of EU migration and asylum law since the Common European Asylum System (CEAS) reform attempts of 2016, which largely failed due to Council deadlock. After four years of negotiations following the September 2020 proposal, all 10 legislative acts were formally adopted on 14 May 2024 and entered into force progressively. Member States have until 12 June 2026 to implement the framework.

The Pact replaces the Dublin III Regulation (Regulation (EU) No 604/2013) with the AMMR as the core instrument for determining which Member State is responsible for an asylum application. It introduces mandatory border screening, a reformed solidarity mechanism, accelerated border procedures, and a new crisis instrument.

Political opposition from Hungary and Poland continued throughout implementation: both countries refused to apply the Pact and launched legal challenges before the CJEU. Civil society organisations, including ECRE, Amnesty International, and Human Rights Watch, have raised concerns about the compatibility of border procedures and expanded safe third country rules with the 1951 Refugee Convention and fundamental rights standards.

## Solidarity Mechanism

The AMMR introduces a mandatory solidarity mechanism to address situations where Member States face disproportionate migratory pressure. It is mandatory but flexible in form:

- **Relocation:** transfer of applicants or beneficiaries of international protection to the contributing Member State
- **Financial contributions:** payment to a solidarity pool managed by the Commission
- **Operational support:** deployment of personnel, equipment, capacity-building assistance

Each year, the Commission assesses pressure levels and triggers a solidarity cycle. A minimum annual solidarity "pool" of 30,000 relocations or EUR 600 million in financial contributions is set as a baseline. Member States can choose their contribution form. Countries under pressure (typically at the external border) can request solidarity.

The mechanism replaces the wholly voluntary ad hoc solidarity arrangements of the 2015-2019 period, which were largely ineffective. However, critics note that Member States can still fulfil solidarity obligations through financial contributions rather than relocation, limiting the practical impact on pressure countries.

## Border Procedures

The Screening Regulation introduces a mandatory pre-entry screening of up to 7 days for all third-country nationals apprehended at the border or rescued at sea who have not yet been authorised to enter. Screening includes: identity verification, security database checks, health checks, vulnerability assessment.

The Asylum Procedures Regulation introduces a mandatory border procedure for asylum applicants from countries with an EU-wide recognition rate below 20%. Under the border procedure:

- Applicants are deemed not to have entered EU territory during processing
- Decision must be taken within 12 weeks (extendable to 16 weeks in pressure situations)
- Return procedure follows immediately if application rejected
- Families with children under 12 may be excluded from border procedure (derogation for Member States)

Civil society concerns focus on: detention-like conditions during screening and border procedures, lack of effective legal assistance, and rapid return decisions with insufficient examination of individual circumstances.

## Safe Third Country

The AMMR and APR expand the application of the safe third country concept. An application can be declared inadmissible if the applicant has a "connection" to a safe third country (previously: "first country of asylum" required prior presence). Criteria for a country to be considered safe: no persecution, no serious harm, principle of non-refoulement respected, readmission agreement or arrangement with the Member State.

This provision is particularly relevant for cooperation with North African and Western Balkan countries. Implementation depends on Member State bilateral agreements.
