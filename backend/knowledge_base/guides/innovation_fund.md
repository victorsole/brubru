# EU Innovation Fund

QUICK FACTS:
- What: one of the world's largest funding programmes for deploying net-zero and innovative technologies
- Funding source: EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) allowance auction revenues
- Total budget: approximately EUR 40 billion (2020-2030), based on sale of 530 million ETS allowances at EUR 75/tonne
- Budget available (30 June 2025): EUR 12.3 billion for projects (EUR 11.6 billion through grant agreements, EUR 0.7 billion not yet allocated)
- Actual payments to projects (30 June 2025): EUR 331.8 million (2.7% of total, less than 1% of expected budget)
- Monetised ETS allowances (30 June 2025): EUR 18.7 billion (EUR 6.7 billion transferred to RRF/REPowerEU, EUR 12.0 billion available)
- Projects selected: 228 (by 30 June 2025). Grant agreements signed: 208. Cancelled: 40 (1 in 5).
- Projects in operation: only 16 (by 30 June 2025)
- Managed by: CINEA (grant management), DG CLIMA (policy oversight), EIB (ETS allowance monetisation)
- Legal basis: EU ETS Directive Article 10a(8), Innovation Fund Regulation
- Predecessor: New Entrants' Reserve 300 (NER 300), 2012-2020
- Sectors: energy-intensive industries, renewable energy, energy storage, CCUS, net-zero mobility and buildings, clean hydrogen
- Geographic scope: all EU Member States + Norway, Liechtenstein, Iceland
- Call types: large-scale (>EUR 7.5M capex), small-scale (<EUR 7.5M), competitive bidding (auctions), net-zero pilot
- Grant rate: up to 60% of relevant costs (up to 100% for competitive bidding)
- Disbursement: milestone-based (40% at financial close, 60% conditional on verified emissions reductions)
- Current open call: 2025 Net-Zero Technologies Call, budget EUR 2.9 billion, deadline 23 April 2026
- ECA Special Report 11/2026: "high potential, but slow progress and little impact on emissions reduction"
- 5 award criteria: GHG emissions avoidance, degree of innovation, project maturity, replicability, cost efficiency
- ECF complement: European Competitiveness Fund (EUR 451 billion MFF 2028-2034) will complement Innovation Fund
- Related: EU Catalyst Partnership (InvestEU blending, EUR 220M from Innovation Fund + EUR 200M from Horizon Europe)

## ECA Special Report 11/2026: Key Findings

Published 20 March 2026. Adopted by Chamber 1 (headed by Joao Leao) on 25 February 2026. Audit covered 2020 to June 2025. Signed by ECA President Tony Murphy.

### Finding 1: resource allocation without structured analysis
The Commission steered Innovation Fund resources to accommodate emerging policy priorities (REPowerEU, European Hydrogen Bank, Net Zero Industry Act, batteries) without a structured analysis of the technological landscape or GHG reduction potential. Divergence between priority-setting and industry response was found: hydrogen competitive bidding proposals fell from 132 (2023) to 61 (2024); battery call received only 14 proposals, 15% of budget remained unused.

### Finding 2: significant asset accumulation
By June 2025, EUR 12.3 billion had accumulated. Actual payments were only EUR 331.8 million (2.7% of total). No mechanism ensures a minimum level of financial resources. ETS auction price volatility (EUR 23-98/tonne, average EUR 66.57) adds uncertainty. The Innovation Fund initially expected EUR 10 billion total; the revised estimate of EUR 40 billion already exceeds this.

### Finding 3: 1 in 5 projects cancelled
Of 228 projects selected, 40 cancelled (20 withdrew before grant agreement, 20 terminated after). Common reasons: EU sanctions, delays from site changes or permitting, technical issues, missing infrastructure, regulatory non-compliance, bankruptcy, partnership changes, supply-chain delays, costs higher than expected. Large-scale projects from the 2021 call were particularly affected.

### Finding 4: delays are common
63 of 208 grant agreements amended to postpone financial close deadlines. Modified projects will enter operation on average 14.8 months later than planned. 56 projects already exceeded agreed deadlines. Average delay for those heading to financial close: 14.5 months. More delays and terminations expected.

### Finding 5: poor emissions reduction results
By December 2024, projects were expected to reduce 0.59 million tonnes CO2eq. Actual achievement: only 5% of expected reductions (25,952 tonnes CO2eq total by June 2025). Only 16 projects in operation. Visited projects delivered technologies but all but one had smaller-than-expected emissions reductions (average 57% of target for the 5 operational projects visited). GHG avoidance methodology may overstate potential (especially for energy storage and solar).

### 3 ECA Recommendations
1. Establish a structured analysis to guide allocation of Innovation Fund resources (target: 2027)
2. Examine additional measures for faster deployment of funds (target: 2030)
3. Improve project assessment, address delays and cancellations: (a) more accurate GHG/maturity assessment, (b) assess need for flexibility on financial close and monitoring period (target: 2028)

## How the Innovation Fund Works

### Funding model
- Grants of up to 60% of relevant project costs (capital + operational costs minus revenues over 10 years)
- Competitive bidding: up to 100% of eligible costs (used for hydrogen auctions and industrial heat decarbonisation)
- Up to 40% disbursed at financial close (not dependent on verified emissions)
- Remaining 60% conditional on verified emissions reductions during monitoring period (3-5 years)
- Projects failing to deliver 75% of stated GHG reductions see funding reduced proportionally

### Project development cycle
1. Feasibility study
2. Project preparation (grant award)
3. Construction (financial close milestone)
4. Monitoring period (entry into operation, 3-10 years)
5. Annual instalments based on verified GHG avoidance

### Selection criteria (5 award criteria, weighting varies by call)
1. Greenhouse gas emissions avoidance (absolute, relative, credibility)
2. Degree of innovation
3. Project maturity (prospect of financial close within 4 years)
4. Replicability
5. Cost efficiency

### Call history
- 2020: large-scale (EUR 1.0B) + small-scale (EUR 0.1B)
- 2021: large-scale (EUR 1.5B) + small-scale (EUR 0.1B)
- 2022: large-scale (EUR 3.0B) + small-scale (EUR 0.1B)
- 2023: large-scale (EUR 1.7B) + small-scale (EUR 0.2B) + RFNBO hydrogen auction + clean-tech manufacturing (EUR 2.1B)
- 2024: large-scale (EUR 1.2B) + small-scale (EUR 0.1B) + deep decarbonisation pilot (EUR 0.2B) + renewable hydrogen (EUR 1.2B) + batteries (EUR 1.0B)
- 2025: Net-Zero Technologies call (EUR 2.9B, deadline 23 April 2026)

## Relationship to Other EU Funding

- **European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)**: proposed under MFF 2028-2034 (EUR 451 billion), will complement Innovation Fund. Both anchored in EU industrial policy framework.
- **REPowerEU**: objectives partially overlap. One beneficiary replaced EUR 230M Innovation Fund grant with EUR 245M from national Recovery and Resilience Plan (more flexible terms, earlier disbursement).
- **Horizon Europe**: complements at earlier TRL. EU Catalyst Partnership blends EUR 220M Innovation Fund + EUR 200M Horizon Europe (clean H2, SAF, DAC, LDES, industrial decarb).
- **LIFE Programme**: complements for industrial decarbonisation at smaller scale.
- **InvestEU**: blending for risk-sharing instruments.
- **Modernisation Fund**: separate ETS-funded instrument for lower-income Member States.
- **Just Transition Fund**: territorial transition support.
