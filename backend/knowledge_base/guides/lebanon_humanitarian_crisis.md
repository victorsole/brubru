## Overview

Lebanon faces a compound humanitarian crisis driven by Israel's military operations, economic collapse, political instability, and the legacy of the Beirut port explosion (2020). The country's GDP has contracted by over 60% since 2019, and basic services (electricity, healthcare, water) remain severely degraded. Israel's ongoing assault has displaced hundreds of thousands, destroyed infrastructure, and complicated humanitarian access.

The EU is one of Lebanon's largest donors, providing humanitarian, development, and stabilisation assistance. The European Parliament's Development Committee (DEVE) regularly monitors the situation and has called for increased aid, accountability in reconstruction, and protection of civilians.

## EU Response Instruments

### Humanitarian Aid (DG ECHO)
- Emergency funding for food, shelter, health, protection
- EU Civil Protection Mechanism activations
- Coordination with OCHA, WFP, UNHCR, UNRWA, ICRC

### Neighbourhood Policy (DG NEAR)
- Association Agreement (2006, not fully implemented)
- EU-Lebanon Partnership Priorities
- Reform conditionality: governance, anti-corruption, rule of law
- Migration cooperation: support for Syrian and Palestinian refugees

### Political Track
- FAC discussions on Lebanon situation
- EU Special Representative for the Middle East Peace Process
- Support for Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF)
- Calls for political reform, new electoral law, anti-corruption measures

## Key Challenges
- Israeli military operations: civilian casualties, displacement, infrastructure destruction
- Economic collapse: hyperinflation, banking sector failure, currency devaluation
- Political deadlock: sectarian power-sharing system, delayed reforms
- Refugee burden: one of the highest per-capita refugee populations globally
- Hezbollah transition: post-ceasefire political landscape
- Reconstruction: scale of destruction, governance gaps, donor fatigue
