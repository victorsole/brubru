# Reform and Growth Facility: Western Balkans and Moldova

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is:** Two new, RRF-style financial instruments that pay out EU money against verified reform milestones rather than against project invoices -- the **Reform and Growth Facility for the Western Balkans** and the separate **Reform and Growth Facility for Moldova**. Both entered into force in 2024-2025 and both sit alongside (not instead of) existing pre-accession funding.
- **Western Balkans Reform and Growth Facility:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1449 of the European Parliament and of the Council, in force 25 May 2024. **EUR 6 billion total (2024-2027): EUR 2 billion in grants + EUR 4 billion in concessional loans.** At least EUR 3 billion of the envelope is channelled through the Western Balkans Investment Framework (WBIF) as grants and loans for infrastructure investment; up to EUR 2.61 billion is disbursed as loans directly to beneficiaries' national budgets to support socioeconomic reforms.
- **Beneficiaries (6):** Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia. Bosnia and Herzegovina's Reform Agenda was approved later than the other five (November 2025); Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia had Reform Agendas endorsed by the Commission in October 2024.
- **Moldova Reform and Growth Facility:** Regulation (EU) 2025/535, entered into force 22 May 2025 (political agreement between Parliament and Council reached March 2025). **EUR 1.9 billion for 2025-2027**, structured as roughly EUR 385 million in grants and EUR 1.5 billion in highly concessional loans (40-year repayment, including a 10-year grace period). This is Moldova's first dedicated EU financial instrument outside NDICI-Global Europe, reflecting its accelerated candidate track.
- **Shared logic, modelled on the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF):** disbursement is NOT tied to spending receipts. Each beneficiary agrees a **Reform Agenda** with the Commission; money is released in **tranches (broadly twice-yearly)** only after the Commission verifies that agreed **reform milestones and targets** have actually been met.
- **Part of the Growth Plan for the Western Balkans:** adopted by the Commission 8 November 2023 (COM(2023) 691), built on four pillars -- (1) economic integration with the EU single market, (2) Western Balkans regional integration via the Common Regional Market, (3) fundamental reforms (rule of law, public administration, economic governance), (4) increased financial assistance, which is exactly the Reform and Growth Facility. Aim: accelerate socioeconomic convergence and give partners a taste of single-market benefits years ahead of formal accession.
- **Moldova equivalent:** the Growth Plan for Moldova, proposed by the Commission 10 October 2024, mirrors the Western Balkans architecture (Reform Agenda, milestone tranches) but does not carry the same four-pillar single-market integration framing since Moldova's accession track runs through the standard enlargement chapters.
- **Sits ALONGSIDE, not instead of, IPA III:** the Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance (Regulation (EU) 2021/1529, EUR 14.162 billion, 2021-2027) remains the main pre-accession financing vehicle for the Western Balkans. The Reform and Growth Facility is an additional, reform-conditioned layer on top of IPA III programming. See `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement` for the full IPA III breakdown.
- **Western Balkans Investment Framework (WBIF) allocation requirement:** the Western Balkans regulation requires that a defined share of the facility (at least EUR 3 billion) flows through WBIF, the existing blending platform that combines IPA III grants, bilateral donor contributions and international financial institution loans to leverage large infrastructure investment.
- **Managed by:** DG ENEST (Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood), Commissioner Marta Kos. Both facilities are run by the same DG since the 2024-2025 split of the former DG NEAR into DG ENEST (east) and DG MENA (south). See `dg_enest_overview`.
- **Conditionality (three tiers, Western Balkans regulation, mirrored for Moldova):**
  1. **Pre-conditions:** functioning democratic institutions and rule of law; for Serbia and Kosovo specifically, constructive engagement in the EU-facilitated normalisation dialogue is an explicit pre-condition for disbursement.
  2. **General conditions:** macro-fiscal stability, sound public finance management, budget transparency.
  3. **Payment conditions:** country-specific reform milestones set out in each beneficiary's Reform Agenda (rule of law, anti-corruption, single-market alignment, green transition, digital transformation, and, for Moldova, seven reform clusters: economic competitiveness, connectivity, green transition, rule of law, digital, human capital, social protection).
- **Georgia note:** a parallel Reform and Growth Facility track for Georgia was under discussion but was shelved after the Commission froze Georgia's accession process in March 2025 over the "foreign agents" law and democratic backsliding.
- **Ukraine is different:** Ukraine's reform-linked, milestone-based EU support runs through the separate **Ukraine Facility** (Regulation (EU) 2024/792, EUR 50 billion, 2024-2027), a sui generis instrument, not a Reform and Growth Facility. See `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026`.
- **Cross-links:** `dg_enest_overview`, `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement`, `eu_neighbourhood_policy_east_south`, `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026`, `mff_2028_2034`, `ipa_iii_pre_accession_assistance` (planned), `taiex_and_twinning_pre_accession_technical_assistance` (planned).

## 1. What the Reform and Growth Facility Is: The RRF-Style Logic

The EU's traditional pre-accession funding model (IPA and its predecessors) reimburses or grants money against projects: infrastructure builds, twinning programmes, technical assistance contracts, calls for proposals. It is effective at building capacity but slow to reward political reform, because disbursement is decoupled from whether a government is actually delivering on rule-of-law or economic-governance commitments.

The Reform and Growth Facility imports the logic the EU built for its own Member States during the COVID-19 recovery: the **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)**. Under the RRF, Member States drew down EUR 723.8 billion against a National Recovery and Resilience Plan, with the Commission releasing tranches only after independently verifying that agreed milestones and targets had been met. The Reform and Growth Facility applies the same mechanics to enlargement partners:

- A single **Reform Agenda** replaces the scattered project pipeline as the reference document.
- Disbursement happens in **tranches**, assessed roughly **twice a year**, against a fixed list of milestones (often qualitative: adoption of a law, publication of an audit, appointment to an independent body) and targets (often quantitative: number of businesses supported, kilometres of interconnection built).
- Both grants and loans are subject to the same conditionality: a government does not get paid simply for spending money, it gets paid for **proving reform delivery**.
- The Commission's assessment process, disbursement decisions (Commission Implementing Decisions) and the underlying Reform Agenda are all published, giving outside trackers (Brubru included) a documented cadence to monitor rather than a black box.

This is a meaningful governance upgrade over legacy IPA-style grant programming, but it is also politically higher-stakes: a government that stalls reform can see a tranche withheld or reduced, which is precisely what happened with Serbia's Reform and Growth Facility disbursement, put under review in 2026 over democratic-governance and rule-of-law concerns.

## 2. The Western Balkans Facility (Regulation (EU) 2024/1449)

Regulation (EU) 2024/1449 of the European Parliament and of the Council, establishing the Reform and Growth Facility for the Western Balkans, entered into force on 25 May 2024. It is the financial-support pillar of the wider **Growth Plan for the Western Balkans**, adopted by the Commission on 8 November 2023 (COM(2023) 691).

**Financial envelope:** EUR 6 billion for 2024-2027, split as:
- **EUR 2 billion in grants**
- **EUR 4 billion in concessional loans**

**Delivery channels:**
- At least **EUR 3 billion** through the **Western Balkans Investment Framework (WBIF)** as grants and loans underpinning infrastructure investments tied to the Reform Agendas (transport corridors, energy interconnection, digital infrastructure).
- Up to **EUR 2.61 billion** disbursed as loans paid directly into beneficiaries' national budgets, to accelerate growth-supporting socioeconomic reforms.

**Beneficiaries (6):** Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia. The Commission endorsed Reform Agendas for Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia in October 2024; Bosnia and Herzegovina's Reform Agenda followed later, approved in November 2025.

**Legal instruments:** disbursement runs through Facility Agreements and Loan Agreements concluded bilaterally between the Commission and each beneficiary, with individual tranches authorised by Commission Implementing Decisions once milestone verification is complete (for example, the Commission's implementing decision of 30 July 2025 approving the first release of funds to Montenegro).

## 3. The Moldova Growth Plan

The Commission proposed a **Growth Plan for the Republic of Moldova** worth up to EUR 1.9 billion for 2025-2027 on 10 October 2024. The underlying Reform and Growth Facility Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2025/535, reached political agreement between Parliament and Council in March 2025 and entered into force on 22 May 2025.

**Financial envelope:** EUR 1.9 billion for 2025-2027, structured as approximately:
- **EUR 385 million in grants**
- **EUR 1.5 billion in highly concessional loans** (40-year repayment period, including a 10-year grace period before repayment begins)

This makes Moldova's facility **Moldova's first dedicated EU financial instrument outside NDICI-Global Europe**, reflecting how far and how fast Moldova's EU accession track has moved since it became a candidate country in June 2022 and opened accession negotiations in June 2024.

**Reform Agenda for Moldova** covers seven clusters: economic competitiveness, connectivity, green transition, rule of law, digital transformation, human capital, and social protection. Disbursement follows the same twice-yearly, milestone-verified tranche logic as the Western Balkans facility. The Commission has already released tranches against verified progress: a EUR 270 million tranche and a further EUR 189 million payment in March 2026 after the Commission assessed that Moldova had met 24 additional reform indicators.

A parallel Reform and Growth Facility track was under discussion for **Georgia**, but was placed on hold after the Commission froze Georgia's EU accession process in March 2025 in response to the "Georgian Dream" government's foreign agents law and post-election democratic backsliding.

Ukraine does not have a Reform and Growth Facility. Its equivalent, milestone-based support runs through the separate **Ukraine Facility** (Regulation (EU) 2024/792, EUR 50 billion, 2024-2027), which combines macro-financial assistance, budget support and reconstruction financing in a sui generis instrument outside both NDICI and the Reform and Growth Facility model. See `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026`.

## 4. Reform Agendas and Milestone-Based Disbursement

Each beneficiary (Western Balkans six, plus Moldova) negotiates a **Reform Agenda** with the Commission: a single document listing specific, time-bound reforms across governance, economic convergence and single-market-alignment themes, each attached to a milestone or target and a disbursement instalment.

**Assessment cadence:** broadly twice a year. The Commission reviews evidence submitted by the beneficiary, consults relevant EU bodies and, where applicable, independent monitoring mechanisms, then issues a Commission Implementing Decision authorising (in full, partially, or not at all) the release of the next tranche.

**What gets assessed:**
- **Milestones** -- typically qualitative steps: a law adopted, an independent oversight body appointed, an audit published, a strategy approved.
- **Targets** -- typically quantitative outcomes: a specified volume of investment mobilised, a number of businesses or households reached, kilometres of energy or transport interconnection completed.

**Consequence of non-delivery:** tranches can be withheld, reduced or delayed. This is the mechanism through which the EU has, in practice, exercised leverage over Serbia's Reform and Growth Facility disbursement amid concerns about democratic governance and rule-of-law backsliding in 2026.

For Brubru tracking purposes, the useful primary-source cadence is: **DG ENEST progress reports** (published alongside the annual EU Enlargement Package each autumn) plus individual **Commission Implementing Decisions** authorising each tranche release, both published on the DG ENEST site.

## 5. Relationship to IPA III and the WBIF

The Reform and Growth Facility does **not** replace the Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance (IPA III). IPA III, established by Regulation (EU) 2021/1529, remains the EU's principal pre-accession financing instrument for the 2021-2027 period, with a EUR 14.162 billion envelope covering rule of law, public administration reform, socioeconomic development, agriculture and rural development (via IPARD), and cross-border cooperation across all IPA III beneficiaries (the Western Balkans six plus Turkiye, whose access is currently limited).

The Reform and Growth Facility is an **additional, reform-conditioned layer stacked on top of IPA III programming**, specific to the Western Balkans and (in a separate, smaller instrument) Moldova. The two instruments interact directly through the **Western Balkans Investment Framework (WBIF)**: WBIF is a blending platform that pools IPA III grants, bilateral donor contributions, international financial institution loans and now Reform and Growth Facility resources to co-finance large-scale infrastructure investment. The Western Balkans regulation's requirement that at least EUR 3 billion of the facility flow through WBIF is what ties the new instrument structurally back into the existing pre-accession financing architecture rather than creating a wholly parallel system.

Full IPA III detail (envelope, thematic clusters, IPARD, beneficiary breakdown) is covered in `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement`.

## 6. Conditionality

Both facilities apply a three-tier conditionality structure, modelled on the Western Balkans regulation and mirrored (with country-specific detail) for Moldova:

1. **Pre-conditions** (must be satisfied before any disbursement can begin): functioning democratic institutions and respect for the rule of law. For **Serbia and Kosovo specifically**, constructive engagement in the EU-facilitated dialogue on the normalisation of their relations is an explicit pre-condition.
2. **General conditions** (assessed on an ongoing basis): macro-fiscal stability, sound public finance management, budget transparency.
3. **Payment conditions** (assessed tranche by tranche): the specific milestones and targets set out in each beneficiary's Reform Agenda, spanning rule of law, anti-corruption, single-market alignment, green transition and digital transformation (plus, for Moldova, connectivity, human capital and social protection).

This layered structure means a beneficiary can, in principle, satisfy general macro-fiscal conditions while still losing a tranche over failure to meet a specific rule-of-law or anti-corruption milestone, or, in Serbia and Kosovo's case, over stalled normalisation-dialogue engagement.

## 7. Single-Market Integration Ahead of Accession

The Growth Plan for the Western Balkans frames the Reform and Growth Facility as the financing arm of a broader strategic bet: that partial, staged integration into the EU single market can begin **years before formal accession**, rather than waiting for it. The Growth Plan's four pillars work together toward this:

1. **Economic integration with the EU single market** -- priority actions on free movement of goods and services, access to the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA), road transport facilitation, energy market integration, digital market development and industrial supply-chain integration.
2. **Western Balkans regional integration** -- the Common Regional Market, using EU-aligned rules to overcome fragmented national markets as a stepping stone toward full single-market participation.
3. **Fundamental reforms** -- each beneficiary's Commission-approved Reform Agenda, covering rule of law, public administration and economic governance.
4. **Financial support** -- the Reform and Growth Facility itself, providing the money to make the first three pillars achievable on an accelerated timetable.

The explicit political ambition (stated in the Growth Plan) is to **double the size of the Western Balkan economies within a decade**. Moldova's Growth Plan mirrors the reform and financing logic without the same formal four-pillar single-market integration framing, since Moldova's path runs through the standard enlargement negotiating cluster structure rather than a bespoke regional-market mechanism.

## 8. Brubru Tracking Angles

- **Tranche-release calendar:** each Commission Implementing Decision authorising a tranche release is a discrete, datable, primary-source event. Track via DG ENEST's funding-and-technical-assistance pages for both facilities; these are the equivalent of RRF "payment request approved" milestones and make good calendar and alert content.
- **Reform Agenda milestone slippage:** when a beneficiary's tranche is delayed, reduced or withheld (as with Serbia's 2026 review), that is a leading indicator of both political and market risk for anyone tracking the country. Cross-reference against `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement` for the IPA III-level context and against DG ENEST's annual Enlargement Package country reports.
- **Serbia-Kosovo normalisation dialogue linkage:** because engagement in the dialogue is an explicit pre-condition, any deterioration or breakthrough in that dialogue is directly financially material to both countries' Reform and Growth Facility access. Worth tracking alongside EEAS-published dialogue updates.
- **WBIF co-financing pipeline:** the WBIF project pipeline (infrastructure calls, blending operations) is the operational face of the EUR 3 billion+ WBIF-channelled share of the Western Balkans facility. Useful for procurement-focused and infrastructure-investment users.
- **Moldova tranche cadence as an accession-speed indicator:** Moldova's facility is smaller but faster-moving than the Western Balkans one; each successfully verified tranche (such as the March 2026 EUR 189 million payment against 24 reform indicators) is a concrete proof point for the pace of Moldova's accession-adjacent reform delivery, useful context for `eu_neighbourhood_policy_east_south` users tracking the Eastern Partnership candidate track.
- **Georgia's frozen track as a comparator:** the shelved Georgia Reform and Growth Facility discussion is a useful contrast case illustrating what happens when the pre-condition of democratic institutions and rule of law is not met before a facility can even be designed, let alone disbursed.
- **Bosnia and Herzegovina's later Reform Agenda approval (November 2025):** worth flagging as the most recent addition to the Western Balkans beneficiary set, useful for users tracking Bosnia-specific accession momentum.
