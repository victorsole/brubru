# ECDC: European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the EU public health agency that identifies, assesses and communicates threats to human health from communicable diseases. Headquartered in **Solna/Stockholm, Sweden**, operational since **May 2005**, roughly 300 staff, monitors over 50 infectious disease topics for around 500 million Europeans.
- **Founding law**: **Regulation (EC) No 851/2004** of the European Parliament and of the Council of **21 April 2004** establishing a European centre for disease prevention and control (CELEX **32004R0851**), published 30 April 2004, in force 20 May 2004.
- **Mandate strengthened**: **Regulation (EU) 2022/2370** of 23 November 2022 amending Regulation (EC) No 851/2004 (CELEX **32022R2370**), part of the post-COVID **European Health Union** package. It expands ECDC surveillance and risk-assessment powers to all serious cross-border health threats (not only communicable disease), creates **EU reference laboratories** and an **EU Health Task Force** for field outbreak response.
- **Operating framework**: ECDC operates inside the wider EU crisis-preparedness architecture set by **Regulation (EU) 2022/2371** on serious cross-border threats to health (CELEX **32022R2371**); see `eu_health_security_framework` for the full framework, including HERA and the reinforced EMA.
- **Director**: **Dr Pamela Rendi-Wagner**, took up post 17 June 2024 for a five-year mandate (2024-2029), previously Austria's Director General for Public Health and Minister of Health.
- **Governance**: a **Management Board** (members nominated by Member States, the European Parliament and the European Commission) sets strategy and budget; an **Advisory Forum** (senior representatives of national public health institutes, plus a non-voting Commission official and civil-society/academic observers) advises the Director on scientific quality and meets at least four times a year.
- **Key surveillance platforms**: **EpiPulse** (the European surveillance portal for infectious diseases, launched 22 June 2021, replacing the older TESSy/EPIS systems); the weekly **Communicable Disease Threats Report (CDTR)**; **RespiCast** (the European Respiratory Diseases Forecasting Hub, launched late 2023 with ISI Foundation and the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, producing ensemble forecasts for influenza-like-illness, acute respiratory infection and COVID-19 indicators); annual Epidemiological Reports.
- **Scientific outputs**: **Eurosurveillance**, ECDC's open-access peer-reviewed journal on epidemiology, surveillance, prevention and control of communicable diseases.
- **Workforce training**: the **EPIET/EUPHEM Fellowship Programme**, a two-year scheme with two paths, field epidemiology (EPIET) and public health microbiology (EUPHEM), for EU/EEA nationals.
- **Flagship conference**: **ESCAIDE** (European Scientific Conference on Applied Infectious Disease Epidemiology), ECDC's annual scientific conference; the 2026 edition (20th) runs 18-20 November as a hybrid event in Stockholm.
- **Related work**: supports the **Health Security Committee** and **HERA** under the Health Union framework, and leads EU-level surveillance on **antimicrobial resistance (AMR)**.

## Context

ECDC was established in 2004 to give the EU a dedicated agency for tracking communicable disease outbreaks after SARS (2003) exposed gaps in cross-border disease intelligence. It became operational in Stockholm in May 2005. For nearly two decades its legal remit was narrower than its practical role: the COVID-19 pandemic showed that the agency needed a broader mandate covering all serious cross-border health threats, not just communicable disease, plus formal powers to set up EU reference laboratories and deploy staff to Member States during outbreaks.

Regulation (EU) 2022/2370 delivered that reform as one of three pillars of the **European Health Union** package (alongside the reinforced European Medicines Agency and the new cross-border threats framework, see `eu_health_security_framework`). It did not replace the founding Regulation (EC) No 851/2004; it amended it, so both instruments remain legally live and are read together.

## What ECDC does

### Surveillance and epidemic intelligence
ECDC runs the EU/EEA's integrated disease-surveillance systems. **EpiPulse** is the single portal where Member States submit indicator-based data (case counts, genomic sequences) and event-based signals (rumours, media reports, informal alerts) for over 50 disease topics, from measles and tuberculosis to emerging threats like mpox or avian influenza spillover. It replaced the previous generation of separate systems (TESSy for surveillance data, EPIS for event-based alerts) with one integrated platform.

The **Communicable Disease Threats Report (CDTR)** is ECDC's weekly bulletin, published every Friday, summarising the week's key epidemic-intelligence signals across the EU/EEA and globally.

### Forecasting and modelling
**RespiCast**, the European Respiratory Diseases Forecasting Hub, is a newer addition (late 2023) built with academic partners (ISI Foundation, London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine). It pools short-term forecasting models from multiple teams into an ensemble forecast for respiratory-disease indicators (influenza-like illness, acute respiratory infection, COVID-19), giving health authorities a rolling few-weeks-ahead view of the respiratory season. ECDC has since layered a companion tool, **RespiCompass**, for longer-horizon scenario modelling of respiratory disease burden.

### Risk assessment and scientific advice
ECDC produces rapid risk assessments for the Commission, Member States and the Health Security Committee whenever a new threat emerges, plus regular Annual Epidemiological Reports covering the full disease list under EU surveillance. Since Regulation (EU) 2022/2370, it can also formally deploy an **EU Health Task Force** of trained experts to support outbreak response in a Member State, and can designate **EU reference laboratories** for testing standardisation.

### Publishing and capacity building
ECDC publishes **Eurosurveillance**, a peer-reviewed, open-access journal that is a standard reference for European infectious-disease epidemiology. It trains the EU's field-epidemiology workforce through the **EPIET/EUPHEM Fellowship Programme**, and convenes the wider public-health community every year at **ESCAIDE**.

### Antimicrobial resistance
ECDC coordinates EU-level AMR surveillance (EARS-Net, ESAC-Net) and reports jointly with EFSA and EMA on antimicrobial consumption and resistance trends across the human-health, animal-health and food chains; see `antimicrobial_resistance_eu` for the dedicated guide.

## Governance in practice

| Body | Composition | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Director | Single appointee, 5-year mandate | Day-to-day management, chief scientific spokesperson |
| Management Board | Member States + European Parliament + Commission nominees | Strategy, budget, work programme adoption |
| Advisory Forum | Senior reps of national public health institutes + non-voting Commission official + observers | Advises Director on scientific quality; meets 4+ times/year |

ECDC does not set binding rules for Member States, since public health remains a national competence under TFEU Article 168, but its risk assessments, surveillance data and technical guidance are the reference point Member States and the Commission use when deciding on coordinated responses.
