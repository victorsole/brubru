# Emissions Accounting in Transport Services -- CountEmissions EU

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Standardised methodology to calculate and report greenhouse-gas emissions from passenger and freight transport services
- Proposal: CountEmissions EU -- Proposal for a Regulation on the accounting of greenhouse gas emissions from transport services, COM(2023) 441 final
- Procedure: 2023/0266(COD) -- ordinary legislative procedure, first reading
- Legal basis: Article 91 + Article 100(2) TFEU (transport); Article 192 TFEU (environment)
- Lead DG: DG MOVE (Mobility and Transport), Unit A.4 (sustainable mobility policy)
- Responsible Commissioner: Apostolos Tzitzikostas (Sustainable Transport and Tourism)
- EP lead committee: TRAN (Transport and Tourism); ENVI opinion
- Council configuration: TTE (Transport, Telecommunications and Energy)
- Scope: all modes -- road freight + passenger, rail, aviation, maritime, inland waterways. Applies to providers who communicate emissions data to clients (e.g. B2B logistics) AND those who offer such communication (B2C ticketing, mobility-as-a-service)
- EPRS At a Glance (20 April 2026): "Emissions accounting in transport services" -- summary of CountEmissions EU proposal and ongoing interinstitutional negotiations. Ref: EPRS_UNK_f745f48ba669
- Reference methodology: ISO 14083:2023 (calculation and reporting of GHG emissions from transport chain operations)
- Related regulations: EU ETS for aviation/shipping (Directive 2003/87/EC, amended by 2023/959); CBAM Regulation (2023/956); Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) Directive (EU) 2022/2464
- Expected impact: harmonised data for corporate scope-3 reporting, consumer ticket labelling (voluntary at first), supply-chain carbon accounting

## Why CountEmissions EU

Currently, transport providers use heterogeneous emissions calculation methods (CO2e or CO2 only, different emission factors, different scope boundaries). Aggregate data is inconsistent, hampers EU climate target monitoring, and creates greenwashing risks. The regulation harmonises:

1. **Common methodology**: based on ISO 14083:2023
2. **Energy and GHG intensity categories**: 6 GHGs included (CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs, SF6, NF3 where relevant); well-to-wake lifecycle boundary
3. **Data sources**: default factors (secondary data), actual measured data (primary), modelled data with documented assumptions
4. **Auditability**: third-party verification for larger transport operators
5. **Digital reporting**: single EU portal for emissions data (forthcoming; linked to Digital Europe Programme)

## Key Provisions (as per Commission proposal + EP first-reading position)

- **Article 1**: scope and objective
- **Article 3**: definitions (transport service, operator, default value, primary data, etc.)
- **Article 5**: methodology (mandatory reference to harmonised technical specification via delegated act)
- **Article 6**: obligations for operators communicating emissions data
- **Article 7**: voluntary labelling for services
- **Articles 8-12**: verification and third-party auditing
- **Article 13**: sanctions (effective, proportionate, dissuasive -- MS set amounts)
- **Transition periods**: 2-year transition for most operators; 4-year transition for SMEs
