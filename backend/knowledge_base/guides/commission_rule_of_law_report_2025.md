# Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Thursday 21 May 2026 — SLOVAKIA RESOLUTION ADOPTED)**: The European Parliament adopted in plenary the motion for a resolution **B10-0245/2026/REV1** "Rule of law, fundamental rights and misuse of EU funds in Slovakia: the need for an EU response" (joint CONT + LIBE; vote on Wednesday 20 May at 12:00). The resolution calls on the Commission to assess whether the Slovak government's conduct constitutes a serious breach of the values referred to in Article 2 TEU and to consider triggering the Rule of Law Conditionality Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092 (CELEX 32020R2092). Demands include: restoration of anti-corruption safeguards, protection of postal-voting rights for Slovaks abroad, and full investigation into alleged misuse of EU funds through the Slovak Agricultural Paying Agency. **Sponsoring Renew MEP**: Sophie Wilmès (MR, Belgium). The text builds on the Slovakia country chapter of the 2025 Rule of Law Report (COM(2025) 700-series, 8 July 2025), which had already flagged persistent concerns on judicial independence, media pluralism (STVR public broadcaster reform), and NGO restrictions. Adopting the resolution does not in itself activate the Conditionality Regulation — it remains a Commission decision under Article 6 of Reg (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092 — but it raises the political cost of inaction.

## Country highlights from the 2025 Report (7 July 2025, Commission findings)

This section is a **verified** summary of the Commission's public 2025 findings. Any user asking about a specific country must be pointed to the dedicated country chapter on `commission.europa.eu/rule-of-law-2025/country-chapters_en` for the full text.

- **Hungary**: most recommendations from 2020-2024 cycles still unimplemented at time of publication (July 2025). Post-election dynamics (April 2026) materially change the outlook -- 2026 Report will be the first post-Fidesz assessment
- **Slovakia**: concerns on judicial independence, media pluralism (STVR public broadcaster reform), and NGO restrictions persist
- **Italy**: media concentration, the Meloni government's influence over RAI, and judicial reform proposals flagged
- **Malta + Cyprus**: investor-citizenship issues (post-CJEU case C-181/23, April 2025) and anti-corruption follow-up
- **France**: use of Article 49.3, emergency powers usage, and police independence flagged
- **Germany**: broadly compliant, some flags on regulatory capture and media ownership concentration
- **Poland**: post-PiS rule-of-law restoration on track; Commission formally closed Article 7(1) procedure June 2024
- **Greece**: Predator spyware scandal (Sonofakis case), press freedom concerns -- Commission renewed scrutiny
- **Candidate countries** (Albania, Montenegro, N. Macedonia, Serbia, Ukraine): first full cycle since 2024 enlargement-adjusted methodology
