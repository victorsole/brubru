# Social Climate Fund (SCF)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal base**: Regulation (EU) 2023/955 of 10 May 2023 establishing the Social Climate Fund (CELEX **32023R0955**), part of the "Fit for 55" package
- **Total envelope (2026-2032)**: up to **EUR 86.7 billion** -- EUR 65 billion from the EU budget (funded by ETS2 auction revenues) plus a mandatory **minimum 25% national co-financing** from Member States (adds roughly EUR 21.7 billion), pushing the combined total to EUR 86.7bn
- **Funding source**: revenues from **ETS2** -- the new emissions trading system covering **buildings and road transport fuels** (extending the EU ETS logic under Directive 2003/87/EC to a second, downstream sector)
- **Who it targets -- three vulnerable groups**: (1) vulnerable households in energy poverty, (2) vulnerable micro-enterprises unable to afford building renovation or zero/low-emission vehicles, (3) vulnerable transport users in transport poverty
- **Eligible measures**: building renovation + heating decarbonisation; zero- and low-emission mobility + public transport support; **temporary direct income support** (transitional measure only, capped, phased out over time)
- **National Social Climate Plans (NSCPs)**: submitted by Member States by **30 June 2025**, assessed and approved by the Commission, integrated with the **National Energy and Climate Plans (NECPs)**
- **Disbursement mechanism**: RRF-style -- milestone- and target-based payments against the approved Plan, not simple reimbursement of costs
- **Payment schedule**: first disbursements from **2026**, i.e. the SCF is deliberately **front-loaded ahead of ETS2 carbon pricing itself** to cushion households before the new carbon cost hits
- **ETS2 timing**: originally scheduled to start pricing emissions on 1 January 2027; **postponed by one year to 1 January 2028** under the November 2025 co-legislator agreement (alongside adoption of the 2040 climate target), following Member State concerns over energy-price and inflation impacts. **Auctioning of ETS2 allowances still begins in 2027** as planned, so ETS2 revenue starts flowing a year before the ETS2 carbon price itself applies to end users -- this is what funds the SCF's 2026 front-loading
- **Lead DGs**: DG CLIMA (Climate Action, ETS2 design + revenue mechanics) and DG EMPL (Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion -- Fund administration, Social Climate Plans, vulnerability assessment methodology)
- **Complements**: Just Transition Fund (JTF), Modernisation Fund, and the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) -- the SCF is the newest, most socially-targeted layer of the EU's climate-transition financing architecture

## What the SCF is and the ETS2 link

The Social Climate Fund exists because of a political trade-off at the heart of "Fit for 55": extending carbon pricing to buildings and road transport (ETS2) would, without compensation, hit lower-income households and rural/peripheral transport users hardest -- precisely the groups least able to absorb higher heating and fuel costs. The SCF is the redistributive counterweight: it recycles ETS2 auction revenue back to Member States, earmarked for measures that reduce the underlying vulnerability (poor building insulation, inefficient heating, car-dependency) rather than simply subsidising fossil-fuel consumption indefinitely.

Structurally, the SCF sits inside Regulation (EU) 2023/955, adopted alongside the ETS2 legal base (the ETS2 provisions themselves amend Directive 2003/87/EC, the core EU ETS directive). The two instruments were negotiated as a package precisely so that the "polluter pays" carbon-pricing extension could not be decoupled from the social-compensation mechanism.

## Who it targets -- three vulnerable groups

The Regulation defines eligibility around three named categories, each linked to a specific harm from ETS2:

1. **Vulnerable households** -- households in energy poverty, or significantly affected by the price impact of including building emissions in the ETS and lacking the means to renovate the building they occupy
2. **Vulnerable micro-enterprises** -- enterprises with fewer than 10 employees and low annual turnover/balance sheet, significantly affected by the price impact and lacking the means to renovate premises or replace vehicles
3. **Vulnerable transport users** -- individuals in transport poverty, or significantly affected by the price impact of including road transport fuel emissions in the ETS, particularly outside urban centres, in rural, insular, peripheral, mountainous, remote or less accessible areas, or in less developed regions/territories

Member States must define the operational criteria for "vulnerable" in their Social Climate Plans, consistent with the Regulation's definitions and subject to Commission assessment.

## Eligible measures

Two categories of intervention, plus a strictly transitional third:

- **Building measures**: integrated building renovations (especially for the worst-performing buildings), decarbonisation of heating and cooling (heat pumps, connection to efficient district heating), and reducing energy poverty in social/affordable housing
- **Mobility measures**: support for zero- and low-emission vehicles, active mobility (cycling, walking infrastructure), and public transport access -- particularly to offset the higher relative cost of road-transport-fuel ETS2 pricing for those without transport alternatives
- **Temporary direct income support**: cash transfers to cushion the immediate ETS2 price shock. This is explicitly transitional and must decrease over time as structural measures (renovation, cleaner vehicles) take effect; it cannot be the Plan's dominant, permanent instrument

## National Social Climate Plans -- timeline and NECP integration

Member States were required to submit their National Social Climate Plans (NSCPs) to the Commission by **30 June 2025**, covering the full 2026-2032 period. Plans set out:

- the vulnerability assessment methodology and estimated number of households/enterprises/transport users covered
- the mix of measures and investments, with indicative costs
- milestones and targets against which disbursement is assessed
- the national co-financing commitment (minimum 25% of estimated plan costs)

The Commission assesses each Plan (comparable in spirit to RRF Recovery and Resilience Plan assessment) and requires **coherence with the Member State's National Energy and Climate Plan (NECP)** under the Governance Regulation -- the SCF is designed to reinforce, not duplicate, national climate and energy planning. Plans can be revised if circumstances change materially (e.g. if the ETS2 price trajectory shifts, as it has with the 2027-to-2028 postponement).

## Disbursement mechanics

Unlike traditional cohesion funds, the SCF disburses against **milestones and targets**, not simple cost-reimbursement -- an RRF-style design deliberately chosen to keep pressure on delivery. Key mechanics:

- Commission pre-financing of up to 13% of the allocated amount upon Plan approval
- Subsequent instalments released twice a year upon satisfactory fulfilment of milestones/targets set out in the approved Plan
- Suspension or reduction of instalments possible if milestones/targets are not achieved
- All measures must comply with the **"do no significant harm" (DNSH)** principle under Article 17 of the Taxonomy Regulation (EU) 2020/852
- National allocation keys weight energy poverty indicators, GHG emissions from household transport fuel combustion, gross national income per capita and population, favouring lower-income Member States with proportionally higher shares

## Relationship to JTF, Modernisation Fund and RRF

The SCF is best understood as one of four overlapping but distinct EU climate-finance instruments, each with a different target population and revenue source:

| Instrument | Legal base | Envelope | Revenue source | Primary beneficiary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Social Climate Fund** | Reg (EU) 2023/955 | EUR 86.7bn (2026-2032) | ETS2 auction revenue + 25% national co-financing | Vulnerable households, micro-enterprises, transport users |
| **Just Transition Fund** | Reg (EU) 2021/1056 | EUR 17.5bn (2021-2027) | MFF + NextGenerationEU | Coal/carbon-intensive transition territories |
| **Modernisation Fund** | Art 10d Directive 2003/87/EC | Variable (ETS1 allowance sales, lower-income Member States) | ETS1 (stationary installations) auction revenue | Lower-income Member States' energy-system modernisation |
| **Recovery and Resilience Facility** | Reg (EU) 2021/241 | EUR 723.8bn (grants+loans, expiring 2026) | NextGenerationEU borrowing | Broad reform + investment, incl. climate (37% minimum climate spend) |

The SCF is narrower and more socially targeted than the JTF (territorially defined) or the Modernisation Fund (Member-State-level energy transition); it is the first EU fund whose eligibility criteria are defined at the level of the individual household, micro-enterprise or transport user rather than the region or Member State. As the RRF winds down in 2026, the SCF becomes one of the few EU-level instruments still channelling money directly to household-level climate-social measures.

## ETS2 timing -- why it matters for the SCF calendar

ETS2 was originally set to start carbon pricing for buildings and road transport fuels on 1 January 2027. In November 2025, alongside adoption of the EU's 2040 climate target, co-legislators agreed to **postpone the start of ETS2 carbon pricing by one year, to 1 January 2028**, in response to Member State concerns about energy-price and inflation impacts on households. Critically, **the auctioning of ETS2 allowances still begins in 2027 as originally planned** -- so ETS2 revenue starts flowing to the EU budget a full year before the carbon price itself is charged to end consumers. This is precisely what allows the SCF to be front-loaded: first payments to Member States begin in 2026, before either the 2027 auctioning or the 2028 carbon-price application, on the strength of projected future ETS2 revenue and the EU budget's own pre-financing capacity.

Practical implication for advocacy and compliance work: any client asking "when does ETS2 hit my heating/fuel bill" should be told **1 January 2028**, not 2027 -- a commonly outdated figure still circulating in older analysis and press coverage from before the November 2025 postponement.
