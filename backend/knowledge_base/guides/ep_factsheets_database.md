# EP Factsheets: the EU's citizens' reference text

## QUICK FACTS
- **Resource:** Fact Sheets on the European Union
- **Home URL:** https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home
- **Published since:** 1979 (created for the first direct elections to the European Parliament)
- **Languages:** 24 official EU languages: BG, HR, CS, DA, DE, ET, EL, EN, FR, GA, HU, IT, LV, LT, MT, NL, PL, PT, RO, SK, SL, FI, SV, ES
- **Structure:** Five chapters (see below)
- **Chapter 1:** How the European Union works (treaties, legal system, decision-making, institutions, financing)
- **Chapter 2:** Economy, transformation and industry (internal market, EMU, competition, trade, digital, energy)
- **Chapter 3:** Cohesion, agriculture and social policies (regional policy, CAP, fisheries, transport, social affairs, education)
- **Chapter 4:** Citizens: fundamental rights, security and justice (Charter, asylum, immigration, judicial cooperation)
- **Chapter 5:** The EU's external relations (CFSP, defence, trade, development, enlargement, neighbourhood)
- **Authors:** EP Policy Departments and the Economic Governance Support Unit (expert staff, independent analysis)
- **Update cadence:** Rolling throughout the year; no fixed annual revision; sheets are updated after Parliament adopts important positions or policies
- **vs EPRS briefings:** Factsheets are evergreen primers (stable, policy-area level); EPRS briefings are topical and time-bound (tied to a specific file, vote, or institutional moment)
- **vs EUR-Lex LEGISSUM:** Factsheets cover a policy area broadly and explain the EU framework; LEGISSUM summarises a single adopted legislative act
- **Citation format:** "Fact Sheets on the European Union, [Sheet title], European Parliament, [year accessed], https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/[N]/[slug]"
- **Brubru use case:** Cite Factsheets for primer-grade "what is X" or "how does the EU work" questions; they are the safest evergreen reference on the EP website

## What the Factsheets Are

The Fact Sheets on the European Union are the official, continuously maintained reference compendium produced by the European Parliament's own expert staff. First published in 1979 to coincide with the first direct elections, they were designed for non-specialists who need a clear, accurate overview of how the EU is built and how its policies work.

Each Factsheet covers one thematic slice of EU policy or institutional architecture. The text is written by staff in the Policy Departments (the internal research units that serve parliamentary committees) and the Economic Governance Support Unit. Because these authors serve committees directly, the coverage reflects the EP's own legislative scope.

The defining characteristic of the Factsheets is their evergreen nature. Unlike a briefing tied to a specific vote, a Factsheet on competition policy or on the Charter of Fundamental Rights is revised whenever a significant development occurs: a major judgment, a new regulation, a treaty amendment. This makes them the most stable written reference the EP produces.

The full collection is freely accessible at https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home without registration. Individual sheets are also available as PDFs. Chapter-level PDFs bundle all sheets within one chapter into a single downloadable document.

## The Five Chapters in Detail

### Chapter 1: How the European Union Works
Covers the historical development of European integration, the successive treaties (Paris, Rome, Single European Act, Maastricht, Amsterdam, Nice, Lisbon), the EU's legal system and the hierarchy of norms, decision-making procedures (ordinary legislative procedure, special procedures, comitology), all EU institutions and bodies, and the EU budget and financing. This chapter is the primary reference for questions about institutional architecture, treaty bases, and legislative process.

### Chapter 2: Economy, Transformation and Industry
Covers the internal market (four freedoms, single market strategy), economic and monetary union (EMU), the euro area and the European Central Bank, competition policy, taxation, trade policy, industrial policy, digital single market, energy union, research and innovation (Horizon Europe), and transport. This chapter is heavily used by professionals in regulated sectors who need to understand the market-access and regulatory framework.

### Chapter 3: Cohesion, Agriculture and Social Policies
Covers cohesion and structural funds (ERDF, ESF+, Cohesion Fund), the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP), fisheries and maritime policy (CFP), transport and trans-European networks (TEN-T), social policy and employment, education and youth (Erasmus+), and culture and sport. This chapter is the reference for beneficiaries of EU funding programmes and those engaged in territorial, rural, or social policy.

### Chapter 4: Citizens: Fundamental Rights, Security and Justice
Covers the Charter of Fundamental Rights, EU citizenship and free movement, the Area of Freedom, Security and Justice (AFSJ), asylum and migration policy, border management (Schengen), police and judicial cooperation in criminal matters, civil judicial cooperation, consumer protection, and public health. This chapter is the reference for rights practitioners, migration specialists, and those working on justice and home affairs.

### Chapter 5: The EU's External Relations
Covers the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP), the Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP), EU trade policy and the common commercial policy, development cooperation and humanitarian aid, the EU's neighbourhood policy (ENP), enlargement policy, and the promotion of human rights and democracy globally. This chapter is the reference for professionals engaged in EU external affairs, trade, or geopolitics.

## Update Cadence

There is no fixed annual edition of the Factsheets. They are updated on a rolling basis by the responsible Policy Department as soon as Parliament adopts an important position or a significant policy development occurs. This means different sheets within the same chapter may carry different update dates.

Practical consequence: when citing a Factsheet, always include the access date. The online version is the authoritative one; PDF downloads may lag by weeks if a sheet was recently revised.

The Factsheets are not a news service. A sheet on, say, the AI Act will be updated after a major EP vote but may not reflect a minor amendment or a Commission delegated act until the Policy Department cycles back to review the text. For real-time legislative tracking, use the EP's Legislative Observatory (OEIL) and the EP's doceo document system.

## How to Cite a Factsheet

**Standard citation format:**
> [Author/Policy Department], "[Sheet title]", in: Fact Sheets on the European Union, European Parliament, [year of access], available at: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/[sheet-number]/[sheet-slug]

**Example:**
> Anastasia Mitronatsiou, "Language Policy", in: Fact Sheets on the European Union, European Parliament, 2026, available at: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/142/language-policy

Where the individual author is not visible from the URL (some sheets list the Policy Department rather than a named author), cite the Policy Department responsible for that chapter.

In informal Brubru Chat answers, the shortened form is acceptable:
> "See the EP Factsheet on [topic] at europarl.europa.eu/factsheets."

## Difference from EPRS Briefings

The European Parliamentary Research Service (EPRS) produces a large volume of publications: briefings, in-depth analyses, studies, and "at a glance" notes. These are distinct from the Factsheets in the following ways:

| Dimension | EP Factsheets | EPRS Briefings / Studies |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Scope | Policy area overview (structural) | Specific file, vote, or event (topical) |
| Lifespan | Evergreen, updated continuously | Time-bound, becomes historical once the legislative moment passes |
| Author | Policy Departments + Economic Governance Support Unit | EPRS Members' Research Service |
| Audience | Non-specialists, citizens, students | MEPs, staff, policy professionals |
| Availability | Factsheets website, 24 languages | EPRS Think Tank portal, mainly EN |
| Citation stability | Stable URL per topic | URL per publication, many become archives |

Use Factsheets when a user needs to understand the EU framework. Use EPRS output when a user needs analysis of a specific current or recent file.

See also: `eprs_european_parliamentary_research_service` (for the full EPRS research catalogue).

## Difference from EUR-Lex LEGISSUM Summaries

EUR-Lex publishes legislative summaries (LEGISSUM) that describe individual adopted EU legal acts in plain language. These are distinct from the Factsheets:

| Dimension | EP Factsheets | EUR-Lex LEGISSUM |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| Coverage | Policy area (multiple acts and policies combined) | One specific legal act |
| Publisher | European Parliament | Publications Office of the EU via EUR-Lex |
| Depth | Broad institutional and policy context | Summary of the specific act's provisions |
| Update trigger | When Parliament adopts new positions or significant developments occur | When the underlying act is amended |
| Cross-reference | Links to treaties, procedures, EP reports | Links directly to the act's CELEX document |

The two resources are complementary. A Factsheet on "Free Movement of Workers" explains the full policy framework; the LEGISSUM for Regulation 492/2011 summarises that specific regulation's operative provisions.

See also: `eurlex_legissum_summaries` (for LEGISSUM coverage and search tips) and `finding_and_citing_eu_law`.

## Brubru Chat Usage Pattern

**When to recommend a Factsheet:**
- User asks "what is X EU policy?" or "how does X work in the EU?": structural explainer questions
- User needs a primer before engaging with a specific legislative file (e.g., "I need to understand EU competition policy before reading the Digital Markets Act")
- User is new to EU affairs and needs orientation on institutions, decision-making, or a broad policy area
- User asks for a reference they can share with a non-specialist colleague

**When NOT to recommend a Factsheet:**
- User needs the text of a specific regulation or directive: send to EUR-Lex
- User needs to track the current status of a legislative procedure: send to OEIL or the Legislative Train
- User needs EPRS analysis of a specific EP vote or file: send to the EPRS Think Tank portal
- User needs the latest Commission proposal: send to EUR-Lex new proposals or EUR-Lex recent OJ

**Chat answer template:**
> "The EP's Fact Sheets on the European Union have a good primer on [topic]. See chapter [N], "[Chapter title]", at https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home. These are written by EP policy experts and updated continuously, so they reflect the current framework."

**Brubru should never** paraphrase Factsheet content as if it were original analysis. Always attribute to the Factsheets and provide the URL.

## Practical Search Tips

**Finding a specific Factsheet:**
1. Go to https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home and browse by chapter
2. Use the search function on the Factsheets homepage (searches sheet titles and content)
3. Individual sheet URLs follow the pattern: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/[N]/[slug]`
4. Example of a confirmed working sheet URL: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/sheet/142/language-policy

**Downloading chapter PDFs:**
Chapter-level PDFs bundling all sheets in a chapter are downloadable from the homepage. They are suitable for offline reference or distribution.

**Changing language:**
The language selector is available on each sheet page. Switch with the two-letter code in the URL: replace `/en/` with `/fr/`, `/de/`, `/es/`, etc. Example: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/fr/sheet/142/language-policy` for French.

**Checking update date:**
Each sheet shows its last updated date on the web version. Always verify before citing in a time-sensitive context.
