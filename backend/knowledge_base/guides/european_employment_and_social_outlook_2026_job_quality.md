# 2026 European Employment and Social Outlook: job quality (Commission, 13 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What was published:** on **13 October 2026** the Commission published the **2026 European Employment and Social Outlook** (press release IP/26/2147): job quality has **improved over the past decade** in three areas, **pay, job security and working time**. The release only NAMES these three areas and gives **no figures for them**; the employment and unemployment rates below are labour-market figures, not measures of job security.
- **Labour market figures in the release:** **employment rate 76.1% in 2025**; **unemployment 6% in 2025**, a historic low. For 2026, Commission analysis says the **energy shock linked to the conflict in the Middle East** is likely to **slow employment growth but not stop it**: about **440,000 fewer jobs** are expected to be created in 2026, with slower hiring rather than a sharp downturn. **Real wages in 2026 are 1.4% above their pre-COVID-19 level**, having recovered the losses of 2022 and 2023 in most Member States.
- **Wages and productivity:** the report says wages can keep rising only if companies become more productive; since the **Minimum Wages Directive** was adopted, minimum wages have increased, especially in lower-wage Member States.
- **AI and work:** AI can raise productivity and job quality, but the effect depends on how it is introduced and governed; it can put pressure on labour demand in some entry-level and routine jobs, which makes worker representation, skills policies and social dialogue more important.
- **Quality Jobs Act:** the release says the Commission "will present the **Quality Jobs Act**" **later this year**, following the **second-phase consultation of EU social partners**. **No date and no procedure number are given**: do not state either.
- **What the release does NOT say about the Quality Jobs Act:** it gives **no content, scope, priority areas or number of measures** for the Act. The points on wages, AI and worker representation above are findings of the **Outlook**, not features of the future Act: never present them as the Act's content, and never offer a summary of its "priority areas".
- **Basis:** the Outlook builds on the **Employment Committee's Job Quality Framework**; the Treaty requires the Commission to report on employment and social trends.

## How to answer
This is a Commission analytical report, not legislation. For the Quality Jobs Act say only what the release says: announced for later in 2026, after the social partners' second-phase consultation.
