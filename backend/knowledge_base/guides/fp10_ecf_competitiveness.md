## Overview

On 16 July 2025, the European Commission proposed FP10 -- the 10th Framework Programme for Research and Innovation (retaining the "Horizon Europe" brand) -- with a budget of EUR 175 billion, nearly doubling the EUR 95.5 billion under the current Horizon Europe (2021-2027). FP10 is part of the broader EUR 2 trillion MFF 2028-2034 package and sits within the European Competitiveness Fund (ECF), an overarching EUR 451 billion envelope that consolidates 14 individual funding instruments under MFF Heading 2.

The central policy question -- and the one stakeholders are actively debating -- is how FP10 and the ECF relate to each other: whether FP10 retains its autonomy as a research excellence programme or becomes subordinated to industrial competitiveness objectives.

### Four Pillars

| Pillar | Focus | Key Bodies |
|--------|-------|------------|
| I: Excellent Science | Frontier research, researcher mobility, research infrastructures | ERC (European Research Council) |
| II: Competitiveness and Society | Collaborative research in strategic priorities, industry partnerships | Joint Undertakings, partnerships |
| III: Innovation | Breakthrough innovation, scaling | EIC (European Innovation Council) |
| IV: European Research Area | Widening participation, R&I ecosystems, science-society | ERA actions, Teaming, Twinning |

Both the ERC and EIC are in line for significant budget increases under the proposal. Pillar II ("Competitiveness and Society") is the pillar most directly linked to the ECF's competitiveness objectives.

### EP Position

EP BUDG co-rapporteurs Siegfried Muresan (EPP, Romania) and Carla Tavares (S&D, Portugal) have called for a 10% budget increase over the Commission's EUR 173 billion proposal (adjusted for inflation), arguing the proposed amount does not match the ambition of closing the innovation gap with the US and China.

### Council Position

Research ministers discussed the FP10 proposal at an informal meeting the day after its presentation on 16 July 2025. Two formal Council debates have taken place since. Slovenia has expressed "frustration" at the slow pace of negotiations. Detailed Council positions are still forming as of March 2026.

### Purpose and Scope

The ECF is designed as a single framework for EU investment in competitiveness across strategic technologies and sectors. It covers the full innovation chain: collaborative research, scaling up, innovation deployment, industrial infrastructure, and manufacturing. It sits within MFF Heading 2, which carries a wider EUR 451 billion envelope (including the lending facility).

### Policy Windows

The ECF organises funding around thematic "policy windows" rather than separate programme-specific calls:

- AI and digital technologies
- Space
- Clean technologies and energy transition
- Biotechnology and health
- Defence
- Health, agriculture, and bioeconomy

### Programmes Consolidated into ECF

The ECF merges 14 existing instruments. Key ones include:

| Current Programme | Regulation | Status under ECF |
|-------------------|------------|-----------------|
| EU4Health | Regulation (EU) 2021/522 | Absorbed into ECF health window |
| Digital Europe Programme | Regulation (EU) 2021/694 | Absorbed into ECF digital window |
| European Defence Fund (EDF) | Regulation (EU) 2021/697 | Absorbed into ECF defence window |
| LIFE Programme | Regulation (EU) 2021/783 | Absorbed into ECF clean tech window |
| EU Space Programme (partial) | Regulation (EU) 2021/696, Regulation (EU) 2023/588 | Partially absorbed |
| EDIP | Amended by ECF | Defence industrial programme |

InvestEU guarantees serve as a horizontal instrument across all ECF windows. A new Competitiveness Coordination Tool is proposed for strategic steering across MFF headings.

### Beneficiaries

SMEs, start-ups, larger companies, universities, research entities, and public bodies. The ECF aims to simplify access compared to the current fragmented landscape of 14 separate programmes with different rules.

## FP10-ECF Link: The Key Question

This is the central debate for the research and innovation community in the 2028-2034 programming period.

### Architecture

FP10 will sit inside the wider EUR 451 billion ECF architecture but **remain autonomous as a standalone programme**. The Commission describes the relationship as "tightly connected": ECF work programmes must integrate and ensure coherence with FP10's Pillar II (Competitiveness and Society), which funds collaborative research in the same priority areas as the ECF windows.

### Division of Roles

| Stage | Lead Programme | Mechanism |
|-------|---------------|-----------|
| Frontier research | FP10 (Pillar I) | ERC grants, curiosity-driven |
| Collaborative research for competitiveness | FP10 (Pillar II) | Partnerships, Joint Undertakings |
| Breakthrough innovation and scaling | FP10 (Pillar III) | EIC Accelerator, blended finance |
| Industrial deployment and manufacturing | ECF | Policy window grants, InvestEU guarantees |
| Infrastructure investment | ECF | Large-scale project funding |

### Synergy Mechanisms

- **Competitiveness Seal**: Projects assessed as high quality under ECF evaluation but not funded (due to budget constraints) can receive a quality label enabling access to national or regional funds (similar to the existing Seal of Excellence for EIC)
- **Work programme coherence**: ECF work programmes must align with FP10 Pillar II priorities to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity
- **Joint Undertakings**: Existing partnerships (e.g., Clean Hydrogen, Key Digital Technologies) will bridge FP10 and ECF

### Research Community Position

A coalition of major university associations -- CESAER, EUA, EU-LIFE, LERU, YERUN, Coimbra Group, and The Guild -- has taken a unified position: FP10 and the ECF should be **"autonomous but complementary"**. Their key demands:

- FP10 must remain focused on research excellence and international collaboration
- ECF should accelerate translation of research results to market and deployment
- Clear governance boundaries needed to prevent ECF industrial priorities from distorting FP10 research agendas
- Adequate ring-fencing of FP10 budget within the ECF envelope
- Bottom-up, investigator-driven research (ERC) must not be subordinated to top-down industrial strategy

### EU-Australia Horizon Europe Association (1 April 2026)

On 31 March 2026, the EU and Australia formally opened negotiations on Australia's association to Horizon Europe. This would make Australia an associated country, enabling Australian researchers to participate in Horizon Europe calls on equal terms. The association is expected to cover the remainder of the 2021-2027 programme and set the groundwork for FP10 participation.

### Open Questions (March 2026)

- How will FP10 budget be ring-fenced within the ECF envelope in practice?
- Will ECF governance structures have influence over FP10 work programme design?
- How will the Competitiveness Coordination Tool interact with FP10 programme committees?
- Will Pillar II partnership budgets be drawn from FP10 or ECF allocations?
- How will "policy windows" align with FP10 cluster structure?

## Committee of the Regions Position (9 October 2025 Plenary)

The CoR adopted an opinion calling for:

- A more ambitious and stable FP10 budget with long-term predictability
- FP10 should promote synergies with cohesion policy (Heading 1)
- Better linkage to Smart Specialisation Strategies (S3) at regional level
- Regional Innovation Valleys welcomed as a tool for reducing the innovation divide
- Citizens' active participation in research and innovation essential
