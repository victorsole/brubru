# EU Competition Law Enforcement

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU antitrust enforcement -- Articles 101 and 102 TFEU
- Legal basis: TFEU Articles 101-109
- Article 101: Prohibits anti-competitive agreements (price fixing, market sharing, bid rigging)
- Article 102: Prohibits abuse of dominant position (predatory pricing, tying, refusal to supply)
- Procedural framework: Regulation (EC) 1/2003
- Lead DG: DG Competition (DG COMP)
- Max fine: 10% of worldwide annual turnover
- Leniency: Full immunity for first applicant; 20-50% reduction for subsequent cooperators
- Settlement discount: 10% fine reduction
- ECN+ Directive: Directive (EU) 2019/1 -- harmonises NCA powers across Member States
- Private enforcement: Damages Directive 2014/104/EU
- Judicial review: General Court (full review + unlimited jurisdiction on fines), Court of Justice (points of law)
- Largest cartel fine: EUR 2.93 billion (truck cartel, 2016)

## Treaty Provisions

### Article 101 TFEU -- Anti-Competitive Agreements

Prohibits agreements, decisions by associations of undertakings, and concerted practices that:
- Have as their object or effect the restriction of competition
- May affect trade between Member States

#### Types of Prohibited Conduct (Art. 101(1))

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Price fixing** | Agreements on prices or pricing components | Cartel fixing raw material prices |
| **Market sharing** | Allocation of markets, customers, or territories | Competitors dividing EU by Member State |
| **Output limitation** | Agreements to limit production or supply | Production quotas |
| **Bid rigging** | Coordinated tendering behaviour | Pre-arranged winning bidder |
| **Information exchange** | Sharing competitively sensitive data | Future pricing or capacity plans |

#### Exemption (Art. 101(3))

An agreement may be exempted if it:
1. Contributes to improving production/distribution or promoting technical/economic progress
2. Allows consumers a fair share of the resulting benefit
3. Does not impose unnecessary restrictions
4. Does not eliminate competition for a substantial part of the products

**Block exemption regulations** provide safe harbours for certain categories (vertical agreements, horizontal cooperation, technology transfer, R&D, specialisation).

### Article 102 TFEU -- Abuse of Dominant Position

Prohibits unilateral conduct by a dominant undertaking that constitutes an abuse.

#### Dominance Assessment

| Factor | Indicators |
|--------|-----------|
| **Market share** | > 40% creates rebuttable presumption; > 50% strong presumption |
| **Barriers to entry** | Regulatory, technical, financial barriers |
| **Countervailing buyer power** | Ability of customers to switch or self-supply |
| **Economies of scale** | Cost advantages unavailable to competitors |

#### Types of Abuse

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Exploitative** | Excessive pricing, unfair trading conditions |
| **Exclusionary** | Predatory pricing, refusal to supply, tying, exclusive dealing, rebates |
| **Discriminatory** | Applying different conditions to equivalent transactions |

## Regulation 1/2003 -- Procedural Framework

### Investigation Powers

#### Requests for Information (Art. 18)

| Type | Legal Basis | Consequences of Non-Compliance |
|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Simple request** | Art. 18(2) | No penalty for non-response (but may escalate) |
| **Decision-based request** | Art. 18(3) | Periodic penalty payments up to 5% of daily turnover |

Undertakings must provide correct and complete information. Supplying incorrect/misleading information: fine up to 1% of turnover.

#### Dawn Raids / Inspections (Art. 20-21)

**Powers during inspections (Art. 20):**
- Enter premises, land, means of transport
- Examine and copy books and records (including electronic)
- Seal business premises and records (for duration of inspection)
- Ask for oral explanations on facts relating to the subject matter
- Inspect private premises (Art. 21) with prior judicial authorisation

**Procedural safeguards:**
- Written authorisation specifying subject matter and purpose
- National competition authority (NCA) officials may assist
- Legal professional privilege (in-house counsel protection varies by jurisdiction)
- Right to legal counsel during inspection (but no right to delay)

### Statement of Objections (SO)

The formal charge document setting out the Commission's preliminary findings:

1. **Issuance**: After investigation, if the Commission intends to adopt an infringement decision
2. **Access to file**: Undertaking granted access to the Commission's case file (excluding business secrets and internal documents)
3. **Written reply**: Undertaking has minimum 4 weeks (typically 8-12 weeks) to respond
4. **Oral hearing**: Right to request an oral hearing before the Hearing Officer
5. **Facts not in SO**: Commission cannot base its final decision on facts not set out in the SO

### Decision Types

#### Infringement Decision (Art. 7)

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Finding | Existence of infringement |
| Order | Cessation of infringement |
| Remedies | Behavioural or structural remedies (proportionate and necessary) |
| Fine | Up to 10% of worldwide annual turnover (see Fining section) |
| Duration | May cover past and ongoing infringement |

#### Commitment Decision (Art. 9)

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| No finding | No formal finding of infringement |
| Commitments | Undertaking offers binding commitments addressing Commission's concerns |
| Market test | Commitments published for third-party comments |
| Duration | Typically 5-10 years |
| Reopening | If circumstances change, commitments breached, or based on incomplete information |

#### Interim Measures (Art. 8)

- Rare: requires prima facie infringement + risk of serious and irreparable harm
- Duration: specified period, renewable
- Used in urgent cases (e.g., refusal to supply threatening market exit)

## Fining Framework

### Commission Fining Guidelines (2006)

#### Step 1: Basic Amount

| Factor | Calculation |
|--------|------------|
| **Relevant sales** | Value of sales directly/indirectly related to the infringement in the EEA |
| **Gravity percentage** | 0-30% of relevant sales (based on nature, market share, scope) |
| **Duration multiplier** | Number of years of infringement (per year) |
| **Entry fee** | Additional 15-25% of relevant sales for horizontal price-fixing/market-sharing cartels |

#### Step 2: Adjustments

| Aggravating | Mitigating |
|-------------|-----------|
| Repeat offender (+100%) | Limited involvement |
| Refusal to cooperate | Genuine uncertainty about legality |
| Ringleader/instigator | Immediate termination after Commission intervention |
| Retaliation against complainants | Cooperation beyond legal obligation |

#### Step 3: Deterrence Adjustment

Additional multiplier for undertakings with particularly large turnover beyond the relevant market.

#### Cap

Final fine cannot exceed **10% of total worldwide annual turnover** of the preceding business year.

### Recent Fine Levels

Fines in major cartel cases regularly reach hundreds of millions of euros. The largest single cartel fine (truck cartel, 2016) totalled EUR 2.93 billion across all participants.

## Leniency Programme

### Full Immunity (Type 1A)

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **First to apply** | Must be first undertaking to provide evidence |
| **Evidence standard** | Evidence sufficient to carry out targeted inspection or find infringement |
| **Cooperation** | Continuous, full cooperation throughout proceedings |
| **Conditions** | Must not have coerced others to participate; must end participation immediately (unless Commission agrees continuation for investigation integrity) |

### Fine Reduction (Type 1B)

| Reduction Range | Requirement |
|-----------------|-------------|
| 30-50% | First undertaking providing significant added value |
| 20-30% | Second undertaking providing significant added value |
| Up to 20% | Subsequent undertakings providing significant added value |

### Marker System

An applicant may request a "marker" to protect its place in the queue while gathering evidence. Marker typically valid for 3-4 weeks.

## Settlement Procedure (Art. 10a Regulation 773/2004)

### Process

1. Commission initiates settlement discussions (at its discretion)
2. Parties acknowledge the infringement, accept liability, and agree to a fine range
3. **10% fine reduction** for settling
4. Simplified SO and streamlined decision
5. Faster resolution (typically 6-12 months shorter than standard procedure)

### Hybrid Settlements

Some parties may settle while others proceed to full investigation. Settlement discount applies only to settling parties.

## ECN Cooperation (Regulation 1/2003, Chapter IV)

### European Competition Network (ECN)

Network of the Commission + 27 NCAs. Key cooperation mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **Case allocation** | Art. 11-14: NCAs inform Commission of new cases; Commission may take over |
| **Information sharing** | Art. 12: Exchange of confidential information between ECN members |
| **Consistent application** | Art. 16: National courts must not contradict Commission decisions |
| **Amicus curiae** | Art. 15: Commission may submit observations to national courts |
| **Advisory Committee** | Art. 14: Consultation before Commission decisions |

### ECN+ Directive (EU) 2019/1

Harmonises NCA powers across Member States:
- Minimum investigative and decision-making powers
- Effective fining powers (minimum: ability to impose fines based on turnover)
- Leniency programme coordination (protection of leniency statements)
- Independence and resources for NCAs

## Judicial Review

### General Court (First Instance)

| Action | Legal Basis | Standard of Review |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Annulment** | Art. 263 TFEU | Full review of law; limited review of complex economic assessments |
| **Fine reduction** | Unlimited jurisdiction (Art. 261 TFEU) | Court may increase, reduce, or cancel fines |
| **Interim measures** | Art. 278-279 TFEU | Suspension of decision pending judgment |

### Court of Justice (Appeal)

- Points of law only
- No reassessment of facts or economic evidence
- Average duration: 15-18 months

## Private Enforcement (Damages Directive 2014/104/EU)

- National courts may award damages for antitrust infringements
- Commission infringement decision is binding proof of infringement in national proceedings
- Limitation period: minimum 5 years from cessation of infringement
- Disclosure rules for evidence held by competition authorities
