## Understanding Professional Standards

### The Position Paper
A professional position paper follows a clear structure:

1. **Executive Summary** - One paragraph with your key ask
2. **Context** - Why this matters to the EU (not to you)
3. **Key Concerns** - Maximum three, clearly numbered
4. **Recommendations** - Specific, with draft legislative text
5. **About** - Brief credibility statement

**Length:** Maximum 2 pages. Decision-makers are busy.
**Tone:** Constructive, solution-oriented, EU-framed

---

### The Stakeholder Map
A professional stakeholder map includes:

- **Complete coverage:** All relevant decision-makers across institutions
- **Attitude assessment:** Supportive / Neutral / Hostile for each
- **Influence rating:** Based on formal role and informal power
- **Priority ranking:** Where to focus engagement
- **Action recommendations:** Specific next steps for each priority

See: `stakeholder_mapping.md` for the full framework

---

### The Amendment
A professional amendment includes:

- **Reference:** Article, paragraph, point being amended
- **Type:** Addition / Deletion / Modification
- **Current text:** What the law says now
- **Proposed text:** Your exact wording
- **Justification:** Why this change serves EU objectives

**Quality test:** Could an MEP table this without changes?

---

### The Meeting Brief
A professional meeting brief includes:

- **Objective:** What you want from this meeting
- **Key messages:** Maximum three points to make
- **Background:** What they already know/think
- **Anticipated questions:** And your answers
- **Ask:** Specific request to close with
- **Leave-behind:** One-page summary for their files

## What Good Advocacy Looks Like

### Timing Excellence
- Engaging during impact assessment phase, before positions harden
- Submitting amendments before deadline, with time for MEP review
- Meeting decision-makers when they're forming views, not defending them
- Following up promptly after key votes or meetings

### Message Discipline
- Consistent positioning across all interactions
- Framing that resonates with EU priorities
- Evidence-based arguments with credible data
- Solutions, not just problems

### Relationship Quality
- Being known as a reliable, credible stakeholder
- Providing useful information, not just asks
- Following through on commitments
- Maintaining contact between files

### Coalition Effectiveness
- Aligned messaging with partners
- Coordinated engagement timing
- Complementary stakeholder coverage
- Shared intelligence

## The Engagement Hierarchy

Professional advocacy uses multiple channels, prioritised by effectiveness:

1. **Direct meetings** - Highest impact for key stakeholders
2. **Strategic events** - Position yourself as convener and expert
3. **Written submissions** - Formal record, supports meeting requests
4. **Coalition actions** - Amplify voice through numbers
5. **Media engagement** - Public pressure when appropriate
6. **Digital presence** - Ongoing visibility and accessibility

## Quality Benchmarks

### Your Position Paper Passes If:
- An MEP's assistant would forward it to the MEP
- A Commission official would reference it in analysis
- A journalist would quote it as industry position
- Your coalition partners would endorse it

### Your Meeting Passes If:
- You made your three points clearly
- You left with a specific next step
- The official remembers you positively
- You can follow up with value

### Your Campaign Passes If:
- Your position influenced the final text
- You built relationships for future files
- You're invited back for next consultation
- Your organisation is recognised as a stakeholder
