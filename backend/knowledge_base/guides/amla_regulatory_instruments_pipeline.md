# AMLA Regulatory Instruments: CDD RTS, Group-Wide Rules, Risk-Assessment Methodology

## QUICK FACTS
- Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA): EU decentralised agency, seat Frankfurt am Main, Germany; established by Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 (AMLAR, CELEX: 32024R1620)
- Chair: Bruna Szego (Italy), appointed December 2024, took office 1 January 2025; AMLA itself became operational 1 July 2025
- EBA-to-AMLA handover completed: 31 December 2025 -- EBA's AML/CFT mandates in the financial sector (EuReCa database, supervisory insights, risk assessments) formally transferred to AMLA. Article 54 AMLAR keeps ALL legacy EBA AML/CFT guidelines and standards in force until AMLA formally replaces them, with transition periods for stakeholders
- AMLA's regulatory-instrument pipeline delivers the technical detail beneath the single rulebook: AMLR Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 (CELEX: 32024R1624) + AMLD6 Directive (EU) 2024/1640 (CELEX: 32024L1640). Both instruments apply/transpose from **10 July 2027** -- the anchor date for nearly every AMLA RTS/ITS/Guideline general-application clause
- Instrument types: Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS, drafted by AMLA, adopted by the Commission as delegated acts), Implementing Technical Standards (ITS, adopted as implementing acts), Guidelines (GL, issued directly by AMLA under AMLAR Art. 43-style "comply or explain")
- Consultations run through 2025-2026; final drafts due to the European Commission by **10 July 2026** for RTS/most ITS, with the bulk of Guidelines following through 2026-2027
- Open consultations (as of July 2026): RTS on cross-border FIU information exchange (AMLD Art. 31(3)); ITS on reporting-suspicions/transaction-record formats (AMLR Art. 69(3)); Guidelines on ongoing monitoring of business relationships (AMLR Art. 26(5), public hearing concluded 2 July 2026); Guidelines on business-wide risk assessment (AMLR Art. 10(4))
- Closed consultations, submission pending: RTS on group-wide requirements for subsidiaries/branches (AMLR Art. 16(4) + 17(3), EC submission due 30 September 2026); RTS on home-host supervisory cooperation (AMLD Art. 46(4)); RTS on customer due diligence (AMLR Art. 28(1), consultation closed 8 May 2026, EC submission due 10 July 2026); RTS on business-relationship/occasional-transaction/lower-threshold identification criteria (AMLR Art. 19(9)); ITS on AML/CFT supervisory cooperation (AMLAR Art. 15(3))
- Final reports already submitted to the Commission: RTS on pecuniary sanctions and administrative measures (AMLD Art. 53(10)); ITS on FIU reporting to EPPO (AMLR Art. 81(1)); ITS on AMLA reporting to EPPO (AMLAR Art. 41(2)); ITS on FIU-to-FIU exchanges (AMLD Art. 31(2)); RTS on risk-profile assessment for obliged entities (AMLD Art. 40(2)); RTS on selection criteria for direct supervision (AMLAR Art. 12(7))
- Direct-supervision selection: first exercise in **2027**, entities transferred to EU-level supervision from **2028**; AMLA will directly supervise **no more than ~40** high-risk, cross-border financial-sector entities. Geographic eligibility trigger: financial institutions/groups operating in **6 or more Member States**. National supervisors report data to AMLA by **15 August 2026**; provisional list of eligible obliged entities finalised by **end-September 2026**
- Official domain: `amla.europa.eu` -- all genuine AMLA email addresses end `@amla.europa.eu`; treat any other domain as phishing/impersonation
- Lead DG for the wider AML Package: DG FISMA. AMLA itself is an independent EU agency, not a DG
- Verify the live consultation calendar and final-submission dates before citing a specific instrument's status -- this pipeline moves fast and dates shift as public hearings conclude

## Why This Matters for Compliance Teams

`eu_anti_money_laundering.md` covers the AML Package's *adoption* (AMLR + AMLD6 + AMLAR, May-June 2024). The operational reality from 2026 onward is different: AMLA does not wait for the AMLR's 10 July 2027 general-application date to start shaping the rules obliged entities must follow. Each RTS, ITS and Guideline AMLA finalises is itself a compliance trigger -- it fixes the granular content of obligations (what documents count as adequate CDD evidence, what a group compliance function must document, which data points feed the risk-assessment methodology) well before the framework applies generally. Firms that wait for 2027 to start implementing will be reading final legal texts, not draft consultation papers, with far less runway to adapt systems and policies.

## The Regulatory Instruments Pipeline

AMLA's mandate under AMLR, AMLD6 and AMLAR requires it to draft dozens of RTS, ITS and Guidelines. The pipeline runs through three stages: **consultation** (AMLA publishes a draft + consultation paper, often with a public hearing) &rarr; **final report** (AMLA adopts the final draft after considering feedback) &rarr; **Commission adoption** (the Commission adopts the RTS/ITS as a delegated or implementing act; Guidelines are issued directly by AMLA and do not require Commission adoption).

## Customer Due Diligence (CDD) RTS -- AMLR Article 28(1)

The draft RTS on CDD specify, in operational detail, how obliged entities must verify customer identity, understand ownership and control structures, and conduct ongoing monitoring on a risk-sensitive and proportionate basis. It sits alongside a companion RTS (AMLR Art. 19(9)) that fixes the criteria for identifying "business relationships", "occasional transactions", "linked transactions" and the lower monetary thresholds that trigger enhanced scrutiny. AMLA opened both consultations on 9 February 2026, held its first public hearing on 24 March 2026 (over 1,600 stakeholders, with a stated focus on capturing non-financial-sector views), closed the consultation window on 8 May 2026, and is required to submit final drafts to the Commission by 10 July 2026. Firms should treat the closed-consultation draft text as the working baseline for 2026-2027 CDD system changes, while checking for amendments in the final report once published.

## Group-Wide Policies RTS -- AMLR Articles 16(4) and 17(3)

Article 16 AMLR requires parent undertakings to implement uniform AML/CFT policies, controls and procedures across every entity under their responsibility, establish a group compliance function, adopt an annual report on implementation, and ensure effective information-sharing within the group -- including branches and subsidiaries located in third countries. The draft RTS (consultation paper published 16 April 2026, public hearing 20 May 2026 with over 650 participants) introduce binding minimum-content requirements: group-wide policies must be formally documented, subject to regular review, and made available to competent authorities on request, with a clear governance structure and defined allocation of functions. AMLA will submit the final draft to the Commission by 30 September 2026. This instrument matters most for cross-border banking groups, insurance groups and CASP groups with subsidiaries or branches outside the EU.

## Risk-Assessment and Selection Methodology -- AMLD Article 40(2) and AMLAR Article 12(7)

Two linked RTS create the common methodology behind AMLA's direct-supervision pool:
- **Risk-profile assessment RTS (AMLD Art. 40(2))**: specifies the data points and criteria national supervisors must use to assess money-laundering and terrorist-financing risk for every obliged entity they supervise -- for the first time, a single harmonised methodology across all Member States' supervisors.
- **Selection-criteria RTS (AMLAR Art. 12(7))**: applies those same data points to determine which entities AMLA itself will select for direct supervision.

Selection mechanics for the first (2027) exercise: national supervisors report standardised data to AMLA by **15 August 2026** using a common reporting template and interpretative note; AMLA finalises the **provisional list of eligible obliged entities by end-September 2026**; the geographic eligibility trigger is operating in **6 or more Member States**; the final selection exercise runs in **2027**; selected entities (capped at roughly **40**) transfer to AMLA's direct EU-level supervision from **2028**. Entities not selected remain under national supervision but inside the same harmonised single rulebook and risk-assessment methodology.

## FIU Cooperation, Information Exchange and EPPO/Europol Interaction

AMLA has already finalised several instruments governing information flow:
- ITS on FIU-to-FIU exchanges (AMLD Art. 31(2)) -- standardises the format for cross-border Financial Intelligence Unit cooperation, succeeding the informal FIU.net arrangements under the previous regime
- ITS on FIU reporting to the European Public Prosecutor's Office (AMLR Art. 81(1)) and ITS on AMLA reporting to EPPO (AMLAR Art. 41(2)) -- both finalised, giving EPPO a standardised channel to receive suspicious-activity intelligence relevant to its own investigations
- RTS on cross-border FIU information exchange (AMLD Art. 31(3)) -- still in open consultation, expected to add further granularity to the finalised FIU-to-FIU ITS
- ITS on AML/CFT supervisory cooperation (AMLAR Art. 15(3)) -- governs how AMLA and national supervisors coordinate, closed consultation

For Brubru users tracking financial-crime enforcement more broadly, cross-reference `eppo_european_public_prosecutor.md` for how EPPO uses the intelligence these formats deliver, and `eu_sanctions_implementation_framework.md` for the adjacent sanctions-evasion typology (shell companies, nominee directors, beneficial-ownership opacity) that both AML supervision and sanctions enforcement target.

## Pecuniary Sanctions and Administrative Measures RTS -- AMLD Article 53(10)

Already finalised and submitted to the Commission, this RTS specifies how national supervisors and AMLA calculate pecuniary sanctions and other administrative measures for AML/CFT breaches, building on the AMLR's headline sanctions ceiling (up to 10% of annual turnover or EUR 10 million, whichever is higher, for serious breaches -- see `eu_anti_money_laundering.md`). Because this instrument reached final-report stage well ahead of most others in the pipeline, it is a reliable near-final reference for how enforcement will actually be quantified once the AMLR applies generally.

## EBA-to-AMLA Transition: What Remains Valid Today

Before the daughter-instrument pipeline is complete, obliged entities are not in a regulatory vacuum. Article 54 AMLAR provides continuity: all pre-existing EBA AML/CFT Guidelines and Regulatory Technical Standards remain legally in force until AMLA formally replaces them, with AMLA committed to giving stakeholders adequate transition periods when it does. The formal handover of EBA's AML/CFT mandates (including the EuReCa database of material AML/CFT weaknesses, supervisory insights and risk assessments) to AMLA completed on **31 December 2025**. Practical implication: any compliance query about "what CDD standard applies today" should default to the still-valid legacy EBA Guidelines unless a specific AMLA RTS/Guideline has already reached final-report or Commission-adoption stage and expressly supersedes it.

## Brubru User Implications

- **Banks, investment firms, insurance and payment institutions:** the CDD RTS and group-wide requirements RTS will become the operational baseline for policy manuals well before 10 July 2027; start gap-analysing current CDD documentation now against the closed-consultation drafts
- **Cross-border groups with subsidiaries/branches in 6+ Member States:** check exposure to the 2027 direct-supervision selection exercise; the provisional eligible-entity list lands end-September 2026
- **Compliance and legal teams:** legacy EBA AML/CFT Guidelines remain valid under AMLAR Art. 54 until AMLA formally supersedes them -- do not assume a gap exists just because AMLA has not yet finalised an instrument
- **FIUs and law-enforcement-adjacent functions:** the finalised FIU-to-FIU and FIU-to-EPPO ITS change the format of cross-border reporting; align internal SAR/STR export pipelines
- **CASPs, real estate, luxury goods, football, and other non-financial obliged entities:** AMLA has explicitly flagged capturing non-financial-sector views in the CDD consultation -- monitor for sector-specific carve-outs or thresholds in the final RTS
- **Policy professionals:** track AMLA's Single Programming Document 2026-2028 for the forward instrument calendar; each new consultation opening is itself a lobbying and comment-submission window
