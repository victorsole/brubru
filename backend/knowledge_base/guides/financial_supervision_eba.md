# EU Financial Supervision: ESAs, MiCA, and DORA

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU financial supervision -- ESAs, MiCA crypto-asset oversight, DORA digital resilience
- Responsible Commissioner: Maria Luís Albuquerque (Financial Services, Portugal)
- EP lead committee: ECON (Economic and Monetary Affairs)
- ESAs: EBA (Paris, banking), EIOPA (Frankfurt, insurance/pensions), ESMA (Paris, securities/markets)
- EBA founding regulation: Regulation (EU) 1093/2010
- ESA Review: Regulation (EU) 2019/2175 -- enhanced governance, supervisory convergence, direct supervision powers
- MiCA: Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 -- Markets in Crypto-Assets (ARTs, EMTs, other crypto-assets)
- MiCA significance thresholds: >10M holders, >EUR 5B market cap, >2.5M transactions/day
- MiCA: EBA directly supervises significant ART/EMT issuers; ESMA convergence role for CASPs
- DORA: Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 -- Digital Operational Resilience Act (ICT risk management for financial sector)
- DORA CTPP oversight: ESAs designate Critical Third-Party Providers via Joint Committee
- Regulatory tools: RTS (binding), ITS (binding), Guidelines (comply-or-explain), Q&As, Opinions
- Joint Committee: Cross-sectoral coordination -- consumer protection, AML, financial conglomerates
- CASP own funds: EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000 depending on services

Guide for European Supervisory Authority staff covering prudential supervision, MiCA crypto-asset oversight, DORA digital operational resilience, and supervisory convergence tools.

## European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs)

### ESA Mandates

| Authority | Acronym | Location | Sector | Founding Regulation |
|-----------|---------|----------|--------|---------------------|
| European Banking Authority | EBA | Paris | Banking, payments, e-money, crypto-assets (MiCA) | Regulation (EU) 1093/2010 |
| European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority | EIOPA | Frankfurt | Insurance, reinsurance, occupational pensions | Regulation (EU) 1094/2010 |
| European Securities and Markets Authority | ESMA | Paris | Securities, markets, collective investment, crypto-asset trading platforms (MiCA) | Regulation (EU) 1095/2010 |

### Governance Structure

| Body | Composition | Decisions |
|------|------------|-----------|
| **Board of Supervisors** | Chairperson + heads of all national competent authorities (NCAs) | Guidelines, recommendations, technical standards, peer reviews |
| **Management Board** | Chairperson + 6 BoS members (rotating) + Executive Director | Work programme, budget, appointments |
| **Chairperson** | Independent, appointed by Council (confirmed by EP) | External representation, Board agenda |
| **Executive Director** | Appointed by Board of Supervisors | Day-to-day management |
| **Joint Committee** | Chairs of all 3 ESAs + Secretariat | Cross-sectoral coordination, consumer protection, AML |

### ESA Review (Regulation (EU) 2019/2175)

Key changes from the 2019 review:
- Enhanced governance (independence requirements for Board members)
- Strengthened supervisory convergence (binding mediation, peer reviews)
- Direct supervision powers for certain entities
- Sustainable finance integration in mandates

## MiCA Supervision (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114)

### Scope

MiCA regulates three categories of crypto-assets:
1. **Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs)**: Crypto-assets pegged to multiple assets/fiat currencies
2. **E-Money Tokens (EMTs)**: Crypto-assets pegged to a single fiat currency
3. **Other crypto-assets**: Utility tokens, other non-ART/non-EMT tokens

### Supervisory Architecture

| Entity Type | Non-Significant | Significant |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------|
| **ART issuers** | Home NCA | EBA (direct supervision) |
| **EMT issuers** | Home NCA | EBA (direct supervision) |
| **CASPs** (Crypto-Asset Service Providers) | Home NCA (ESMA convergence role) | Home NCA (enhanced supervision) |

### Significance Assessment

EBA assesses whether an ART/EMT issuer is **significant** based on:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Customer base | > 10 million holders |
| Value of tokens issued / market capitalisation | > EUR 5 billion |
| Number/value of transactions | > 2.5 million transactions/day or > EUR 500 million/day |
| Significance of cross-border activities | Issuer/holders in > 7 Member States |
| Interconnectedness with financial system | Linkage to systemically important financial institutions |
| Classification by issuer | Issuer itself requests classification as significant |

### EBA Direct Supervision of Significant Issuers

| Power | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **On-site inspections** | Inspect premises, systems, records (with NCA assistance) |
| **Off-site monitoring** | Continuous review of own funds, reserve assets, governance |
| **Supervisory measures** | Require corrective actions, restrict activities, withdraw authorisation |
| **Information requests** | Binding requests for information from issuer, group entities, outsourcing providers |
| **Supervisory colleges** | Chair and coordinate colleges for cross-border issuers |

### Reserve Management Requirements

For ARTs and EMTs, issuers must:
- Maintain reserve assets equal to the value of tokens in circulation
- Invest reserves in low-risk, liquid instruments
- Segregate reserves from issuer's own assets
- Submit reserve reports to the competent authority
- Undergo independent audits of reserve composition

### Own Funds Requirements

| Entity | Minimum Own Funds |
|--------|-------------------|
| ART issuer (non-significant) | Higher of EUR 350,000 or 2% of average reserve assets |
| ART issuer (significant) | Higher of EUR 350,000 or 3% of average reserve assets |
| EMT issuer | As per E-Money Directive (EUR 350,000 minimum) |
| CASP | EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000 depending on services |

## DORA: Critical Third-Party Provider (CTPP) Oversight

### DORA Framework (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554)

DORA establishes uniform requirements for ICT risk management in the financial sector:
- ICT risk management framework
- ICT-related incident reporting
- Digital operational resilience testing
- ICT third-party risk management
- Critical Third-Party Provider (CTPP) oversight framework

### DORA Implementing Technical Standards (ITS) Update (March 2026)

**Regulation (EU) 2026/722** (CELEX: 32026R0722) -- Commission Implementing Regulation of 26 March 2026 amending the implementing technical standards laid down in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/3172 as regards the implementation and use of templates for ICT-related incident reporting under DORA. This updates the incident reporting templates that financial entities must use when reporting major ICT-related incidents to their competent authorities.

### CTPP Designation

The ESAs (through the Joint Committee) designate CTPPs based on:

| Criterion | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Systemic impact** | Degree of substitutability, number and type of financial entities relying on the provider |
| **Concentration risk** | Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) measuring market concentration |
| **Dependencies** | Criticality of ICT services provided (core banking, payment processing, cloud infrastructure) |
| **Cross-border dimension** | Services provided to financial entities in multiple Member States |

### CTPP Designation Process

1. ESAs collect information from financial entities on their ICT third-party arrangements
2. Joint Committee analyses concentration risk and systemic importance
3. **Designation decision** published by the relevant ESA (Lead Overseer)
4. CTPP may challenge the designation
5. Designated CTPP subject to ongoing oversight framework

### Lead Overseer Powers

| Power | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Information requests** | Demand any information, data, documentation from the CTPP |
| **General investigations** | Examine books, records, interview personnel |
| **On-site inspections** | Access CTPP premises (including data centres in third countries) |
| **Recommendations** | Issue recommendations on ICT risk, security, and resilience |
| **Follow-up** | Assess CTPP's response; report non-compliance to competent authorities |
| **Penalties** | Request financial entities to suspend/terminate arrangements if recommendations not followed |

### Oversight Fees

CTPPs pay oversight fees to the Lead Overseer ESA, calculated based on:
- Turnover from ICT services to EU financial entities
- Number and type of financial entities served
- Annual basis, notified by the ESA

## Supervisory Convergence Tools

### Regulatory Products

| Tool | Binding Force | Process |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS)** | Binding (once adopted by Commission) | ESA drafts, Commission adopts via delegated act |
| **Implementing Technical Standards (ITS)** | Binding (once adopted by Commission) | ESA drafts, Commission adopts via implementing act |
| **Guidelines** | Comply-or-explain basis | ESA consults, publishes; NCAs must state compliance |
| **Recommendations** | Non-binding | ESA addresses to NCAs or financial institutions |
| **Q&As** | Clarificatory (non-binding) | ESA responds to stakeholder questions |
| **Opinions** | Advisory | ESA issues on own initiative or at EP/Council/Commission request |

### Peer Reviews

ESAs conduct peer reviews of NCA supervisory practices:
1. Reference period and scope defined
2. Self-assessment questionnaires to NCAs
3. On-site visits to selected NCAs
4. Peer review report with findings and recommendations
5. Follow-up assessment (typically 2 years later)

### Supervisory Colleges

For cross-border financial institutions:

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Chair** | Consolidating supervisor (home NCA) |
| **Members** | Host NCAs, ESA (observer or participant), ECB (for SSM banks) |
| **Function** | Information sharing, joint risk assessment, coordinated supervision |
| **Binding mediation** | ESA can mediate disputes between NCAs; binding decisions possible |

## On-Site Inspection Methodology

### Preparation Phase

1. **Scoping**: Define inspection objectives, risk areas, time frame
2. **Documentation review**: Analyse off-site monitoring data, previous findings
3. **Inspection mandate**: Formal authorisation (for EBA direct supervision) or delegation letter (for NCA inspections)
4. **Team composition**: Lead inspector, sector specialists, IT auditors

### Execution Phase

| Activity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Opening meeting** | Present scope, timeline, document requests |
| **Document review** | Policies, procedures, board minutes, risk reports, incident logs |
| **Interviews** | Key function holders (CRO, CISO, CTO, Head of Compliance, CEO/CFO) |
| **System walkthroughs** | Examine IT infrastructure, controls, monitoring tools |
| **Testing** | Sample-based verification of compliance (controls testing, transaction testing) |
| **Closing meeting** | Preliminary findings, timeline for formal report |

### Reporting

1. **Draft inspection report** sent to supervised entity
2. Entity provides **written comments** (typically 2-4 weeks)
3. **Final inspection report** with findings and recommendations
4. **Follow-up plan**: Remediation actions, deadlines, escalation if not addressed

## Off-Site Monitoring and Early Warning

### Key Monitoring Indicators

| Category | Indicators |
|----------|-----------|
| **Capital** | CET1 ratio, leverage ratio, MREL compliance, own funds composition |
| **Liquidity** | LCR, NSFR, liquidity buffer composition, funding concentration |
| **Asset quality** | NPL ratio, Stage 2/3 migration, forbearance ratio |
| **Profitability** | ROE, cost-to-income ratio, NIM, fee income share |
| **Operational** | Incident reports, outsourcing dependency, cyber incidents |
| **Governance** | Board composition, fit and proper assessments, internal audit findings |

### Early Warning Triggers

| Severity | Response |
|----------|----------|
| **Watch** | Enhanced monitoring frequency, additional data requests |
| **Alert** | Supervisory dialogue, request for recovery plan update |
| **Escalation** | On-site inspection, formal supervisory measures |
| **Crisis** | Activation of resolution framework, communication with SRB |

## Joint Committee and Cross-Sectoral Coordination

### Joint Committee Structure

| Body | Function |
|------|----------|
| **Joint Committee** | Cross-sectoral consistency, consumer protection, AML, financial conglomerates |
| **Sub-Committee on Risk** | Cross-sectoral risk analysis, Joint Risk Report |
| **Sub-Committee on Consumer Protection** | Cross-sectoral consumer trends, complaints analysis |
| **Sub-Committee on AML** | Anti-money laundering coordination (until AMLA becomes operational) |

### Joint Oversight Venture (JOV)

For DORA CTPP oversight, the Joint Committee may establish a JOV with staff from multiple ESAs to coordinate oversight of a CTPP serving entities across banking, insurance, and securities sectors.
