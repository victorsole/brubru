# European Commission — Who Does What (Departments, Services & Executive Agencies)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What this is**: the master map of **which Commission body does which policy**, verified body-by-body against each one's own page (31 May 2026). The Commission organises into **Directorates-General (DGs)**, a small number of **service departments**, and **executive agencies** (plus interinstitutional **offices**).
- **Important**: bodies people assume are "services" are in fact **Directorates-General** — including **OLAF, HERA, ECHO, Eurostat, Translation (DGT), Interpretation (SCIC), Human Resources (HR), the Joint Research Centre (JRC), Communication (COMM) and Digital Services (DIGIT)**. The Commission lists them all among its DGs. The **only** genuine "service departments" are **DPO, EC Library, FPI and the Historical Archives Service**.
- **The College (von der Leyen II, 2024–2029)**: President Ursula von der Leyen + 6 Executive Vice-Presidents (incl. the High Representative) + 20 Commissioners = **27 members**. They decide **collegially**. Verified portfolios are listed below.
- **Name change to know**: DG TRADE is now the **Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security**.
- **Machine-readable key**: each body has a **corporate-body authority code** (`http://publications.europa.eu/resource/authority/corporate-body/{CODE}`) used to tag its news, publications and acts. See `eu_commission_transparency_and_college_agenda.md`.
- **Source**: each body's `commission.europa.eu/about/departments-and-executive-agencies/{slug}_en` page. Engineering reference: `docs/api/eu_commission_data_access.md`.

## How to read this
A **Directorate-General (DG)** is a department headed by a Director-General. Most are **policy DGs** (they own a body of law and propose initiatives); some are **functional/horizontal DGs** (translation, interpretation, HR, IT, statistics, science, communication, audit, legal, anti-fraud, health-emergency). A few DG-level bodies self-describe as a "department" or "service" (Secretariat-General, Legal Service, Internal Audit Service) but the Commission lists them among its DGs. A **service department** is a small specialised body (data protection, library, foreign-policy instruments, archives). An **executive agency** only runs funding programmes for parent DGs — it makes no policy.

## Offices & interinstitutional bodies
- **OP (Publications Office)** — publishes the Official Journal; runs EUR-Lex, the EU Vocabularies and WhoisWho.
- **OIB / OIL** — Offices for Infrastructure & Logistics (Brussels / Luxembourg).
- **EPSO** — the European Personnel Selection Office: recruitment for all EU institutions.
- **ESA** — the **European School of Administration**: training for staff of all EU institutions and agencies.

## Common confusions (so Brubru gets it right)
- **OLAF, HERA, ECHO, Eurostat, DGT, SCIC, HR, JRC are Directorates-General**, not "services". The only service departments are DPO, EC Library, FPI, HAS.
- **DG ≠ executive agency.** A DG makes policy; an executive agency only disburses funding. "Who funds Horizon Cluster 5?" → CINEA; "who sets climate policy?" → DG CLIMA.
- **Connect vs DIGIT**: DG CNECT makes **digital policy for Europe**; DG DIGIT runs the **Commission's own IT**.
- **Decentralised EU agencies** (EMA, EFSA, EEA, ENISA, ACER, FRA, EIGE, AMLA, Europol, Frontex…) are **separate legal bodies**, not Commission departments.
- **Trade is EU-exclusive**; the DG is now **Trade and Economic Security**.
