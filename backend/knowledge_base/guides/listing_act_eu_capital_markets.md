# EU Listing Act: prospectus, MAR and MiFID II amendments

## QUICK FACTS

- **Package name:** EU Listing Act -- part of the Capital Markets Union (CMU) agenda, now folded into the successor Savings and Investment Union (SIU) framework
- **Three legal instruments**, all adopted by the European Parliament and Council on **23 October 2024**, published in the Official Journal on **14 November 2024**, entered into force **4 December 2024** (20 days after publication):
  1. **Regulation (EU) 2024/2809** (CELEX 32024R2809) -- amends the Prospectus Regulation (EU) 2017/1129, the Market Abuse Regulation (EU) 596/2014 and MiFIR (EU) 600/2014
  2. **Directive (EU) 2024/2810** (CELEX 32024L2810) -- new stand-alone directive on multiple-vote-share structures for companies seeking admission to trading on a multilateral trading facility (MTF), including SME growth markets
  3. **Directive (EU) 2024/2811** (CELEX 32024L2811) -- amends MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU) and **repeals the 2001 Listing Directive (2001/34/EC)**, folding its residual provisions into MiFID II
- **Origin:** Commission proposal COM(2022) 762, published 7 December 2022, as part of the Capital Markets Union initiative to broaden market-based financing for EU companies
- **Legal base:** Article 114(1) TFEU (internal market harmonisation) -- the same base as the Prospectus Regulation, MAR and MiFID II/MiFIR themselves
- **Aim:** reduce the cost and complexity of going public and staying public in the EU, simplify secondary listings for already-listed issuers, widen the SME growth market pipeline, and let founders retain control after an IPO via multiple-vote shares -- responding to the long-running decline in EU listings and IPO volumes relative to the US and UK
- **Phased application** (three key dates):
  - **4 December 2024** -- first tranche in force immediately: expanded managers'-transactions exemptions, simplified buy-back reporting, new prospectus-exemption formats, relaxed Universal Registration Document (URD) pre-approval
  - **5 March 2026** -- EU Follow-on prospectus replaces the simplified disclosure regime for secondary issuances; EU Growth issuance prospectus replaces the EU Growth prospectus
  - **5-6 June 2026** -- remaining Regulation provisions apply (general de minimis threshold, standardised prospectus format/sequence/page limits, inside-information delay exemptions); Directive (EU) 2024/2811 transposition deadline **5 June 2026**, applicable from **6 June 2026** (includes the MiFID II research-unbundling relief)
  - **4 December 2026** -- Directive (EU) 2024/2810) (multiple-vote shares) transposition deadline
- **Prospectus headline changes:** exemption threshold raised to EUR 12 million (EUR 5 million optional Member State derogation, replacing the EUR 1 million floor); dilution exemption raised from 20% to 30% of already-admitted shares; page limits introduced (equity prospectus circa 300 pages, summary and other formats shorter); language flexibility to use "a language customary in the sphere of international finance" alongside the national language
- **MAR headline changes:** intermediate steps of a protracted process no longer trigger immediate ad hoc disclosure by default; market-sounding regime simplified; insider-list content simplified (particularly for SMEs); share buy-back reporting simplified; sanctions made more proportionate to company size
- **MiFID II headline change:** research-unbundling relief -- investment firms may again bundle payment for investment research with execution commissions under conditions, reversing part of the original MiFID II unbundling mandate, to boost SME research coverage
- **Multiple-vote shares:** Directive (EU) 2024/2810 lets Member States permit companies to keep multiple-vote share structures when listing on an MTF (including SME growth markets); Member States may cap the voting ratio and must build in safeguards (sunset clauses, qualified-majority protections for ordinary shareholders on key decisions)
- **Regulator:** ESMA develops the implementing technical standards, regulatory technical standards and guidelines (simplified insider-list formats, issuer-sponsored research standards, prospectus supplement guidelines, SME growth market registration criteria)
- Cross-link with the **Savings and Investment Union (SIU)**: the SIU Communication (March 2025) explicitly builds on the Listing Act as its "listing rules simplification" foundation for the next wave of capital-markets reform

## Context

The EU Listing Act responds to a persistent structural problem: EU public equity markets have shrunk in relative terms against the US and UK, and fewer EU companies choose to go public, with growth companies increasingly preferring US listings or staying private longer (funded by venture capital and private equity). The Commission's December 2022 proposal, COM(2022) 762, diagnosed three cost points along the listing lifecycle: pre-IPO (fragmented and costly investment research, discouraging analyst coverage of small caps), IPO (a heavy, slow, expensive prospectus-drafting and approval process), and post-IPO (uncertainty over what counts as "inside information" requiring immediate disclosure, and disproportionate ongoing compliance burden for smaller issuers).

The package sits inside the Capital Markets Union (CMU) agenda that began with the 2015 Hill Action Plan and continued through the 2020 CMU Action Plan. It is one of the few CMU-era files to reach full adoption; most CMU initiatives (harmonised insolvency rules, a genuine 28th-regime company law, deeper pension integration) remain unfinished business, which is precisely why the Commission relaunched the agenda as the Savings and Investment Union (SIU) in March 2025 (see `savings_and_investment_union`). The SIU Communication names "listing rules simplification -- building on the Listing Act package adopted October 2024" explicitly as one of its Pillar 2 building blocks, so the Listing Act is best read as the completed first phase of a still-running reform arc, not a standalone one-off.

## The three instruments

### Regulation (EU) 2024/2809 -- Prospectus, MAR and MiFIR amendments

This is a directly-applicable regulation (no national transposition needed) that amends three existing regulations in a single legislative act:

- **Prospectus Regulation (EU) 2017/1129:** raises the offer-size exemption threshold (companies raising below the threshold need no prospectus at all), introduces the standardised **EU Follow-on prospectus** for issuers already listed for at least 18 months seeking further capital (shorter, templated, faster approval), replaces the **EU Growth prospectus** with the **EU Growth issuance prospectus** for SME growth market issuers, imposes page limits and a standardised sequence/format on all prospectus types, raises the dilution exemption from 20% to 30% of shares already admitted to trading, and permits prospectuses in a language customary in international finance (in practice, English) alongside the national-language requirement.
- **Market Abuse Regulation (EU) 596/2014 (MAR):** narrows the default trigger for immediate ad hoc disclosure of inside information during protracted, multi-step processes (a long-standing issuer complaint -- M&A negotiations and similar processes previously risked forcing premature disclosure at each intermediate step); simplifies the market-sounding regime; simplifies insider-list content (especially for issuers on SME growth markets); simplifies share buy-back programme reporting; and scales sanctions more proportionately to issuer size, with SME-specific alternatives to flat fines.
- **MiFIR (EU) 600/2014:** consequential amendments aligning transparency and reporting obligations with the revised MAR and prospectus regime.

### Directive (EU) 2024/2810 -- Multiple-vote-share structures

A new stand-alone directive (not an amendment to an existing one) permitting companies to retain multiple-vote share structures when listing on a multilateral trading facility (MTF), including SME growth markets, provided the Member State of incorporation allows it. Rationale: founders and early investors of high-growth companies frequently avoid EU listings, or list in the US, specifically to preserve dual-class or multiple-vote control structures that many EU national company laws restrict or prohibit on regulated markets. The directive harmonises the previously fragmented national landscape by:

- allowing Member States to set a maximum voting ratio for multiple-vote shares relative to ordinary shares (several Member States, including France, have already legislated ratios such as 25:1 for MTF listings)
- requiring that qualified-majority decisions at general meetings be calculated with additional safeguards (based on votes cast and share capital/total shares represented, or by separate class votes) so multiple-vote holders cannot unilaterally push through fundamental changes
- requiring proportionality and transparency safeguards, including sunset clauses that can extinguish enhanced voting rights on a transfer to third parties, on the occurrence of specified trigger events, or after a fixed period

Transposition deadline: **4 December 2026**.

### Directive (EU) 2024/2811 -- MiFID II amendments and repeal of the Listing Directive

Amends MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU) and formally repeals **Directive 2001/34/EC (the Listing Directive)**, the older EU framework on the admission of securities to official listing, folding its remaining live provisions into MiFID II so issuers and trading venues no longer have to cross-reference two overlapping directives. Substantive MiFID II changes:

- **Research-unbundling relief:** investment firms may again combine (bundle) payment for third-party investment research with execution commissions, subject to conditions -- a partial reversal of the original MiFID II Article 24 unbundling mandate, which the market widely blamed for a collapse in sell-side research coverage of small and mid-cap issuers after 2018 (see `mifid_ii_directive` for the original inducements/unbundling regime)
- Establishes a framework for a European code of conduct on issuer-sponsored research, addressing conflict-of-interest concerns that unbundling was originally meant to solve
- Consequential SME growth market provisions restructured into MiFID II Article 33 (SME growth markets), aligning admission and ongoing-disclosure criteria with the revised Prospectus Regulation and MAR

Transposition deadline: **5 June 2026**; national measures apply from **6 June 2026**.

## Key changes in detail

### Prospectus exemptions and thresholds

| Item | Before Listing Act | After Listing Act |
|---|---|---|
| Offer-size exemption floor | EUR 1 million (mandatory floor, Member States could set exemption up to EUR 8 million) | EUR 12 million exemption threshold (EUR 5 million optional Member State derogation); EUR 1 million floor removed |
| Dilution exemption (secondary issuances) | 20% of shares already admitted to trading over 12 months | 30% of shares already admitted to trading over 12 months |
| Secondary-issuance disclosure | Simplified prospectus regime / recovery prospectus | EU Follow-on prospectus (from 5 March 2026) |
| SME/growth-issuer disclosure | EU Growth prospectus | EU Growth issuance prospectus (from 5 March 2026) |
| Prospectus length | No hard page cap | Standardised page limits (equity prospectus circa 300 pages) |
| Prospectus language | National language (with some Member State flexibility) | National language OR a language customary in international finance |

### MAR simplification highlights

- Protracted-process intermediate steps: no automatic immediate-disclosure obligation at each stage (issuer may delay until the process concludes or a defined milestone is reached, subject to confidentiality safeguards)
- Simplified, shorter insider lists, with lighter-touch requirements for SME growth market issuers
- Simplified share buy-back and stabilisation reporting
- Sanctions scaled to company size, with SME-proportionate alternatives to flat fines

### MiFID II research-unbundling relief

Reverses part of the original 2018 MiFID II inducements regime specifically for research: firms may bundle research payments with execution fees again under defined conditions, aimed at reviving small/mid-cap equity research coverage that contracted sharply after unbundling took effect. Applicable from 6 June 2026. Cross-reference `mifid_ii_directive` Article 24(7)-(9) for the baseline inducements regime this relief modifies.

## Lineage

The Listing Act is the completed legislative output of the Commission's first-wave Capital Markets Union (CMU) push under the von der Leyen I Commission. It follows and interacts with the **Prospectus Regulation (EU) 2017/1129** (which it amends rather than replaces), **MAR (EU) 596/2014**, **MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU)** and **MiFIR (EU) 600/2014**, and it formally closes out the **2001 Listing Directive (2001/34/EC)**, one of the oldest still-live EU capital-markets instruments.

Under the von der Leyen II Commission, the Listing Act is explicitly cited as the foundation for the **Savings and Investment Union (SIU)** Pillar 2 "listing rules simplification" work-stream (Communication of 19 March 2025, Commissioner Maria Luis Albuquerque). Where the Listing Act focused on issuer-side and intermediary-side burden reduction, the SIU broadens the frame to retail savings mobilisation and pension-capital integration -- see `savings_and_investment_union` for the successor agenda, and `eu_omnibus_iv_small_midcap` for the parallel, but legally separate, corporate-simplification track for small mid-cap companies.
