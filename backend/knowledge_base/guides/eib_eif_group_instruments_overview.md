# EIB Group Instruments: EIB, EIF, Advisory Services, ELENA

## QUICK FACTS
- **EIB Group = two legal entities**: the **European Investment Bank (EIB)**, the lending arm, plus the **European Investment Fund (EIF)**, the EIB Group subsidiary specialising in SME/mid-cap **equity investment and guarantees**. The EIF is not a separate bank -- it is part of the EIB Group and reports through the same governance chain.
- **Scale (2025 results)**: EIB own lending signatures **EUR 86 billion**; **EIB Group total financing (EIB + EIF, own resources + third-party resources) EUR 100 billion** -- a record year. The Group's 2025-2027 Operational Plan target is EUR 95 billion/year, exceeded in 2025.
- **Legal basis**: Articles 308-309 TFEU + the **Statute of the EIB (Protocol No. 5** annexed to the Treaties). The EIB is owned by the **27 EU Member States** (shareholders in proportion to GDP at accession), not by the European Commission -- it is a distinct legal person under EU primary law.
- **President: Nadia Calviño**, EIB Group President since **1 January 2024** (former Spanish Minister for Economy). She chairs both the EIB Management Committee and the EIF Board of Directors. Hedge: verify against `eib.org/en/about/governance-and-structure/statutory-bodies/management-committee` if this guide is read after mid-2027 (her term structure should be checked for renewal).
- **EIB as the largest InvestEU implementing partner**: the EIB Group (including the EIF) has access to roughly **75% of the EUR 26.2 billion EU budget guarantee** backing InvestEU, and is the programme's main implementing partner across all four policy windows (sustainable infrastructure, research/innovation/digitalisation, SMEs, social investment/skills).
- **EIB is also the largest EFSD+ (European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus) implementing partner** -- the EU's principal external-investment guarantee instrument for Africa and the EU Neighbourhood, delivered under the Team Europe / Global Gateway umbrella alongside other European development finance institutions.
- **Advisory hubs** (technical assistance, not financing): **InvestEU Advisory Hub** (project pipeline support across all InvestEU windows); **ELENA** (European Local Energy Assistance -- grants up to 90% of project development costs for energy efficiency, renewables and sustainable transport, projects normally >EUR 30 million, EUR 374 million distributed across 206 projects by end 2025); **JASPERS** (Joint Assistance to Support Projects in European Regions -- EIB's largest advisory programme, co-funded with the Commission, free technical support for cohesion-fund infrastructure projects, over 2,400 assignments and EUR 355 billion mobilised across 36 countries in 20 years); **fi-compass** (joint EIB-Commission advisory platform for managing authorities setting up financial instruments under shared-management funds -- ERDF, Cohesion Fund, EAFRD).
- **TechEU programme** (launched 2025): flagship EIB Group initiative to mobilise **EUR 250 billion by 2027** for start-ups, scale-ups and innovative companies, backed by a EUR 70 billion EIB Group commitment over 2025-2027. Verticals include **Cleantech TechEU** (EUR 18 billion first wave), **BioTechEU** (EUR 10 billion, 2026-27), **Housing TechEU** (EUR 400 million), plus digitalisation/AI, defence and critical raw materials strands. Delivered via the **TechEU Platform** (portal + "Investment Readiness Checker").
- **European Tech Champions Initiative (ETCI)**: EIF-managed fund-of-funds backing growth-stage VC/PE funds that invest in European tech scale-ups. **ETCI 1** (launched 2023 with France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Belgium, Netherlands): up to EUR 3.9 billion, ~15 funds, targeting EUR 20 billion+ mobilised. **ETCI 2.0** (December 2025): EIB + EIF Boards committed EUR 1.25 billion of own funds, targeting roughly 4x the mobilisation of ETCI 1; the EIF has since launched a EUR 15 billion fund-of-funds wave to back around 100 growth-stage European VC funds.
- **Ukraine**: EIB Group delivered a **record EUR 1.5 billion in 2025** financing (energy, water, heating, transport, healthcare, education, SME support) under the EU's EUR 50 billion Ukraine Facility / Ukraine Investment Framework (capacity ~EUR 9.6 billion: EUR 7.8 billion guarantee cover + EUR 1.8 billion blended finance, targeting up to EUR 40 billion mobilised). Further packages continued into 2026 (e.g. a >EUR 600 million package announced 2026 for urgent reconstruction).
- **Defence-financing pivot (2025)**: EIB Board approved an updated dual-use definition, **removing the previous 50% civilian-revenue threshold** for dual-use projects, and approved a **EUR 17.7 billion** security and defence financing package (November 2025) covering semiconductors, cybersecurity, space infrastructure and military mobility. The EIB's EUR 8 billion Strategic European Security Initiative (SESI) became a permanent, uncapped cross-cutting policy goal. Intermediated financing for defence-supply-chain SMEs was tripled to EUR 3 billion.
- **Legacy instrument**: **EFSI (European Fund for Strategic Investments, the "Juncker Plan")**, 2015-2020, the InvestEU predecessor; also the COVID-19 Pan-European Guarantee Fund (EGF), a temporary EIB Group vehicle to backstop SME liquidity during the pandemic, now wound down.
- **Key distinction from the Commission**: the EIB is the EU's "house bank" but a **separate legal entity governed by its own Statute**, not a Commission department or agency. It does not initiate legislation, is not part of the College, and its Board of Governors is composed of Member State finance ministers (not Commissioners), though the Commission holds a seat as observer/participant in specific governance and co-funds several joint instruments (JASPERS, fi-compass, ELENA, InvestEU).

## EIB vs EIF: the split

| | EIB | EIF |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Lending arm: loans, guarantees, equity, blending, at scale, direct to sovereigns, banks, corporates, large infrastructure | Equity + guarantees intermediary for SMEs and mid-caps, deployed through funds and financial intermediaries, not directly to end-borrowers |
| Typical ticket | Tens of millions to billions (own-resource loans, project finance, framework loans) | Smaller intermediated tickets via VC/PE funds, guarantee facilities, or fund-of-funds |
| Ownership | Owned directly by the 27 Member States | Owned by the EIB (majority shareholder), the European Commission, and public/private financial institutions |
| Signature products | Investment loans, framework loans, project bonds, EFSD+ guarantees, Ukraine Facility guarantees | ETCI, EIC Fund advisory (as EIB Group investment advisor), COSME/InnovFin-successor guarantee products, venture debt, business angel co-investment |
| Governance | Board of Governors (Member State finance ministers) + Board of Directors + Management Committee | EIF Board of Directors (chaired by the EIB Group President) |

Rule of thumb for Brubru disambiguation: if the instrument is a **direct large loan or a guarantee to a bank/sovereign for infrastructure**, it is EIB. If it is **equity into a fund, a venture-debt facility, or an SME guarantee scheme delivered through intermediaries**, it is EIF. The EIC Fund (see `eic_fund_equity_investment.md`) sits at the seam: it is a Luxembourg SICAV-RAIF for which the **EIB Group** (not the EIF specifically) acts as investment advisor of record.

## Governance

- **Legal basis**: Articles 308 and 309 TFEU, plus the **Statute of the EIB (Protocol No. 5)** annexed to the TEU/TFEU. This makes the EIB a Treaty-level institution with its own legal personality, distinct from the Commission, the Council or any EU agency.
- **Ownership**: the **27 Member States** are the shareholders, holding capital in proportion to their economic weight at the time of accession.
- **Board of Governors**: normally the Ministers of Finance/Economy of the 27 Member States. Sets general lending policy, approves the balance sheet, decides capital increases, and appoints the Board of Directors and Management Committee.
- **Board of Directors**: one director nominated by each Member State plus one by the Commission (with alternates), responsible for lending, borrowing and guarantee decisions.
- **Management Committee**: the permanent collegiate executive body, chaired by the EIB Group President, running day-to-day operations.
- **Audit Committee**: independent body verifying that operations comply with the Statute and best banking practice.
- **EIF governance**: the EIF Board of Directors is chaired by the EIB Group President (Calviño chairs both bodies), with the EIB as majority shareholder alongside the Commission and other financial institutions.

## President and strategy

**Nadia Calviño** became EIB Group President on **1 January 2024**, succeeding Werner Hoyer. She previously served as Spain's Minister for Economy and Digital Transformation and as First Deputy Prime Minister. Under her presidency the EIB Group has pursued a strategic pivot toward:
- **Competitiveness and technological sovereignty** (TechEU, ETCI, AI gigafactory financing with the Commission).
- **Security and defence** (dual-use eligibility reform, SESI made permanent, record 2025 defence financing).
- **Continued climate leadership** (the EIB remains one of the world's largest providers of climate finance, alongside the new priorities rather than instead of them).
- **External action** (Ukraine, EFSD+/Global Gateway, enlargement-country pre-accession financing).

The Group's rolling **Operational Plan** (currently covering 2025-2027) sets the annual signature target (EUR 95 billion for 2025, exceeded at EUR 100 billion) and allocates it across these strategic priorities.

## EIB Group as InvestEU and EFSD+ implementing partner

- **InvestEU**: the EIB Group (EIB + EIF together) is described by the EIB itself as "the main implementing partner" of InvestEU, with access to roughly **75% of the EUR 26.2 billion EU budget guarantee**. It deploys across all four InvestEU policy windows: sustainable infrastructure; research, innovation and digitalisation; SMEs; and social investment and skills. See `investeu_programme.md` for the full InvestEU architecture (windows, other implementing partners, guarantee mechanics).
- **EFSD+**: under the EU's external-action financial architecture (NDICI-Global Europe umbrella), the EIB is the **largest implementing partner** of the EFSD+ Guarantee, alongside other Team Europe development finance institutions, deploying guarantees to de-risk private investment in Africa, the EU Neighbourhood and enlargement countries. See `efsd_plus_external_action_guarantee.md` for the full guarantee architecture and the Global Gateway link (`global_gateway_strategy.md`).

## Advisory services

Advisory work is technical assistance and project-preparation support, distinct from financing itself. The four flagship hubs:

1. **InvestEU Advisory Hub** -- single-entry-point advisory support for public and private project promoters seeking InvestEU-backed financing, covering all four policy windows.
2. **ELENA (European Local Energy Assistance)** -- joint EIB/Commission/InvestEU initiative providing grants covering up to 90% of project-development costs for energy efficiency (buildings, industrial facilities, public lighting, district heating, smart grids), residential renovation, and sustainable transport (alternative-fuel fleets, urban mobility). Eligible recipients: public entities and qualifying private promoters; investment value normally above EUR 30 million and non-profit in nature. By end 2025: EUR 374 million distributed across 206 projects, supporting over EUR 12.7 billion in total investment.
3. **JASPERS (Joint Assistance to Support Projects in European Regions)** -- launched 2005, co-funded by the Commission, the EIB's largest advisory programme. Free technical advice and training to national, regional and local authorities preparing cohesion-fund and Just Transition Fund projects across transport, energy, water/environment, climate, urban development, circular economy and capacity building. Over 2,400 assignments and EUR 355 billion mobilised across 36 countries over 20 years.
4. **fi-compass** -- joint EIB/Commission advisory platform helping managing authorities, financial intermediaries and other stakeholders design, set up and implement financial instruments (loans, guarantees, equity, risk-sharing) under EU shared-management funds, notably the ERDF/Cohesion Fund and the EAFRD.

## TechEU, ETCI and scale-up financing

**TechEU** is the EIB Group's response to the European "scale-up gap" -- the well-documented shortfall in growth-stage capital that pushes European tech companies to US markets for late-stage rounds and IPOs. Structure:
- **EUR 250 billion mobilisation target by 2027**, underpinned by a **EUR 70 billion EIB Group own commitment** over 2025-2027.
- Sector verticals: **Cleantech TechEU** (first wave, EUR 18 billion target), **BioTechEU** (EUR 10 billion, 2026-27, launched jointly with the Commission for the biotech sector), **Housing TechEU** (EUR 400 million, advanced materials and engineering for affordable/sustainable housing, targeting mid-caps and large companies), plus digitalisation/AI, defence, space tech and critical raw materials strands.
- Delivery mechanism: the **TechEU Platform**, a one-stop-shop portal including an "Investment Readiness Checker" that lets start-ups and scale-ups in cleantech, life sciences and digital technologies identify which EIB Group product (equity, equity-type, debt, guarantee, advisory) fits their stage.

**ETCI (European Tech Champions Initiative)** is the EIF-managed fund-of-funds complement to TechEU, targeting the growth-stage VC/PE fund layer rather than direct company investment:
- **ETCI 1** (2023, with France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Belgium, Netherlands): up to EUR 3.9 billion, ~15 growth-stage funds, targeting EUR 20 billion+ mobilised.
- **ETCI 2.0** (December 2025): EIB + EIF Boards committed EUR 1.25 billion of own funds; targeted at roughly 4x the mobilisation of ETCI 1. A subsequent EIF fund-of-funds wave of EUR 15 billion aims to back around 100 growth-stage European VC funds.

Distinguish ETCI (fund-of-funds, invests in VC/PE funds, EIF-managed) from the **EIC Fund** (direct equity into individual companies, EIB Group as investment advisor -- see `eic_fund_equity_investment.md`) and from the **Scaleup Europe Fund** (EUR 5 billion fund-of-funds managed by EQT, anchored by the EIF, later-stage focus -- see `eic_scaleup_europe_fund_2026.md`).

## Ukraine and defence financing

- **Ukraine**: the EIB Group operates under the EU's **EUR 50 billion Ukraine Facility** (2024-2027), with a specific **Ukraine Investment Framework** capacity of roughly **EUR 9.6 billion** (EUR 7.8 billion guarantee cover + EUR 1.8 billion blended finance), targeting up to EUR 40 billion of mobilised investment. In 2025 the Group delivered a **record EUR 1.5 billion** for energy, water/heating, transport, healthcare, education and SME support; further packages (including one exceeding EUR 600 million) followed into 2026 for urgent reconstruction and housing/connectivity/private-sector growth. See `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026.md` for the full recovery-financing picture across EU institutions.
- **Defence**: 2025 marked a structural pivot. The EIB Board approved an updated **dual-use definition**, removing the prior requirement that dual-use projects derive over 50% of revenue from civilian use -- military/police infrastructure with civilian utility is now eligible with no minimum civilian-revenue threshold. A **EUR 17.7 billion** security and defence financing package was approved in November 2025 (semiconductors, cybersecurity, space infrastructure, military mobility). The EIB's **Strategic European Security Initiative (SESI)**, originally an EUR 8 billion time-bound envelope, became a **permanent, uncapped** cross-cutting policy goal. Intermediated liquidity for defence-supply-chain SMEs was tripled to EUR 3 billion (first deal signed with Deutsche Bank). See `european_defence_union.md` for the broader EU defence-financing architecture (EDIP, SAFE, ReArm Europe).

## Legacy instruments

- **EFSI (European Fund for Strategic Investments)**, the "Juncker Plan", 2015-2020: the EIB Group vehicle whose success and institutional lessons led directly to the design of InvestEU as its permanent successor.
- **COVID-19 Pan-European Guarantee Fund (EGF)**: a temporary EIB Group vehicle (2020-2021) that backstopped SME liquidity across the EU during the pandemic; now wound down, its risk-sharing design informed later EIF guarantee products.

## How the EIB differs from the Commission

- The EIB is a **Treaty-level institution with its own legal personality** (Protocol No. 5), not a Commission Directorate-General, agency or executive agency.
- It is **owned by the Member States**, not governed by the College of Commissioners; its Board of Governors is composed of national finance ministers.
- It does **not have legislative initiative** and plays no role in the ordinary legislative procedure; its instruments (loans, guarantees, equity, advisory) are financial, not regulatory.
- It **partners with the Commission** on numerous joint instruments (InvestEU, EFSD+, JASPERS, fi-compass, ELENA, the EIC Fund as investment advisor, TechEU's biotech and AI-gigafactory strands) -- these are co-designed and often co-funded, but the EIB Group retains independent investment decision-making within its own governance.
- For "who does what" inside the Commission itself, see `commission_guide.md` and `european_commission_who_does_what.md`; the EIB Group sits outside that map entirely.

## Brubru engagement angles

Companies and public promoters most likely to intersect with EIB Group instruments:
- **Growth-stage tech scale-ups** seeking late-stage equity or debt: point to TechEU Platform / Investment Readiness Checker, and to ETCI-backed VC/PE funds as an indirect route.
- **Deep-tech/biotech/space companies already through EIC Accelerator or STEP**: likely also EIC Fund territory (EIB Group as investment advisor) -- see `eic_fund_equity_investment.md` for the due-diligence pack and co-investment terms.
- **Municipalities and public authorities preparing energy-efficiency or sustainable-transport projects** above EUR 30 million: ELENA grant for project-development costs.
- **Regional/national authorities preparing cohesion-fund infrastructure**: JASPERS free advisory support.
- **Managing authorities designing shared-management financial instruments**: fi-compass.
- **Defence-supply-chain SMEs and mid-caps**: the tripled EUR 3 billion intermediated financing window and the relaxed dual-use eligibility rules widen who can now qualify.
- **Companies or financial institutions operating in Ukraine, the EU Neighbourhood or Africa**: EFSD+ and Ukraine Investment Framework guarantees de-risk EIB/EIF-backed private investment there.
