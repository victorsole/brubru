## REACH Regulation - Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006
CELEX: 32006R1907

The Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) regulation is the EU's main chemicals legislation. It entered into force on 1 June 2007 and is directly applicable in all Member States.

### Core Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Registration** | Manufacturers/importers must register substances produced/imported >1 tonne/year with ECHA |
| **Evaluation** | ECHA evaluates registration dossiers and substance risks |
| **Authorisation** | Substances of Very High Concern (SVHCs) require authorisation for continued use (Annex XIV) |
| **Restriction** | Substances posing unacceptable risks can be restricted across the EU (Annex XVII) |

### Key REACH Annexes

| Annex | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Annex I | General provisions for assessing substances and preparing chemical safety reports |
| Annex II | Guide for compiling Safety Data Sheets |
| Annexes VII-XI | Standard information requirements for registration (by tonnage band) |
| Annex XIV | Authorisation list (substances requiring authorisation for use) |
| Annex XV | Dossiers for proposing restrictions, SVHCs, or harmonised classification |
| Annex XVII | Restrictions on manufacture, placing on market, and use |

## REACH Targeted Revision (2023-ongoing)

### Commission Proposal - COM(2023) 245
Procedure: 2023/0234(COD)

The European Commission proposed a targeted revision of REACH in April 2023 as part of the Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability. Key elements:

**One Substance, One Assessment (OSOA):**
- Streamline hazard and risk assessment across EU agencies (ECHA, EFSA, EMA, EEA)
- Avoid duplication where the same substance is assessed under multiple regulations
- ECHA to become the central hub for chemical safety data

**Registration reform:**
- New information requirements for polymers (polymer registration obligation)
- Updated information requirements for low-tonnage substances (1-10 tonnes)
- Strengthen dossier evaluation and compliance checks

**Authorisation and restriction reform:**
- Simplify restriction procedures to reduce timeline from 7+ years to ~2-3 years
- Introduce generic approach to risk management for most harmful substances
- Reform authorisation to better handle applications and substitution plans

**Mixture Assessment Factor (MAF):**
- Account for combined exposure to multiple chemicals (cocktail effect)
- Apply an additional assessment factor to account for unintentional mixture exposure

### Legislative Status (as of early 2026)
- Lead committee: **ENVI** (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety)
- The proposal is in first reading in the European Parliament
- The revision is considered one of the key files of the Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability

## Major REACH Restriction Proposals (2023-2026)

### Universal PFAS Restriction
The largest restriction proposal in REACH history, submitted by 5 EU countries (Denmark, Germany, Netherlands, Norway, Sweden) in January 2023.

**Scope:**
- Covers approximately 10,000 per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) -- "forever chemicals"
- Would ban manufacture, use, and placing on the market of all PFAS in the EU
- Phased approach with time-limited derogations for critical uses (e.g., medical devices, semiconductors)

**Timeline:**
- January 2023: Restriction proposal submitted to ECHA
- 2023-2024: Public consultation (>5,600 comments received)
- ECHA RAC/SEAC opinions expected in 2025-2026
- Commission decision and adoption expected 2026-2027
- Transition periods of 18 months to 12 years depending on use

**Key sectors affected:** Textiles, food contact materials, electronics, construction, automotive, medical devices, firefighting foams

### Microplastics Restriction - Regulation (EU) 2023/2055
CELEX: 32023R2055

Restricts intentionally added microplastics in products placed on the EU market. Covers:
- Cosmetics, detergents, agricultural products, toys, medical devices
- Phased transition periods (4-12 years depending on product type)
- First restrictions took effect October 2023 (e.g., loose glitter, microbeads in cosmetics)

### Lead in Ammunition and Fishing - Regulation (EU) 2023/923
CELEX: 32023R0923

Restricts lead in outdoor shooting (shot) and fishing (sinkers/lures):
- Ban on lead shot in wetlands (already in effect since February 2023)
- Broader lead shot and fishing tackle restrictions phased in through 2027

### Bisphenol A Restriction
ECHA RAC/SEAC opinions adopted in 2024. Proposes restricting bisphenol A (BPA) and structurally similar bisphenols in:
- Thermal paper (already restricted since January 2020)
- Food contact materials, consumer products
- Workplace exposure limits

## Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability (CSS)

Adopted by the Commission in October 2020 (COM(2020) 667). The overarching policy framework driving REACH reform and related legislative updates.

**Key commitments:**
- Extend the generic approach to risk management (ban most harmful chemicals in consumer products)
- Introduce Mixture Assessment Factor
- Reform REACH authorisation and restriction processes
- Regulate polymers under REACH
- Ensure essential use concept (restrict non-essential uses of most harmful substances)
- Strengthen enforcement and compliance

## Safe and Sustainable by Design (SSbD) Framework

### Commission Recommendation (EU) 2026/510
CELEX: 32026H0510
Published: 10 March 2026 (OJ L)

On 6 March 2026, the Commission published a revised European assessment framework for safe and sustainable by design (SSbD) chemicals and materials. This recommendation:

- Updates the SSbD criteria for assessing whether chemicals and materials are designed to be safe and sustainable throughout their lifecycle
- Aligns with the Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability (CSS) objective of promoting innovation in safer alternatives
- Provides voluntary guidance for industry, research institutions, and public authorities on evaluating chemical safety and sustainability from the design stage
- Supports the transition to toxic-free material cycles under the European Green Deal

**Relevance to REACH:** The SSbD framework complements REACH by encouraging upstream innovation in safer chemical design, reducing the need for downstream restrictions and authorisations. Companies developing alternative substances can use the SSbD criteria to demonstrate safety and sustainability advantages.
