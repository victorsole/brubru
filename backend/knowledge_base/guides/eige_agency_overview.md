# EIGE: European Institute for Gender Equality

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the EU's decentralised knowledge agency for gender equality. Headquartered in **Vilnius, Lithuania**, with a small liaison office in **Brussels**. Founded on paper on **20 December 2006**, it opened its doors and became operational at an official ceremony in Vilnius on **20-21 June 2010**, the first EU agency based in one of the Baltic States.
- **Founding law**: **Regulation (EC) No 1922/2006** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 December 2006 on establishing a European Institute for Gender Equality (CELEX **32006R1922**).
- **Mandate**: gathers and centralises EU-level expertise on gender equality; collects, analyses and disseminates comparable gender-disaggregated data and statistics with an intersectional perspective; develops methodological tools for gender mainstreaming; supports EU institutions and Member States with evidence for policymaking; raises citizens' awareness. EIGE does not set binding rules, gender equality policy stays a shared/national competence.
- **Director**: **Carlien Scheele**, in post since February 2020, reappointed by the Management Board on 21 June 2024 for a further five-year term (to 2029).
- **Governance**: a **Management Board** of one representative per Member State plus one European Commission representative (three-year terms) sets the work programme and budget; an **Experts' Forum**, an advisory network of national and civil-society gender-equality experts, feeds scientific and practical input.
- **Gender Equality Index**: EIGE's flagship annual composite score (0-100) tracking gender equality across the EU. The **2025 edition** ("Sharper data for a changing world", published **2 December 2025**) reset the methodology, so scores are not comparable with earlier years. **EU-27 score: 63.4/100.** Domains: health 86.2 (highest), money 73.9, work 69.3, time 65.0, knowledge 57.4, power 40.5 (lowest), plus cross-cutting violence and intersecting-inequalities domains. Sweden, France, Denmark rank highest; Cyprus lowest (47.6). At the current pace, full equality is at least 50 years away.
- **Gender Statistics Database**: EIGE's open repository of EU and Member State gender-disaggregated statistics (employment, education, health, violence, decision-making, time use), the reference dataset behind the Index.
- **Gender-based violence work**: measurement frameworks (incl. cyber violence) plus, since 2014, coordination of comparable national **administrative data** on violence against women, including femicide. With Eurostat and FRA, co-delivered the **EU Gender-based Violence Survey (EU-GBV)**, first EU-27 results (114,023 women) published 2024. Under **Article 44 of Directive (EU) 2024/1385**, EIGE now builds the EU-wide data-collection coordination system and common methodology.
- **Gender mainstreaming toolkits**: Gender Impact Assessment, Gender Budgeting, Gender-responsive Public Procurement, Gender-responsive Evaluation, GEAR tool (academia/research), via EIGE's Gender Mainstreaming Platform.
- **Beijing Platform for Action**: since 2013, technical support to Council presidencies reviewing the 12 BPfA areas of concern; Beijing+30 report published 2025.
- **Istanbul Convention**: EIGE supports implementation (EU acceded May 2023) via research and violence-data collection; formal treaty monitoring sits with the Council of Europe's GREVIO, not EIGE.
- **Enlargement support**: Regional Coordination Meetings with EU candidate countries/potential candidates (Western Balkans, Turkiye) on gender data; the 16th meeting ran December 2025.

## Context

EIGE was created by Regulation (EC) No 1922/2006 to give the EU a single, dedicated body for gathering and centralising expertise on gender equality, after years of that work being scattered across Commission units, national institutes and ad hoc studies. It took roughly three and a half years to become operational: the Institute formally opened in Vilnius on 20-21 June 2010, with European Commission Vice-President Viviane Reding, Lithuanian President Dalia Grybauskaite and the European Parliament's FEMM Committee chair among the speakers at the launch. It was the first EU agency headquartered in one of the Baltic States.

EIGE is a **decentralised EU agency**, legally distinct from the Commission and answerable to its own Management Board, in the same family as agencies like ECDC or EFSA (see `eu_commission_executive_agencies` for the distinction between decentralised agencies and Commission executive agencies, which are a different legal category).

## What EIGE does

### The Gender Equality Index
EIGE's best-known product is the annual **Gender Equality Index**, a composite score from 0 (total inequality) to 100 (full equality) built from indicators grouped into domains: work, money, knowledge, time, power and health, plus two cross-cutting domains, violence against women and intersecting inequalities (age, disability, migrant background, family type). The 2025 edition rebuilt the methodology and indicator set, so scores are not directly comparable with pre-2025 editions; the EU-27 stood at 63.4/100, with power the weakest domain (40.5, despite steady progress) and health the strongest (86.2). Country rankings and thematic deep-dives (in 2025: digital transition, online gender-based violence, youth) accompany the headline score each year. Chatbot answers should always check for a newer edition before citing the 2025 figures as current.

### Gender statistics and evidence
The **Gender Statistics Database** is EIGE's open data platform, the largest EU repository of gender-disaggregated statistics feeding the Index and used directly by the Commission, Parliament committees (notably FEMM) and Member State authorities for evidence-based policymaking.

### Gender-based violence measurement
EIGE has run EU-wide administrative-data collection on violence against women (including femicide) since 2014, publishing country profiles and methodological reports. It co-delivered the EU Gender-based Violence Survey with Eurostat and FRA (results published from 2024), and it has been given a formal statutory role under Article 44 of the Combating Violence Against Women Directive (EU) 2024/1385 to build a common EU methodology and coordination system for ongoing data collection.

### Gender mainstreaming tools
Through its Gender Mainstreaming Platform, EIGE produces practical toolkits used by national ministries, EU institutions and universities: Gender Impact Assessment (screening a law, policy or programme for its differential effect on women and men before adoption), Gender Budgeting (integrating a gender perspective into public budget processes), Gender-responsive Public Procurement, Gender-responsive Evaluation, and the GEAR tool for gender equality in research organisations and academia.

### Monitoring international commitments
EIGE supports EU-level implementation tracking for two major international frameworks: the UN **Beijing Platform for Action** (technical support to Council presidencies since 2013, area-by-area indicator reviews, a Beijing+30 report in 2025) and the Council of Europe's **Istanbul Convention** (research, data and measurement support since the EU's accession in May 2023; formal treaty compliance monitoring itself sits with the Council of Europe's GREVIO expert body, not EIGE).

### Regional and enlargement cooperation
EIGE regularly convenes gender-equality authorities and statisticians from EU candidate countries and potential candidates (the Western Balkans, Turkiye) at Regional Coordination Meetings, supporting alignment with EU gender-equality data standards ahead of accession.

## Governance in practice

| Body | Composition | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Director | Single appointee, five-year mandate (renewable) | Day-to-day management; current: Carlien Scheele, in post since Feb 2020, reappointed Jun 2024 |
| Management Board | One representative per Member State + one Commission representative, three-year terms | Adopts the work programme and budget, sets strategic direction |
| Experts' Forum | National gender-equality bodies, social partners, civil society and academic experts, three-year terms | Advisory network feeding scientific and practical expertise into EIGE's work |
