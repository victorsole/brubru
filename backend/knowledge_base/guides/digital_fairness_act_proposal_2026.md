# Digital Fairness Act: the Commission's proposal of 11 November 2026 (COM(2026) 1100)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Status: PROPOSED on Wednesday 11 November 2026, NOT adopted, NOT in force.** The Commission tabled **COM(2026) 1100 final**, procedure **2026/0356(COD)** (both printed on the proposal's cover): a Regulation "on promoting fair business-to-consumer commercial practices in the offline and digital single market, and repealing Directive 2005/29/EC (Digital Fairness Act)". Parliament and the Council now negotiate it (ordinary legislative procedure, Article 114 TFEU).
- **It turns the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC) into a directly applicable Regulation** and applies to ALL traders dealing with consumers, online and offline, NOT only to online platforms (the Digital Services Act covers platforms).
- **What the proposal contains (article numbers of the proposal):** dark patterns banned, blacklist in Annex I (Article 10); influencer marketing labelled "advertisement" or "commercial collaboration" (Article 11); no profiling-based advertising to minors or with sensitive data (Article 12); no personalised prices that harm minors or vulnerable consumers (Article 13); virtual currencies shown in real money, off by default for minors (Article 14); paid loot boxes with disclosed odds, NOT offered to minors (Article 15); pay-to-win and pay-to-progress banned for minors (Article 16); addictive design features (autoplay, unrelated notifications, streak penalties) that the consumer can switch off (Article 17); ticket and virtual-queue price transparency (Articles 18, 19); free trials, renewal reminders, online cancellation, a human in customer service, AI agents buying for a consumer (Articles 20 to 27).
- **Penalties (Article 35):** set by Member States; in coordinated cross-border actions the maximum fine must be at least **4% of the trader's annual turnover** in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million when turnover is unknown. The Commission could fine up to **6% of worldwide annual turnover**, only under the enforcement proposal below.
- **Dates (Article 40):** in force on the twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal; **applies 12 months after entry into force**. No date yet: it must first be adopted.
- **Same day, two companion proposals (the "Consumer Package"):** (1) a revision of the **Consumer Protection Cooperation Regulation (EU) 2017/2394**: the Commission could investigate and fine directly when a breach is likely to affect consumers in at least two thirds of Member States with two thirds of the EU population and the company is NOT an SME; (2) a simplification Directive amending the **Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU**.
- **The Commission's own estimate:** up to EUR 13.7 billion a year saved by consumers.
- **Parliament's think tank, 10 November 2026, three briefings:** on the Act (ECTI_BRI(2026)772666), on addictive design (ECTI_BRI(2026)789729) and on the vulnerable consumer (ECTI_BRI(2026)789730).
- **What was NOT read:** the two companion proposals (COM numbers not known here; described from the press release IP/26/2407 and Q&A QANDA/26/2408), the impact assessment, Annex I, the recitals. No rapporteur or committee yet.
