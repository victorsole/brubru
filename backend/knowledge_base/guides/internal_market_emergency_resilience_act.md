# Internal Market Emergency and Resilience Act (IMERA)

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Regulation (EU) 2024/2747 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 9 October 2024 establishing a framework of measures related to an internal market emergency and to the resilience of the internal market and amending Council Regulation (EC) No 2679/98 (Internal Market Emergency and Resilience Act)
- Short name: IMERA (formerly proposed as the Single Market Emergency Instrument, SMEI)
- CELEX: 32024R2747

## What it does
IMERA gives the EU a standing framework to keep goods, services and people moving across the single market during a crisis (pandemic, conflict, natural disaster, major supply shock). It replaces ad hoc, uncoordinated national responses -- like the export bans and border closures seen in early COVID-19 -- with a graduated, coordinated EU mechanism. It is the single-market crisis counterpart to instruments such as the Critical Medicines Act and the Chips Act on the supply-security side.

## Three modes (graduated escalation)
1. **Contingency mode (default / always on):** Commission and Member States run early warnings, stress-test simulations and preparedness exercises; an Internal Market Emergency and Resilience Board coordinates.
2. **Vigilance mode (activated by the Council):** closer monitoring of supply chains for strategically important goods and services; Member States build up reserves and monitor.
3. **Emergency mode (activated by the Council for severe, wide disruption):** targeted, proportionate measures -- coordinated/joint procurement, priority-rated information requests to companies, and restrictions on national measures that would fragment the single market (e.g. intra-EU export bans are constrained).

## Governance
- **Internal Market Emergency and Resilience Board** -- Commission-chaired body of Member State representatives that advises on activation and coordination. First formal meeting: **4 June 2026**.
- Activation of vigilance/emergency mode is by Council implementing act on a Commission proposal; each mode is time-limited and reviewable.

## The IMERA package (three acts adopted together, 9 October 2024)
| CELEX | Instrument | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 32024R2747 | Regulation (EU) 2024/2747 | The IMERA Regulation itself -- the emergency/resilience framework + Board |
| 32024R2748 | Regulation (EU) 2024/2748 | Emergency conformity-assessment procedures, presumption of conformity, common specifications and market surveillance for products under Regulations (CPR, PPE, gas appliances, GPSR, Machinery, etc.) |
| 32024L2749 | Directive (EU) 2024/2749 | Same emergency conformity procedures across 10 product Directives (Machinery, EMC, ATEX, RED, Pressure Equipment, Lifts, etc.) |

## Obligations
- **Member States:** participate in monitoring, simulations and Board meetings; refrain from single-market-fragmenting national measures during emergency mode; notify draft crisis measures.
- **Companies:** during emergency mode may receive priority-rated requests for information on production capacity, stocks and supply chains for crisis-relevant goods/services; benefit from emergency conformity procedures that keep products on the market.

## Why it matters for users
- **Health / pharma (EFPIA):** IMERA is the horizontal crisis backstop behind sector tools like the Critical Medicines Act; emergency mode can trigger coordinated procurement and information requests across medicine supply chains. See also `critical_medicines_act`, `ema_medicines_supply`.
- **Industry / manufacturing:** the 2748/2749 emergency conformity procedures determine how products reach the market in a declared single-market emergency.
