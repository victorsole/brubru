## Why this matters
Around **7 million EU citizens travel or live in third countries where their own Member State has no embassy or consulate**. Article 23 TFEU and Directive (EU) 2015/637 give them the right to ask any other Member State's consulate for help in situations such as serious illness, accident, victim of crime, arrest, evacuation in crisis, or loss of travel documents. The EU Emergency Travel Document (ETD), Council Directive 2019/997/EU, lets unrepresented citizens get a one-trip travel document when their passport is lost or stolen abroad. The 2023/0441(CNS) proposal modernises both instruments, adding rules on financial cost-sharing among Member States, digital ETD issuance, and clearer triggers for crisis support (incl. lessons learned from the 2020-2022 COVID-19 repatriation, the 2021 Kabul evacuation, the 2023 Sudan evacuation, the 2024 Lebanon evacuation, and the 2026 Middle East operations).

## Key changes
- **Digital-first ETD**: machine-readable, biometric where feasible; faster issuance from EU delegations and consulates of represented Member States
- **Cost-sharing**: clearer reimbursement rules between MS for consular assistance to unrepresented citizens
- **Crisis preparedness**: explicit role for EEAS and EU Delegations in crisis lead-State coordination, joint consular teams (JCT)
- **Family member coverage**: extends to non-EU family members travelling with EU citizens (subject to safeguards)
- **Information rights**: consolidated obligations on Member States to inform citizens about consular protection rights
