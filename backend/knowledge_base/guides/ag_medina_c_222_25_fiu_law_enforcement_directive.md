# Advocate General Medina, Case C-222/25 (Rahapesu Andmebüroo): Financial Intelligence Units fall under the Law Enforcement Directive (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **This is an Advocate General's OPINION, NOT a judgment.** Delivered on **8 October 2026** by Advocate General **Laila Medina** in Case **C-222/25 | Rahapesu Andmebüroo** (Court press release No 145/26). It is **not binding** on the Court of Justice; the judges have begun deliberating and the **judgment will be given at a later date**. Never state the opinion as the Court's ruling.
- **Core view:** the processing of personal data by a **Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU)** carrying out its mission to prevent, detect and combat money laundering and terrorist financing falls under the **Law Enforcement Directive (Directive (EU) 2016/680, CELEX 32016L0680)**, NOT under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, CELEX 32016R0679). FIUs meet both conditions that take processing out of the GDPR: they are competent authorities for preventing and detecting criminal offences, and the processing serves that purpose. This does not depend on the FIU's organisational structure.
- **Access by the person concerned may be restricted.** A total ban on access can be compatible with an FIU's strict confidentiality mission, but any restriction must respect **proportionality** (limited in scope and duration to what is necessary), rest on **legislation with clear and precise rules** and minimum safeguards against abuse, and allow the supervisory authority and courts to control restrictions.
- **Indirect access:** when direct access is excluded, the person's rights must be protected through the **supervisory authority**, which must be able to hold confidential discussions with the FIU to determine which data the person needs for an effective judicial remedy.
- **Refusal without reasons:** EU law lets an FIU refuse an access request without giving reasons if disclosure would endanger the public interest, but it must tell the person that they can exercise their rights **indirectly through the supervisory authority**.
- **The Estonian case:** the Estonian FIU (RAB) refused two requests (December 2019 and January 2020) and later another request about ongoing investigations. The Estonian data protection authority ordered it to reconsider; lower courts ordered disclosure; the Estonian Supreme Court referred questions to the Court of Justice. The Advocate General notes that the applicable Estonian legal framework does not seem to set clear conditions for restricting access; the **national court** must assess compliance.
