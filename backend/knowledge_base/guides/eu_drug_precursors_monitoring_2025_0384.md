## What the proposal does

The proposal **modernises and merges** the EU's two drug-precursor regulations (2004/273 + 2005/111) into a single framework, addressing the **explosive growth of synthetic drug production** in the EU and globally over 2020-2025 — most notably **methamphetamine, MDMA, cocaine cutting agents, and synthetic opioids** (the EU has so far avoided a US-scale fentanyl crisis but precursor flows are being monitored).

Key elements:

### 1. Unified intra-EU + extra-EU regime
- Single Regulation covering both internal-market chemicals trade AND third-country imports/exports
- One single competent-authority interface per Member State
- Reduces administrative duplication for legitimate operators

### 2. Updated list of scheduled substances
- **Annex IA / IB / II** (substances of concern) updated to include recently-listed synthetic precursors (alpha-PVP precursors, MDP2P alternatives, mephedrone analogues, fentanyl precursors, nitazene analogues)
- Faster delegated-act procedure for adding emerging precursors — currently a major lag, with new precursors often listed only after a year-plus of market evidence

### 3. New economic-operator obligations
- **Registration** of all operators trading in scheduled substances (currently only certain Annexes)
- **Suspicious-transaction reporting** harmonisation across Member States (similar to AMLD pattern)
- **End-user verification** strengthening — especially for online sales of laboratory chemicals
- **Container-tracking** integration with the EU Customs Single Window

### 4. Enforcement and intelligence
- Mandatory **EMCDDA** (now the European Union Drugs Agency, EUDA) intelligence sharing
- **Customs cooperation** with non-EU exporting countries (China, India primarily; also Mexico)
- **Joint Operations** via Frontex + Europol + EUDA

### 5. Criminal-law interaction
- The **Commission proposal aligns** with the **Anti-Drug-Trafficking Directive** (Council Framework Decision 2004/757/JHA, as amended in 2024) — separate but parallel criminal-law instrument

### 6. Online-marketplace obligations
- **Online intermediaries** facilitating sale of scheduled substances must conduct **enhanced due diligence**
- Cross-link with **DSA Article 31** (traceability of traders) — the proposal does NOT amend the DSA but creates a sectoral overlay

### 7. Health-policy embedding
- The proposal embeds in the **EU Drugs Strategy 2021-2025** (and the upcoming successor 2026-2030)
- Coordination with **WHO and UNODC** scheduling decisions

## Why a joint INTA+LIBE rapporteurship

The file is **trans-sectoral**: it touches:
- **External-trade chemicals controls** (INTA competence — common commercial policy under Article 207 TFEU)
- **Internal-market chemicals controls** (IMCO competence — but ceded here)
- **Drug-control and crime prevention** (LIBE competence under Article 84 TFEU)
- **Customs cooperation** (cross-cutting under Article 33 TFEU)

Under **EP RoP Rule 58** ("Procedure with associated committees" or "Procedure with joint committee responsibility"), INTA and LIBE share the lead. Each rapporteur covers their committee's section; joint amendments tabling is coordinated.

## Stakeholder map

- **EUDA** (formerly EMCDDA) — drug-trends monitoring + Early Warning System
- **DG TAXUD + Customs Union competent authorities** — implementation
- **DG TRADE + Sanctions** — third-country export controls
- **DG HOME + Internal Security DG** — drug-control policy
- **Cefic** (European Chemical Industry Council) — legitimate operators' association
- **EPF** (European Pharmaceutical Industry Federation) — pharma-precursor angle
- **Europol + Eurojust** — cross-border enforcement
- **National competent authorities** — Member State customs + drug-control bodies
- **NGOs**: EuroNPUD (people who use drugs); Correlation Network — harm-reduction angle

## Compliance implications for industry

Companies handling scheduled chemicals will face:
- Stricter registration + record-keeping
- More frequent on-site inspections
- Harmonised suspicious-transaction reporting (potentially 24h notification)
- New online-sales due-diligence regime
- Higher fines under the harmonised penalty regime

For **online intermediaries** (e.g. chemical marketplaces, lab-equipment platforms): meaningful new due-diligence overlay.
