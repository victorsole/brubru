DG ENEST is the operational home of EU enlargement policy and Eastern neighbourhood cooperation. It manages the largest individual EU external-spending instrument (the Ukraine Facility at EUR 50 billion) alongside the pre-accession pipeline for twelve candidate and potential candidate countries.

---

## Architecture: the post-MENA carve-out

When the Von der Leyen II College took office in December 2024, the former single DG NEAR was split into two separate Directorates-General reflecting distinct policy mandates and geopolitical contexts:

- **DG ENEST** (this guide): Enlargement track and Eastern Partnership. Covers EU accession negotiations, pre-accession assistance, and the Ukraine and Moldova support architecture.
- **DG MENA** (see planned guide `dg_mena_overview`): Mediterranean, Middle East and Gulf cooperation. Covers the European Neighbourhood Policy southern dimension, including the southern Neighbourhood countries (Algeria, Egypt, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Libya, Morocco, Palestine, Syria, Tunisia) and relevant Gulf partnerships.

The operational boundary matters for advocacy: a policy file touching accession conditionality, IPA grants, Western Balkans single-market integration, or Ukraine reconstruction lands with DG ENEST. A file touching Euro-Mediterranean Association Agreements, southern ENP or Gulf dialogues lands with DG MENA. Both DGs coordinate closely with EEAS on political dimensions and with DG INTPA on development-finance methodology (see planned guide `dg_intpa_overview`).

DG ENEST inherits the enlargement methodology, the accession-chapter cluster system, and the neighbourhood conditionality toolbox from DG NEAR. Contractual continuity: all previously signed IPA Financing Agreements, TAIEX frameworks, and Twinning contracts remain valid under DG ENEST administration.

## Enlargement methodology and conditionality

The current enlargement methodology, reformed in 2020, applies a cluster-based approach grouping the 35 negotiating chapters into six thematic clusters:

1. **Fundamentals** (rule of law, fundamental rights, democratic institutions, public administration reform, economic criteria): opened first, closed last; conditionality anchors disbursements
2. **Internal Market**
3. **Competitiveness and Inclusive Growth**
4. **Green Agenda and Sustainable Connectivity**
5. **Resources, Agriculture and Cohesion**
6. **External Relations**

Progress is assessed annually in the **EU Enlargement Package**, published by DG ENEST each autumn. The Package includes individual country reports and a Communication setting the strategic frame. Conditionality is explicit: regression on rule of law or fundamental rights triggers the "reversibility" clause -- chapters can be re-opened and funding suspended. Serbia's Reform and Growth Facility tranche was under review in 2026 due to democratic governance concerns.

For Western Balkans, the Reform and Growth Facility (see below) has created a parallel conditionality track tighter than the accession chapter cycle: six-monthly assessments of reform-agenda milestones, with disbursements conditional on performance in rule of law, anti-corruption, single-market alignment, green transition, and digital transformation.

## Ukraine Facility (Regulation (EU) 2024/792)

**Budget:** EUR 50 billion for 2024-2027
**URL:** `https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/funding-technical-assistance/ukraine-facility_en`

The Ukraine Facility is the single largest EU external-support instrument ever created for one country. It replaced the Macro-Financial Assistance+ and the solidarity mechanisms that were patched together during 2022-2023. The facility has three structural pillars:

**Pillar I: Ukraine Plan support (EUR 38.5 billion)**
EUR 5.5 billion in grants and EUR 33 billion in loans channelled through the Ukrainian state budget for macro-financial stability, essential services, and reform implementation. Disbursements are conditional on quarterly milestones drawn from the Ukraine Plan, an agreed reform-and-investment document Ukraine submitted to the Commission.

**Pillar II: Ukraine Investment Framework (EUR 9.6 billion)**
EUR 7.8 billion in EU guarantees and EUR 1.8 billion in blending grants designed to leverage public and private investment in reconstruction. The framework operates similarly to the Western Balkans Investment Framework and the EFSD+ architecture under NDICI -- Global Europe.

**Pillar III: Union Accession Assistance (EUR 4.8 billion)**
Technical assistance, capacity building, and interest-rate subsidies to support Ukraine's alignment with the EU acquis as it progresses through accession negotiations. This pillar directly intersects with the accession-chapter cluster work.

As of June 2026, the EU had disbursed nearly EUR 2.8 billion in recent quarterly payments. At least 20 per cent of support must benefit sub-national authorities and climate/environmental objectives. An independent Audit Board monitors implementation through June 2028. The Ukraine Plan was amended in October 2025 (EU Decision 2025/2157) to reflect reconstruction priorities. For the EU loan guarantee and macro-financial architecture, see `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026`.

## Western Balkans Growth Plan and Reform and Growth Facility (Regulation (EU) 2024/1449)

**Budget:** EUR 6 billion (EUR 2 billion in grants + EUR 4 billion in concessional loans), 2024-2027
**In force:** 25 May 2024
**URL:** `https://enlargement.ec.europa.eu/enlargement-policy/growth-plan-western-balkans_en`

The Growth Plan for the Western Balkans was adopted by the Commission in November 2023 and accompanied by the Reform and Growth Facility regulation. It aims to integrate the six Western Balkan candidate countries into the EU single market ahead of full accession and to double the size of Western Balkan economies within the decade.

Each beneficiary country submits a **Reform Agenda** identifying priority reform areas across six clusters: rule of law and fundamental rights, public administration, competitiveness and inclusive growth, green transition, digital transition, and regional integration. The Commission carries out six-monthly assessments and disburses accordingly.

Key disbursement milestones as of June 2026:
- Reform Agendas approved: Albania, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia (October 2024); Bosnia and Herzegovina (December 2025)
- EUR 158.9 million disbursed to Albania, Montenegro and North Macedonia (20 May 2026) -- the third tranche since entry into force

The Growth Plan also provides for a **Common Regional Market** access-deepening track, cross-border infrastructure investment via the Western Balkans Investment Framework (WBIF), and a youth and education component (Western Balkans Youth Lab).

## Moldova Growth Plan (Regulation (EU) 2025/535)

**Budget:** EUR 1.9 billion for 2025-2027
**Regulation:** EU Regulation 2025/535, approved March 2025
**Key fact:** Largest EU financial support package ever for Moldova

The Moldova Growth Plan mirrors the Western Balkans Reform and Growth Facility architecture: payments are conditional on Moldova fulfilling its Reform Agenda for Moldova, assessed twice yearly. Seven reform clusters apply: economic competitiveness, connectivity, green transition, rule of law, digital, human capital, and social protection.

As of March 2026, EUR 207.9 million in total disbursements: EUR 270 million pre-financing (July 2025), EUR 18.9 million (September 2025), EUR 189 million (March 2026).

Moldova is simultaneously progressing through accession negotiations (candidate since June 2022; first cluster opened with Ukraine at the 18-19 June 2026 European Council). The Growth Plan is designed to embed reform momentum ahead of full single-market integration and eventual accession.

## IPA III: Instrument for Pre-accession Assistance (Regulation (EU) 2021/1529)

**Budget:** EUR 14.162 billion for 2021-2027 (EUR 14.99B total including external assigned revenues)
**CELEX:** 32021R1529
**Beneficiaries:** Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Türkiye; Moldova, Ukraine, and Georgia participate in specific IPA III components as associated countries

IPA III finances the institution-building and structural reforms required for EU accession. Thematic priorities are established in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2128: rule of law, anti-corruption, fundamental rights, public administration reform, green transition, digital transformation, and private sector development.

IPA III includes a dedicated **IPARD III** rural development window for Western Balkans agricultural holdings, processing, organic farming, and rural infrastructure. Climate mainstreaming accounts for 26.2 per cent of the IPA III budget; biodiversity for 3.7 per cent. Cross-border cooperation may receive up to 3 per cent of the IPA III envelope, with EU co-financing up to 85 per cent.

For grant mechanics, application processes, and IPARD III specifics, see `eu_funding_ipa_enlargement`.

## NDICI -- Global Europe: Eastern Partnership window (Regulation (EU) 2021/947)

The Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument -- Global Europe (EUR 79.5 billion, 2021-2027) is the overarching external-action financing regulation. DG ENEST manages the **Eastern Neighbourhood** geographic programme under NDICI, covering the six Eastern Partnership countries. This window funds:

- Association Agreement implementation support (EU-Ukraine, EU-Moldova, EU-Georgia Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Areas)
- Civil society, media freedom, and rule-of-law programmes (including Belarus civil-society-in-exile)
- Armenia: Resilience and Growth Plan EUR 270 million (2025-2029), mobilising up to EUR 1.6 billion via EFSD+ guarantees

NDICI is managed jointly with EEAS (political conditionality decisions) and DG INTPA (programming and financial methodology for development-cooperation components). For southern Neighbourhood countries, management passes to DG MENA. See planned guide `dg_intpa_overview` for development-finance architecture.

## TAIEX and Twinning

Both instruments are managed by DG ENEST and serve all enlargement and neighbourhood partner countries. They are peer-learning tools, not grant programmes in the traditional sense.

**TAIEX (Technical Assistance and Information Exchange)**
Provides short-term expert missions, study visits, workshops, peer assessments, and legal-text reviews. Beneficiaries are public administrations and public bodies (not civil society, private sector, or individuals). Requests are submitted on a rolling basis through the TAIEX online portal. Coverage spans all EU acquis chapters plus cross-cutting priorities: judiciary reform, environment, digital, public health, and customs.

**TAIEX@30:** In 2024-2025, DG ENEST marked the 30th anniversary of TAIEX with a rebranding campaign ("TAIEX@30 -- Better Institutions, Better Lives"). The programme has delivered more than 100,000 expert engagements since 1995.

**Twinning**
Longer-term institutional partnerships between a public body in a candidate or partner country and a counterpart in one or more EU Member States. Projects are typically one to three years in duration and cover specific acquis chapters. Twinning contracts are publicly tendered; results are published on the Twinning portal. Unlike TAIEX, Twinning projects are formally contracted and have defined deliverables and benchmarks.

## Coordination with EEAS, DG INTPA, and EEAS Special Representatives

**EEAS:** The European External Action Service manages the political track of enlargement and neighbourhood relations. Delegation heads in candidate countries report to EEAS. DG ENEST provides the technical conditionality and financial management layer. The HRVP chairs the Stabilisation and Association Process meetings (Western Balkans) and leads the Association Councils (Eastern Partnership). See planned guide `eeas_institution_hrvp_kallas`.

**DG INTPA:** The Directorate-General for International Partnerships manages the global development cooperation portfolio, including the EFSD+ guarantee mechanism that underpins several NDICI blending operations. DG ENEST and DG INTPA share methodology on results frameworks, monitoring, and the Global Gateway investment platform. See planned guide `dg_intpa_overview`.

**EEAS Special Representative for the Western Balkans Dialogue (Belgrade-Pristina dialogue):** Appointed by the HRVP, the Special Representative manages the normalisation dialogue between Serbia and Kosovo, which is formally separate from but feeds into the accession conditionality assessment. Progress (or regression) on the dialogue affects Serbia's and Kosovo's chapter positions.

## Brubru engagement angles

**Candidate-country public affairs:** Organisations operating in Western Balkans candidate countries use Brubru to monitor accession-chapter progress, track Reform Agenda milestone assessments, and anticipate legislative alignment requirements. The Reform and Growth Facility disbursement calendar creates predictable windows where governments must demonstrate progress, generating policy-window opportunities.

**EU acquis monitoring for accession states:** Legal and compliance professionals in Ukraine and Moldova need to track EU acquis developments affecting their aligned sectors. Brubru can map which active EU legislative files will require transposition under the accession track and predict timelines using the legislative-procedure pipeline.

**Ukraine reconstruction and IPA III tenders:** Procurement professionals monitoring ENEST-funded projects use Brubru to track Financing Decision publications, calls for proposals, and WBIF/Ukraine Investment Framework project pipelines.

**TAIEX and Twinning matchmaking:** EU Member State administrations looking for peer-learning partnership opportunities with candidate-country counterparts can use Brubru to identify active TAIEX calls and Twinning contract tenders.
