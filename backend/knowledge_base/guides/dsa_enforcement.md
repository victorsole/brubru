## Overview

The **Digital Services Act (DSA)**, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, is the EU's horizontal framework for online intermediary services. It establishes a graduated system of obligations for different categories of digital services, with the most stringent requirements applying to Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) and Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs).

The DSA replaced the e-Commerce Directive's (2000/31/EC) liability framework for intermediaries while preserving the country-of-origin principle. It introduces:

- **Due diligence obligations** for all intermediary services (notice and action, transparency reporting, terms of service)
- **Additional obligations** for hosting services and online platforms (complaint mechanisms, trusted flaggers, online marketplace trader identification)
- **Systemic risk obligations** for VLOPs/VLOSEs (risk assessments, independent audits, crisis response, data access for researchers)

The Commission enforces the DSA directly for VLOPs and VLOSEs, while national Digital Services Coordinators (DSCs) supervise smaller platforms.

## Enforcement Cases

### TikTok -- Case DSA.100109

The most advanced enforcement case under the DSA:

- **Proceeding opened:** 19 February 2024 (preliminary investigation since November 2023)
- **Scope:** Systemic risks related to addictive design features, child protection, and algorithmic transparency
- **Key concerns:**
  - "Rabbit hole" effect of the recommendation algorithm, particularly for minors
  - Age verification measures insufficient to prevent underage access
  - Lack of transparency on algorithmic amplification of harmful content
  - Inadequate risk assessment and mitigation for mental health impacts on young users
- **Commission Decision:** 5 December 2025 -- found TikTok in breach of DSA obligations
- **Summary published:** OJ 13 March 2026 (reference 52026DSA100109)
- **Significance:** First completed enforcement proceeding resulting in a finding of non-compliance under the DSA
- **Potential penalty:** Up to 6% of TikTok's global annual turnover

### X (formerly Twitter) -- Ongoing Proceedings

- **Proceeding opened:** 18 December 2023
- **Scope:** Systemic risks related to illegal content dissemination, dark patterns in the verified accounts programme (blue checkmarks), and advertising transparency
- **Key concerns:**
  - Reduction in content moderation capacity following ownership change
  - Blue checkmark system creating confusion about account authenticity
  - Non-compliant advertising repository (incomplete, not machine-readable)
  - Insufficient researcher data access under Article 40
- **Second proceeding opened:** July 2024, focused on deceptive design of the blue checkmark feature
- **Status:** Ongoing as of March 2026

### Meta (Facebook/Instagram) -- Ongoing Proceedings

- **Proceeding opened:** May 2024
- **Scope:** Systemic risks related to elections and civic discourse (initially linked to 2024 European Parliament elections)
- **Key concerns:**
  - Effectiveness of political advertising transparency measures
  - Recommendation systems and political content amplification
  - Deepfake and AI-generated content labelling
- **Additional proceeding:** November 2024, regarding the "pay or consent" model for data processing (intersection with GDPR)
- **Status:** Ongoing as of March 2026

### AliExpress -- Ongoing Proceedings

- **Proceeding opened:** March 2024
- **Scope:** Online marketplace obligations, particularly regarding illegal and unsafe products
- **Key concerns:**
  - Product safety of goods sold to EU consumers
  - Trader identification and traceability (Article 30)
  - Notice and action mechanisms for illegal products
  - Risk mitigation for counterfeit goods
- **Status:** Ongoing as of March 2026

## Obligations by Category

### All Intermediary Services
- Designate a legal representative in the EU (if established outside the EU)
- Transparency reporting (annual or semi-annual for VLOPs/VLOSEs)
- Terms of service must reference content moderation policies, including algorithmic decision-making
- Cooperate with national authorities on removal orders

### Hosting Services (additionally)
- Notice and action mechanisms for reporting illegal content
- Statement of reasons when content is removed or restricted
- Notification to law enforcement of suspected serious criminal offences

### Online Platforms (additionally)
- Internal complaint-handling system for content moderation decisions
- Out-of-court dispute settlement mechanisms
- Trusted flagger programmes with priority processing
- Measures against misuse (suspension of repeat infringers)
- Online marketplace trader identification ("know your business customer" -- KYBC)
- Ban on dark patterns: no manipulative interface design to influence user decisions
- Advertising transparency: real-time repository of all ads, targeting parameters disclosed to users
- Minors: prohibition of profiling-based advertising targeted at minors

### VLOPs and VLOSEs (additionally)
- **Systemic risk assessments:** Annual assessment of systemic risks, including illegal content dissemination, negative effects on fundamental rights, electoral integrity, public health, and gender-based violence
- **Risk mitigation measures:** Implement reasonable, proportionate, and effective mitigation measures based on risk assessments
- **Independent audits:** Annual independent audit of compliance with DSA obligations
- **Recommender system transparency:** At least one option for a recommendation system not based on user profiling
- **Crisis response:** Participate in crisis protocols activated by the Commission (e.g., pandemics, armed conflicts, terrorism)
- **Researcher data access (Article 40):** Provide vetted researchers with access to data for studying systemic risks
- **Supervisory fee:** Annual fee to the Commission, proportionate to the number of EU users

## Systemic Risk Assessment

VLOPs and VLOSEs must assess the following systemic risks annually (Article 34):

1. **Dissemination of illegal content** through their services
2. **Negative effects on fundamental rights** (dignity, privacy, non-discrimination, children's rights, freedom of expression)
3. **Negative effects on civic discourse and electoral processes**, and on public security
4. **Negative effects on gender-based violence**, public health, and the protection of minors
5. **Actual or foreseeable negative effects** relating to the design, functioning, or use of the service, including through algorithmic amplification

Risk assessments must be forwarded to the Commission and the relevant Digital Services Coordinator. The Commission may issue guidelines on risk assessment methodology.

## Transparency Reports

The DSA establishes a layered transparency reporting regime:

| Provider Type | Frequency | Key Contents |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| All intermediaries | Annual | Removal orders received, content moderation actions, automated tool use |
| Online platforms | Annual | Complaints received and outcomes, out-of-court settlements, trusted flagger notifications |
| VLOPs/VLOSEs | Semi-annual | All of the above, plus: active EU users, systemic risk details, audit results, researcher access requests |

The Commission publishes a consolidated database of transparency reports and maintains a publicly accessible advertising repository for VLOPs/VLOSEs.

## National Digital Services Coordinators

Each Member State must designate one DSC as the primary supervisory authority:

- **Role:** Supervise platforms established in their territory (country-of-origin principle), handle complaints from users, coordinate with other DSCs and the Commission
- **Powers:** Information requests, on-site inspections, interim measures, penalties (up to 6% of provider's annual income/turnover at national level)
- **European Board for Digital Services:** Advisory body composed of all 27 DSCs, coordinates cross-border enforcement and issues opinions

### Coordination Challenges
- **Ireland:** As the country of establishment for many major platforms (Meta, Google, TikTok, Apple, Microsoft), Ireland's DSC (Coimisiun na Meain/Media Commission) bears a disproportionate supervisory burden
- **Consistency mechanism:** Where multiple Member States are affected, the DSC of establishment must consult other DSCs before taking enforcement action
- **Commission intervention:** The Commission may intervene and open its own proceedings against VLOPs/VLOSEs if it considers that a DSC is not taking adequate action

## Interaction with Other EU Law

### Digital Markets Act (DMA)
The DSA and DMA form the EU's "digital constitution" but have distinct scopes. The DSA addresses content and user safety; the DMA addresses market contestability. A platform may be both a VLOP under the DSA and a gatekeeper under the DMA (e.g., Google, Meta, Apple, Amazon). Enforcement proceedings under both instruments can run in parallel.

### AI Act
The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) imposes additional obligations on AI systems, including recommender algorithms. Where a VLOP's recommender system qualifies as a high-risk AI system, it must comply with both the DSA's transparency requirements and the AI Act's conformity assessment. The AI Act's provisions on general-purpose AI models also affect content moderation tools.

### AVMSD (Audiovisual Media Services Directive)
Video-sharing platforms (e.g., YouTube, TikTok) are subject to both the DSA and the revised AVMSD (Directive 2018/1808). The AVMSD applies sector-specific rules on harmful and illegal audiovisual content, advertising around audiovisual content, and European works promotion. The DSA adds horizontal due diligence obligations.

### GDPR
Tension exists between DSA transparency obligations (particularly advertising repositories and researcher data access) and GDPR data protection requirements. The "pay or consent" model used by Meta for ad-free subscriptions is under scrutiny as both a DSA and GDPR issue.

### Terrorist Content Regulation
Regulation (EU) 2021/784 requires hosting service providers to remove terrorist content within one hour of receiving a removal order. This sector-specific obligation operates alongside the DSA's general notice and action framework.
