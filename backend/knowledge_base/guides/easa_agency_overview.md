# EASA: European Union Aviation Safety Agency

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)
- Seat: Cologne, Germany
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2018/1139 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 4 July 2018 on common rules in the field of civil aviation and establishing a European Union Aviation Safety Agency (the "Basic Regulation"), which repealed and replaced the earlier Regulations (EC) 216/2008, (EC) 552/2004, (EC) 1592/2002 and (EEC) 3922/91
- Type: EU decentralised (regulatory) agency, operationally independent, under the political oversight of DG MOVE (Mobility and Transport)
- Established: 2002 (as the European Aviation Safety Agency); renamed European Union Aviation Safety Agency under the 2018 Basic Regulation
- Executive Director: Florian Guillermet (verify live, as leadership can change)
- Governance: Management Board (Member State representatives + Commission, oversight and budget), Executive Director (day-to-day management, safety decisions), Board of Appeal (independent body for appeals against EASA decisions)
- Staff: 800+ across the agency, plus representations in several non-EU countries
- Core mandate: certification of aircraft, engines, propellers and parts (type certificates); oversight of design, production and maintenance organisations; setting and enforcing common rules for airworthiness, air operations, aircrew licensing, aerodromes and air traffic management/air navigation services (ATM/ANS); standardisation inspections of national civil aviation authorities; environmental protection (aircraft noise and emissions certification)
- Newer domains: unmanned aircraft systems (drones/UAS) and U-space, rotorcraft and innovative air mobility (eVTOL/UAM), aviation cybersecurity (Part-IS), safety promotion and accident-prevention support alongside the European Union Aviation Safety Agency's role in the European Plan for Aviation Safety (EPAS)
- International role: negotiates and implements bilateral aviation safety agreements (for example with the US FAA) on behalf of the EU; represents the EU technically at ICAO
- Related EU initiatives EASA supports: Single European Sky (SES) implementation, SESAR air traffic management modernisation, ReFuelEU Aviation sustainable-fuel rules, U-space regulatory framework for drones

EASA is the EU's civil aviation safety regulator: it certifies aircraft and organisations, writes and enforces the detailed technical rules that sit underneath the Basic Regulation, and checks that national aviation authorities apply those rules consistently across the Union.

## What EASA does

### Certification
EASA issues type certificates for aircraft, engines, propellers and parts designed or produced in the EU, and validates foreign-designed products for EU use. It also certifies design and production organisations, and approves maintenance organisations, directly at EU level for the highest-risk categories, while national authorities handle lower-risk categories under EASA oversight.

### Rulemaking
Under the Basic Regulation, EASA drafts the detailed "Implementing Rules" and "Acceptable Means of Compliance" that the Commission adopts, covering airworthiness, air operations, aircrew licensing and medical fitness, aerodromes, and ATM/ANS. EASA's rulemaking programme is published and consulted on before adoption.

### Oversight and standardisation
EASA carries out standardisation inspections of national civil aviation authorities to verify they are applying EU aviation safety rules correctly, and can recommend corrective action where gaps are found.

### Domains
EASA's regulatory scope is organised by domain: aircraft and products (certification, airworthiness), air operations, aircrew and medical, aerodromes, air traffic management and air navigation services (ATM/ANS), drones/UAS and U-space, rotorcraft and innovative air mobility, general aviation, environment (noise and emissions), and cybersecurity in aviation (Part-IS).

### Environmental protection
EASA certifies aircraft noise and emissions performance and produces the European Aviation Environmental Report jointly with the European Environment Agency and the European Commission, feeding into policy such as ReFuelEU Aviation.

### Drones and U-space
EASA regulates unmanned aircraft systems (UAS/drones) across open, specific and certified risk categories, and sets the regulatory framework for U-space, the digital traffic-management layer for drone operations at low altitude, closely linked to the SESAR 3 Joint Undertaking's Digital European Sky work.

### Safety strategy
The European Plan for Aviation Safety (EPAS), published and updated by EASA, sets the EU's aviation safety priorities and actions, informed by data collected through mandatory occurrence reporting and safety risk analysis.

## Institutional position

EASA operates under the political responsibility of the European Commission (DG MOVE) but takes independent, evidence-based technical and safety decisions, particularly on certification. Its Management Board, drawing on Member State civil aviation authorities and the Commission, sets the agency's budget and work programme; the Executive Director runs day-to-day operations and signs certification decisions; the Board of Appeal hears challenges to EASA decisions independently of the Executive Director and Management Board.

## Why it matters for EU policy engagement

Aviation manufacturers, airlines, drone operators, air navigation service providers and aerodrome operators interact with EASA directly for certification and rule compliance, separately from the broader legislative process in Parliament and Council. Stakeholders tracking aviation dossiers should follow both the legislative track (Council/Parliament amendments to the Basic Regulation or related acts) and EASA's own rulemaking and opinion process, which produces the binding technical detail.
