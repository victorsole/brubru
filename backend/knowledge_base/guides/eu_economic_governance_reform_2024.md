# Economic Governance Reform (April 2024): Reformed Stability and Growth Pact

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the reformed EU economic-governance framework (informally, the "new Stability and Growth Pact"), the largest overhaul of EU fiscal-surveillance rules since the Six-Pack/Two-Pack of 2011-2013.
- **In force since**: 30 April 2024.
- **Three legal acts** (the reform package):
  - **Regulation (EU) 2024/1263** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2024 on the effective coordination of economic policies and on multilateral budgetary surveillance and repealing Council Regulation (EC) No 1466/97 (**preventive arm**). CELEX 32024R1263.
  - **Council Regulation (EU) 2024/1264** of 29 April 2024 amending Regulation (EC) No 1467/97 on speeding up and clarifying the implementation of the excessive deficit procedure (**corrective arm**). CELEX 32024R1264. Adopted by the Council alone (TFEU Art. 126(14), not co-decision).
  - **Council Directive (EU) 2024/1265** of 29 April 2024 amending Directive 2011/85/EU on requirements for budgetary frameworks of the Member States (**national fiscal frameworks**). CELEX 32024L1265. Council-only act; transposition deadline 31 December 2025.
- **Core mechanism**: a single operational indicator, the **net expenditure path** (also called net primary expenditure path), replaces the old multi-indicator structural-balance framework. Net expenditure = government expenditure net of (i) interest expenditure, (ii) discretionary revenue measures, (iii) EU-fund-matched spending, (iv) national co-financing of EU programmes, (v) cyclical unemployment-benefit spending, and (vi) one-offs/temporary measures.
- **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plans (MTFSPs)**: each Member State submits a single plan combining fiscal, reform and investment commitments, replacing the old separate Stability/Convergence Programmes and National Reform Programmes. Base adjustment period is **4 or 5 years** (aligned to the national electoral/legislative cycle); extendable by **up to 3 additional years** (so 4-7 or 5-8 years total) if backed by a qualifying set of reforms and investments that support debt sustainability and growth. First-round plans (2025-2028) were due **20 September 2024** (several Member States, e.g. Finland, Cyprus, obtained short extensions into October 2024 under Art. 36).
- **Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA)**: the Commission calculates a country-specific, risk-based **reference trajectory** for net expenditure using its DSA methodology, ensuring debt is put or kept on a plausibly downward path (or stays below 60% of GDP) and the deficit is brought/kept below 3% of GDP over the medium term. Member States can deviate from the reference trajectory but must justify the alternative in their own plan.
- **Treaty reference values unchanged**: 3% of GDP deficit and 60% of GDP debt (Protocol No 12 TFEU) remain the anchors; the reform changes the operational path to reach/maintain them, not the thresholds themselves.
- **Two safeguards** (apply during the adjustment period):
  - **Debt sustainability safeguard**: debt above 90% of GDP must fall by at least 1 percentage point/year on average; debt between 60-90% of GDP must fall by at least 0.5 pp/year on average.
  - **Deficit resilience safeguard**: Member States must maintain (or reach) a structural deficit safety margin below the 3% of GDP Treaty reference value, of 1.5% of GDP once the deficit is below 3%.
- **Reformed Excessive Deficit Procedure (EDP)**: retains both the deficit-based EDP (>3% of GDP) and the debt-based EDP (>60% of GDP and not diminishing satisfactorily), but the debt-criterion assessment methodology was substantially revised while the deficit-criterion assessment stayed largely unchanged. A **control account** tracks cumulative deviations from the agreed net expenditure path, triggering closer monitoring or EDP steps if breaches accumulate.
- **First MTFSP endorsement wave**: ECOFIN endorsed net expenditure paths and MTFSPs for 21 Member States on 21 January 2025 (Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden); Hungary's path was set alongside its EDP recommendation (18 February 2025); Austria and Lithuania followed later (Austria July 2025, alongside Austria's NEW excessive deficit procedure opened 8 July 2025 and Romania's corrective path revision). Belgium was a late submitter (19 March 2025). **Verify the current live count of endorsed plans and open EDPs each time this guide is used: the roster changes with every ECOFIN/Spring-Package cycle.**
- **Enforcement**: financial sanctions (up to 0.05% of GDP per six-month period, capped) remain available for euro-area Member States as a last resort under the debt-based/deficit-based EDP, alongside reputational and reporting sanctions (annual progress reports) for all Member States.
- **National Independent Fiscal Institutions (IFIs)** and the **European Fiscal Board** play a strengthened advisory/monitoring role under Directive (EU) 2024/1265, though the European Fiscal Board itself has flagged that its own formal mandate was not substantially expanded by the reform.
- **Responsible DG**: DG ECFIN (Economic and Financial Affairs), under the Commissioner for Economy and Productivity.
- **LATEST (18 August 2026) - the National Escape Clause has been broadened from defence to energy security.** Commission Notice, *Guidance to Member States on Broadening the Scope of the National Escape Clause for Energy Security*, CELEX `52026XC04514`, ELI http://publications.europa.eu/resource/eli/C/2026/4514/oj. Trailed in the **European Semester Spring Package 2026**. The legal base is unchanged: **Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1263**, the same national escape clause already used for defence.
  - **What qualifies**: nationally financed measures with a direct budgetary impact that strengthen the structural resilience of the European energy system and accelerate the move away from fossil fuels. Named examples: household and business support to cut fossil-fuel reliance, electrification of end-use sectors, electricity grid investment, clean energy storage (batteries), energy saving, clean generation capacity, and hydrogen infrastructure with near-term energy-security value (including Projects of Common Interest and Projects of Mutual Interest, and repurposing existing gas infrastructure for exclusive hydrogen use).
  - **What is explicitly excluded**: cuts to excise or other taxes on fossil fuels, fixed prices, fossil-fuel subsidies, income-based support, and any measure delivering only indirect energy savings without a structural improvement in resilience. Price-relief measures do NOT qualify however they are framed.
  - **Caps**: the overall NEC ceiling stays at **1.5% of GDP**. Energy security measures carry their own dedicated caps of **0.3% of GDP a year** and **0.6% of GDP cumulatively over 2026-2028**, assessed on the total fiscal cost of eligible measures using **ex post** data. A Member State already close to the 1.5% ceiling on defence may request an exemption to exceed it, which implies additional fiscal adjustment in a later plan; the 0.3%/0.6% energy caps still apply.
  - **Additionality**: only measures **decided after 28 February 2026** are eligible. Retroactive application is expressly ruled out.
  - **Duration**: the NEC runs **until 2028**. After 2028 any surviving energy measures must be absorbed by national re-prioritisation, and the resulting higher deficit or debt feeds into the requirements of the next medium-term fiscal-structural plan.
  - **Procedure**: the request is at each Member State's discretion, assessed by the Commission under Article 26 and **approved by the Council on a Commission recommendation**. Requests were invited by **end-July / mid-August 2026**, with later ones still considered. The Notice says Commission recommendations **could** be ready in September and Council adoption **could** take place at the October ECOFIN - both conditional in the source, so do not state either as settled.
  - **Reporting**: no ESA national-accounts category captures these measures, so Member States face **enhanced reporting** against a Commission template, updated before each surveillance cycle (**before 15 April** in spring, **before 15 October** in autumn).
  - **Control account**: unchanged in operation. The **augmented** control account works in two steps - first test whether a positive cumulative deviation is explained by defence flexibility, then whether the remainder falls within the energy security flexibility. Deviations not covered by the NEC are still registered as debits.
  - **Stated driver**: the Notice names the war in the Middle East as having increased the urgency of accelerating the transition away from fossil fuels.
- **Legislative history**: Commission proposed the package 26 April 2023; Council and Parliament reached political agreement 10 February 2024 (Consilium press release); European Parliament adopted its position 23 April 2024; formally adopted 29 April 2024; published in the Official Journal and entered into force 30 April 2024.

Guide for EU fiscal-policy, ECON/BUDG/CONT-committee and Member-State finance-ministry professionals covering the 2024 reform of the EU's economic-governance framework: what changed, how the new net-expenditure-path mechanism works, the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan cycle, and the reformed Excessive Deficit Procedure.

## Why the Reform Happened

The pre-2024 Stability and Growth Pact (Regulation (EC) 1466/97 preventive arm + Regulation (EC) 1467/97 corrective arm, as amended by the Six-Pack 2011 and Two-Pack 2013) was widely criticised as too complex (multiple indicators: structural balance, expenditure benchmark, debt rule, MTO), pro-cyclical, and poorly enforced. The general escape clause was activated 2020-2023 (COVID-19 and the energy-price shock following Russia's invasion of Ukraine), during which the old rules were suspended. The Commission launched a public review of economic governance in February 2020, paused it during the pandemic, and relaunched it with an orientation communication in November 2022 followed by the legislative proposal on 26 April 2023. The stated goals of the reform: simplify the framework to a single operational indicator, strengthen national ownership of fiscal plans, and better link fiscal consolidation to growth-enhancing reforms and investment (particularly green and digital transitions, and defence).

## The Three Pillars

### 1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1263: Preventive Arm

Repeals and replaces Council Regulation (EC) 1466/97. Establishes:
- The **net expenditure path** as the single operational indicator for fiscal surveillance (replacing structural balance, expenditure benchmark and Medium-Term Objective).
- The **Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP)**, combining what used to be separate Stability/Convergence Programmes (fiscal) and National Reform Programmes (structural).
- The Commission's **Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA)** and **reference trajectory** methodology, technically underpinned by a common DSA framework shared with the IMF-style stochastic debt-projection approach.
- The **debt sustainability safeguard** and **deficit resilience safeguard** (see QUICK FACTS).
- A **control account** recording cumulative deviations (in percentage points of GDP) from the agreed net expenditure path, with a deviation threshold (0.3% of GDP annually / 0.6% of GDP cumulatively) that can trigger a "significant deviation" procedure.
- Provision for a **general escape clause** (EU-wide severe downturn) and **national escape clauses** (exceptional circumstances outside a Member State's control), allowing temporary deviation from the agreed path.

### 2. Regulation (EU) 2024/1264: Corrective Arm / Excessive Deficit Procedure

Amends (does not repeal) Council Regulation (EC) 1467/97. Council-only act under TFEU Art. 126(14): the European Parliament was consulted but this pillar did not go through ordinary legislative procedure like Regulation 2024/1263. Key changes:
- **Deficit-based EDP** (Art. 126(2)(a) TFEU, deficit >3% of GDP): assessment methodology largely unchanged from the pre-reform rules.
- **Debt-based EDP** (Art. 126(2)(b) TFEU, debt >60% of GDP and not diminishing satisfactorily): assessment methodology substantially revised to align with the new net-expenditure-path logic: a Member State broadly complying with its Council-endorsed net expenditure path is presumed to be reducing debt satisfactorily.
- **Corrective net expenditure path**: when an EDP is opened, the Council sets (on a Commission recommendation) a corrective net expenditure path requiring an annual structural adjustment (minimum 0.5% of GDP for deficit-based EDPs), with a deadline for correction.
- Financial sanctions retained for euro-area Member States as a last-resort enforcement tool.

### 3. Council Directive (EU) 2024/1265: National Budgetary Frameworks

Amends Council Directive 2011/85/EU. Requires Member States to:
- Operate public accounting systems covering all general-government subsectors on an accrual basis, subject to internal control and independent audit.
- Base annual and multiannual fiscal planning on realistic, up-to-date macroeconomic and budgetary forecasts.
- Establish a credible medium-term budgetary framework with a fiscal-planning horizon of at least 3 years.
- Publish information on contingent liabilities and on fiscal costs of natural disasters/climate-related shocks.
- Strengthen the role and independence of **national Independent Fiscal Institutions (IFIs)** in monitoring compliance with the domestic and EU fiscal rules.
- Transposition deadline: **31 December 2025**.

## The Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan Cycle

| Step | Actor | Detail |
|------|-------|--------|
| Reference trajectory | Commission | DSA-based, risk-differentiated net expenditure trajectory shared with each Member State ahead of plan drafting |
| Technical dialogue | Commission + Member State | Bilateral exchanges (criticised by the European Fiscal Board as insufficiently multilateral/transparent) |
| Plan submission | Member State | MTFSP submitted to Council and Commission (first round: due 20 September 2024, extensions granted case by case) |
| Commission assessment | Commission | Recommendation to the Council on whether to endorse the net expenditure path and, where relevant, the reform/investment package underpinning an extended adjustment period |
| Council endorsement | Council (ECOFIN) | Council Recommendation setting the binding net expenditure path (adopted by qualified majority on a Commission recommendation) |
| Annual progress reports | Member State | Yearly reporting on implementation, feeding into the control account |
| Subsequent plans | Member State | New MTFSP submitted by 30 April of the last year of the plan in force (for later cycles, once the first round is bedded in) |

## Cross-Reference: Old vs New Framework

| Element | Pre-2024 SGP | Reformed SGP (2024) |
|---------|--------------|----------------------|
| Operational indicator | Structural balance + expenditure benchmark + debt rule (multiple, sometimes conflicting) | Single net expenditure path |
| Planning document | Separate Stability/Convergence Programme + National Reform Programme | Single Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan |
| Adjustment horizon | Annual, MTO-anchored | 4-5 years, extendable to 7-8 with reforms/investment |
| Debt-based EDP trigger | 1/20th rule (mechanical) | DSA-based, path-compliance presumption |
| National ownership | Low (Commission/Council-driven benchmarks) | Higher (Member State designs its own path within DSA guardrails) |
| Fiscal frameworks directive | 2011/85/EU | Amended by 2024/1265 (IFI role, climate risk disclosure) |

## What Did Not Change

- The **Treaty reference values** of 3% of GDP (deficit) and 60% of GDP (debt) under Protocol No 12 TFEU are unchanged: only the path to reach/maintain them has changed.
- The **legal basis** for the corrective arm remains TFEU Art. 126 (excessive deficit procedure); the preventive arm remains grounded in TFEU Art. 121 (broad economic policy guidelines) and Art. 126.
- The **European Semester** annual cycle (Country Reports, Country-Specific Recommendations, Spring/Autumn Packages) continues to house the MTFSP assessment and EDP steps: see `european_semester_communication.md` for the full calendar.
- **Financial sanctions** for euro-area non-compliance remain available in principle, though rarely applied in practice (a longstanding SGP enforcement weakness that the reform did not resolve).
