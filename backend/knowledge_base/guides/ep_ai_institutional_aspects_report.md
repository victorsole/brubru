# European Parliament report on the institutional aspects of artificial intelligence (2025/2118(INI))

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is:** an **own-initiative report (INI)** of the European Parliament's **Committee on Constitutional Affairs (AFCO)**, "on the institutional aspects of Artificial Intelligence in the context of the European integration", procedure **2025/2118(INI)**. It is a **non-binding resolution, NOT legislation**: it changes no law and creates no body by itself. It treats AI as a **constitutional question** (institutional balance, democratic scrutiny, elections, the Treaties), NOT as product regulation, which is the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).
- **Status (checked 24 September 2026):** **awaiting the AFCO committee vote**. AFCO has the draft report, the amendments and both opinions on its **1 October 2026** agenda; no AFCO compromise amendments or voting list were published by 24 September, so **1 October is NOT confirmed as the vote**. OEIL's **indicative** plenary date: **19 October 2026** (Strasbourg). Never state that Parliament has adopted it.
- **Rapporteur:** **Emmanouil Kefalogiannis** (European People's Party, EPP, Greece). **Shadow rapporteurs:** Vytenis Andriukaitis (Socialists and Democrats, S&D), Ernő Schaller-Baross (Patriots for Europe, PfE), Gheorghe Piperea (European Conservatives and Reformists, ECR), Helmut Brandstätter (Renew Europe), Leoluca Orlando (Greens/European Free Alliance, Greens/EFA), Christine Anderson (Europe of Sovereign Nations, ESN). OEIL lists no shadow for The Left.
- **Documents:** draft report **PE779.285** (25 March 2026; 23 paragraphs, 13 recitals); **180 AFCO amendments**, PE787.899 (29 April 2026).
- **Opinions, both ADOPTED:** Civil Liberties (LIBE), rapporteur Lukas Mandl (EPP), **24 June 2026, 66 in favour, 0 against, 4 abstentions**; Internal Market (IMCO), rapporteur Gheorghe Piperea (ECR), **25 June 2026, 33 in favour, 5 against, 1 abstention**.
- **Its one concrete institutional proposal: a "European Parliament AI Observatory"**, a permanent analytical service INSIDE Parliament's administration, across committees, **budget-neutral**, with **NO regulatory or enforcement powers**. It is **NOT the Commission's AI Office** and must not duplicate it. The draft asks the Chair of the AI Board (the Member States' forum under the AI Act) to invite it, under Article 65(2) of the AI Act.
- **Other asks:** a structured Parliament-Commission-Council dialogue on AI's institutional implications; Parliament's participation in global AI forums (UN "AI for Good", OECD, Council of Europe, G7/G20) "in accordance with sincere cooperation", with the Commission's competences respected; a **global international convention or treaty on AI** (nuclear technology as the analogy); AI tools to **monitor how EU law is transposed and applied** in Member States; systematic assessment of AI risks to **elections**, preparing a **reform of European electoral law under Article 223 TFEU** (the 2018 electoral act, Council Decision 2018/994, is called "ill-suited" to AI); and a "structured and inclusive reflection on the possible evolution of the **Treaties** in the medium to long term".
- **Most contested paragraph: 22, Treaty change (12 amendments, 2 deletions).** Delete: Renew (Brandstätter, Gozi) and PfE (Schaller-Baross and co-signers). ESN: keep any response "within the competences conferred by the existing Treaties". Renew ALSO tabled the opposite, reform "more necessary and pressing than ever". The rapporteur himself softens "is necessary" to "may be necessary".
- **The Observatory (paragraph 11, 9 amendments):** PfE wants "consideration" of it; ESN wants an assessment of whether existing capacity suffices; Renew "to consider"; S&D and ECR keep "establishment"; Greens/EFA delete its AI Board seat (paragraph 17) and the AI-monitoring paragraphs 13-14.
- Details and sources below. Related: `ai_act_regulation`, `afco_institutional_framework_review`.

## The draft report (PE779.285, 25 March 2026)

**Legal hooks cited:** Articles 2, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14 TEU; Articles 16, 114, 173, 179, 180 and 181 TFEU; the Charter; the Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI (signed 5 September 2024); the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 65(2) and 70); the AI Continent Action Plan (COM(2025) 165) and Apply AI Strategy (COM(2025) 723); Council conclusions of 21 May 2024 and 20 November 2025; Rule 55 and Annex VI of Parliament's Rules of Procedure (AFCO's remit).

**Five ideas:**
1. **AI governance is constitutional, not only technical** (paragraphs 1-2): it affects the exercise of public authority, democratic legitimacy and the institutional balance of Article 13 TEU; AI in the institutions must stay human-centred and aligned with Article 2 TEU.
2. **The European Parliament AI Observatory** (paragraphs 11-17, recitals H-I): analytical support for committees on AI governance, institutional balance and oversight; tracks AI use in public administration and gaps, overlaps and inconsistencies in the EU's AI law; builds Parliament's institutional memory; cooperates with national parliaments and national competent authorities (Article 70 AI Act).
3. **Parliament in global AI governance** (paragraphs 3-9), including a dedicated international treaty on AI (paragraph 8) and further reflection beyond the Council of Europe convention (paragraph 9).
4. **Elections** (paragraphs 19-20): AI risks to electoral integrity; the Observatory to support monitoring of elections and European political parties; groundwork for electoral-law reform under Article 223 TFEU.
5. **Treaty change** (paragraphs 21-22): digital and AI governance "progressively testing the adequacy" of Treaty provisions on competences, accountability and scrutiny.

The explanatory statement: "artificial intelligence governance is not solely a matter of technical regulation but a constitutional question."

## The opinions

- **LIBE** (PE787.655, Lukas Mandl): adopted 24 June 2026, 66-0-4. Abstentions: ESN (Alexander Sell, Milan Uhrík, Roberto Vannacci) and PfE (Jorge Buxadé Villalba). Its amendments add citations of the Digital Omnibus on AI (COM(2025) 836) and the wider Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837).
- **IMCO** (PE787.702, Gheorghe Piperea): adopted 25 June 2026, 33-5-1. Against: ESN (Arno Bausemer, Volker Schnurrbusch), PfE (Elisabeth Dieringer, Pál Szekeres), The Left (Hanna Gedin); abstention PfE (Jaroslav Bžoch). Its amendments add competitiveness, SME and "avoid unnecessary burdens" language and an integrated digital single market.

## How to read it

- The committee vote should be comfortable: neither opinion drew more than 5 votes against. The fight is over wording.
- Watch paragraph 22: a hedged Treaty reference ("may be necessary") is the likely landing.
- The Observatory is likely to survive in some form; whether its AI Board seat survives is the open point.
- Beyond AFCO, it is Parliament's opening position on electoral-law reform, any future Treaty-change process, and Parliament's place next to the AI Office and AI Board while the AI Act is being simplified.
