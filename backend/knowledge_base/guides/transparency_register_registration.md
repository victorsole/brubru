# EU Transparency Register: how to register, and what registration obliges you to do

## QUICK FACTS

- **What this guide is:** the operational half of the EU Transparency Register. The companion guide `transparency_register_eu` explains what the register *is* (the 2021 Interinstitutional Agreement, the six sections, the institutional architecture). This guide answers the practical questions: am I eligible, how do I register, what do I have to declare, and what am I committed to afterwards.
- **Source:** the Joint Transparency Register Secretariat's official Guidelines, https://transparency-register.europa.eu/guidelines_en (Part I general information, Part II how to register, Part III registrants' responsibilities). Revised for the new register released 2 April 2024.
- **Registration is formally VOLUNTARY, and effectively mandatory in defined situations.** The Guidelines say so in as many words: registration "is voluntary and does not imply that the signatory institutions have given registrants any form of recognition, approval or accreditation." What makes it binding is **conditionality**: an institution decides a given activity requires prior registration, and registration then becomes a prerequisite for that activity. Each institution applies this individually, so the measures differ between them.
- **Who must register:** any natural or legal person, or formal or informal group, network or association, carrying out activities intended to influence the formulation or implementation of EU policy or legislation, or the decision-making of any EU institution, body, office or agency. Eligibility does not depend on legal form, size, location, type or goals, nor on the communication channel used, nor on where the activity is carried out.
- **The single registration principle:** register **once**. A multinational, or an NGO with chapters in several countries, covers the network, corporate group or similar entities in one entry, including their activities and resources.
- **How long it takes:** you register online, logging in to your restricted space with an **EU Login** account. With the information to hand the form takes roughly **20 to 30 minutes** across 15 headings.
- **APP number is not REG number.** On starting the form you get an application number (`APP xxxx`). Only after the Secretariat assesses and validates it do you receive the public registration number, format `REG xxxxxxxx-yy`. Your entry is not published until validation.
- **Unsaved applications expire after 21 days.** If you do not submit, the data entered is lost.
- **Two obligations after you are in:** observe the **code of conduct** (16 rules, all equally weighted), and carry out a **compulsory annual update** every 12 months, validating all data and recommitting to the code. Reminders go to the contact person 4 weeks and 2 weeks before the deadline.
- **Worst case for breach:** removal, plus a ban on re-registering of between **20 working days and 2 years**, and publication of the measure on the register's website.

## 1. Eligibility: the test, and the exclusions people get wrong

Activities are "covered by the register" when carried out with the objective of influencing the formulation or implementation of EU policy or legislation, or the decision-making processes of the EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies. Article 3(2) of the IIA gives examples:

- organising or participating in meetings, conferences or events, and similar contacts with the institutions
- contributing to or participating in consultations, hearings and similar initiatives, including expert groups and intergroups
- organising communication campaigns, platforms, networks and grassroots initiatives
- preparing or commissioning policy and position papers, **amendments**, opinion polls, surveys, open letters and other communication material, and commissioning or carrying out research

The register exists to show **organised or collective** interests, not the personal interests of an individual acting strictly in a personal capacity and not in association with others.

**Monitoring is the distinction most often misread.** Following or monitoring EU law and policy is *not* covered when done for academic or journalistic purposes, out of personal interest, or to exercise a right (a researcher's or journalist's interview, or petitioning the European Parliament). It **is** covered when it forms part of proactive intelligence gathering offered as part of a range of services, as by a public affairs provider, because it can lead to direct contact with the institutions, and it should then be declared.

**Activities beginning or under preparation still qualify**, provided you can give appropriate detail of the specific activities you are about to engage in.

### The legal-advice exclusion, stated precisely

Article 4 of the IIA excludes the provision of legal and other professional advice where it consists of:

1. representing clients in conciliation or mediation aimed at preventing a dispute going before a judicial or administrative body;
2. advice given to help clients ensure their activities comply with the existing legal framework; or
3. representing clients and safeguarding their fundamental or procedural rights, such as the right to be heard, to a fair trial and to a defence in administrative proceedings.

**The corollary matters as much as the rule.** Law firms and individual lawyers carrying out representation activities before the EU institutions on behalf of clients, outside those three situations, **do** qualify as interest representatives and are eligible to be entered in the register. "We are a law firm" is not an exemption.

## 2. Data quality, and what the Secretariat actually does with your application

Data entered must be complete, accurate and coherent, and is public except for fields marked with an eye-slash sign. The Secretariat screens every new application and assesses (i) eligibility and (ii) the quality of the data. It may ask for additional information to substantiate eligibility or improve data quality. If the application satisfies the requirements it is validated and published; either way you are told the outcome.

The financial information required depends on the type of interest you represent, selected at heading 8 and supplied at heading 14.

## 3. The 15 headings of the form

| Heading | What it asks |
|---|---|
| 1 | Applicant name and form (legal status, or a network/platform with no legal status) |
| 2 | Contact details, head office and EU liaison office |
| 3 | Person with legal responsibility |
| 4 | Person in charge of EU relations |
| 5 | Contact person (receives validation and reminder emails) |
| 6 | Persons with access to the registration |
| 7 | Goals and remit (free text) |
| 8 | **Interests represented**, which determines the financial data at heading 14 |
| 9 | Specific activities covered by the register |
| 10 | Number of people involved in the heading 9 activities |
| 11 | Fields of interest |
| 12 | Membership and affiliation (read with the single registration principle) |
| 13 | Category of registration |
| 14 | **Financial data**, in a variant set by the heading 8 choice |
| 15 | Code of conduct confirmation |

**Heading 8 drives heading 14.** Choose by principal activity, not merely legal status, among: promoting your own or your members' interests; advancing the interests of clients as an intermediary; or not representing commercial interests.

**Newly formed entities** may tick "no financial year closed" and defer financial data to the first or at latest the next annual update, but this status is usable for a maximum of **24 months**.

## 4. Submission and validation

Once the 15 headings are complete, the Summary page offers a **Run check** button that tests completeness; it is available at any point while filling the form. On Submit, the contact person gets an acknowledgement and the status becomes "submitted". You must answer any Secretariat requests for further information before validation. On validation the contact person receives the identification details. **Do not leave it unsubmitted: entered data is deleted after 21 days.**

## 5. Conditionality: what registration actually unlocks

Registration becomes a prerequisite wherever an institution has adopted a conditionality measure. Documented examples:

- **Commission:** Commissioners and members of their cabinets meet only registered interest representatives.
- **European Parliament:** registration is required to apply for long-term accreditation, to take part in intergroups, or to speak at public hearings of Parliament committees.
- **Agencies:** any EU institution, body, office or agency may adopt its own measure. The Guidelines give the worked example of an agency making meetings with its senior management conditional on registration, notified to the Management Board, so an organisation dealing only with that agency is still caught.
- **Member States** may adopt measures under national law concerning their permanent representations to the EU.

Separately, **complementary transparency measures** encourage registration without requiring it: providing a REG number when responding to a Commission public consultation or roadmap is recommended, not compulsory, and registrants can opt into institutional news alerts by field of interest.

All conditionality and complementary measures are published on the register's "Conditionality" page.

## 6. After registration: the standing obligations

- **Observe the code of conduct at all times.** 16 rules and principles, all equally important, which you must communicate to your staff and representatives. Observance is a condition of eligibility: only registrants observing the code remain in the register.
- **Annual update, every 12 months**, from the first year of registration: validate all data and recommit to the code. The contact person is reminded 4 and 2 weeks ahead. An early annual update can be used deliberately to reset the deadline.
- **Partial updates** let you revise specific headings, such as financial information, without moving the annual deadline. They do **not** discharge the annual update obligation.
- **Be transparent in contact with the institutions**: you are encouraged to state that you are registered and give your REG number.
- **Cooperate with the Secretariat**, which may monitor or investigate any registration at any time.

### Complaints, sanctions and appeal

Any natural or legal person may lodge a complaint alleging non-observance of the code. The Secretariat investigates under Annex III of the IIA. A decision of non-observance can lead to removal and, depending on seriousness, to a **ban on re-registering of 20 working days to 2 years** and **publication of the measure** on the register website.

Registrants may request review by the register's **Management Board** within **20 working days** of notification. Beyond that, remedies are an appeal to the **Court of Justice** or a complaint to the **European Ombudsman**.

The Secretariat may also suspend registrations containing unwarranted or self-promotional statements, or content contradicting the EU's fundamental values under Article 2 TEU, such as incitement to hatred or racism.
