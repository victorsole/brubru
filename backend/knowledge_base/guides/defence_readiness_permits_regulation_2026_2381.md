# Defence readiness permits, Regulation (EU) 2026/2381: a 42-working-day limit and tacit approval

## QUICK FACTS
- **NOT YET in force on 3 November 2026. It will enter into force on 23 November 2026, and even then applies ONLY to permit applications submitted from 24 February 2027** (Article 14). No application is covered before that date. OJ L of 3 November 2026; signed 21 October 2026. CELEX 32026R2381, ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/2381/oj , procedure 2025/0172(COD), COM(2025) 821.
- **Who decides what qualifies:** each Member State determines which projects are "defence readiness projects" (Article 3). There is no EU list.
- **Single point of contact:** each Member State designates one by 24 February 2027 (Article 4(1)); a decentralised State may have several, with one interface per project (Article 4(2)).
- **The clock, two different numbers:** completeness check within **14 working days** (Article 7(1)). Permit-granting process **at most 42 working days** from notification of a complete application (Article 8(1)). The authority may extend it twice, by up to 60 working days, for exceptional nature, complexity, location or size, or exceptional risks to health, safety or the environment: **total at most 102 working days** (Article 8(5)). 42 is the rule, 102 the exception. Member States may set shorter limits (Article 8(7)).
- **Tacit approval (Article 8(8)):** with no outcome within the limit, the permits "shall be deemed to be granted". The contact point informs the promoter within 8 working days and states any conditions; **affected parties may challenge** such permits (Article 8(9)).
- **Opt-out (Article 8(10)):** by national law adopted by **24 November 2027**, Member States may exclude tacit approval in strictly limited and exceptional cases, case by case, on evidence of **grave risk to human health or national security**. Without that national law, tacit approval applies.
- **It does NOT remove other duties:** a recital says tacit approval is "without prejudice to Member States' obligations to perform case-by-case examinations, assessment procedures or public consultations" under EU or national law; Article 11 keeps the Aarhus and Espoo Convention obligations. **How a deemed permit works while an assessment is unfinished is not spelled out in the articles read: do not present it as settled, and suggest a lawyer.**
- **Courts:** disputes are treated as urgent where national law has urgency procedures, respecting the right to an effective remedy (Article 10).
- **Who is bound:** Member States and their authorities. Companies are "project promoters" (Article 2(8)) and benefit; this Regulation sets no compliance deadline for them.
- **Follow-up:** annual statistics including tacit approvals (Article 12); Commission evaluation by 31 December 2028, then every four years (Article 13).
