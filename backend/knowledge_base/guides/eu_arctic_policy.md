# EU Arctic Policy and the 2026 Strategy Update

## QUICK FACTS
- **Current policy:** Joint Communication *"A stronger EU engagement for a greener, peaceful and prosperous Arctic"*, **13 October 2021** — CELEX **52021JC0027**. A Joint Communication of the Commission and the High Representative, **not a legislative act**: it has no CELEX-numbered obligations and nothing in it binds an operator.
- **STATUS: being updated during 2026.** The Commission has said it will update the Arctic policy in the light of the changed geopolitical and geoeconomic context, building on the 2021 Communication.
- **Call for evidence + public consultation:** launched **23 December 2025**, closed **16 March 2026**. Managed by **DG MARE** (Maritime Affairs and Fisheries).
- **What the update covers:** climate change, sustainable development, environmental protection, **security**, and international cooperation — with security, defence, connectivity and economic security named as additions to the 2021 scope.
- **EU Arctic Forum 2026:** Brussels, **1-2 September 2026**, with side events on **3 September**, run by the Commission with the **EEAS**, alongside the **Indigenous Peoples' Dialogue** and the **Arctic Youth Dialogue**.
- **Special Adviser for EU-Arctic relations:** **Jyrki Katainen**, designated by the Commission (IP/26/799), reporting to **Commissioner Jozef Síkela** (International Partnerships).
- **Adoption date of the updated policy: NOT ANNOUNCED.** The Commission has said only that future action will be formulated in the light of the consultation results. **Do not state a date for it.**

## What this is, and what it is not

The EU's Arctic policy is a **strategy, not a statute**. The 2021 Joint Communication sets out political
commitments and priorities; it creates no obligations on companies and confers no rights. When a user
asks "what does the EU Arctic law require", the correct answer is that **there is no Arctic law**: the
policy is delivered through instruments that exist elsewhere — the climate acquis, fisheries management,
research funding, space and connectivity programmes, and external action.

Three EU Member States have Arctic territory (Denmark through Greenland, Finland and Sweden), and the EU
is also an Arctic actor through the European Economic Area, Svalbard-related science, and its
observer-adjacent engagement with the Arctic Council.

## Why it is being updated in 2026

The 2021 text was written before Finland and Sweden joined NATO and before the security picture in the
European Arctic changed. The stated reason for the update is the **changed geopolitical and geoeconomic
context**. The scope grows accordingly: the 2021 themes of climate, sustainable development and
environmental protection remain, and **security, defence, connectivity and economic security** are added.

That is the shift to watch. An Arctic policy that used to be read as environmental and scientific is
being re-framed around security and strategic dependency, which changes which DGs own it and which
instruments deliver it.

## The 2026 process, in order

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| **13 October 2021** | Joint Communication 52021JC0027 adopted |
| **23 December 2025** | Call for evidence and public consultation opened (DG MARE) |
| **16 March 2026** | Consultation closed |
| **1-2 September 2026** | EU Arctic Forum, Brussels, with the Indigenous Peoples' Dialogue and the Arctic Youth Dialogue; side events 3 September |
| **not announced** | Adoption of the updated policy |

Consulted: EU Member States, **Indigenous and local communities**, industry, civil society and academia.
The Indigenous Peoples' Dialogue is a standing feature of EU Arctic engagement, not a one-off.

## Governance

**Commissioner Jozef Síkela** (International Partnerships) carries the file politically and opened the
2026 Forum. **DG MARE** ran the consultation. The **EEAS** co-organises the Forum, which reflects the
external-action half of the policy. **Jyrki Katainen** — a former Finnish Prime Minister and former
Commission Vice-President — was designated **Special Adviser for EU-Arctic relations** and reports to
Síkela. A Special Adviser is an appointed personal adviser, not an institution, and holds no decision
power of their own.

## What Brubru should say when asked

- **"Is there an EU Arctic law?"** No. There is a Joint Communication and an ongoing policy update.
- **"When will the new Arctic strategy be adopted?"** Not announced. Say so rather than estimating.
- **"What will change?"** The stated additions are security, defence, connectivity and economic security,
  on top of the 2021 climate, sustainable-development and environmental-protection themes.
- **"Can I still respond to the consultation?"** No — it closed on 16 March 2026.
