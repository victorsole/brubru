## What this is, and what it is not

The EU's Arctic policy is a **strategy, not a statute**. The 2021 Joint Communication sets out political
commitments and priorities; it creates no obligations on companies and confers no rights. When a user
asks "what does the EU Arctic law require", the correct answer is that **there is no Arctic law**: the
policy is delivered through instruments that exist elsewhere, the climate acquis, fisheries management,
research funding, space and connectivity programmes, and external action.

Three EU Member States have Arctic territory (Denmark through Greenland, Finland and Sweden), and the EU
is also an Arctic actor through the European Economic Area, Svalbard-related science, and its
observer-adjacent engagement with the Arctic Council.

## Why it is being updated in 2026

The 2021 text was written before Finland and Sweden joined NATO and before the security picture in the
European Arctic changed. The stated reason for the update is the **changed geopolitical and geoeconomic
context**. The scope grows accordingly: the 2021 themes of climate, sustainable development and
environmental protection remain, and **security, defence, connectivity and economic security** are added.

That is the shift to watch. An Arctic policy that used to be read as environmental and scientific is
being re-framed around security and strategic dependency, which changes which DGs own it and which
instruments deliver it.

## Governance

On the Commission's tentative agenda the new strategy is listed under the **President's** responsibility,
and Costa describes it as updated jointly by **the Commission and the EEAS**, and **HR/VP Kallas** announced
its October timing in Rovaniemi. **Commissioner Jozef Síkela** (International Partnerships) opened the
2026 Forum and attended the Rovaniemi summit. **DG MARE** ran the consultation. The **EEAS** co-organises the Forum, which reflects the
external-action half of the policy. **Jyrki Katainen**, a former Finnish Prime Minister and former
Commission Vice-President, was designated **Special Adviser for EU-Arctic relations** and reports to
Síkela. A Special Adviser is an appointed personal adviser, not an institution, and holds no decision
power of their own.

## What Brubru should say when asked

- **"Is there an EU Arctic law?"** No. There is a Joint Communication and an ongoing policy update.
- **"When will the new Arctic strategy be adopted?"** The Commission currently plans it for the College
  meeting of 20 October 2026 in Strasbourg (tentative agenda, marked tbc); Kallas said "later in October".
  Hedge until adoption is confirmed.
- **"What will be new?"** Security "at its core" (Kallas), concrete projects, capabilities such as icebreakers,
  and a link to the next long-term EU budget (Costa).
- **"What will change?"** The stated additions are security, defence, connectivity and economic security,
  on top of the 2021 climate, sustainable-development and environmental-protection themes.
- **"Can I still respond to the consultation?"** No, it closed on 16 March 2026.
