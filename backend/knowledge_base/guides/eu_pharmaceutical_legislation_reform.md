- **The amendment counts predicted the outcome.** The most contested provisions in committee are exactly the ones the 2026 agreement settled. Directive **Article 85** (patents and SPCs) 85 amendments; **Article 81**, which carried the Commission's six-year data protection baseline, **73** and is now **deleted in full** with eight years inserted into Article 80; **Article 83** (unmet medical need) 73, kept as the trigger for the 12-month prolongation; **Article 63** (package leaflet and electronic product information) 69. Regulation **Article 40**, the transferable voucher, **55**, with several members moving to delete it outright, and it survived capped at EUR 490 million; **Article 71** (orphan market exclusivity) 55, settled as nine, eleven and four years; **Article 72** (modulating those periods) 47.
- **Council consolidated texts**: **6366/26** (Regulation) and **6367/26** (Directive), both LIMITE and carrying tracked changes. Flattening them to plain text merges deletions with insertions, so Article 80(2) reads "for a period of two yearsone year". Resolve the revision marks before quoting any figure.
- **The six-year baseline was rejected outright.** The Commission proposed cutting regulatory data protection to six years with earned extensions. In the agreed Directive, Article 81(1), which carried the six-year rule, is **deleted in full**, and **"eight years" is an insertion** into Article 80(1). Plus one additional year under Article 41(1) of the revised Regulation. Market protection then falls from the proposed two years to **one year**, prolongable by 12 months.
- **The 12-month market-protection prolongation** is earned by any one of: an unmet medical need under Article 83; or, for a new active substance, trials using a relevant evidence-based comparator agreed with the Agency plus filing in the Union first (or within 90 days of a first filing elsewhere); or comparator trials plus trials run in more than one Member State; or, where a comparator is not possible or appropriate, multi-Member-State trials plus EU-first filing. For a conditional marketing authorisation the unmet-medical-need prolongation applies only if a full authorisation follows within four years.
- **Orphan market exclusivity is tiered (Article 71 of the agreed Regulation)**: **nine years** generally, **eleven years** for **breakthrough** orphans under Article 70, **four years** for orphans authorised on well-established use. The top tier was renegotiated, not renumbered: the proposal gave ten years to orphans "addressing a high unmet medical need" and the agreed text raises it to eleven and renames the category. Several orphan authorisations for the same active substance share **one** exclusivity period, running from the first orphan authorisation in the Union.
- **The antimicrobial voucher is tightly capped (Article 40 of the agreed Regulation)**: the Commission may grant a transferable data exclusivity voucher to a "priority antimicrobial" by implementing act on the Agency's assessment, worth **12 additional months of data protection** for one authorised product. It may be sold, used **once**, on a **single centrally authorised product**, within that product's first four years of data protection. Used on any product other than the priority antimicrobial itself, it applies only in the **fifth or sixth year** of protection and only if that product's annual gross Union sales stayed below **EUR 490 million** in each of its first four years. The scheme is time-limited and capped by total voucher count to bound the cost to national health systems; Parliament and Council may extend either later.
- **Compulsory licensing overrides the protection.** Data and market protection are suspended for a compulsory licensee under **Regulation (EU) 2025/2645** on compulsory licensing for crisis management, or under national frameworks, for the duration and territory of the licence.
- **New transparency duty**: national competent authorities must publish the list of nationally authorised products under regulatory protection, showing the prolongation applied to each, and the Agency compiles a register of links to those lists. The regime becomes auditable from outside for the first time.

## Overview

The most comprehensive reform of EU pharmaceutical law in over 20 years. The Commission proposed replacing both the Directive on medicinal products for human use (2001/83/EC) and the Regulation on the centralised authorisation procedure (726/2004) with updated instruments addressing innovation incentives, supply chain resilience, antimicrobial resistance, and patient access.

The European Parliament adopted its first reading on 10 April 2024. After four trilogue sessions (June-December 2025), negotiators reached a provisional agreement on 11 December 2025. Formal adoption by Council and EP second reading are pending.

## Key Provisions (Provisional Agreement)

### Data Exclusivity and Market Protection
- 8 years regulatory data protection + 1 year market protection after marketing authorisation
- Maximum combined protection capped at 11 years
- Additional 12 months each for: unmet medical needs, new active substance meeting specific clinical trial conditions, new therapeutic indications with significant clinical benefits

### Orphan Medicines
- Breakthrough orphan products for diseases with no available treatment: up to 11 years market exclusivity
- Tiered system based on disease prevalence and clinical benefit

### Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)
- Transferable data exclusivity voucher: 12 additional months protection for priority antimicrobials
- Mandatory medical prescriptions for all antimicrobials
- Required antimicrobial stewardship plans during authorisation
- Mandatory environmental risk assessments for antimicrobial manufacturing

### Generic and Biosimilar Access
- Enhanced "Bolar" exemption: patent rights not infringed when studies/trials conducted for marketing authorisations or health technology assessments
- Day-one launch provisions for generics after data exclusivity expiry

### Supply Chain Resilience
- Companies must establish shortage prevention plans
- Dual monitoring system: national level + EU level (EMA)
- EMA maintains a critical medicines shortages list
- Links to the Critical Medicines Act (separate legislation)

### Compounding and Hospital Exemption
- **Magistral formulations:** Hospital pharmacies can now prepare compounded medicines up to 7 days in advance (current law requires individual prescription first). Applies only to pharmacies serving a hospital, based on estimated medical prescriptions
- **Partial harmonisation:** New directive tightens and partially harmonises compounding rules across Member States
- **Hospital ATMP exemption:** New data collection, reporting, and annual review obligations for ATMPs prepared under hospital exemption. EMA to assess whether adapted regulatory framework is needed for less complex ATMPs
- **Industrial-scale boundary:** Clarified that production exceeding individual patient needs triggers full GMP and marketing authorisation
- Full analysis: see `pharma_sector_regulatory_landscape.md`

### Regulatory Modernisation
- Electronic submission in common format
- Unlimited validity for marketing authorisations (no administrative renewal burden)
- Regulatory sandboxes for innovative therapy development (first time in EU pharma law)
- AI Act Service Desk for compliance support
- Streamlined EMA procedures
