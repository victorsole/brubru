## QUICK ANSWERS

**What does this Regulation do?** It reforms the three European Supervisory Authorities (EBA, EIOPA, ESMA) and amends three related financial regulations (MiFIR, the Benchmarks Regulation, and the transfer-of-funds/AML regulation). It is the centrepiece of the wider "ESAs Review" package (alongside Reg (EU) 2019/2176 on the ESRB and Directive (EU) 2019/2177).

**What is the single biggest change?** EBA (European Banking Authority) becomes the EU's anti-money-laundering and countering-terrorist-financing (AML/CFT) hub for the **entire financial sector**, not just banking. New EBA Article 9a gives it a leading, coordinating and monitoring role, a central AML database, and a permanent internal AML committee of all Member States' AML authorities. New Article 9b lets EBA request a national authority to investigate breaches and consider sanctions (the authority must respond within 10 working days). These AML powers were a provisional solution pending a Commission assessment due by 11 January 2022.

**What are the six regulations it amends, and when do they apply?**

| 2 | Reg (EU) No 1094/2010 (EIOPA founding reg) | 1 January 2020 |
| 3 | Reg (EU) No 1095/2010 (ESMA founding reg) | 1 January 2020 |
| 4 | Reg (EU) No 600/2014 (MiFIR) | 1 January 2022 |
| 5 | Reg (EU) 2016/1011 (Benchmarks Regulation) | 1 January 2022 |
| 6 | Reg (EU) 2015/847 (transfers of funds) | 1 January 2020 |

**What new convergence tools do the ESAs get?** Union strategic supervisory priorities (Article 29a: up to two priorities at least every three years by 31 March); strengthened peer reviews with ad hoc peer review committees (Article 30); a public web-based questions-and-answers tool (Article 16b); no-action letters (Article 9c); whistleblower / reporting-person protection (Article 17a, linked to the Whistleblower Directive (EU) 2019/1937); a fitness-and-propriety information-exchange system across the three ESAs (Article 31a); coordination groups (Article 45b); and strengthened third-country equivalence monitoring (Article 33).

**What does ESMA gain directly (from 1 January 2022)?** Direct authorisation and supervision of data reporting services providers (APAs, CTPs, ARMs) transferred from national authorities to ESMA via MiFIR (new Articles 48a to 48n), plus direct data-gathering powers and fining powers; and Union-level supervision of critical benchmarks and recognition of third-country benchmark administrators via the Benchmarks Regulation.

**Does it deal with sustainable finance?** Yes. The ESAs must identify and report ESG-related risks to financial stability, consider them in Union-wide stress tests, and provide guidance on embedding sustainability in EU financial legislation (recitals 7 to 10). It also strengthens their fintech oversight role.

**Is it still in force?** Yes. It is an amending regulation, so its changes are now embedded in the six target regulations. The AML mandate it gave EBA has since been complemented by the separate EU AML package establishing the Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA), which is NOT part of this regulation.

## OVERVIEW

Regulation (EU) 2019/2175 is the "ESAs Review": the first major overhaul of the European System of Financial Supervision (ESFS) since it became operational in January 2011. The ESFS was created on the recommendations of the de Larosière group after the financial crisis, combining micro-prudential supervision (the three ESAs) with macro-prudential supervision (the ESRB).

The review pursued three goals: concentrate the financial sector's AML/CFT supervision in EBA after a series of European bank money-laundering scandals; sharpen the ESAs' supervisory-convergence toolkit; and equip the system for sustainable finance and technological innovation. It also expanded ESMA's direct supervisory remit to trading-data providers and critical benchmarks.

It is an omnibus amending regulation built on Article 114 TFEU and adopted under the ordinary legislative procedure. The changes apply in two waves: the authority-level and AML changes (Articles 1, 2, 3, 6) from 1 January 2020, and the markets and benchmarks changes (Articles 4, 5) from 1 January 2022.

## STRUCTURE

- **Preamble** (59 recitals): de Larosière origins, AML rationale, convergence tools, sustainable finance, fintech, ESMA direct supervision, governance, equivalence.
- **Article 1** amends the EBA founding regulation (the largest set of changes, including the AML articles 9a/9b/9c and the convergence tools).
- **Article 2** amends the EIOPA founding regulation (mirrors the convergence tools).
- **Article 3** amends the ESMA founding regulation (mirrors the convergence tools).
- **Article 4** amends MiFIR (Regulation (EU) No 600/2014): ESMA direct supervision of data reporting services providers.
- **Article 5** amends the Benchmarks Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/1011): ESMA supervision of critical benchmarks and third-country administrators.
- **Article 6** amends the transfer-of-funds regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/847): EBA AML guidelines.
- **Article 7** entry into force and staggered application.

## KEY NUMBERS

- 6 regulations amended; 3 ESAs reformed.
- Application waves: 1 January 2020 (Articles 1, 2, 3, 6) and 1 January 2022 (Articles 4, 5).
- Union strategic supervisory priorities: up to 2, at least every 3 years (by 31 March).
- EBA AML request response time for competent authorities: 10 working days.
- AML regulatory technical standards deadline: 31 December 2020.
- Commission AML assessment due: 11 January 2022.
- Board of Supervisors transparency record to the European Parliament: within 6 weeks of each meeting.
- Management Board: Chairperson plus 6 members, term 2.5 years (renewable once).
