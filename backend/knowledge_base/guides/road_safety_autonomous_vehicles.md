Guide for professionals working on road safety, advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), autonomous/automated driving, and vehicle type-approval in the EU. Covers the regulatory framework from vehicle safety requirements through to connected and automated mobility.

## Core Vehicle Safety Framework

### General Safety Regulation (GSR) - Regulation (EU) 2019/2144
CELEX: 32019R2144

The cornerstone regulation making advanced safety features mandatory in all new vehicles. Entered into force 6 July 2022 for new type-approvals, and 7 July 2024 for all new vehicles sold.

**Mandatory ADAS systems (Annex II):**

| System | Abbreviation | Category |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Intelligent Speed Assistance | ISA | M1, N1 |
| Alcohol interlock installation facilitation | AIF | M1, N1 |
| Drowsiness and attention warning | DAW | M1, N1 |
| Advanced driver distraction warning | ADDW | M1, N1 |
| Emergency stop signal | ESS | M1, N1, M2, M3, N2, N3 |
| Reversing detection | REV | M1, N1, M2, M3, N2, N3 |
| Event data recorder | EDR | M1, N1 |
| Accurate tyre pressure monitoring | TPMS | M1, N1 (extended) |
| Advanced emergency braking system | AEBS | M1, N1 (new), M2, M3, N2, N3 |
| Emergency lane-keeping system | ELKS | M1, N1 |
| Driver availability monitoring | DAM | For vehicles with automated driving systems |
| Vulnerable road user detection | VRU | N2, N3 (trucks) |
| Lateral warning device | LWD | N2, N3 (trucks) |

**Key dates:**
- 6 July 2022: New type-approvals must include mandatory systems
- 7 July 2024: All new vehicles sold must include mandatory systems
- Delegated acts specify technical requirements for each system

**Delegated and implementing acts under GSR:**
- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/1341 (cyber security and software updates)
- Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/545 (ISA, EDR, AEBS, ESS for M1/N1)
- Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/1426 (detailed provisions for AEBS, ELKS, etc.)

### Type-Approval Framework - Regulation (EU) 2018/858
CELEX: 32018R0858

The overarching framework for EU motor vehicle type-approval. Replaced Directive 2007/46/EC. All vehicles, systems, components and separate technical units must be type-approved before being placed on the EU market.

Key provisions:
- **Market surveillance** requirements (Chapter XI) -- strengthened post-Dieselgate
- **National authorities** required to perform compliance verification tests
- **Recall procedures** for vehicles with serious safety risks
- References UNECE regulations for technical requirements
- Applies to M, N, O category vehicles

### Vehicle Categories Reference

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| M1 | Passenger vehicles (up to 8 seats + driver) |
| M2 | Buses/minibuses (up to 5 tonnes) |
| M3 | Buses/coaches (over 5 tonnes) |
| N1 | Light commercial vehicles (up to 3.5 tonnes) |
| N2 | Medium commercial vehicles (3.5-12 tonnes) |
| N3 | Heavy commercial vehicles (over 12 tonnes) |
| L | Two/three-wheel vehicles, quadricycles |

## Automated and Autonomous Driving

### EU Framework for Automated Driving

The EU approach to automated driving regulation operates at multiple levels:

**SAE Automation Levels referenced in EU law:**
- Level 0-2: Driver assistance (ADAS) -- regulated under GSR 2019/2144
- Level 3: Conditional automation -- driver must be ready to take over
- Level 4: High automation -- vehicle handles all driving in defined conditions
- Level 5: Full automation -- no human intervention needed

**Current regulatory status (as of 2025):**
- Levels 0-2: Fully regulated and mandatory under GSR
- Level 3: Permitted in some Member States under national law + UNECE R157
- Level 4-5: Framework under development at EU level

### UNECE Regulations (Referenced in EU Type-Approval)

The EU incorporates UNECE (United Nations Economic Commission for Europe) regulations through the type-approval framework:

| UNECE Reg. | Subject | Relevance |
|------------|---------|-----------|
| R13-H | Braking (passenger cars) | Foundation for AEBS requirements |
| R79 | Steering equipment | Governs automated steering functions (ALKS, lane keeping) |
| R131 | AEBS (heavy vehicles) | Advanced emergency braking for trucks/buses |
| R152 | AEBS (light vehicles) | Advanced emergency braking for cars |
| R157 | ALKS (Automated Lane Keeping Systems) | First regulation for Level 3 automated driving (motorway, up to 60 km/h, extended to 130 km/h) |
| R159 | MOIS (Moving Off Information System) | Vulnerable road user detection for trucks |

**R157 (ALKS) is particularly significant:** It is the first international regulation to permit a Level 3 automated driving function. Originally limited to 60 km/h, an amendment extended the speed limit to 130 km/h.

### Proposed Regulation on Automated Driving Systems

The European Commission is developing a comprehensive framework for automated driving:
- Complements UNECE regulations with EU-specific requirements
- Addresses liability, data access, cybersecurity, and market surveillance
- Connected to the revision of the Motor Insurance Directive (2021/0381(COD))

### Connected and Automated Mobility (CAM)

EU strategy documents and actions:
- **Communication COM(2018) 283**: "On the road to automated mobility"
- **CCAM Partnership** (Connected, Cooperative and Automated Mobility): Horizon Europe partnership funding R&D
- **C-ITS (Cooperative ITS) Delegated Regulation**: Day-1 C-ITS services (hazardous location warnings, traffic conditions)

## Road Infrastructure Safety

### Road Infrastructure Safety Management Directive - Directive (EU) 2019/1936
CELEX: 32019L1936

Amends Directive 2008/96/EC. Extends safety management procedures beyond the TEN-T network to motorways and primary roads. Key elements:
- Network-wide road safety assessments
- Road safety audits and inspections
- Consideration of vulnerable road users (pedestrians, cyclists)
- Use of digital tools for road safety management

### Cross-Border Enforcement Directive - Directive (EU) 2024/1258
CELEX: 32024L1258

Facilitates cross-border exchange of information on road-safety-related traffic offences. Replaces Directive 2015/413/EU. Covers:
- Speeding
- Drink-driving and drug-driving
- Non-use of seat belt
- Running a red light
- Use of mobile phone while driving
- Driving in a forbidden lane
- New: overloading, dangerous overtaking, failing to keep safe distance

### Driving Licence Directive Revision
Procedure: 2023/0053(COD)

Commission proposal COM(2023) 127 to modernise driving licence rules:
- Digital driving licence (available on smartphone)
- Graduated licensing for novice drivers
- Provisions for vehicles with automated driving capabilities
- Self-assessment of fitness to drive for elderly drivers
- Cross-border validity improvements

## Cybersecurity for Vehicles

### UNECE R155 (Cyber Security Management System)
Referenced in EU type-approval since July 2022. All new vehicle types must have a certified Cyber Security Management System (CSMS).

### UNECE R156 (Software Update Management System)
Referenced in EU type-approval. Vehicles capable of over-the-air (OTA) software updates must have a certified Software Update Management System (SUMS).

### Cyber Resilience Act - Regulation (EU) 2024/2847
CELEX: 32024R2847

Horizontal cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements. Motor vehicles are excluded (covered by type-approval/UNECE), but connected vehicle components and aftermarket digital products may fall under CRA.

## Liability and Insurance

### Motor Insurance Directive Revision - 2021/0381(COD)
Amending Directive 2009/103/EC. Addresses:
- Coverage of autonomous vehicles
- Definition of "use of vehicle" in automated mode
- Cross-border claims involving automated vehicles

### AI Liability Directive Proposal - 2022/0303(COD)
Would create fault-based liability framework applicable to AI systems including automated driving:
- Presumption of causality for AI-related damage
- Access to evidence held by AI system providers
- Applies to high-risk AI systems (which includes autonomous vehicles under AI Act Annex III)

## EU Road Safety Policy

### EU Road Safety Policy Framework 2021-2030
Goals:
- Zero road deaths by 2050 ("Vision Zero")
- Halve deaths and serious injuries by 2030 (vs 2019 baseline)
- "Safe System" approach: safe vehicles, safe roads, safe speeds, safe road users

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) monitored by the Commission:
- Speed: % of vehicles within limit
- Seat belt and helmet use
- Alcohol and drug-driving rates
- Vehicle safety rating (Euro NCAP)
- Infrastructure safety rating (EuroRAP)
- Emergency response time (eCall)

### eCall Regulation - Regulation (EU) 2015/758
CELEX: 32015R0758

Mandatory in-vehicle emergency call system for all new M1 and N1 vehicles since April 2018. Automatically calls 112 in case of serious accident, transmitting vehicle location.
