# EU-China Relations

## QUICK FACTS
- Relationship type: "partner, competitor, systemic rival" (2019 EU-China Strategic Outlook, endorsed by European Council)
- Bilateral trade volume: approx. EUR 750 billion/year (goods + services); China is EU's largest source of imports and second-largest trading partner overall after the US
- EU-China Comprehensive Agreement on Investment (CAI): concluded December 2020, suspended by EP May 2021 (retaliation for Xinjiang sanctions), not ratified
- Current status (2026): no CAI in force; bilateral relations managed through existing trade instruments and ad hoc summits
- High-level summits: EU-China Summit (Heads of State/Government level, typically annual); last held 7-8 December 2023 in Beijing
- Key EU concern axes: (1) trade imbalance + market access asymmetry, (2) subsidised Chinese exports (EVs, solar, steel), (3) technology transfer and security risks (Huawei, 5G), (4) Taiwan Strait/South China Sea, (5) China's support for Russia since February 2022 invasion
- Responsible Commissioner: Commissioner for Trade (Maroš Šefčovič); EVP for Clean/Competitive Transition (Teresa Ribera — FSR, anti-subsidy); VP for Foreign Affairs (Kaja Kallas)
- EP stance: increasingly hawkish since 2021 CAI suspension; several resolutions on Xinjiang, Hong Kong, Taiwan; DROI committee active
- College orientation debate (29 May 2026): EU-China strategic direction — PRESIDENT-led, feeds into June 2026 European Council conclusions on strategic autonomy

## EU-China Strategic Context

The EU's "partner, competitor, systemic rival" tripartite framing (2019) remains official policy but the competitive and rival dimensions have grown significantly since 2022:

- **Russia-Ukraine war:** China's stated neutrality but de facto support for Russia (dual-use goods, no participation in sanctions) sharply damaged EU-China trust. EU has warned of "consequences" for companies circumventing Russia sanctions via Chinese intermediaries.
- **Economic coercion:** China's trade restrictions on Lithuania (2021-2022) over Taiwan Representative Office naming triggered the EU's Anti-Coercion Instrument (ACI), adopted 2023.
- **Technology security:** Huawei/ZTE 5G restrictions in most Member States; EU Chips Act partly motivated by China risk; CRA and NIS2 both reference supply-chain risks.
- **Market access asymmetry:** EU companies face barriers in China (joint-venture requirements, data-localisation, procurement exclusion) while Chinese firms operate freely in the EU. FSR, IPI and CBAM are partly designed to rebalance this.

## Key Legislative and Policy Instruments Affecting EU-China Trade

| Instrument | CELEX / Reference | Status | China relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign Subsidies Regulation | 32022R2560 | In force (Jul 2023) | JD.com/CECONOMY case; EV subsidies ex officio investigations |
| Anti-Coercion Instrument (ACI) | 32023R2675 | In force (Dec 2023) | Lithuania precedent; tool available if China coerces a MS |
| Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) | 32023R0956 | Transitional phase 2023-2026; full from 2026 | Chinese steel, cement, aluminium exporters most exposed |
| EU Chips Act | 32023R1781 | In force (Sep 2023) | EU supply chain diversification away from China |
| Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA) | 32024R1252 | In force (May 2024) | China controls >60% of critical raw material processing; CRMA sets 65% non-single-country cap |
| Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA) | 32024R1735 | In force (Jun 2024) | Resilience criteria for solar panels (Chinese market share ~90% of EU imports) |
| International Procurement Instrument (IPI) | 32022R1031 | In force (Aug 2022) | Reciprocity lever for Chinese public procurement access |
| Anti-dumping measures — Chinese EVs | Multiple TDI | Definitive duties from Nov 2024: BYD 17.0%, Geely 18.8%, SAIC 35.3%, other cooperating 18.3%, others 35.3% | CELEX: 32024R2679 |

## June 2026 European Council — China Dimension

The June 2026 EUCO (18-19 June, Brussels) is expected to address:
- Strategic autonomy and de-risking from China in critical sectors
- Trade rebalancing — response to Chinese overcapacity in EVs, solar, steel
- Russia sanctions circumvention via Chinese entities
- Tech Sovereignty Package (CAIDA, Chips Act 2) framing vis-à-vis China competition

The College orientation debate (29 May 2026) directly prepares the Commission's input to the EUCO conclusions.
