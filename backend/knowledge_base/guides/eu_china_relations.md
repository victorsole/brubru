## EU-China Strategic Context

The EU's "partner, competitor, systemic rival" tripartite framing (2019) remains official policy but the competitive and rival dimensions have grown significantly since 2022:

- **Russia-Ukraine war:** China's stated neutrality but de facto support for Russia (dual-use goods, no participation in sanctions) sharply damaged EU-China trust. EU has warned of "consequences" for companies circumventing Russia sanctions via Chinese intermediaries.
- **Economic coercion:** China's trade restrictions on Lithuania (2021-2022) over Taiwan Representative Office naming triggered the EU's Anti-Coercion Instrument (ACI), adopted 2023.
- **Technology security:** Huawei/ZTE 5G restrictions in most Member States; EU Chips Act partly motivated by China risk; CRA and NIS2 both reference supply-chain risks.
- **Market access asymmetry:** EU companies face barriers in China (joint-venture requirements, data-localisation, procurement exclusion) while Chinese firms operate freely in the EU. FSR, IPI and CBAM are partly designed to rebalance this.

## June 2026 European Council — China Dimension

The June 2026 EUCO (18-19 June, Brussels) is expected to address:
- Strategic autonomy and de-risking from China in critical sectors
- Trade rebalancing — response to Chinese overcapacity in EVs, solar, steel
- Russia sanctions circumvention via Chinese entities
- Tech Sovereignty Package (CAIDA, Chips Act 2) framing vis-à-vis China competition

The College orientation debate (29 May 2026) directly prepares the Commission's input to the EUCO conclusions.
