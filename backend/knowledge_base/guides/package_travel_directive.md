## Overview

The **Package Travel Directive** (Directive (EU) 2015/2302) is the EU's core instrument protecting consumers who book package holidays and linked travel arrangements. It establishes pre-contractual information requirements, rules on contract changes and cancellation, liability of organisers, and insolvency protection schemes.

The 2015 Directive replaced the original 1990 Package Travel Directive (90/314/EEC) and was designed to modernise the framework for the digital age. However, the rapid evolution of online booking platforms, the proliferation of "dynamic packages" (where consumers assemble travel components from different providers in a single booking process), and the lessons of the COVID-19 pandemic exposed significant gaps.

During the pandemic, millions of EU consumers struggled to obtain refunds for cancelled package holidays. Many organisers offered vouchers instead of cash refunds, insolvency protection funds proved inadequate in several Member States, and the distinction between "packages" and "linked travel arrangements" became increasingly blurred in practice.

The Commission proposed a revision on 29 November 2023 (COM(2023)905), and the European Parliament adopted its first reading position on 12 March 2026.

## Key Changes in the Revision

### 1. Extended Protection for Dynamic Packages

The most significant change addresses the grey area of "dynamic packages" -- combinations of travel services that consumers assemble themselves on a single platform:

- **Expanded definition:** The concept of "package" is broadened to capture combinations of travel services booked through a single online interface, even where the consumer actively selects individual components
- **Click-through packages:** Where a consumer is transferred from one trader's website to another and books within a defined time window, the arrangement is now treated as a package (lowered from the current 24-hour window to a shorter trigger)
- **Platform responsibility:** Online platforms that facilitate the combination of travel services bear greater responsibility for ensuring package travel protections apply

### 2. Improved Insolvency Protection

The COVID-19 crisis demonstrated that insolvency protection schemes varied dramatically across Member States, with some funds unable to cover mass cancellation events:

- **Minimum coverage levels:** Member States must ensure insolvency protection covers the full value of pre-payments, not just partial refunds
- **Mutual recognition:** Insolvency protection valid in one Member State must be recognised across the EU, addressing the problem of cross-border package organisers exploiting weaker national schemes
- **Stress testing:** National insolvency protection bodies must conduct periodic stress tests based on crisis scenarios (pandemic, airline collapse, geopolitical disruption)
- **Fund adequacy:** Minimum capitalisation requirements for national guarantee funds, calibrated to the volume of packages sold in each Member State

### 3. Enhanced Refund Rights

Building on the pandemic experience, the revision strengthens refund provisions:

- **14-day refund deadline:** Organisers must refund consumers within 14 days of cancellation (tightened from the current "without undue delay" standard, which proved unenforceable during COVID-19)
- **Voucher rules:** Vouchers may only be offered as an alternative to cash refunds if the consumer actively and explicitly accepts them. Vouchers must be transferable and valid for at least 12 months, with automatic cash-out at expiry
- **Extraordinary circumstances:** Clearer definition of "unavoidable and extraordinary circumstances" (pandemics, natural disasters, geopolitical events) that trigger cancellation rights, drawing on lessons from COVID-19, volcanic ash disruptions, and conflict zones
- **Partial performance:** Where a package is partially performed (e.g., hotel available but flights cancelled), the consumer is entitled to a proportionate price reduction and may terminate the contract without penalty

### 4. Digital Booking Platforms

The revision addresses the role of digital platforms in the package travel ecosystem:

- **Information obligations:** Platforms must clearly indicate whether a combination of travel services constitutes a package or linked travel arrangement, using standardised icons and language
- **Transparency on organisers:** Platforms must identify the organiser responsible for each package, including the organiser's insolvency protection scheme and contact details
- **Dark patterns:** Prohibition of design practices that steer consumers away from understanding their package travel rights (aligned with the broader EU approach to manipulative online practices)
- **Review and rating integrity:** Where platforms display reviews of package travel services, they must ensure reviews relate to verified purchases

### 5. Sustainability Information

A new dimension added during the legislative process:

- **Carbon footprint disclosure:** Organisers of packages involving air travel must provide an estimate of the package's carbon footprint, using a standardised methodology
- **Sustainable alternatives:** Where available, organisers must present lower-carbon transport alternatives (rail, coach) alongside air travel options
- **Ecolabel integration:** Accommodation providers with EU Ecolabel or equivalent certifications must be identifiable in booking interfaces

## EP Vote: 12 March 2026

The European Parliament adopted its first reading position on 12 March 2026 in Strasbourg. Key aspects of the EP position:

### Adopted Amendments
- **Stronger platform liability:** The EP went further than the Commission proposal in holding digital platforms responsible for insolvency protection where they act as de facto organisers
- **Shorter refund period:** The EP pushed for a 10-day refund deadline (Commission proposed 14 days), though this remains a negotiating position for trilogues
- **Accessibility requirements:** Packages must comply with the European Accessibility Act requirements, ensuring travel arrangements are accessible to persons with disabilities
- **Multi-modal packages:** Explicit coverage of packages combining different transport modes (flight + train, ferry + bus)

### Political Groups
- **EPP:** Supported the revision with emphasis on balanced obligations for SME travel operators
- **S&D:** Pushed for strongest possible consumer refund rights and platform accountability
- **Renew:** Focused on digital platform transparency and cross-border enforcement
- **Greens/EFA:** Championed the sustainability disclosure provisions, sought mandatory carbon offsetting (not adopted)
- **ECR:** Cautious on additional regulatory burden for small travel agencies

### Vote Result
The text was adopted by a large majority, reflecting broad political consensus on strengthening consumer protection in the travel sector.

## Consumer Rights: What Changes in Practice

For EU consumers, the revised Directive means:

| Situation | Current Rules | After Revision |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| Dynamic package booked online | May fall outside Directive scope | Covered as a package |
| Cancellation refund timeline | "Without undue delay" (vague) | 14 days maximum (EP: 10 days) |
| Voucher instead of refund | Often imposed by organisers | Consumer must actively choose voucher |
| Organiser insolvency | Coverage varies by Member State | Minimum EU-wide coverage levels |
| Platform role | Limited obligations | Must identify organiser, explain rights |
| Carbon footprint | No obligation | Disclosure required for air packages |

## Impact on the Travel Industry

### Tour Operators
- Must adapt insolvency protection to meet new minimum standards
- Enhanced pre-contractual information requirements
- Faster refund processing systems needed (14-day deadline)
- Small and medium operators: transitional period to comply with new IT and reporting requirements

### Online Travel Agencies (OTAs)
- Booking.com, Expedia, and similar platforms face new obligations to identify when combinations constitute packages
- Must display standardised package travel rights information
- Greater liability exposure for insolvency protection in certain configurations

### Airlines
- Where airlines sell packages (flight + hotel combinations), they fall under the Directive as organisers
- Sustainability disclosure requirements for air-inclusive packages

### National Authorities
- Consumer protection authorities must adapt enforcement mechanisms
- Insolvency protection funds require recapitalisation in several Member States
- Cross-border enforcement cooperation under the CPC Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/2394) becomes more important

## Historical Context

The Package Travel Directive has evolved through three generations:

1. **Directive 90/314/EEC** (1990): First EU package travel rules, designed for the era of high-street travel agencies and printed brochures
2. **Directive (EU) 2015/2302** (2015): Modernised for online booking, introduced "linked travel arrangements" concept
3. **Current revision** (2023-2026): Post-pandemic overhaul addressing dynamic packages, platform economy, and insolvency resilience
