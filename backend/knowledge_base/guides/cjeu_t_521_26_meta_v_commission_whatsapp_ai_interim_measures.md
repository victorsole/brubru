# Meta v Commission (T-521/26): the WhatsApp Business API and AI Competitors, Interim Measures Challenged

## QUICK FACTS
- **Status: PENDING before the General Court. Action lodged, NO hearing and NO ruling as of 11 November 2026.** Case **T-521/26, Meta Platforms v Commission**, action brought on **7 August 2026**, notice published in the Official Journal C series on 9 November 2026 (C/2026/5627). Everything below is what the APPLICANT asks and argues, NOT a finding of the Court.
- **What Meta challenges:** the Commission's decision of **9 June 2026 in Case AT.41034, "Exclusion of AI Competitors from WhatsApp"**, adopted under **Article 8(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1/2003** (interim measures in an antitrust case under Article 102 TFEU, abuse of a dominant position). This is competition law, NOT the Digital Markets Act.
- **What the decision does, as described in the notice:** it mandates **free access to the WhatsApp Business API**, prohibits Meta from charging any fee, lasts **three years** (Article 5 of the decision) and provides for periodic penalty payments for non-compliance.
- **What Meta asks the Court:** annul the decision in full; or annul Articles 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 as far as they mandate free access, ban any fee and impose periodic penalty payments; or annul Article 5 as far as it sets a three-year duration; and order the Commission to pay costs.
- **Eight pleas (Meta's arguments):** (1) market definition and dominance wrongly assessed and reasoned; (2) no prima facie infringement of Article 102 TFEU, wrong legal framework; (3) in the alternative, the conduct does not depart from competition on the merits; (4) no capability of exclusionary effects in the EEA-wide market for general purpose AI assistants; (5) conditions for interim measures under Article 8 not met; (6) the measures are disproportionate; (7) Meta's objective justifications wrongly rejected; (8) no adequate reasons for imposing free access.
- **Why it matters:** interim measures are rare in EU antitrust; the case tests how far the Commission can order access to a messaging channel for rival AI assistants before a final decision on the merits.
- **What was NOT read:** the Commission's decision of 9 June 2026 itself (only the Court notice), and any application for suspension of the decision. The main antitrust investigation continues separately.
