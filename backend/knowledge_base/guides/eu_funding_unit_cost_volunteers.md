# Unit Cost Decision: Volunteers

## QUICK FACTS
- Volunteers are persons working on a non-compulsory basis for an organisation without payment.
- Unit costs allow beneficiaries to declare the notional value of volunteer work as eligible personnel costs (co-financing) under Articles 181, 186 and 190 of the Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046.
- Unit costs are set per EU/EEA Member State (four groups: EUR 157 to EUR 47 per day) and per third country (four groups: EUR 92 to EUR 17 per day).
- Daily rates can be divided by eight to calculate hourly rates for flexibility (EUR 19.63 to EUR 5.88 per hour in EU/EEA; EUR 11.50 to EUR 2.13 per hour in third countries).
- Volunteer costs cannot exceed 50% of total project funding sources (including in-kind contributions).
- Programmes must explicitly authorise volunteer costs in the call for proposals or grant invitation; unit costs apply when approved.
- The system avoids actual cost reimbursement; beneficiaries remain unpaid. Travel, accommodation, insurance and social security costs can be claimed separately as eligible costs.
- Commission Decision C(2019)2646 of 10 April 2019 authorises these unit costs across all EU-funded programmes (Erasmus+, European Solidarity Corps, CERV, Horizon Europe, etc.).
- Beneficiaries must establish internal procedures to record volunteer hours, verify volunteer registration, and report volunteer activity in grant agreements.

## Who counts as a volunteer under this decision

A volunteer is a person who carries out work on a non-compulsory basis for an organisation without payment. Volunteer activities can take place in any EU/EEA Member State or third country. The nature of the activity is flexible: unit costs are not tied to specific job categories (volunteers may undertake administrative, technical, construction or specialist work). The key requirement is that the volunteer:

1. Works without payment (non-compulsory, no salary or fee);
2. Is formally registered or documented as a volunteer by the organisation;
3. Has their hours logged and verified by the organisation;
4. Performs work that is relevant to the action's planned outputs.

## Why the unit cost exists (capturing in-kind contributions)

Volunteer work represents an in-kind contribution. The Financial Regulation recognises that organisations often rely on unpaid labour but have historically struggled to quantify its value in grant applications. Unit costs solve three problems:

1. **Notional valuation**: Volunteers are by definition unpaid, so no actual salary exists to claim. A unit cost assigns a fair notional value to the volunteer's time, enabling organisations to count the contribution as eligible co-financing.

2. **Equity of access**: Without unit costs, organisations dependent on volunteering (youth centres, civil society, sports clubs, advocacy groups) face a funding gap compared to organisations with paid staff. Unit costs help level the playing field.

3. **Predictability and simplicity**: Unit costs provide a transparent, standardised method that programme managers and beneficiaries can apply consistently. Beneficiaries do not need to justify their own estimates, and auditors have a clear reference point.

The Commission chose the lowest administrative staff rate from Erasmus+ multi-country data (2008-2010 real costs) as the baseline, discounted to 75% to ensure sound financial management and prevent profit. This approach is objective, data-driven and applicable across all programmes and countries.

## Eligibility conditions for the volunteer and the activity

### Volunteer Status

The volunteer must be:
- **Formally registered**: The organisation must have documented evidence that the person is a volunteer (membership, contract, commitment letter, or formal statement).
- **Non-compulsory**: Volunteering is a free choice; the volunteer has not been contractually required to work.
- **Unpaid**: The volunteer receives no salary, fee or payment for the work.

### Activity Scope

The volunteer's work must:
- **Be relevant to the action**: The activity should contribute to achieving the planned outputs described in the grant agreement.
- **Be time-tracked**: Hours or days of work must be recorded in a time-log or timesheet.
- **Be verified**: The organisation's internal procedures must confirm that the hours are genuine and match the action's scope.

### Limitations

1. **50% ceiling**: Volunteer costs cannot exceed 50% of the total eligible costs of the action (including all other sources of co-financing).
2. **Non-reimbursable costs**: Unit costs cover only the notional value of time. They do not cover travel expenses, accommodation, insurance, social security or subsistence costs. These must be claimed separately under actual eligible costs if the beneficiary incurs them.
3. **Call authorisation**: The call for proposals or grant invitation must explicitly state that volunteer costs are eligible and will be valued at unit costs. Volunteer costs cannot be claimed if the call is silent on the matter.

## Documentation: registration, hours log and declaration

Beneficiaries must establish effective internal control procedures to manage volunteer costs:

### Registration
- Maintain a volunteer roster with names, dates of engagement, and confirmation of volunteer status.
- Retain evidence of the volunteer's commitment (signed declaration, membership form, registration letter).

### Hours and Time Tracking
- Require volunteers to record daily or weekly hours worked on the action in a timesheet or log.
- The timesheet must state the date(s), number of hours or days, and the activity performed.
- The organisation's representative (project manager, coordinator) must review and sign the timesheet to verify accuracy.

### Declaration
- In interim or final project reports, beneficiaries declare the total volunteer hours/days claimed.
- The declaration must be accompanied by supporting documentation: volunteer roster, signed timesheets, and a summary reconciliation.
- Auditors will verify that volunteer names, hours and costs are consistent with the documentation.

### Flexibility
- Organisations can use either daily rates or hourly rates, depending on the nature of the volunteer's work. Hourly rates suit roles with variable schedules or part-time involvement.
- The organisation should choose at the outset and apply the rate consistently throughout the action.

## Programmes where volunteer unit costs are most relevant

Volunteer unit costs are authorised under any EU grant programme where the call for proposals or invitation explicitly permits them. The most common users are:

1. **Erasmus+** (education, training, youth, sport): Youth volunteering, European Solidarity Corps short-term assignments, youth worker placements. Volunteer costs are heavily used in Key Action 1 (mobility) and Key Action 2 (strategic partnerships).

2. **European Solidarity Corps (ESC)**: Volunteering is the core activity. Unit costs apply to volunteer project members and facilitate organisations in co-financing ESC-funded activities.

3. **Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values (CERV)** (formerly Rights, Equality and Citizenship): Projects supporting civil society, democracy, and rights advocacy often use volunteers. Unit costs enable grassroots organisations to participate.

4. **Horizon Europe** (research and innovation): Some calls, especially in social sciences and capacity-building, authorise volunteer costs for research support, community engagement or dissemination activities.

5. **Europe for Citizens** and other social / civil society programmes: Calls promoting civic participation, cultural exchange and dialogue often permit volunteer contributions.

6. **Customs, Justice, and Health Programmes** (JUST, CHAFEA, EU4Health): Specific calls may authorise volunteer costs for public health campaigns, citizen education or legal assistance projects.

Beneficiaries should check the call text and programme guide to confirm that volunteer costs are eligible for the specific action they are proposing.
