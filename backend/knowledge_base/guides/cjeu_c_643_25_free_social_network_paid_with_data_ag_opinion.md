# Advocate General Medina, C-643/25 (19 November 2026): a social network paid with personal data is not "free"

## QUICK FACTS
- **Opinion of Advocate General Laila Medina, Thursday 19 November 2026, Case C-643/25, Verbraucherzentrale Bundesverband ("Free" digital service)**, press release No 169/26. **An opinion, NOT the judgment**: the Court of Justice decides later and is not bound by it.
- **The case:** Facebook's registration page in February 2015 said "Facebook is and remains free". The German federation of consumer organisations (vzbv) challenged it; German courts dismissed the claim (free means no money); the Bundesgerichtshof referred the question to the Court of Justice. Defendant: Meta Platforms Ireland.
- **The Advocate General's proposal:** giving personal data and consenting to their commercial use is a **form of payment** under the **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC**. A service therefore **cannot be described as "free"** where consumers must provide such data for commercial use, **unless the data are processed only to provide the service or to comply with legal obligations**.
- **Reasoning:** "payment" in the Directive is not limited to money (the Court already accepts non-money consideration with economic value and a definite link to the practice); user data plus consent have economic value for the platform (advertising revenue); the Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770 and the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU already recognise data-for-service models, so calling the same service "free" under the UCPD would be inconsistent; describing a product as "free" when payment is required is on the blacklist (prohibited in all circumstances), so no case-by-case test of whether consumers were misled.
- **Why it matters:** if the Court follows, "free" claims by ad-funded platforms paid with data become an unfair commercial practice per se across the EU; it also bears on the pay-or-consent debate and on the Digital Fairness Act (guide on the Digital Fairness Act).
