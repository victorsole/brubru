# Franco-German Letter and Non-Paper on Trade Resilience: a New "Systemic Reaction" Instrument (5 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is:** a **joint letter from French President Macron and German Chancellor Merz to Commission President von der Leyen**, with an enclosed **Franco-German non-paper dated 5 October 2026** ("Strengthening European resilience, addressing global macroeconomic imbalances and market distorting practices"). Both were published by Politico. It is a **political request, NOT a Commission proposal and NOT law**; no legislative text exists.
- **The ask:** two **new legal instruments**, "without delaying the full use of the existing set of instruments". (1) A **diversification instrument** to reduce dependencies and supply-chain concentration. (2) An instrument for a **"decisive and systemic reaction"** when third countries undermine a level playing field "by political or economic means", drawing on **regulating access to the single market**.
- **Design of instrument (2), per the non-paper:** swift activation by a **comitology-like procedure: Commission proposal, adopted unless a qualified majority of Member States opposes it** (the letter: "activation by reversed qualified majority"); **country-agnostic**; covers all distortions, "from a single production to a whole sector, from subsidies to currency manipulation"; a very wide range of trade remedies and other tools; must weigh the **Union interest**; dialogue and negotiated proposals with affected countries. The **letter** adds "powerful measures, up to an immediate cut-off from the internal market if needed". Neither document names China (press reporting links it to the EU's trade deficit with China).
- **Existing trade defence, per the non-paper:** more and swifter anti-dumping, anti-subsidy and safeguard investigations; more **ex officio** cases (to shield complainant Member States from retaliation); a **sector-wide approach**; urgent cases named: **chemicals, PET, plug-in hybrid vehicles**; consider **not applying the "lesser duty rule"** where that is in the Union's interest; more staff for the trade-defence units; **targeted revisions** of the anti-dumping, anti-subsidy and safeguard regulations (circumvention, product scope, faster procedures).
- **Other points in the non-paper:** persistently high **non-compliance of imports** (e.g. small parcels) should lead to **access restrictions**; **exchange-rate misalignment** to be discussed (Eurogroup, ECB, Commission, "without prejudice to Article 219 TFEU"); an **inventory of possible retaliation measures** and coordination at European Council level; **"European preference"** in strategic sectors and agreeing the **Industrial Accelerator Act by the end of 2026**.
- **Existing tool for comparison:** the **Anti-Coercion Instrument, Regulation (EU) 2023/2675** (CELEX 32023R2675), which needs a lengthy investigation and a qualified majority of Member States to act. The Franco-German idea would make it much harder for Member States to block quick action. The new instrument is NOT the same as the Anti-Coercion Instrument.
- **Timing:** the documents follow the **European Council of 18-19 June 2026** and the **Franco-German Council of Ministers of 17 July 2026** and look ahead to the **European Council of 15-16 October 2026**. Press reports that Trade Commissioner **Šefčovič** was due in Beijing on **Thursday 8 October** for the last round of a four-month process on rebalancing trade with China.
- **Do not state:** that the Commission has agreed, that a proposal has a date, or that China is named in the text.

## Why it matters
If the Commission follows, it would add a third pillar to EU trade policy beside trade defence and the Anti-Coercion Instrument: a fast, comitology-style tool that regulates access to the single market itself. Companies exposed to imports (chemicals, PET, vehicles) and to supply-chain concentration (AI, semiconductors, quantum, critical raw materials, batteries, clean-energy components, biotech, financial services are the sectors the non-paper lists for derisking) should watch the October summit.

## FAQ
**Is this a Commission proposal?** No. It is a letter from two heads of state or government asking the Commission to propose new instruments.
**What would the new instrument do?** Per the letter, allow "powerful measures, up to an immediate cut-off from the internal market if needed", activated by a reversed qualified majority.
**Does it replace trade defence?** No: the non-paper asks for fuller use of the existing toolbox and says the new instruments come "in parallel".
