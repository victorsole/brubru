# EUIPO SME Fund ("Ideas Powered for Business")

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: The **SME Fund**, delivered under the **"Ideas Powered for Business"** umbrella, is a voucher-based grant scheme that partially reimburses EU small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) for the fees they pay to protect intellectual property (IP): trade marks, designs, patents, plant varieties and, since a recent edition, craft and industrial geographical indications (GIs).
- **Who runs it**: Managed and delivered by the **European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO)**, headquartered in **Alicante, Spain** (**NOT the European Commission**). The fund is **jointly funded by EUIPO and the European Commission**, and delivered in cooperation with national and regional IP offices across the Member States. Brubru users regularly confuse this with a Commission-run scheme; it is not. Do not confuse it either with EIC funding (see "Distinction from EIC funding" below).
- **Launched**: First edition **11 January 2021**. Has run in **annual editions every year since** (2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026), each with its own budget envelope, application window and rules. Figures below are for the most recently verified edition (2026) and should always be checked against the live EUIPO page for the current year, since amounts and open/closed voucher status shift within a single year as funds are exhausted.
- **2026 edition budget**: approximately **EUR 18 million** (roughly EUR 17.1 million for Vouchers 1-2 combined, EUR 900,000 shared between Vouchers 3-4). Historical editions have varied (the 2021 launch edition was EUR 20 million). Treat the exact split as indicative, not fixed across years.
- **2026 application window**: **2 February 2026 to 4 December 2026**, or until funds are exhausted (whichever comes first). Historically, high-demand vouchers (IP Scan, Trade Marks and Designs) have run out of budget well before the December close, sometimes within weeks or months of opening. **Always check current voucher availability before advising a client to apply**: a voucher can be marked "exhausted" mid-year.
- **Vouchers on offer (2026 edition; verify against the live page for the current cycle)**:
  - **Voucher 1 (IP Scan)**: subsidises an IP pre-diagnostic/audit service delivered by a national or EU IP office, at roughly **90% of cost**, up to a ceiling in the region of **EUR 1,500-1,620** (figures vary slightly by source/year, hedge downward if citing to a client and point them to the live page).
  - **Voucher 2 (Trade Marks and Designs)**: subsidises official application fees for national, EU (EUIPO) and international/regional trade mark and design registrations, historically at rates in the **50-75%** range depending on the edition and territory (EU/national fees typically at the higher rate, non-EU/international fees typically at a lower rate around 50%), with a per-company ceiling that has moved between roughly **EUR 700 and EUR 1,500** across editions. **Do not quote a single fixed percentage or ceiling without checking the current year**: this voucher's terms have changed edition to edition.
  - **Voucher 3 (Patents)**: subsidises patent-related costs (filing/search fees, and in some editions professional drafting/filing costs) typically at **50-75%**, up to a ceiling in the region of **EUR 1,000-1,500** depending on the specific cost category.
  - **Voucher 4 (Community Plant Varieties)**: subsidises Community Plant Variety Office (CPVO) registration and examination fees, typically at around **75%**, up to a ceiling in the region of **EUR 1,500**.
  - **Voucher 5 (Craft and Industrial Geographical Indications (CIGI))**: a newer voucher (introduced alongside the craft/industrial GI regime under Regulation (EU) 2024/1143, see `gi_craft_industrial_2024_1143.md`) subsidising CIGI application costs at up to roughly **50%**, capped around **EUR 2,000** per beneficiary. EUIPO also operates the CIGI registry itself, so this voucher and that registry are run by the same office.
  - **All percentages and ceilings above must be treated as indicative and hedged** when quoted to a user: verify the live figure on the current-year EUIPO SME Fund page before giving a firm number, and always tell the user to check final terms on the official page before applying.
- **Eligibility**: open to **SMEs established and registered in the EU** (the standard EU SME definition: fewer than 250 employees, turnover ≤EUR 50 million or balance sheet ≤EUR 43 million; company registration in a Member State is a threshold eligibility check on the application portal). One company can normally apply for multiple different vouchers, but usually not for the same voucher twice within the same edition once it has received support for that IP right.
- **How to apply**: Entirely online via the **EUIPO SME Fund portal**, in yearly windows (2026: 2 February to 4 December, funds permitting). No physical/paper applications. Application requires an EUIPO user account; each voucher has its own online application form and required annexes (proof of SME status, proof of payment of underlying IP fees, etc., depending on voucher).
- **Scale**: informally estimated at around **20,000-30,000 SME applications per year** across all vouchers combined since the scheme's 2021 launch (figure directionally accurate but not independently pinned to a single official EUIPO annual report at time of writing: hedge if quoting precisely).
- **NOT the European Commission and NOT the EIC**: the SME Fund pays for **registration/protection fees for existing IP rights**. It does **not** fund innovation projects, R&D, prototyping, or scale-up capital: that is the role of the **European Innovation Council (EIC)** (`eic_overview.md`, `eic_accelerator_process.md`). A start-up can and often should use both: EIC funding to develop and scale the innovation, SME Fund vouchers to cheaply protect the IP that results from it.

## What the SME Fund Is, and Who Runs It

The **Ideas Powered for Business SME Fund** is the flagship SME-support instrument of the EUIPO, the EU agency responsible for registering and administering **EU trade marks** and **registered Community designs**, and (since Regulation (EU) 2024/1143) the **craft and industrial geographical indication register**. EUIPO is based in Alicante, Spain, and is financially self-sufficient from registration fees: it is a fee-funded agency, not a Commission service, though it works closely with DG GROW (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs) on EU-wide IP policy.

The SME Fund itself is **co-financed jointly by EUIPO and the European Commission**, reflecting the shared EU policy interest in helping SMEs protect and commercialise their intangible assets: trade marks, designs, patents, plant varieties and, more recently, craft/industrial geographical indications. National and regional IP offices across the Member States act as delivery partners for some voucher categories (particularly IP Scan, which is often delivered locally).

For Brubru users, the operative distinction to hold onto is: **EUIPO runs and pays the SME Fund; the European Commission co-funds it and sets the broader IP policy framework (the EU IP Action Plan and the SME Strategy) that the Fund implements, but does not administer the vouchers directly.**

## The Vouchers

Each annual SME Fund edition is structured around a small number of discrete vouchers, each targeting a different IP right. The set of vouchers, their reimbursement rates and their ceilings have **changed between editions** (2021 launched with 2 vouchers at different rates than 2026's 5 vouchers), so any number quoted should be sourced to a specific edition and, ideally, verified live.

- **Voucher 1 (IP Scan)**: an IP pre-diagnostic/audit for SMEs that have not yet formally engaged with the IP system, typically delivered through a national or regional IP office. Historically the entry point into the Fund for first-time applicants, and historically one of the fastest vouchers to exhaust its annual allocation.
- **Voucher 2 (Trade Marks and Designs)**: the highest-volume voucher by application count, covering official EUIPO/national/international trade mark and registered design application fees. This is usually the voucher most relevant to a start-up protecting its brand.
- **Voucher 3 (Patents)**: covers patent filing/search fees and, in some editions, a portion of professional patent-drafting costs, at national or European Patent Office (EPO) level.
- **Voucher 4 (Community Plant Varieties)**: covers CPVO registration/examination fees; niche but important for agri-food and plant-breeding SMEs.
- **Voucher 5 (Craft and Industrial GIs (CIGI))**: the newest addition, aligned to the craft/industrial geographical indication regime EUIPO now administers under Regulation (EU) 2024/1143 (see cross-reference below). Relevant to SMEs producing regionally-rooted craft or industrial goods (ceramics, glassware, textiles, cutlery, stoneware, etc.) seeking EU-wide GI protection.

**Reimbursement mechanics**: vouchers are **reimbursement grants**, not upfront subsidies. The SME typically pays the underlying official fee first (to EUIPO, a national IP office, the EPO or the CPVO, as applicable), then claims back the eligible percentage from EUIPO via the SME Fund portal, subject to the per-voucher ceiling. Because ceilings and percentages move edition to edition, Brubru should always direct the user to the live EUIPO SME Fund page for the current year's exact terms rather than quoting last year's figures as current.

## How to Apply

1. Applications open in an **annual window** announced on the EUIPO SME Fund portal (2026 edition: 2 February to 4 December 2026, funds permitting).
2. Applicants need an **EUIPO user account** and apply **entirely online**, voucher by voucher.
3. Each voucher has its own form and required supporting documents (typically: proof of SME status/company registration, proof of payment of the underlying official IP fee, and sometimes a declaration of prior support received).
4. **Funds are allocated on a rolling, first-come-first-served basis within the annual envelope**: a voucher category can close mid-year once its budget line is exhausted, well before the nominal December closing date. This has happened repeatedly in past editions to the IP Scan and Trade Marks and Designs vouchers, which see the highest application volume.
5. Because of this, the practical advice to a Brubru user considering the SME Fund is: **check the live "voucher status" (open/exhausted) on the EUIPO SME Fund page before starting an application, and apply early in the annual window if funds run out fast in past years for that voucher.**

## Eligibility

- Must be a genuine **SME established in the EU** under the standard EU definition (fewer than 250 employees; annual turnover ≤EUR 50 million or annual balance sheet total ≤EUR 43 million).
- Registration/incorporation in an EU Member State is checked at application stage.
- Generally, an SME can apply for **more than one voucher type** in the same edition (e.g. IP Scan plus Trade Marks and Designs), but usually **cannot claim the same voucher twice for the same right** within one edition.
- No sector restriction: open to SMEs in any sector that hold or are seeking to register an eligible IP right.

## Distinction from EIC Funding

This is one of the most common points of confusion for Brubru users, and worth stating plainly:

| | **EUIPO SME Fund** | **EIC (European Innovation Council)** |
|---|---|---|
| Runs it | EUIPO (Alicante), co-financed with the Commission | European Commission (DG RTD) via EISMEA + EIC Fund |
| Funds what | **Registration/protection fees** for existing IP rights (trade marks, designs, patents, plant varieties, GIs) | **Innovation projects**: R&D, scale-up, deeptech development, equity investment |
| Instrument type | Voucher reimbursement grant (fee rebate) | Blended finance: grants + equity (EIC Accelerator), pure grants (Pathfinder), fund-of-funds equity (Scaleup Europe Fund) |
| Typical amount | Hundreds to low thousands of EUR per voucher | Grants up to EUR 2.5 million; blended finance up to EUR 17.5 million (Accelerator) |
| Relevant guides | This guide | `eic_overview.md`, `eic_accelerator_process.md` |

The two are **complementary, not competing**: an SME can receive EIC Accelerator funding to develop and commercialise an innovation, and separately use SME Fund vouchers to cheaply register the trade mark, design or patent that protects the resulting product or brand. Brubru should never present the SME Fund as an alternative source of "innovation funding": it does not fund R&D, prototyping or commercialisation activity, only the administrative fees of securing IP rights.

## Link to the IP Action Plan and the SME Strategy

The SME Fund is the delivery mechanism for two broader EU policy commitments:

- The **EU Intellectual Property Action Plan** (Commission communication, 2020), which set out to make IP protection more accessible, affordable and enforceable for European businesses, with SMEs identified as chronically under-using the IP system relative to large firms.
- The **EU SME Strategy for a sustainable and digital Europe** (Commission communication, 2020), which flagged IP costs and complexity as a barrier to SME competitiveness and growth, alongside access to finance and administrative burden.

The SME Fund operationalises both: it is the concrete, transactional instrument (a voucher an SME can actually claim) sitting underneath the higher-level strategic commitments in those two documents. When Brubru discusses the EU SME Strategy or the IP Action Plan in the abstract, the SME Fund is the answer to "what does this actually give me as an SME."

## Brubru Engagement Angles

- **Start-ups protecting their brand cheaply**: a start-up filing its first EU trade mark or registered design can recover a meaningful share of the official fee via Voucher 2. This is directly relevant to Brubru's Tenderator/EIC-adjacent startup audience who are budget-constrained on non-core legal spend.
- **IP due diligence before fundraising**: Voucher 1 (IP Scan) is a natural early step for a start-up preparing for a funding round or accelerator application, since investors and EIC Accelerator evaluators increasingly expect a basic IP audit.
- **Craft and industrial SMEs**: Voucher 5 (CIGI) is a direct bridge to Brubru's geographical indications work (`gi_craft_industrial_2024_1143.md`). A craft manufacturer applying for GI protection can also claim partial reimbursement of the application cost through the same EUIPO administrative system.
- **Pair with EIC guidance**: whenever Brubru surfaces EIC Accelerator or EIC Fund content to a start-up, flag the SME Fund as a complementary, low-effort follow-on action once the innovation has a protectable brand, design or patent.
- **Annual timing reminder**: because vouchers close mid-year once exhausted, Brubru's My EU Bubble / Tenderator surfaces should treat the SME Fund as a **time-sensitive, apply-early opportunity** each year rather than an evergreen, always-open scheme.

## What Brubru Should Surface

- Always name **EUIPO (Alicante)** as the body that runs the Fund, and clarify the **Commission co-financing** role rather than implying the Commission administers it directly.
- Always distinguish the SME Fund (IP registration fee rebates) from the **EIC** (innovation project funding): never conflate the two when a user asks about "EU funding for start-ups."
- Hedge exact voucher percentages and ceilings: state the approximate range found in the most recently verified edition, and direct the user to the live EUIPO SME Fund page for the current year's confirmed figures before they apply.
- Flag that **vouchers can close mid-year** once their budget line is exhausted: advise early application, and note that voucher availability should be checked live before committing significant time to an application.
- Cross-reference the **craft/industrial GI regime** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1143) when discussing Voucher 5, since EUIPO administers both the CIGI registry and the corresponding voucher.
