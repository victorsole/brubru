# Dutch data protection authority fines Uber EUR 824,990,000 for automated decision-making (October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What:** the Dutch data protection authority, the **Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP)**, fined Uber **EUR 824,990,000** (about EUR 825 million) under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679, CELEX 32016R0679). The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) published it as national news on **8 October 2026**.
- **Who decided:** the AP, a national authority. NOT the European Commission and NOT the EDPB (the EDPB only lists national decisions on its news page). The AP was the lead authority under the GDPR One-Stop-Shop procedure because Uber's European headquarters are in the Netherlands.
- **Provisions infringed (exactly two):** Article 22 (decisions based solely on automated processing, including profiling) and Article 13 (information to be given to the person whose data are collected).
- **The conduct:** drivers' accounts were deactivated by fully automated decisions, temporarily when fraud was suspected or customer reviews were too low, and permanently when low reviews persisted. Drivers lost the income they earned through Uber while deactivated. The AP also found that Uber did not inform drivers sufficiently about the automated decision-making.
- **Origin of the case:** complaints from **171 French Uber drivers**, submitted through the Ligue des droits de l'Homme (LDH) to the French authority CNIL, which passed the case to the AP. The incidents took place between **2018 and 2022**.
- **Status:** Uber has stopped the violations and **has appealed the fine**; there is no final judicial decision yet.
- **Not stated in the EDPB summary:** the date of the AP decision, how the amount was calculated, and any comparison with other fines. Give the amount exactly as stated and add no ranking, no extra reasoning and no detail about the AP's findings beyond the bullets above.
- Source (EDPB national news, 8 October 2026): https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/dutch-dpa-fines-uber-eur-824-990-000-for-unlawful-automated-decision-making-and-insufficient_en

## Why it matters

The case concerns Article 22 GDPR (solely automated decisions) and the Article 13 information duty in platform work, where an automated deactivation removed drivers' income. It is also a worked example of the One-Stop-Shop: complaints lodged in France (CNIL) were handled by the authority of the country where the company's European headquarters are (the Netherlands).
