# European Parliament Immunity, Privileges, and Waivers

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: **Protocol No 7 on the Privileges and Immunities of the European Union** (PPI), annexed to the Treaties; Articles 7-9 PPI cover MEPs specifically. Article 8 PPI = absolute immunity for opinions expressed and votes cast in the performance of MEP duties. Article 9 PPI = the parliamentary immunity that mirrors the national immunity of the MEP's own Member State (while travelling to / from / sitting in Parliament).
- Lead EP committee: **JURI** (Legal Affairs) — JURI handles all immunity-related requests under EP Rules of Procedure Rules 5-9. JURI Chair: Ilhan Kyuchyuk (Renew, Bulgaria, from June 2024 mandate).
- Procedure: MEP receives a national prosecution / criminal-investigation notification → the relevant national authority transmits a **request for waiver of immunity** (or, less commonly, a request for **defence of immunity**) via diplomatic channels to the EP → EP Rules of Procedure require the President to refer the request to JURI → JURI rapporteur drafts a report → JURI adopts an in camera decision → plenary votes (typically by show of hands; no amendments allowed) → final decision is notified to the national authority.
- Waiver consequence: a successful waiver **does NOT** mean the MEP is guilty — it only means the national criminal procedure can resume. A refused waiver freezes the national procedure until either the MEP's mandate ends or the EP reconsiders.
- Annual volume: approximately 30-60 immunity requests per legislature; the share that are GRANTED (waiver = MEP can be prosecuted) is historically high (around 70-80%). EP almost always defers to national judicial authorities except when there is clear evidence of **fumus persecutionis** (political persecution).
- Internal EP rules: **EP Rules of Procedure Rules 5 (Privileges and Immunities), Rule 6 (Waiver of Immunity), Rule 7 (Defence of Privileges and Immunity), Rule 8 (Urgent Action by the President), and Rule 9 (Procedures on Immunity)**.

## How an Immunity Request Travels Through Parliament

1. National prosecutor / investigating judge issues a formal request — usually addressed to the Permanent Representation of the Member State in Brussels.
2. Permanent Representation transmits the request to the EP via diplomatic channels (formally to the EP President's cabinet).
3. EP President announces the request in plenary the following session and refers it to JURI under Rule 9.
4. JURI Bureau appoints a rapporteur (rotating; sensitive cases often go to senior members).
5. Rapporteur drafts a report. The MEP concerned has the right to be heard before JURI (Rule 9(6)) and to submit written observations.
6. JURI adopts the report in camera by simple majority.
7. Plenary votes the report — usually placed on the Wednesday or Thursday agenda of the Strasbourg plenary; the vote is on the JURI recommendation as a whole, by show of hands.
8. EP notifies the national authority of the decision.

## Why It Matters in 2026

- **Politico investigation (18 May 2026)**: published a critical investigation accusing the European Parliament of **overreach** in shielding top lawmakers from national prosecutors. The piece argued that the EP has refused waiver requests in cases where the underlying alleged conduct (financial irregularities, conflict of interest, public-procurement opacity) does not on its face appear to be politically motivated, and that the EP's interpretation of *fumus persecutionis* has drifted to cover an ever-wider category of cases. The investigation calls for clearer JURI rapporteur-selection rules and for a public, written reasoning standard on each refusal.
- The political stakes: any tightening of immunity rules requires a Treaty change (Protocol No 7 is treaty-rank). The only EP-internal lever is to tighten Rule 5-9 of the Rules of Procedure, which is what AFCO 2025/2262(REG) on Rule 135 partially attempts (although that file is technically about appointments to Union agencies). Substantive Rules-of-Procedure reform on immunity would be a separate AFCO file.
- Ongoing Qatargate / Pfizergate / Pegasus-spyware files: several high-profile waiver requests remain pending in JURI from earlier legislatures; legal academic commentary (notably Verfassungsblog, EJIL:Talk!) has flagged inconsistent decision patterns.

## What Brubru Should Surface

- Always distinguish **waiver of immunity** (Rule 6) from **defence of immunity** (Rule 7) — they are NOT the same procedure. A defence is initiated by the MEP themselves; a waiver is initiated by the national authority.
- Always note that an EP refusal does NOT acquit the MEP — it only halts the national procedure for the duration of the mandate.
- Always cite **Protocol No 7** (not "the immunity Treaty") and Rules 5-9 of the EP Rules of Procedure.
- Cross-link with `eppo_european_public_prosecutor.md` whenever the underlying alleged offence is a PIF (Protection of EU Financial Interests) crime, since the EPPO may also be investigating in parallel.
- Cross-link with `eu_anti_corruption_directive.md` (if it exists) and any DG JUST anti-corruption files.
