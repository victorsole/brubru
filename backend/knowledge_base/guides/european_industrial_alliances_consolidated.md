# European Industrial Alliances: master reference of all EU industrial alliances

## QUICK FACTS
- **What they are:** European Industrial Alliances are informal, Commission-convened cooperation forums, NOT legal entities and NOT direct EU funding instruments. They bring together industry, public authorities, civil society, researchers and investors around a single strategic value chain to share information, agree roadmaps, and de-risk investment.
- **Legal status:** No treaty base of their own; each alliance is set up by a Commission Communication, Terms of Reference or Declaration, with a General Assembly, a Steering Board/Committee, and thematic working groups. Membership is normally open to any organisation active in the relevant value chain, subject to Commission approval of eligibility criteria.
- **Relationship to IPCEIs:** Alliances do NOT prejudge or guarantee an Important Project of Common European Interest (IPCEI). They map project pipelines, identify bottlenecks and coordinate investment signals; individual Member States and companies still need to notify a distinct IPCEI under state-aid rules (Article 107(3)(b) TFEU) for cross-border, breakthrough-innovation projects to receive the enhanced state-aid clearance. Several alliances (Battery, Hydrogen, Microelectronics/Semiconductors) fed directly into IPCEIs approved by DG COMP.
- **13 alliances tracked here (chronological, launch year in brackets):** European Battery Alliance (2017), Circular Plastics Alliance (2018), European Clean Hydrogen Alliance (2020), European Raw Materials Alliance (2020), European Alliance for Industrial Data, Edge and Cloud (2021), Industrial Alliance on Processors and Semiconductor Technologies (2021), Renewable and Low-Carbon Fuels Value Chain Industrial Alliance (2022), Alliance for Zero-Emission Aviation (2022), European Solar Photovoltaic Industry Alliance (2022), European Industrial Alliance on Small Modular Reactors (2024), Critical Medicines Alliance (2024), European Connected and Autonomous Vehicle Alliance (2025), Critical Chemicals Alliance (2025).
- **Lead DGs span five services:** DG GROW (Battery, Circular Plastics, Clean Hydrogen, Raw Materials, Solar PV, Critical Chemicals), DG CNECT (Industrial Data/Edge/Cloud, Processors and Semiconductors, and the ECAVA secretariat), DG MOVE (Renewable and Low-Carbon Fuels), DG DEFIS (Zero-Emission Aviation), DG ENER (Small Modular Reactors), and HERA/DG SANTE (Critical Medicines).
- **How to join:** Each alliance publishes its own Call for Expression of Interest / membership form on its dedicated page under `single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/industry/industrial-alliances_en` (or the sectoral DG site for MOVE/DEFIS/ENER/HERA alliances). Applications are reviewed against published eligibility and transparency criteria (EU Transparency Register registration is typically required for industry associations and companies).
- **Master landing page:** `single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/industry/industrial-alliances_en` lists all alliances coordinated by DG GROW; alliances led by other DGs sit on their own sectoral sites (see table below).
- **Strategic umbrella:** Alliances are one of the Commission's standard tools under the Clean Industrial Deal (2025) and the earlier New Industrial Strategy for Europe (2020, updated 2021) framework for building resilient, competitive EU value chains without creating new spending programmes.

## How alliances are structured

Most alliances share a common governance template:
- **General Assembly:** plenary body of all members, meets at least once or twice a year, sets priorities and adopts recommendations/opinions.
- **Steering Board or Steering Committee:** smaller coordinating group (often Commission-chaired or co-chaired with an industry body) that defines deliverables and workplans.
- **Working groups:** thematic sub-groups (for example the RLCF Alliance's four roundtables, or the SMR Alliance's project-based working groups) that do the technical work between plenaries.
- **Secretariat:** usually the lead DG or an EU agency/body (EIT Raw Materials for ERMA, HERA for the Critical Medicines Alliance).
- **Outputs:** roadmaps, strategic action plans, investment project pipelines, and recommendations to the Commission and Member States. These outputs are advisory; alliances cannot adopt binding legislation or commit EU budget lines themselves.

## Relationship to IPCEIs and state aid

Important Projects of Common European Interest (IPCEIs) let Member States grant enhanced state aid for cross-border projects that deliver breakthrough innovation with strong positive spillovers and cannot otherwise be financed by the market (Article 107(3)(b) TFEU, assessed by DG COMP under the IPCEI Communication). Several alliances have been the launchpad for subsequent IPCEIs:
- The European Battery Alliance underpinned IPCEI on Batteries (2019) and IPCEI EuBatIn (2021).
- The European Clean Hydrogen Alliance's project pipeline fed IPCEI Hy2Tech (2022), IPCEI Hy2Use (2022) and IPCEI Hy2Infra (2023).
- The Processors and Semiconductor Technologies Alliance fed IPCEI ME/CT (Microelectronics and Communication Technologies, 2023) and IPCEI on Cloud Infrastructure and Services.

An alliance membership or roadmap listing is not, by itself, a state-aid clearance; individual projects must still be separately notified and approved.

## How to join an alliance

1. Check the alliance's dedicated page (DG GROW alliances: `single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/industry/industrial-alliances/[alliance-name]_en`; other alliances: the lead DG's sectoral site, for example `transport.ec.europa.eu` for RLCF, `defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu` for AZEA, `energy.ec.europa.eu` for the SMR Alliance, `health.ec.europa.eu` for the Critical Medicines Alliance, `digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu` for the CNECT-led alliances).
2. Register on the EU Transparency Register if representing a company, association or organised interest (most alliances require or strongly encourage this).
3. Submit the alliance-specific Expression of Interest or membership application, which is typically reviewed against published eligibility criteria (legal establishment in the EU/EEA, activity in the relevant value chain, no exclusive commitment conflicts).
4. Once accepted, members can join the General Assembly and opt into relevant working groups; most alliances publish member lists on their websites.

## Verification notes

Every alliance name, launch date and lead DG above was checked against the European Commission's own alliance pages and press releases (single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu, transport.ec.europa.eu, defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu, energy.ec.europa.eu, health.ec.europa.eu) as of July 2026. Two points to flag for users:
- The Alliance for Zero-Emission Aviation is coordinated by DG DEFIS (Defence Industry and Space), not DG MOVE, despite covering an aviation/transport value chain. This is a common misattribution; verify against `defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu` before citing.
- ECAVA (European Connected and Autonomous Vehicle Alliance) is the successor/rebrand of earlier informal automotive-sector dialogue; its secretariat sits with DG CNECT working alongside DG RTD, DG GROW and DG MOVE, reflecting the cross-cutting nature of connected/autonomous vehicle technology (digital, industrial, research and transport policy all apply).
