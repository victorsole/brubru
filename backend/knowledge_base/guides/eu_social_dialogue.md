# EU Social Dialogue and Collective Bargaining

## Overview

European Social Dialogue is the process through which EU-level social partners (employers' and workers' organisations) are consulted, negotiate, and conclude agreements on employment and social policy. It is distinct from collective bargaining, which is the negotiation process between employers and trade unions on working conditions, typically at national, sectoral, or company level.

Social Dialogue is enshrined in Articles 151-155 TFEU. The Commission must consult social partners before submitting social policy proposals (Art. 154), and social partners may negotiate framework agreements that can be implemented by Council decision (Art. 155).

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Social Dialogue | Collective Bargaining |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|
| Level | EU-wide (cross-industry or sectoral) | National, sectoral, or company |
| Parties | EU social partners (ETUC, BusinessEurope, SGI Europe, SMEunited) | National trade unions and employer associations |
| Legal basis | Art. 151-155 TFEU | National labour law, ILO conventions |
| Output | Framework agreements, joint opinions, guidelines | Collective agreements on pay, hours, conditions |
| Enforcement | Council decision or autonomous implementation | National courts and labour inspectorates |
| Scope | Employment policy, working conditions, social protection | Terms and conditions of employment |

### Sectoral Social Dialogue

44 sectoral social dialogue committees (formalised by Commission Decision 98/500/EC of 20 May 1998) covering specific industries (e.g., transport, chemicals, agriculture, banking, telecommunications, healthcare, professional football). 65 European employers' organisations and 15 trade union federations participate, representing approximately 185 million workers and over 6 million firms. Each committee adopts between 30-50 joint outcome positions per year.

## Treaty Framework

| Article | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Art. 151 TFEU | Objectives: promotion of employment, improved living/working conditions, social protection, social dialogue |
| Art. 152 TFEU | EU recognises and promotes role of social partners, facilitating dialogue, respecting their autonomy |
| Art. 153 TFEU | EU competence to support and complement Member States in working conditions, social security, worker information/consultation |
| Art. 154 TFEU | Commission obligation to consult social partners before proposing social policy legislation (two-stage consultation) |
| Art. 155 TFEU | Social partner agreements may be implemented by Council decision on joint request |

## Key CJEU Jurisprudence

### Collective Bargaining and Market Freedoms

| Case | Year | Subject |
|------|------|---------|
| C-438/05 Viking Line | 2007 | Right to collective action vs freedom of establishment. CJEU ruled that strike action can restrict freedom of establishment and must be proportionate. Landmark case limiting industrial action in cross-border contexts. |
| C-341/05 Laval | 2007 | Posted workers and collective bargaining. Swedish unions' blockade of Latvian construction company restricted freedom to provide services. CJEU limited ability of host-state unions to impose collective agreement terms beyond Posted Workers Directive minimum. |
| C-346/06 Rüffert | 2008 | Public procurement and minimum wages. German Land requirement to pay collective agreement wages in public contracts went beyond Posted Workers Directive requirements. Restricted to posted workers context. |
| C-319/06 Commission v Luxembourg | 2008 | Posted workers. Luxembourg's transposition of Posted Workers Directive went beyond permitted requirements. |

### Collective Agreements and Competition Law

| Case | Year | Subject |
|------|------|---------|
| C-67/96 Albany | 1999 | Collective agreements on supplementary pension schemes fall outside Art. 101 TFEU (competition law) when concluded between employers and workers and directly contributing to improved employment conditions. Key immunity established. |
| C-115/97, C-116/97, C-117/97 Brentjens | 1999 | Confirmed Albany doctrine: mandatory affiliation to sectoral pension fund established by collective agreement does not violate competition law. |
| C-222/98 van der Woude | 2000 | Extended Albany doctrine to health insurance schemes established by collective agreement. |

### Posted Workers

| Case | Year | Subject |
|------|------|---------|
| C-113/89 Rush Portuguesa | 1990 | Freedom to provide services includes bringing own workforce. Foundation case for posted workers framework. |
| C-43/93 Vander Elst | 1994 | Host Member State cannot require work permits for third-country nationals lawfully employed and posted by service provider from another Member State. |

### Recent Developments

| Case | Year | Subject |
|------|------|---------|
| C-19/23 Denmark v Parliament and Council | 2023 | Upheld Adequate Minimum Wages Directive (2022/2041). Denmark challenged legal basis; CJEU confirmed Art. 153(1)(b) TFEU as valid basis. Validates EU competence to set framework for collective bargaining promotion. |
| C-660/20 Lufthansa CityLine | 2022 | Collective agreement provisions on fixed-term contracts. CJEU interpreted Framework Agreement on fixed-term work (Directive 1999/70/EC). |

### European Pillar of Social Rights (Principle 8)

Principle 8 of the European Pillar of Social Rights specifically concerns social dialogue: "Social partners shall be consulted on the design and implementation of economic, employment and social policies according to national practices. They shall be encouraged to negotiate and conclude collective agreements in matters relevant to them, while respecting their autonomy and the right to collective action."
