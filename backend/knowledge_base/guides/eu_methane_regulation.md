# EU Methane Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1787)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Monday 20 July 2026)**: DG ENER published **"Commission provides clarity on how to effectively implement EU rules to reduce methane emissions"** (energy.ec.europa.eu newsroom, 20 July 2026). ⚠ **The content of this item was not retrievable at the time of writing — only its title and date are confirmed** (from the DG ENER newsroom listing). Do **not** describe what the clarification says until the document itself has been read. It sits in the same track as the December 2025 Council discussion on **pragmatic implementation of the importer requirements** (below), which is the live friction point.
- **Full name**: Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 of the European Parliament and of the Council on the reduction of methane emissions in the energy sector and amending Regulation (EU) 2019/942.
- **Entered into force: 4 August 2024.** The **first EU-wide rules** on methane emissions from the energy sector.
- **Scope**: the fossil **gas, oil and coal** industry — it binds **operators, undertakings and importers**. Methane is the second-largest greenhouse-gas contributor to climate change after CO2.
- **Two halves**: (1) domestic **MRV plus mitigation** duties on EU operators; (2) **import requirements** extending equivalent monitoring/reporting expectations to fossil energy placed on the Union market from third countries. The import side is the politically contested half.
- **Methane Transparency Database**: being prepared by the Commission, **scheduled to launch September 2026**.

## Core obligations
- **Measurement, reporting and verification (MRV)**. Reporting builds on the industry **OGMP 2.0** framework (which already covers roughly **42% of global oil and gas production**), shifting from generic estimates to direct measurement, checked by **independent accredited verifiers**. By **5 August 2025** operators had to submit a report quantifying source-level methane emissions using at least generic emission factors.
- **Leak detection and repair (LDAR)** — **Article 14**. Operators must survey for and repair leaks, and submit LDAR programmes to competent authorities: **by 5 May 2025 for existing sites**, or within six months for new sites.
- **Venting and flaring**: restricted, permitted only in specific exceptional circumstances. **Article 17** sets a **flaring efficiency requirement of at least 99%**.
- **Inactive, temporarily plugged, and permanently plugged/abandoned wells** — **Article 18(6) and (9)**. Where emissions are detected, operators must remediate, reclaim and permanently plug, where technically feasible and environmentally sustainable. A **mitigation plan is due by 5 August 2026**, to be **implemented within 12 months**. Where the responsible party lacks the financial means or cannot be identified, the **Member State** carries the obligation.
- **Closed and abandoned underground coal mines** — **Article 25**. From **5 May 2026**, methane emissions must be measured in all closed and abandoned underground coal mines where operations ceased **after 3 August 1954**; measurement equipment must be installed to allow a representative estimate of annual emissions. Operators submit yearly source-level estimates to the authority **by 5 August 2026 and annually thereafter**. Special rules/exemptions apply to flooded mines.
- **Rapid reaction mechanism for "super-emitting" events** — **Article 31**: the Commission establishes a mechanism for facilities or infrastructure emitting methane at very high rates.
- **Implementing acts pending**: the Commission is to set **reporting templates** and **standards for measurement and quantification**. Until those apply, operators must follow **state-of-the-art industry practices** — a live compliance ambiguity.
- **Penalties**: non-compliance risks financial penalties and, in some framings, loss of market access.

## The importer question (the live political friction)
The Regulation applies to an **"importer"**, defined in **Article 2(59)** as a natural or legal person who, in the course of a commercial activity, **places crude oil, natural gas or coal originating from a third country on the Union market**. Whether "placing on the market" captures only entities **physically importing** into the EU, or extends to **traders who never handle the physical asset**, has been a genuine interpretive dispute. Commission guidance has indicated the obligations attach to **physical imports**, an approach comparable to REACH. Treat this as guidance-level interpretation, not settled law.

Institutional track on this point:
- **9 December 2025** — the **Energy Union Task Force** discussed implementation of the Methane Regulation alongside energy prices.
- **15 December 2025** — **EU energy ministers** supported the Commission's approach on a **pragmatic implementation of the importer requirements** and endorsed the compliance solutions identified in the **Network of Competent Authorities**.
- **20 July 2026** — DG ENER publishes the implementation-clarity item above (content unverified; likely in this same track).

## International dimension
- **Global Methane Pledge**: launched by the Commission and the United States at **COP26 (2021)**, targeting a **30% cut in methane emissions by 2030**. **Canada took over from the US as co-convenor in 2025.**
- **23 June 2026** — Canada-EU joint statement supporting the UN Secretary-General's Call to Action on Methane.
- **International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO, UNEP)**: reaffirmed by global leaders at COP29.
