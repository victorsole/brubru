## Overview

The SMR Strategy sets out 9 actions to accelerate the development and deployment of Small Modular Reactors in Europe. SMRs are a new class of nuclear reactors that can be factory-built, transported to site, and deployed in modules. They offer lower upfront capital costs, shorter construction times, and flexibility for industrial applications beyond electricity generation.

Treaty basis: Euratom Treaty (1957, separate from TFEU) for nuclear matters; Article 194 TFEU for energy policy aspects.

## 3 Categories of SMRs

| Category | Output | Readiness | Examples |
|----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Light Water SMRs (LW-SMRs) | 50-300 MWe | Most mature, closest to deployment | NUWARD (France), UK SMR (Rolls-Royce) |
| Advanced Modular Reactors (AMRs) / Gen IV | 10-300 MWe | Medium-term (2030s-2040s) | Molten salt, high-temperature gas, lead-cooled |
| Microreactors | <10 MWe | Emerging | Remote areas, military bases, disaster relief |

## Industrial Use Cases

SMRs are not just for electricity -- they serve multiple industrial applications:

| Application | Context |
|-------------|---------|
| Chemical industry | 150 industrial clusters across EU need 200-550C process heat (currently fossil-fuelled) |
| District heating | 50% of EU final energy goes to heating/cooling; 75% from fossil fuels |
| Data centres | 70 TWh current consumption, projected 115 TWh by 2030. SMRs provide baseload 24/7 power |
| Hydrogen production | High-temperature electrolysis using SMR heat |
| Synthetic fuels | E-fuels for aviation and maritime |
| Desalination | Water-scarce regions |
| Maritime transport | Nuclear-powered vessels |
| Defence | Military installations, forward operating bases |
| Disaster relief | Rapid deployment for emergency power |

## 9 Strategic Actions

| # | Action | Detail |
|---|--------|--------|
| 1 | Focus Alliance on most promising designs | Concentrate resources on limited number of SMR designs closest to deployment |
| 2 | Competitive EU supply chain | Align with Industrial Accelerator Act (COM(2026) 100) local content requirements |
| 3 | Industrial standards | Fleet approach and modular manufacturing standards via CEN and ISO |
| 4 | De-risking schemes | EUR 200 million InvestEU top-up from Innovation Fund until 2028; also Innovation Fund and Scale Up Europe Fund |
| 5 | IPCEI on innovative nuclear | Important Project of Common European Interest currently in design phase |
| 6 | Net-Zero Acceleration Valleys | "SMR Valleys" for co-located nuclear-industrial clusters |
| 7 | Protect EU supply chain | FDI screening, merger control, IP protection for intra-EU flows |
| 8 | SMR Coalition | Policy/regulatory/licensing cooperation. Regulatory Sandboxes + Joint Early Reviews. Grant scheme for EU regulators |
| 9 | International cooperation | Partnerships with US, Canada, UK, Japan, South Korea, IAEA, OECD-NEA, Generation IV International Forum |

## Nuclear Illustrative Programme (PINC) -- March 2026

The Commission published the Nuclear Illustrative Programme (COM(2026)0120) and accompanying Staff Working Document (SWD(2026)0084) in March 2026. The PINC is the Commission's comprehensive assessment of nuclear energy investment, capacity, and safety across the EU, required under Article 40 of the Euratom Treaty.

Key aspects:
- Assessment of nuclear energy's role post-adoption of the SMR Strategy
- Investment needs for existing fleet life extensions and new builds (including SMRs)
- Nuclear fuel supply chain resilience (reducing dependency on Russian enrichment)
- Safety and waste management framework under Directives 2009/71/Euratom and 2011/70/Euratom
- Workforce and skills requirements for the nuclear sector
