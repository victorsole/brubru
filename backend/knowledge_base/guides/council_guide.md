# Understanding the Council of the EU

How the Council works and how to navigate it.

## Structure Overview

The Council is where member states negotiate and adopt legislation. It operates at multiple levels:

1. **European Council** - Heads of state/government, sets strategic direction
2. **Council of Ministers** - Ministers by policy area, formal adoption
3. **COREPER** - Ambassadors/deputies, political filtering
4. **Working Parties** - National experts, technical negotiation

## Council Configurations

The Council meets in 10 different formations:

| Configuration | Acronym | Key Topics |
|--------------|---------|------------|
| General Affairs | GAC | Horizontal coordination, enlargement |
| Foreign Affairs | FAC | CFSP, trade, development, defence |
| Economic and Financial | ECOFIN | Budget, taxation, financial services |
| Justice and Home Affairs | JHA | Migration, police, judicial cooperation |
| Employment, Social Policy | EPSCO | Labour law, social security |
| Competitiveness | COMPET | Internal market, industry, research |
| Transport, Telecom, Energy | TTE | Infrastructure, digital, energy |
| Agriculture and Fisheries | AGRIFISH | CAP, fisheries |
| Environment | ENVI | Climate, environmental protection |
| Education, Youth, Culture, Sport | EYCS | Education policy, cultural programmes |

## The Preparatory Machinery

### Working Parties (150+)
- National experts from capitals/permanent representations
- Technical line-by-line negotiation
- Where most text is agreed
- Meet frequently in Brussels

### COREPER
**COREPER II** (Ambassadors):
- Prepared by Antici Group
- Handles: foreign affairs, ECOFIN, JHA, general affairs
- More political, horizontal files

**COREPER I** (Deputies):
- Prepared by Mertens Group
- Handles: environment, transport, social, competitiveness
- More technical, sectoral files

### Special Committees
- **SCA** (Special Committee on Agriculture) - CAP files
- **PSC** (Political and Security Committee) - CFSP/CSDP
- **Trade Policy Committee** - Trade agreements

## Decision-Making

### Voting Methods
- **Qualified Majority (QMV)**: 55% of states + 65% of population
- **Simple Majority**: 15 of 27 states (procedural matters)
- **Unanimity**: All 27 must agree (taxation, CFSP, etc.)

### A-Points vs B-Points
- **A-points**: Agreed at lower level, adopted without discussion
- **B-points**: Require ministerial discussion

### General Approach
Council's negotiating position before trilogue. Not legally binding but politically important.

## The Presidency

### Rotating System
- Each member state holds presidency for 6 months
- Sets agendas, chairs meetings, drives negotiations
- Trio system: 3 consecutive presidencies coordinate 18-month programme

### Presidency Powers
- Determines which files to prioritise
- Controls meeting schedules
- Proposes compromise texts
- Represents Council in trilogue

## Key Documents

### Where to Find Them
- **Document Register**: Agendas, conclusions, meeting outcomes
- **Voting Results Database**: How each country voted
- **Press Releases**: Official statements (unspun)

### Document Types
- **Presidency compromise text**: Latest negotiating draft
- **Room documents**: Non-papers for discussion
- **Council conclusions**: Agreed political statements
- **General approach**: Negotiating mandate

## Influencing the Council

### Access Points
- **Permanent Representations**: Each member state has one in Brussels
- **Capital ministries**: Often more accessible than Brussels
- **Presidency team**: Key during their term
- **Working party delegates**: Technical expertise matters

### Timing
- **Before working party**: Shape national positions
- **During presidency**: Engage the chairing country
- **Before COREPER**: Last chance for political changes
- **Before ministerial**: Emergency interventions only

### What Works
- Technical expertise over political pressure
- Understanding national sensitivities
- Building coalitions across member states
- Being realistic about what's achievable

## Council vs Parliament

| Aspect | Council | Parliament |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Access | More closed | More open |
| Documents | Often restricted | Generally public |
| Negotiations | Behind closed doors | Committee hearings public |
| Key moment | General approach | Committee vote |
| Pressure points | National positions | Rapporteur + shadows |

## Challenges

### Opacity
- Many discussions not public
- Delegates Portal (internal) has key documents
- Room documents often confidential
- Need to rely on informal contacts

### Moving Target
- 27 national positions evolving
- Presidency compromises change frequently
- Blocking minorities can form quickly
- Implementation varies by country
