# EuroHPC JU: Supercomputers, AI Factories, AI Gigafactories and Integrated Quantum

## QUICK FACTS
- Body: European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU), a public-private partnership under Article 187 TFEU, seat in Luxembourg
- Legal basis: established by Council Regulation (EU) 2018/1488, recast by Council Regulation (EU) 2021/1173 (current governing regulation), amended by Council Regulation (EU) 2024/1732 of 17 June 2024 (AI Factories / start-up initiative), amended again by Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 of 16 January 2026 (AI Gigafactories plus a dedicated quantum technologies pillar; published in the Official Journal 19 January 2026, entered into force 20 January 2026)
- Members (38 total): all EU Member States, plus Associated Countries Albania, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Turkiye and the United Kingdom, plus Israel; 3 private members represent industry (ETP4HPC, BDVA, QuIC)
- Budget 2021-2027: EUR 8.2 billion total, of which the EU contributes EUR 4.1 billion (Digital Europe Programme EUR 2.1 billion, Horizon Europe EUR 1.7 billion, Connecting Europe Facility-2 EUR 320 million); participating states match EU funding; private members target roughly EUR 900 million
- Supercomputers: 12 systems across Europe -- JUPITER (Germany, first EU exascale system, operational), LUMI (Finland), LEONARDO (Italy), MareNostrum 5 (Spain) and MeluXina (Luxembourg) among the pre-exascale/petascale tier already operational; Alice Recoque (France) is a second exascale system under deployment. Full table below.
- AI Factories: 19 established as of mid-2026 (7 selected December 2024, 6 more March 2025, 6 more October 2025), plus 13 AI Factory Antennas extending free support to SMEs and startups in additional locations. Verify current count before citing -- calls remain open and this figure moves.
- AI Gigafactories: introduced by Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 (16 January 2026); large-scale facilities for training trillion-parameter AI models, each targeting compute capacity above 100,000 advanced AI processors, roughly matching hyperscaler-scale training clusters (Google, Microsoft/OpenAI, Meta, AWS, xAI); public-private partnership structure with dedicated funding, procurement and start-up/scale-up protection rules; first calls launched 2026
- Quantum: EuroHPC JU is deploying quantum computers integrated on-site with existing EuroHPC supercomputers (6 host sites selected in Czechia, Germany, Spain, France, Italy and Poland); the LUMI-Q consortium (9 countries) is deploying an IQM quantum computer (Halocene H4) at LUMI in Finland from 2027. **EuroQCI (European Quantum Communication Infrastructure) is a separate DG CNECT-led initiative for quantum key distribution and secure communications, not owned or operated by EuroHPC JU** -- do not conflate the two when answering Brubru queries.
- Access modes for AI Factories/supercomputers include extreme-scale, regular, benchmark, development, AI and data-intensive, and commercial access; exact eligibility and pricing vary by call, verify against `eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes_en` before quoting specifics to a user

EuroHPC JU is the EU's most consequential digital-sovereignty body: it owns or co-owns Europe's flagship supercomputers, runs the AI Factories programme that gives startups and researchers access to AI-optimised compute, hosts the newer AI Gigafactories initiative for frontier-scale AI training, and is building Europe's integrated quantum-HPC capability. It sits inside the Digital Europe Programme's High-Performance Computing objective (see `digital_europe_programme.md`) but has its own legal personality, budget and governance as a joint undertaking, and its remit has expanded twice since 2024 to keep pace with the AI Continent Action Plan.

## What EuroHPC JU Is

The European High Performance Computing Joint Undertaking is a legal and funding entity combining EU, national and private resources to build, deploy and operate a federated, secure, hyper-connected supercomputing and data infrastructure across Europe. It was created in 2018 (Council Regulation (EU) 2018/1488) to end Europe's historical dependence on US and Asian supercomputing capacity, then recast in 2021 under the current governing act, Council Regulation (EU) 2021/1173, which set the 2021-2027 budget and structure. Its mission has since been widened twice by amending regulations: the 2024 amendment added the AI Factories mandate, and the January 2026 amendment added AI Gigafactories and a dedicated quantum technologies pillar.

Governance combines public members (the European Commission and the 38 participating states/associated countries) with three private members representing the HPC, big data and quantum industries (ETP4HPC, BDVA, QuIC). This hybrid structure lets EuroHPC blend EU programme money (Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe, Connecting Europe Facility) with national co-investment and private capital, giving it a materially larger effective budget than a Commission-only instrument.

## AI Factories

AI Factories are dynamic ecosystems built around AI-optimised EuroHPC supercomputers, combining dedicated AI compute capacity with a one-stop-shop of support services (business development advice, technical support, testing environments) for startups, SMEs, researchers, industry and the public sector wishing to train or fine-tune AI models in Europe. The legal basis is Council Regulation (EU) 2024/1732 of 17 June 2024, which amended Regulation (EU) 2021/1173 specifically to enable this initiative and to boost European leadership in trustworthy AI.

Selection ran in three waves:
- **December 2024** (first 7): Finland, Germany, Greece, Italy, Luxembourg, Spain, Sweden
- **March 2025** (6 more): Austria, Bulgaria, France, Germany, Poland, Slovenia
- **October 2025** (6 more): Czechia, Lithuania, Netherlands, Romania, Spain, Poland

That brings the established total to 19 AI Factories, complemented by 13 AI Factory Antennas that extend free, tailored support (without hosting a full-scale AI supercomputer themselves) to additional Member States and regions. Named examples include IT4LIA (Italy) and HammerHAI. Under the original 2024 funding envelope, the EU contribution for acquiring new or upgraded AI-oriented EuroHPC supercomputers was estimated at EUR 400 million, with a total programme budget (EU plus co-investment) of up to EUR 800 million; the maximum EU contribution for establishing and running a single AI Factory's support services was set at EUR 15 million over a maximum of 3 years. Verify current per-Factory funding terms against the live EuroHPC calls page, since later waves and the 2026 amendment may adjust these ceilings.

Users eligible for AI Factory access include startups, SMEs, researchers, academia, industry and public administrations; access is generally described as free or subsidised for the support-service layer, with compute access governed by the access modes below.

## AI Gigafactories

AI Gigafactories are the next scale tier above AI Factories: large-scale facilities dedicated to developing and training next-generation AI models containing trillions of parameters, combining supercomputing infrastructure, AI-optimised computing capacity, data-centre support, secure cloud access and specialised AI services. Each planned Gigafactory targets compute capacity exceeding 100,000 advanced AI processors, intended to put European facilities roughly on par with the training infrastructure operated by leading AI companies (Google, Microsoft in partnership with OpenAI, Meta, Amazon Web Services, xAI).

The legal basis is Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 of 16 January 2026, amending Regulation (EU) 2021/1173. The Council reached its position in December 2025 and the regulation entered into force on 20 January 2026 (published in the Official Journal on 19 January 2026), extending EuroHPC JU's objectives to facilitate the creation and operation of AI Gigafactories through public-private partnerships involving Member States and industry, and adding a dedicated quantum technologies pillar to the same legal instrument. The regulation sets funding and procurement rules for Gigafactory partnerships and includes specific protection measures for startups and scale-ups participating in or relying on Gigafactory capacity. First calls under this framework opened in 2026; verify current call status and any confirmed Gigafactory site selections before citing specifics, as site selection was still in progress at time of writing.

Brubru users should not conflate AI Factories (operational since 2024/2025, mid-scale, startup/SME-facing support ecosystems) with AI Gigafactories (2026-onward, frontier-scale training infrastructure aimed at trillion-parameter models). They share a legal home (EuroHPC JU, Regulation (EU) 2021/1173 as amended) but are materially different in scale, purpose and maturity.

## Quantum: EuroHPC Integrated Quantum vs EuroQCI

EuroHPC JU is deploying quantum computers physically integrated on-site with existing EuroHPC supercomputers, enabling hybrid classical-quantum workflows where quantum processors handle specific algorithmic steps alongside conventional HPC systems. Six host sites were selected for the first wave of EuroHPC quantum computers: Czechia, Germany, Spain, France, Italy and Poland. A prominent example is LUMI-Q, a pan-European consortium of 9 countries (Czechia, Finland, Sweden, Denmark, Poland, Norway, Germany, Belgium, Netherlands) that selected IQM to deploy an advanced quantum computer (Halocene H4) alongside the LUMI supercomputer in Finland, with delivery planned from 2027 followed by phased upgrades. The January 2026 amending regulation (Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150) formalised a dedicated quantum technologies pillar within EuroHPC JU's legal mandate, covering this integrated quantum-HPC capability.

This is legally and operationally distinct from **EuroQCI (European Quantum Communication Infrastructure)**, a separate DG CNECT-led initiative focused on quantum key distribution (QKD) and quantum-secure communications networks, not on quantum computing. EuroHPC's role is quantum computing integrated with supercomputing; EuroQCI's role is quantum-secure communications infrastructure. Brubru should not describe EuroQCI as "hosted by EuroHPC JU" -- the two are complementary EU quantum initiatives with different legal bases, objectives and governance, and conflating them is a factual error to avoid.

## Access Modes

EuroHPC and AI Factory compute access is organised through several access modes, each with different eligibility, cost and allocation rules: extreme-scale access (flagship scientific/industrial projects requiring the largest resource allocations), regular access, benchmark access (short-term testing before a full application), development access (early-stage code preparation), AI and data-intensive access (tuned for AI Factory workloads), and commercial access (paid access for private-sector users outside grant-funded schemes). Exact terms, application windows and pricing differ by call and by system; verify current terms at `eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes_en` before advising a specific user on eligibility.

## Brubru Engagement Angles

- **Funding vs infrastructure questions:** distinguish "how do I get compute access" (AI Factories access modes, EDIH signposting) from "how is EuroHPC funded" (Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe, CEF-2, Member State and private co-investment) -- see `digital_europe_programme.md` for the funding-instrument view.
- **AI Factory vs AI Gigafactory confusion:** users researching EU AI compute policy frequently conflate the two; always clarify scale (mid-scale startup-facing vs frontier trillion-parameter training) and maturity (operational since 2024-2025 vs 2026-onward calls).
- **Supercomputer access queries:** route researchers and industrial users to the correct access mode (extreme-scale vs regular vs commercial) and to the specific host system relevant to their country or sector, not just "EuroHPC" generically.
- **Quantum queries:** never describe EuroQCI as part of EuroHPC JU; they are separate initiatives with separate legal bases. EuroHPC's quantum remit is integrated quantum-HPC computers (LUMI-Q and the 6 selected sites), not quantum-secure communications.
- **Digital sovereignty narrative:** EuroHPC JU is the concrete infrastructure layer behind the AI Continent Action Plan's compute ambitions; when a user asks how the EU plans to compete on AI compute with the US and China, EuroHPC's supercomputers, AI Factories and AI Gigafactories are the primary answer, not a footnote to InvestAI.
- **Moving figures:** AI Factory count, AI Gigafactory site selections, and supercomputer deployment status all change during 2026. Hedge any headline number and point to the live EuroHPC JU pages for the current count.
