# Public Access to Documents: Regulation (EC) 1049/2001

## QUICK FACTS
- **Current law:** Regulation (EC) No 1049/2001 of 30 May 2001 regarding public access to European Parliament, Council and Commission documents
- **CELEX:** 32001R1049
- **EUR-Lex:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32001R1049
- **OJ:** OJ L 145, 31.5.2001, p. 43
- **Legal basis:** Article 255 TEC (now Article 15(3) TFEU) + Article 42 Charter of Fundamental Rights (right of access to documents)
- **Scope:** EP, Council, Commission -- extended in practice to agencies, EEAS, bodies via inter-institutional agreements
- **Core rule:** Every citizen, natural or legal person residing in a Member State has right of access to documents
- **Response deadline:** 15 working days (extendable by another 15), tacit refusal if no reply
- **Revision procedure:** 2011/0073(COD) -- ordinary legislative procedure, co-decision; **dormant since 2011**
- **PETI draft report (23 April 2026):** PETI committee opened revival of file 2011/0073(COD) -- "Amendment to Regulation (EC) No 1049/2001 regarding public access to European Parliament, Council and Commission documents" (see https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/archives/9/peti). Procedurally complex: original Commission proposal COM(2008) 229 from 2008 long stalled. PETI exploring whether fresh Commission proposal is needed or dormant file can be reactivated.
- **Case law anchor:** Turco (C-39/05 P), Sweden v Commission (C-64/05 P), ClientEarth (C-612/13 P), Council v Access Info Europe (C-280/11 P) -- narrow reading of Article 4 exceptions, broader transparency in legislative processes
- **Commission Implementing Decision 2006/291/EC** (Euratom/ECSC): transparency rules for Commission
- **EP Rules of Procedure** Rule 115-116: transparency and public access to EP documents
- **Related:** Aarhus Regulation (EC) 1367/2006 on access to environmental information + CJEU Whirlpool Europe/ClientEarth jurisprudence

## Structure of Regulation 1049/2001

### Article 2 -- Beneficiaries
Any Union citizen, natural or legal person residing or having registered office in a Member State. Institutions **may** grant access to third-country nationals (discretionary).

### Article 3 -- Definitions
"Document" = any content produced or received by the institution (text, image, sound recording, audiovisual, electronic file). Covers draft documents, internal notes, trilogue documents, meeting minutes, procedural correspondence.

### Article 4 -- Exceptions (narrow, to be interpreted strictly)
Institutions refuse access where disclosure would undermine:
1. Public interest (security, defence, international relations, financial/monetary policy, Member State/Commission privacy of proceedings)
2. Privacy and integrity of the individual (in line with Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 on EU data protection)
3. Commercial interests of a natural/legal person, intellectual property
4. Court proceedings and legal advice, inspections/investigations/audits

Most litigated: Article 4(3) "space to think" exception for ongoing decision-making processes, Article 4(2) third indent legal advice (see Turco).

### Article 7-8 -- Application procedure
15 working days initial response; 15 working days extension possible; confirmatory application if refused; right of action before the General Court + complaint to the European Ombudsman.

### Article 10 -- Registers
Each institution publishes a public register of documents. Commission register (`ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register`), Council register, EP register.

### Article 11 -- Sensitive documents
Classified documents (SECRET/CONFIDENTIAL UE/EU): access only with originator's consent; additional safeguards.

## Why the Revision (2011/0073(COD)) Matters

The 2008 Commission proposal (COM(2008) 229) aimed to codify CJEU case law and expand scope to all EU institutions + agencies. It stalled in Council despite multiple EP readings. Key dissensus:

- **Expansion of "document" definition:** EP wanted broader coverage (email traffic, trilogue documents); Council resisted
- **Legislative trilogue transparency:** post-Access Info Europe (C-280/11 P), trilogue documents should enjoy wider disclosure, but practice diverges
- **Commission expert groups:** access to preparatory documents (Commission position: drafts exempt under Article 4(3))
- **Space-to-think doctrine:** scope of Article 4(3) exception for internal deliberations
- **Database extracts:** whether structured data extracts count as "documents"

## PETI 23 April 2026 Revival

PETI committee published a draft report under procedure 2011/0073(COD), signalling fresh political attention to the file. Context:
- European Ombudsman complaint trend: transparency the #1 subject of complaints 2022-2025 (see discharge briefings EPRS_UNK_139742a59274 + European Ombudsman annual report 2024)
- **PETI draft report on Ombudsman annual report 2024 (23 April 2026, procedure 2025/2138(INI))** -- parallel track highlighting systemic transparency failures
- Access Info Europe and civil society pressure on trilogue transparency
- Increased use of "off-the-record" Council mini-lateral meetings
- Generative AI / LLM access to EU documents underscores need for machine-readable registers

## Key CJEU Case Law Summary

| Case | Year | Key holding |
|---|---|---|
| Turco (C-39/05 P) | 2008 | Legal advice exception (Art 4(2) third indent) does not apply generally to legislative process legal advice |
| Sweden v Commission (C-64/05 P) | 2007 | Member State cannot unilaterally veto disclosure of its document held by Commission |
| Council v Access Info Europe (C-280/11 P) | 2013 | Identity of Member States in Council working group positions is disclosable; narrow "space to think" |
| ClientEarth (C-612/13 P) | 2015 | Broad disclosure for environmental information under Aarhus Regulation |
| Commission v Breyer (C-213/15 P) | 2017 | Court submissions are "documents" under 1049/2001 |
| Pablo Aguado v Commission (T-265/22) | 2023 | Data extracted from databases = "documents" |

## EU Transparency Ecosystem

- **Commission documents register:** `ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register`
- **Council transparency:** `consilium.europa.eu/en/documents-publications/public-register`
- **EP public register:** `europarl.europa.eu/RegistreWeb`
- **EU Transparency Register:** `transparency-register.europa.eu` (lobbying -- different legal base)
- **European Ombudsman:** complaints on maladministration including access refusals -- `ombudsman.europa.eu`
- **Open Data Portal:** `data.europa.eu` (machine-readable datasets, not the same as document access)
