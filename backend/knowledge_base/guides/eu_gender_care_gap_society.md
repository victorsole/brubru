# EU Gender Care Gap and Care Society Strategy

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Thursday 21 May 2026 — RESOLUTION ADOPTED)**: The European Parliament adopted in plenary **P10_TA(2026)0190** "Advancing towards a care society: addressing the gender care gap" (joint EMPL + FEMM responsibility). Source: EP press-room item "Closing the gender care gap: better support for formal and informal carers" (21 May 2026, status "Adopted - Plenary session"). The resolution sets out Parliament's recommendations for advancing gender equality in formal and informal care work and asks the Commission to propose, in the second half of 2026, a comprehensive **EU Care Strategy update** (the original European Care Strategy was adopted as COM(2022) 440 in September 2022) plus a binding EU-wide **Care Society Directive** covering minimum standards for the recognition, remuneration, and social protection of informal carers, and minimum quality requirements for formal long-term care.
- **EP procedure**: own-initiative report (INI), joint EMPL + FEMM. T-document and A-report references will be confirmed at the next OEIL sweep once `P10_TA(2026)0190` is indexed.
- **Background instrument 1**: **European Care Strategy** — Commission Communication **COM(2022) 440** of 7 September 2022, accompanied by two Council Recommendations: on early childhood education and care (the "Barcelona targets" revision) and on long-term care.
- **Background instrument 2**: Council Recommendation of 8 December 2022 on access to affordable, high-quality long-term care (OJ C 476, 15 December 2022).
- **Background instrument 3**: Council Recommendation of 8 December 2022 revising the **Barcelona targets** for early childhood education and care.
- **Related directive**: Work-Life Balance Directive **(EU) 2019/1158** (CELEX **32019L1158**) — minimum 10 days paternity leave, 4 months parental leave (2 non-transferable), 5 days carers' leave per year, right to request flexible working arrangements for carers and parents of children under 8.
- **Lead DG**: DG EMPL (Employment, Social Affairs and Inclusion)
- **Commissioner**: Roxana Mînzatu (People, Skills and Preparedness)
- **EP lead committees**: EMPL (Employment and Social Affairs) + FEMM (Women's Rights and Gender Equality)

## What the EP resolution covers

The resolution frames care as a **public good** rather than a private/household responsibility, and asks the Commission to:

1. **Recognise informal care work** in EU and national statistics, including via a harmonised satellite account for unpaid care work in national accounts (cross-reference to Eurostat's Time Use Survey methodology).
2. **Address the gender pay and pension gap** rooted in unpaid care responsibilities (women perform on average 13 hours more unpaid care work per week than men across the EU-27 — Eurofound 2023).
3. **Set binding minimum standards for long-term care** including: ratio of care workers per resident, mandatory training and supervision, transparent grievance procedures, and migration-of-care-workers safeguards (cross-link with the **Talent Pool Regulation** procedure).
4. **Recognise and remunerate informal carers**: mandatory access to social security entitlements (pension credits, health insurance, unemployment), respite-care rights, and carer's-leave reform building on Directive 2019/1158.
5. **Address sectoral undervaluation** of care work via collective bargaining promotion and minimum-wage strategies — cross-link to the **Adequate Minimum Wages Directive (EU) 2022/2041** and the **Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970**.
6. **Tackle migration and ethnic-origin inequalities** in the care workforce (over-representation of migrant women in undeclared, underpaid care work).

## Why care is now an EU policy priority

Care is a converging point of several EU 2025-2030 priorities:

- **Demographic transition**: Eurostat's "Demography of Europe 2026" edition (published 21 May 2026) confirms the EU-27 old-age dependency ratio is on track to reach **57.1% by 2050** (vs 33.4% in 2022). This drives demand for long-term care.
- **Gender equality**: closing the EU Gender Equality Strategy 2020-2025 gap; care responsibilities remain the single largest driver of women's part-time employment.
- **Affordable Housing Plan + Cost-of-Living agenda**: care affordability is part of the cost-of-living crunch on Europe's middle class.
- **Anti-Poverty Strategy 2026** (Social Package, 6 May 2026 adoption): lone carers are among the highest-risk groups for poverty.

## What happens next

- The EP resolution is non-binding but politically anchors Parliament's expectations for the Commission's 2027 Work Programme.
- Expect the Commission to respond within the standard 3-month window (Inter-Institutional Agreement on Better Law-Making, Rule 138 of EP's RoP).
- Commissioner Mînzatu has signalled openness to a Care Society legislative proposal in the second half of 2026 as part of the wider Social Package implementation.
- Watch the **27 May 2026 College of Commissioners** for any cross-reference in the Outermost Regions strategy (care-worker shortages are acute in OR territories).
