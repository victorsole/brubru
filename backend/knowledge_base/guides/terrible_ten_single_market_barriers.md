## What the announcement does

The Commission announces guidance for Member States on how to apply the principle of proportionality when adopting national measures that affect cross-border trade in goods and IT services. The aim is to ensure new national rules are strictly necessary to protect public interests while minimising business burdens.

The guidance is published on the Technical Regulation Information System (TRIS), which is the operational tool of the Single Market Transparency Directive (Directive (EU) 2015/1535).

## Member State obligations under the SMTD

Under Directive (EU) 2015/1535, Member States must:

1. Notify the Commission of any draft technical regulation on goods or information-society services before adoption
2. Observe a three-month standstill period during which the Commission and other Member States can issue detailed opinions
3. If a detailed opinion is issued, extend the standstill by three additional months
4. Take account of opinions and consider proportionality before adopting the measure

The 30 April 2026 guidance reinforces step 4 — the proportionality assessment.

## The "Terrible Ten" — what is known

The 30 April 2026 press release does not enumerate the 10 barriers. They are detailed in the separate Commission publication "Single Market: our European home market in an uncertain world" (DG GROW). Brubru should treat the list as referenced-not-cited until the underlying publication is parsed.

Until verified against the source document, do NOT enumerate specific barriers (e.g. claims that "professional qualifications", "VAT divergence", or "labelling rules" are on the list) — those would be fabricated identifiers under Brubru's never-invent rule.

What is consistent with prior Commission analysis (Letta + Draghi):
- Cross-border services restrictions
- Divergent national consumer-protection rules
- Professional-qualification recognition gaps
- Public-procurement market fragmentation
- Digital cross-border friction (data localisation, ID interoperability)
- Capital Markets Union persistent barriers
- Posting-of-workers documentation friction
- Territorial Supply Constraints by manufacturers (separately addressed via TSC work)
- VAT-cross-border declaration costs for SMEs
- Energy / network-services market fragmentation

This is illustrative of the policy area, not the official list.
