# EU Protection of Migrant Workers + Fight Against Illegal Employment (DG HOME, 13 May 2026)

## QUICK FACTS

- **LATEST (Wednesday 13 May 2026)**: **Commission announces operational package to better protect migrant workers and fight illegal employment** (DG HOME press release, IP/26 series). Two-track action:
  1. **Two implementation reports** adopted: on the **Employers Sanctions Directive (2009/52/EC)** and on the **Seasonal Workers Directive (2014/36/EU)** — assessing Member State enforcement of the existing legal framework
  2. **EUR 10 million call for expression of interest** under the Asylum, Migration and Integration Fund (**AMIF**) — Specific Action **AMIF/2026/SA/2.4.1**, up to **90% EU co-financing**, supporting multi-stakeholder projects in high-risk sectors (agriculture, care, construction, transport, hospitality)
  3. **2026 evaluation launch** of the Employers Sanctions Directive — preparing the ground for a potential targeted revision
  4. **European Labour Authority (ELA) mandate review** — sharper focus on third-country worker protection
  5. **Intensified Talent Partnerships** cooperation with third countries
- **Member State application deadline (AMIF call)**: **28 August 2026**
- **Lead DG**: DG HOME (Migration and Home Affairs)
- **Responsible Commissioner**: **Magnus Brunner** (Migration and Home Affairs, Austria)
- **EP lead committee**: LIBE (Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs) — with EMPL (Employment and Social Affairs) for the labour-rights dimension
- **Council configuration**: JHA (Justice and Home Affairs) + EPSCO (Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs)
- **Type**: Implementation reports + funding call + evaluation launch — NOT a new legislative proposal. This is the operational enforcement layer for legal migration policy
- **Enforcement scale (2024 baseline)**:
  - Nearly **600,000 targeted inspections** in high-risk sectors
  - **28,000+ detections** of irregularly employed migrant workers
  - **EUR 200 million+** in annual financial sanctions imposed on employers

## What the 13 May 2026 package does

This is the **operational follow-up** to the existing legal-migration framework, not a new proposal. The Commission's read: the legal architecture exists (Employers Sanctions Directive 2009 + Seasonal Workers Directive 2014 + Single Permit Directive 2011/98/EU recast 2024 + Talent Pool Regulation forthcoming) but **enforcement is uneven across Member States** and **third-country workers remain disproportionately exposed to exploitation** in five sectors.

The package therefore moves on **three operational levers** simultaneously:

1. **Diagnose** — two implementation reports tell Member States where they fall short
2. **Fund** — EUR 10M AMIF call seeds multi-stakeholder projects in high-risk sectors
3. **Prepare revision** — the 2026 evaluation of 2009/52/EC opens the door to a targeted update later

## Legal framework (what this package builds on)

### Employers Sanctions Directive — Directive 2009/52/EC
- **CELEX**: 32009L0052
- **Adopted**: 18 June 2009, OJ L 168, 30.6.2009
- **Legal basis**: Article 79(2) TFEU (legal migration policy)
- **Core obligations**:
  - Member States must **prohibit employment of third-country nationals staying illegally** (Art. 3)
  - **Employer due diligence**: copy/check residence permit before recruitment, notify competent authority (Art. 4)
  - **Financial sanctions** + **criminal sanctions** for serious cases (Arts. 5, 9): sanctions for repeat offences, work in particularly exploitative conditions, employment of minors, victim trafficking
  - **Subcontracting chain liability** (Art. 8): principal contractor liable for sanctions if sub-contractor breaches
  - **Back-pay** obligation: employer pays outstanding wages + social-security contributions for the entire period of illegal employment
  - **Member State inspections** (Art. 14): risk-based, targeted at sectors at highest risk
- **2026 evaluation**: launching now (announced 13 May 2026); a revised proposal is foreseeable for 2027-2028 once evaluation conclusions are tabled

### Seasonal Workers Directive — Directive 2014/36/EU
- **CELEX**: 32014L0036
- **Adopted**: 26 February 2014, OJ L 94, 28.3.2014
- **Legal basis**: Article 79(2)(a), (b) TFEU
- **Core mechanism**: harmonised conditions for third-country seasonal workers (max 5-9 months/year), equal treatment with national workers on pay, working conditions, social security
- **Sectors covered (mostly)**: agriculture, horticulture, tourism, hospitality
- **2026 implementation report**: documents Member State enforcement gaps

### Companion legal instruments (cross-link)
- **Single Permit Directive 2011/98/EU recast as Directive (EU) 2024/1233** — single application procedure for residence + work permit; equal-treatment rights for third-country workers — see `eu_migration_asylum_pact`
- **Talent Pool Regulation** (forthcoming) — see `eu_talent_pool_regulation_2026` — Commission proposal Q3 2025, OEIL ref forthcoming; creates an EU-level matchmaking tool between EU employers facing labour shortages and third-country job-seekers
- **Anti-Trafficking Directive 2011/36/EU** (revised by Directive (EU) 2024/1712) — labour-trafficking dimension overlaps with exploitative employment
- **Posted Workers Directive 96/71/EC** (revised by 2018/957) — distinct regime for intra-EU postings; some posted-worker fraud overlaps with illegal-employment patterns

## The AMIF call (AMIF/2026/SA/2.4.1)

- **Budget**: EUR 10 million
- **Co-financing rate**: up to **90% EU contribution**
- **Eligible applicants**: Member States, social partners, NGOs, sectoral employer organisations, labour inspectorates, multi-stakeholder consortia
- **Eligible actions**: prevention, detection, prosecution and victim-protection projects in high-risk sectors (agriculture, care, construction, transport, hospitality)
- **Deadline**: **28 August 2026**
- **Specific Action ID**: AMIF/2026/SA/2.4.1
- **Source**: EU Funding & Tenders Portal — search "AMIF Specific Action 2.4.1"

## The five high-risk sectors

The Commission's targeting reflects where inspectorate data (2023-2024) shows the highest incidence of irregular employment of third-country nationals:

1. **Agriculture + horticulture** — seasonal labour, isolated rural sites, language barriers
2. **Care** (long-term care + domestic work) — private households, low inspector visibility, informal contracts
3. **Construction** — subcontracting chains, posted-worker abuse overlaps
4. **Transport + logistics** — cross-border mobility complicates inspection
5. **Hospitality** — high seasonal demand, cash-economy elements

The AMIF call expects projects to focus on at least one of these sectors.

## Why this matters now

- **Demographic + labour-market pressure**: EU labour shortages in the five target sectors are structural; third-country labour is part of the answer, but only if it operates within the legal framework
- **Strategic Agenda 2024-2029** commitment to fight illegal migration AND attract talent — this package keeps both legs visible: enforcement + facilitation
- **AI Act + posted-worker rules + ELA mandate review** all touch the same employer-control surface — this package coordinates rather than fragments
- **Member State accountability**: the two implementation reports name uneven enforcement; the AMIF call rewards Member States that step up
