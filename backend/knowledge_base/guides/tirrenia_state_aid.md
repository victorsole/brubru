# Commission Decision (EU) 2020/1412 — Tirrenia / CIN State Aid

## QUICK FACTS

- **CELEX:** 32020D1412
- **Decision date:** 2 March 2020
- **Published:** OJ L 332, 13.10.2020, pp. 1-100
- **Case references:** SA.32014, SA.32015, SA.32016 (11/C) (ex 11/NN)
- **Member State:** Italy
- **Beneficiaries:** Tirrenia di Navigazione S.p.A. (seller / insolvent operator); Compagnia Italiana di Navigazione S.r.l. (CIN) (acquirer)
- **Sector:** Maritime passenger and freight cabotage (connections between mainland Italy, Sardinia, Sicily and smaller islands)
- **Legal basis:** Articles 106(2) and 107(1) TFEU; 2011 SGEI Framework; 2005 SGEI Decision (2005/842/EC); 2004 Rescue and Restructuring Guidelines
- **Outcome:** Partial compatibility + partial incompatibility + recovery of EUR 25,203,063.89 (rescue aid) + EUR 11,421,300 (diverted ship-upgrade funds) + tax exemptions; no economic continuity to CIN

## Context

From 1 January 2009 until 18 July 2012, Italy paid public-service compensation to Tirrenia di Navigazione under the prolongation of initial maritime Conventions dating from 1991. These covered twelve routes connecting the Italian mainland with Sardinia, Sicily and the Tremiti Islands. Tirrenia was State-owned (via Fintecna / Ministry of Economy and Finance) and entered extraordinary administration in August 2010.

Italy conducted a privatisation tender for the Tirrenia business branch (ships, assets and a new eight-year Convention) beginning September 2010. Only one binding offer was received, from CIN (a consortium of Onorato Partecipazioni, Grimaldi and Marinvest): EUR 380,100,000 with a deferred-payment element. The sale contract was signed on 25 July 2011; ownership transferred on 19 July 2012 following merger clearance. CIN continued operating most of the same routes under the new Convention until 18 July 2020.

The Commission opened a formal investigation in October 2011 covering six groups of measures. It received observations from Italy, CIN, Grimaldi, and third-party competitor GNV. The decision runs to more than 540 recitals and addresses each measure individually.

---

## The Six Measures Assessed

### 1. PSO Compensation to Tirrenia (1 January 2009 to 18 July 2012) and Berthing Priority

Italy paid annual compensation capped at EUR 80,010,000 for 2009 and EUR 72,685,642 per year thereafter (Law No 166/2009). An associated berthing priority at Italian ports was granted under Article 19-ter of the same law.

**State aid?** Yes. Both constituted State resources, were imputable to Italy, were selective and distorted competition (recitals 267-316). Altmark 4 was not met because the Convention was simply prolonged rather than competitively re-tendered, and no efficient-operator benchmark analysis was conducted.

**Compatible?** Yes, under Article 106(2) TFEU and the 2011 SGEI Framework (and for nine of twelve routes additionally under the 2005 SGEI Decision). The Commission confirmed: genuine SGEI with no manifest error on route selection; parameters established in advance via CIPE Directive; annual route-by-route accounts submitted; no overcompensation (annual audits confirmed net costs exceeded compensation in 2010-2012) (recitals 432-484).

### 2. New Convention and Privatisation Package Awarded to CIN (from 18 July 2012)

The package comprised: the new eight-year Convention with compensation ceiling EUR 72,685,642/year; the Tirrenia business branch (ships, assets, port access rights, the Tirrenia brand); a deferred payment structure (EUR 200,100,000 fixed upfront + EUR 180,000,000 in three deferred instalments); berthing priority.

**State aid?** No. The Commission found all four Altmark conditions satisfied (recitals 324-408, Article 5):

- Altmark 1: SGEI designation was justified route by route.
- Altmark 2: Parameters (CIPE Directive, ceiling set by law) were established in advance.
- Altmark 3: No overcompensation; CIN's realised return 2012-2018 was approximately 3.4% against the 6.5% foreseen, leaving a EUR 47 million shortfall.
- Altmark 4: Despite only one binding bid, the Commission confirmed sufficient safeguards (wide publication, minimum price by independent expert Banca Profilo, disclosure of CIN's bid to competing parties, explicit invitation to submit higher offers, 16-day extension).

### 3. Prolongation of Rescue Aid Guarantee (28 August 2011 to 18 September 2012)

The Commission had approved EUR 40 million in rescue aid for Tirrenia/Siremar by earlier decision. A restructuring/liquidation plan was due by 28 August 2011. Italy failed to submit one. The guarantee was called on 11 July 2011 (Tirrenia drew EUR 25,203,063.89); repayment occurred on 18 September 2012.

**Incompatible:** The prolongation from 28 August 2011 to 18 September 2012 constituted incompatible aid. Recovery: EUR 25,203,063.89 principal (substantially pre-repaid) plus outstanding interest from 28 February and 23 March 2011 (tranche disbursement dates) and 11 July 2011 (guarantee call date).

### 4. Ship-Upgrade Funds Used for Liquidity

EUR 12,051,900 was allocated by the 2010 Law for Tirrenia to upgrade vessels to Stockholm Agreement safety standards. Tirrenia drew EUR 4,657,005.35 via a Banca Carige offset arrangement; only EUR 630,600 was demonstrably spent on upgrades (vessel Clodia). The remaining EUR 11,421,300 was used for liquidity. CIN ultimately paid for the remaining required upgrades from its own funds.

**Incompatible:** EUR 11,421,300 constitutes incompatible operating aid from 6 October 2010. Recovery interest runs from that date.

### 5. Indirect Tax Exemptions on Privatisation Transfers

Law No 111/2011 exempted asset transfers in the privatisation from registration duty, land registry fees, and stamp duty.

**Incompatible:** These were not SGEI-linked and could not be justified under Article 106(2) TFEU (Tirrenia was no longer providing SGEI at that stage) or Article 107(2)/(3) TFEU. Recovery amount to be calculated by Italy.

### 6. Corporate Income Tax Exemption on Sale Proceeds

Law No 111/2011 exempted the sale proceeds from the Tirrenia business branch from corporate income tax.

**Incompatible:** Same grounds. Recovery to be effected by withholding the exemption when liquidation proceeds are distributed.

## The Altmark Four Conditions

The Commission in this case provides a thorough worked example of each Altmark condition in a maritime context:

**Altmark 1 (genuine SGEI):** Private operators must not adequately provide equivalent year-round continuity, regularity, capacity and quality. The Commission assessed thirteen routes individually. Routes where competition existed but did not meet PSO standards (especially off-season) qualified.

**Altmark 2 (parameters established in advance):** The CIPE Directive of 9 November 2007 prescribed the compensation methodology (WACC/CAPM-based return on capital, a risk premium for PSO volatility, eligible cost categories) before the relevant periods began. The 2009 Law set the ceiling. Both were public and transparent.

**Altmark 3 (no overcompensation):** Route-by-route accounts, certified annually, are the primary tool. For both Tirrenia (2009-2012) and CIN (2012-2018) the Commission verified audited accounts against the ceiling. Both showed undercompensation.

**Altmark 4 (competitive tender or efficient-operator benchmark):** The September 2010 tender satisfied this despite a single bid, because: (a) 21 parties expressed interest; (b) 11 conducted due diligence; (c) Banca Profilo set a EUR 380 million minimum; (d) CIN's bid was disclosed; (e) remaining parties were invited to beat it; (f) a 16-day extension was granted. The Commission explicitly stated this distinguished the case from SNCM.

---

## SGEI Compatibility under Article 106(2) TFEU

Where Altmark 4 fails (as it did for Tirrenia's prolongation), the aid must be assessed under Article 106(2) TFEU and the appropriate SGEI instrument:

- **2011 SGEI Framework** (OJ C 8, 11.1.2012): applies to all routes; requires genuine SGEI, compliant entrustment act, appropriate duration, separated accounts, no overcompensation, non-discriminatory procurement.
- **2005 SGEI Decision** (2005/842/EC): applies additionally to nine routes where annual passenger traffic did not exceed 300,000; these are notification-exempt and can be declared compatible directly.
- **Three high-traffic routes** (Napoli-Palermo, Civitavecchia-Olbia, Genova-Porto Torres): only the SGEI Framework applies; individually assessed.

All conditions were met: SGEI justified; CIPE Directive served as the compliant entrustment instrument; annual audit accounts submitted; no overcompensation detected.

## Economic Continuity: Does CIN Inherit Recovery Liability?

The Commission applied the Fortischem / Italy v Commission (Belgium) economic-continuity test to determine whether CIN was obligated to repay the incompatible measures owed by Tirrenia.

Key findings (recitals 521-541):

| Factor | Finding |
|---|---|
| Scope of transfer | Only essential SGEI assets; no liabilities; workforce not automatically transferred |
| Transfer price | Market price confirmed by independent Ecorys study; comparable to or above Banca Profilo EUR 380m valuation |
| Identity of owners | Completely different (State-owned Fintecna vs private CIN consortium) |
| Timing | Sale contract signed 25 July 2011, BEFORE Commission investigation opened 5 October 2011 |
| Business logic | Route continuation was logical consequence of genuine public need, not circumvention of recovery |

**Conclusion:** No economic continuity. CIN does not inherit the recovery obligation (Article 8 of the Decision).
