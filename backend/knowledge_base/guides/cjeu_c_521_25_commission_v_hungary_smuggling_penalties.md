# Commission v Hungary, C-521/25 (15 October 2026): converting prison sentences for people smuggling into "special reintegration detention" breaches EU law

## QUICK FACTS
- **Court of Justice, judgment of 15 October 2026, Case C-521/25, Commission v Hungary** (infringement action; press release No 150/26). **The Court upheld the Commission's action: Hungary failed to fulfil its obligations.**
- **The EU law breached:** Articles 1 and 3 of **Council Directive 2002/90/EC** (defining the facilitation of unauthorised entry, transit and residence) and Article 1(1) and (3) of **Council Framework Decision 2002/946/JHA** (the penal framework against that facilitation). Together they require **effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties**.
- **Naming:** the Court's English press release headlines "trafficking in human beings", but the legal basis it cites is the facilitation ("people smuggling") package above, and the Commission's complaint was a risk of impunity for **facilitating illegal immigration**. Say "people smuggling" and cite the two acts.
- **The Hungarian measure:** a **2023 government decree** automatically converted a final custodial sentence (where the court also ordered expulsion) into **"special reintegration detention"**: the person had to leave Hungary within **72 hours** of release and could not return for the length of the sentence. The decree was **repealed on 19 August 2025**; **2,525 people** had been placed under the scheme (the Court's figure).
- **Why it fails:** not **effective** (the sentence is not served); not **dissuasive** (at worst, deportation and a ban on return); not **proportionate** (conversion and release are automatic, with no individual assessment).
- **What follows:** Hungary must comply with the judgment. No financial penalty was imposed by this judgment; if Hungary did not comply, the Commission could bring a second action seeking one (Article 260(2) TFEU).
- **Not read:** the full judgment; this guide follows the Court's press release.
