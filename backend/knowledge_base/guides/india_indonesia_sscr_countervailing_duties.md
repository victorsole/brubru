# India and Indonesia Stainless Steel Countervailing Duties (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/433)

## QUICK ANSWERS

**What does this Regulation do?** Imposes definitive countervailing (anti-subsidy) duties on imports of stainless steel cold-rolled flat products (SSCR) from India and Indonesia, and amends the parallel anti-dumping Regulation (EU) 2021/2012 on the same product so the two duties do not double-count export subsidies.

**Who pays what (definitive countervailing duty, Article 1(2))?**

| Country / Company | Countervailing duty | TARIC additional code |
|---|---|---|
| India — Jindal Stainless Limited | **4.3 %** | C654 |
| India — Jindal Stainless Hisar Limited | **4.3 %** | C655 |
| India — Chromeni Steels Private Limited | **7.5 %** | C656 |
| India — all other companies | **7.5 %** | C999 |
| Indonesia — PT. Indonesia Ruipu Nickel and Chrome Alloy (IRNC) | **21.4 %** | C657 |
| Indonesia — PT. Jindal Stainless Indonesia | **0 %** | C658 |
| Indonesia — non-sampled cooperating company | **13.5 %** | See Annex 2 |
| Indonesia — all other companies | **20.5 %** | C999 |

**Combined countervailing + anti-dumping rates** (Article 2 / recital 1067, after avoiding double-counting under Article 24(1)):

- Chromeni Steels (India): 7.5 % CVD + 35.3 % AD = **42.8 %** (highest combined)
- Jindal Stainless / Jindal Hisar (India): 4.3 % CVD + 10.0 % AD = **14.3 %**
- IRNC Group (Indonesia): 21.4 % CVD + 9.3 % AD = **30.7 %**
- All other Indonesian: 21.4 % CVD + 19.3 % AD = **40.7 %**

**Did provisional duties get collected?** No. The Commission informed parties on 20 October 2021 that it would not impose provisional measures and would continue the investigation (recital 51).

**Why was Jindal Indonesia given a 0 % duty?** Its subsidy margin was 0.02 %, below the de minimis threshold of Article 14(5), so no countervailing duty was imposed on it (recital 942).

**What was the biggest Indonesian subsidy?** The provision of **nickel ore for less than adequate remuneration (LTAR)**. Indonesia holds about 21 million tonnes of nickel reserves (~20 % of the world's known nickel ore). Through the 2009 Mining Law's domestic-processing requirement and nickel-ore export bans (11 January 2014, then a complete ban in 2020), the Government of Indonesia kept the domestic ore price far below world prices, subsidising its stainless-steel producers. The nickel-ore LTAR amount for the IRNC Group was 9.64 % (recital 542).

**Why is China involved in an Indonesia case?** Production was concentrated in the **Morowali Industrial Park** (managed by the Sino-Indonesian company IMIP, operating since October 2013). Preferential financing reached the Indonesian producers from Chinese policy banks and the China-ASEAN Investment Cooperation Fund under a long-standing China-Indonesia bilateral cooperation framework (Joint Declaration of 25 April 2005 onwards). The Commission attributed that Chinese support to the Government of Indonesia and countervailed it — the same **cross-border subsidy attribution doctrine** first applied in the GFF case (Reg 2020/776, China + Egypt). The Regulation explicitly cites the GFF investigation (recitals 16 and 941).

**How does it interact with the steel safeguard?** SSCR is covered by the EU steel safeguard (Reg (EU) 2019/159, prolonged to 30 June 2024). Article 3 prevents over-stacking: once the above-quota safeguard tariff applies and exceeds the combined AD+CVD, only the safeguard duty is collected; collection of the rest is suspended.

**Is this still in force?** Yes, under the normal anti-subsidy framework. Definitive measures run five years (subject to expiry review). The clock runs from entry into force in March 2022.

## OVERVIEW

This is a definitive anti-subsidy (countervailing) measure on stainless steel cold-rolled flat products, run in parallel with a separate anti-dumping investigation on the same product. Both investigations share the same Union industry definition, sampled producers, period considered and investigation period, so their injury, causation and Union-interest analyses are "mutatis mutandis identical" (recital 5).

The case followed a complaint lodged by EUROFER on 4 January 2021; the Commission initiated on 17 February 2021 (notice OJ C 57). The investigation period ran 1 July 2019 to 30 June 2020, with injury trends examined from 1 January 2017.

**India.** Two producer groups (Chromeni Steels and the vertically integrated Jindal Group) accounted for 100 % of Indian SSCR exports to the EU. Countervailable schemes were the Advanced Authorisation Scheme (AAS), Duty Drawback Scheme (DDS), Export Promotion of Capital Goods Scheme (EPCGS), Merchandise Export from India Scheme (MEIS), and the provision of chromium ore for LTAR (Jindal only). Subsidy margins: Chromeni 7.56 %, Jindal Group 4.37 %, residual 7.56 % (recitals 302-303).

**Indonesia.** The sampled groups were the IRNC Group (Tsingshan-linked, in the Morowali Industrial Park, vertically integrated from nickel ore) and the non-integrated PT. Jindal Stainless Indonesia. The dominant subsidy was nickel ore for LTAR, supported by preferential financing flowing from China under the bilateral cooperation framework, plus coal LTAR, land LTAR, income-tax holidays and import-duty exemptions. Subsidy margins: IRNC Group 21.4 %, Jindal Indonesia 0.02 % (de minimis), other cooperating 13.5 %, residual 21.4 % (recital 1332).

**Injury.** The EU had 13 known producers and about 3.1 million tonnes of production. Over 2017 to the IP: Union consumption fell 17 %, production fell 16 %, capacity utilisation fell from 84 % to 68 %, profitability collapsed from 7.6 % to 0.4 %, cash flow fell 52 % and return on investments fell 80 %. Subsidised imports rose around 55 % and nearly doubled their joint market share (from ~3.0 % to ~5.8 %). Price undercutting was 5.8 % and 13.4 % (India) and 12.4 % (Indonesia). The Commission found material injury under Article 8(5) (recital 1009) and a strong causal link (recital 1015); COVID-19 and the consumption decline did not break the link.

The duties were set at the full subsidy amount under Article 15(1), third subparagraph — the lesser-duty (injury-margin) cap was not applied because no information showed it was in the Union interest to do so (recitals 1057-1059).

## STRUCTURE

- **Preamble** (1,081 recitals): initiation, sampling, product scope, India subsidy findings (Section 3), Indonesia subsidy findings including nickel-ore LTAR and the China-Indonesia cross-border subsidy (Section 4), injury (Section 5), causation (Section 6), Union interest (Section 7), definitive measures (Section 8), final provisions (Section 9).
- **Article 1** — imposes the definitive countervailing duty; duty table by company and TARIC code; commercial-invoice condition for individual rates.
- **Article 2** — amends the parallel anti-dumping Regulation (EU) 2021/2012 (revised AD duty table; automatic increase if the CVD is later modified or removed; refund mechanism).
- **Article 3** — interaction with the steel safeguard (Reg (EU) 2019/159): no concurrent application with the above-quota safeguard tariff.
- **Article 4** — entry into force (day after publication).
- **Annexes** — cooperating non-sampled Indonesian company list and the commercial-invoice declaration.
