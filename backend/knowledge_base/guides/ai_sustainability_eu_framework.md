# AI and Sustainability: the EU Regulatory Framework

## QUICK FACTS
- **Topic**: How EU law treats the environmental impact of artificial intelligence — energy use, water use, hardware footprint, data-centre obligations, GPAI sustainability disclosures, and the upcoming Cloud and AI Development Act
- **The compatibility question is settled in policy.** The EU has moved past asking whether AI and sustainability are compatible. The legislative architecture below operationalises the compatibility — what remains for industry is compliance, not philosophical debate.
- **Anchor instruments (already in force or adopted)**:
  - **AI Act**: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, CELEX 32024R1689. Article 95 + Recital 142 explicitly invite Codes of Conduct on environmental sustainability of AI systems. Article 53(1)(d) requires GPAI providers to disclose information used for training (including energy and resource consumption where measurable). Article 56 covers Codes of Practice.
  - **GPAI Code of Practice** (signatories taskforce active 2026): three workstreams cover (i) Transparency, (ii) Copyright, (iii) Safety and Security — energy and resource consumption disclosure sit inside the Transparency workstream as model-card baseline obligations. Brubru tracks signatory updates in `apply_ai_strategy_public_sector` and `ai_act_regulation`.
  - **Energy Efficiency Directive**: Directive (EU) 2023/1791 (CELEX 32023L1791), Article 12. Mandates Member States to ensure data centres ≥1 MW total installed power report energy consumption, water use, renewable energy, and waste-heat reuse to a Commission-managed European database.
  - **Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364** on the data-centre rating scheme — sets the reporting fields, methodology and threshold. First reporting year ran 2024; consolidated public release expected H2 2026.
  - **EU Code of Conduct for Data Centre Energy Efficiency** (JRC-managed, voluntary). Sets PUE, WUE, ERE benchmarks; major hyperscalers + colocation operators are signatories; complements the EED mandatory regime.
  - **CSRD**: Directive (EU) 2022/2464 (CELEX 32022L2464). ESRS E1 (climate change) requires Scope 2 + Scope 3 GHG disclosure tied to digital infrastructure usage; large companies must reflect AI compute as part of operational + value-chain emissions.
  - **EU Climate Law**: Regulation (EU) 2021/1119 + 2026 amendment Regulation (EU) 2026/667 — 90% net GHG reduction by 2040; AI energy demand is captured indirectly via electricity-sector pathways.
  - **Critical Raw Materials Act**: Regulation (EU) 2024/1252 — gallium, germanium, rare earths drive AI hardware (GPUs, HBM stacks, data-centre cooling). Strategic-projects pipeline reduces single-supplier exposure (China dominance).
  - **Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR)**: Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 — empowers product-specific delegated acts for AI hardware (servers, GPUs, networking) including future Digital Product Passport (DPP) requirements.
  - **AI Continent Action Plan** (Commission Communication 9 April 2025): EUR 200 billion InvestAI, including the GigaFactories network (5 gigafactories: 4 confirmed + 1 in pipeline) — sustainability dimension is built into the GigaFactories siting (renewable-rich regions) and energy-efficiency criteria.
  - **Apply AI Strategy** (COM(2025) 723, 8 October 2025): explicitly cross-cuts sustainability through the sectoral deep-dives (energy, environment, health, public sector).
- **Anchor instruments (forthcoming, on the College tentative agenda)**:
  - **Cloud and AI Development Act** — College adoption scheduled **Wednesday 27 May 2026** (per SEC(2026) 2564 tentative agenda). Lead: EVP Henna Virkkunen. Part of the Tech Sovereignty Package. Will set the EU framework for sovereign cloud capacity, AI compute access, and operational obligations on data-centre providers.
  - **Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy** — same College meeting 27 May 2026, lead EVP Teresa Ribera. The energy-policy counterpart of the Cloud and AI Development Act.
  - **Chips Act 2** — same College meeting 27 May 2026, lead EVP Henna Virkkunen. Includes hardware energy-efficiency criteria for AI accelerators.
  - **Open Source Strategy** — same College meeting 27 May 2026, lead EVP Henna Virkkunen.
  - **Strengthening energy security + Electrification action plan** — Energy Package, College adoption rescheduled to **10 June 2026** (was 19 May, moved per SEC(2026) 2564). Frames the grid + electrification capacity that AI compute will rely on.
- **Why this question is settled in policy, not in opinion**: between the AI Act sustainability hooks (Article 95 + Recital 142 + GPAI disclosures), the EED mandatory data-centre reporting (≥1 MW threshold), the Critical Raw Materials Act on AI hardware feedstocks, the Climate Law 2040 binding target, the CSRD Scope 2+3 disclosures, and the upcoming Cloud and AI Development Act on 27 May, the EU has built a multi-instrument compliance stack on the environmental footprint of AI compute. The remaining work for industry is execution and reporting, not debate about whether the two are compatible.

## The 9 instruments map
The compliance stack splits cleanly into three layers:

### Layer 1 — AI as a regulated technology (the substance)
- **AI Act Articles 53, 56, 95** + **Recital 142** + **GPAI Code of Practice (Transparency workstream)**. Disclosure of training-energy + resource consumption is required for GPAI providers placing systems on the EU market.

### Layer 2 — AI compute as data-centre operations (the infrastructure)
- **EED Article 12** + **Reg (EU) 2024/1364**: mandatory annual reporting for any data centre ≥1 MW (PUE, water use, renewable share, waste-heat reuse).
- **EU Code of Conduct for Data Centre Energy Efficiency** (JRC voluntary): the operational baseline most signatories follow.
- **Forthcoming: Cloud and AI Development Act (27 May 2026)**: will tighten the operational layer for cloud providers and AI compute access.

### Layer 3 — AI as part of corporate sustainability disclosure (the reporting)
- **CSRD ESRS E1, E5**: Scope 2 + Scope 3 emissions tied to digital infrastructure, including AI training and inference.
- **Climate Law (2026/667 amendment)**: 90% net GHG reduction by 2040, the binding ceiling for the entire compute footprint.

### Hardware feedstock (cross-cutting)
- **Critical Raw Materials Act (Reg 2024/1252)** — strategic projects + monitoring for AI-relevant raw materials (gallium, germanium, rare earths used in GPUs and HBM stacks). Single-supplier exposure to China is the headline policy concern.
- **ESPR + future Digital Product Passport** — product-specific energy efficiency for servers, networking, GPUs.
