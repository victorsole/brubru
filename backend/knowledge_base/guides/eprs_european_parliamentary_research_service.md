# EPRS -- the EP's in-house research engine

## QUICK FACTS

- **Full name:** European Parliamentary Research Service (EPRS)
- **Status:** Directorate-General of the European Parliament Secretariat; reports to the Secretary-General of the EP
- **Primary mission:** Provide MEPs, EP committees, and EP bodies with independent, authoritative research and analysis on any policy question; it does NOT draft legislation or issue political positions
- **Size:** approximately 250 staff (researchers, librarians, lawyers, information specialists); the largest in-house parliamentary research service of any parliament in the world
- **Headquarters:** Brussels (Altiero Spinelli building) and Strasbourg; Library reading rooms in both seats
- **EPRS page:** `https://the-secretary-general.europarl.europa.eu/en/directorates-general/eprs`
- **Think Tank portal (full catalogue):** `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/home`
- **Think Tank advanced search:** `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/research/advanced-search`
- **EPRS blog:** `https://epthinktank.eu/`
- **Eight contributing units:** Members' Research Service (MERS), Library (LIBS), Comparative Law unit, Prospective Studies (PES), Impact Assessment (IMPA), European Added Value (EAVA), Scientific Foresight (SOP), Economic Governance Support (EGOV), Policy Departments (POLDEP)
- **Product taxonomy (six types):** At a Glance, Briefing, In-Depth Analysis, Study, EU Fact Sheets on the EU, Documentation
- **Think Tank filter by contributor:** use the "Source" dropdown on the advanced-search page to limit results to any single unit (e.g. "EGOV", "MERS", "EAVA", "IMPA")
- **Citation format:** Author(s), *Title*, Publication type, PE number, European Parliamentary Research Service, European Parliament, Month Year. URL: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/PE[number]`
- **Related guides:** `legislative_train_observatory`, `european_parliament_structure`, `european_parliament_powers`, `european_parliament_personnel`, `ep_documents_and_open_data`, `eu_law_application_monitoring`, `jrc_capitalism_sustainability_democracy`
- **Planned cross-links (not yet live):** `stoa_science_technology_panel`, `ep_factsheets_database`


## What EPRS is

EPRS was created in 2013 by merging the former EP Library with three separate research and analysis bodies that had operated separately within the EP Secretariat. The consolidation created a single DG whose remit covers every stage of the policy cycle: background reading, impact appraisal, legal comparative analysis, forward-looking foresight, and post-adoption evaluation.

EPRS produces output for three audiences. The first is individual MEPs, who can request personalised briefings and country-specific analyses through the Members' Research Service. The second is EP committees and interparliamentary delegations, which commission studies and analyses for their specific legislative or oversight work. The third is the wider public: almost every EPRS publication is freely downloadable from the Think Tank portal, and most are translated into multiple EU languages.

EPRS is strictly non-partisan. It does not advise MEPs on how to vote. Its impact assessment unit produces appraisals of Commission proposals, not recommendations. This independence is the feature that distinguishes EPRS output from committee secretariat notes, which are drafted to support a specific rapporteur's position.

EPRS works in parallel with -- and is distinct from -- the EP's five Policy Departments (POLDEP), which are organised thematically and serve committee secretariats directly. The Policy Departments were formally integrated into EPRS in 2014, though they retain their own identity and publication series.


## The eight units and what each produces

### Members' Research Service (MERS)

MERS is the frontline unit for individual MEPs. It produces:

- **At a Glance** notes (typically 2 pages): rapid summaries of a Commission proposal, a plenary agenda item, or a policy development, produced within 48 hours of a major event
- **Briefings** (4-10 pages): structured analysis of a single file or policy question, with background, state of play, and key issues for MEPs
- **Personalised requests**: an MEP's office can submit a confidential research request directly to MERS; the response is provided to that MEP first, then published if not sensitive
- **At a Glance: Plenary** series: produced for every EP plenary session, one note per item on the agenda

MERS also publishes the "EU Legislation in Progress" series: a rolling set of Briefings that track each Commission proposal from tabling through adoption, updated at every major procedural step. This series is one of the most cited EPRS outputs because it is the only tool that keeps a single narrative document up to date across the whole lifecycle.

### Library (LIBS)

The EP Library provides bibliographic services, curated reading lists, and documentation packages. Its published output includes:

- **Reading lists** on policy topics: curated bibliographies drawing on academic, think-tank, and official sources
- **Library Briefings**: concise overviews (usually 4-6 pages) of a policy topic, often produced as background for a committee hearing
- **Key Players** series: profiles of institutional actors in a specific policy area, useful for mapping the landscape before a stakeholder engagement

The Library does not produce legal analysis or impact appraisals: it is a research-facilitation unit, not a policy analysis unit.

### Comparative Law unit

The Comparative Law unit produces analyses comparing how different EU Member States have transposed or applied EU law, how national parliaments have reacted to Commission proposals, and how EU rules compare with equivalent frameworks in third countries. Its output is especially valued by MEPs and legal staff working on directives where transposition divergence is a political issue.

### Prospective Studies (PES)

The Prospective Studies unit conducts forward-looking research on medium- and long-term policy challenges. It produces:

- **Mapping the cost of non-Europe** studies: analyses of the economic cost of the absence of EU-level action in a particular field
- **Future of Europe** reports: scenario-based analyses of how specific policy areas (e.g. the energy system, the labour market, digital infrastructure) might evolve over a 10-20 year horizon
- Cross-cutting research on megatrends (climate transition, demographic change, technological disruption) as they affect EU policy

### Impact Assessment (IMPA)

The Impact Assessment unit performs three categories of work:

1. **Initial appraisals of Commission Impact Assessments (IAs)**: For every Commission proposal accompanied by an IA, IMPA produces a 2-6 page appraisal within weeks of the proposal's tabling, assessing the quality of the Commission's own analysis (completeness, evidence base, proportionality testing). These are published as "Initial Appraisals" and are standard reading for committee legal advisors.
2. **Substitute impact assessments**: Where the Commission did not produce an IA for a proposal (which is unusual but occurs for urgent measures), IMPA can produce its own assessment.
3. **Ex-post impact assessments**: Evaluations of legislation adopted 5 or more years ago, examining whether it achieved its stated objectives and what its actual costs and benefits have been.

IMPA's initial appraisals are the fastest EPRS products to appear after a proposal: they are typically published within 4-8 weeks of a Commission adoption.

### European Added Value (EAVA)

The European Added Value unit produces studies examining whether EU-level action generates value that Member States acting alone could not achieve. EAVA output feeds directly into the European Parliament's own legislative initiative (INI) procedure: before the EP asks the Commission to produce a proposal, it frequently commissions an EAVA study to demonstrate that there is a legal basis and that EU action would add value.

EAVA studies are often the starting point for a Citizens' Legislative Initiative response or an EP-initiated report. They are longer than standard briefings (50-150 pages) and combine legal, economic, and comparative analysis.

### Scientific Foresight (SOP)

The Scientific Foresight unit (sometimes labelled STOA in older documents, as it supports the Science and Technology Options Assessment Panel of the EP) produces horizon-scanning and technology-assessment research. Its output covers:

- **Technology assessment reports** on emerging technologies (AI, synthetic biology, quantum computing, space) and their regulatory implications
- **Science and Technology Options Assessment (STOA) panel publications**: longer studies commissioned by the STOA Panel and carried out by EPRS researchers or external experts
- **In-Focus notes** on specific scientific controversies or evidence gaps relevant to pending legislation

Scientific Foresight output is particularly relevant to LIBE (digital rights, AI), ITRE (industrial research), ENVI (chemicals, food science), and AGRI (GMO, pesticides) committees.

### Economic Governance Support (EGOV)

The Economic Governance Support unit is focused exclusively on euro-area economic governance: the Stability and Growth Pact, the European Semester, the Banking Union, the Capital Markets Union, the ESM, and macroeconomic surveillance. Its output includes:

- **Deep-dive analyses** on the functioning of euro-area surveillance tools
- **Background notes** for ECON committee hearings and ECOFIN Council-related plenary debates
- **Comparative tables** on Member State fiscal positions and compliance with EU fiscal rules

EGOV is a specialist unit: its publications are technical and presuppose familiarity with macroeconomic frameworks. It does not produce general economic policy briefings.

### Policy Departments (POLDEP)

The five Policy Departments are thematically organised research units that serve EP committee secretariats directly. Unlike MERS (which serves individual MEPs) or EAVA (which serves the EP as an institution), the Policy Departments are assigned to clusters of committees:

| Policy Department | Committees served | Main policy areas |
|---|---|---|
| POLDEP A -- Economic and Scientific Policy | ECON, EMPL, ITRE, IMCO, TRAN, REGI, AGRI, PECH, ENVI, DEVE | Economy, industry, environment, agriculture |
| POLDEP B -- Structural and Cohesion Policies | REGI, AGRI, PECH, ENVI, CULT, EMPL | Cohesion, fisheries, culture, social affairs |
| POLDEP C -- Citizens' Rights and Constitutional Affairs | LIBE, AFCO, PETI, AFET, JURI | Justice, rights, constitutional affairs, foreign affairs |
| POLDEP D -- Budgetary Affairs | BUDG, CONT | Budget, discharge |
| POLDEP EXPO -- Foreign Affairs | AFET, DROI, SEDE, INTA, DEVE | External relations, trade, defence |

Policy Department studies are typically larger and more detailed than MERS briefings (30-200 pages). They are commissioned by the relevant committee, not by individual MEPs, and the committee secretariat is the primary recipient. All are published on the Think Tank portal.


## Product types and audience

| Product type | Typical length | Primary audience | Turnaround | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| At a Glance | 2 pages | MEPs, political advisors | 24-72 hours | Produced for every plenary item; no footnotes |
| Briefing | 4-12 pages | MEPs, policy officers, committee staff | 2-6 weeks | Most common EPRS product; standard citation unit |
| In-Depth Analysis | 20-60 pages | Committee staff, legal advisors, researchers | 6-16 weeks | Usually externally peer-reviewed |
| Study | 50-300 pages | Committee secretariats, academic citation | 3-12 months | Often co-authored with external experts |
| EU Fact Sheet | 2-4 pages per sheet | General public, journalists, students | Updated each mandate | The "Fact Sheets on the EU" series covers 70+ policy areas |
| Documentation / Reading List | 2-8 pages | MEPs, policy researchers | On demand or planned | Bibliographic, not analytical |

The **EU Fact Sheets on the EU** series deserves separate attention. It is the authoritative plain-language reference for what the EU does in each policy area. Each fact sheet covers legal basis, current state of play, next steps, and key EP positions. The series is updated at the start of each parliamentary mandate and periodically during the mandate when a major new act is adopted. Sheets are available in all 24 EU official languages.

The **"EU Legislation in Progress" Briefings** (a MERS product) are the most practically useful output for Brubru users tracking active files: one Briefing per proposal, updated at each major procedural stage, providing a stable single-document history of a dossier.


## The Think Tank database

The EP Think Tank (`https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/home`) is the public catalogue of all EPRS publications. It holds tens of thousands of documents dating back to the early 2000s, with the full catalogue from 2013 (post-merger) comprehensively indexed.

### Basic search

The homepage search bar accepts full-text queries. Results are ranked by relevance. Each result shows: title, publication type (At a Glance, Briefing, etc.), PE number, contributing unit (source), date, and a direct PDF download link.

### Advanced search

The advanced search (`https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/research/advanced-search`) allows filtering by:

- **Source (contributing unit):** Select "MERS", "POLDEP A", "EAVA", "IMPA", "EGOV", "LIBS", "PES", or "SOP" to restrict results to a single unit
- **Document type:** At a Glance, Briefing, In-Depth Analysis, Study, Fact Sheet, or Documentation
- **Date range:** From and to date pickers (publication date)
- **Language:** Filter by the language version (EN, FR, DE, IT, ES, NL, and others)
- **Topic:** Subject-matter tags organised by policy area

For Brubru users tracking a specific file, the most efficient approach is to enter the proposal's short title or Commission reference (e.g. "AI Act", "CBAM", "NIS2") in the search bar and then apply the "EU Legislation in Progress" filter or "Briefing" type filter. This returns the MERS tracking briefing series for that dossier.

### PE numbers as stable identifiers

Every EPRS publication carries a PE number (e.g. PE 738.420). The PE number is the stable identifier for the document and should be used in citations, database records, and tracking systems. The direct URL format is:

`https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/PE[number-without-dot]`

For example, PE 738.420 is accessible at `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/PE738420`.

Documents in the Think Tank are also indexed by the EP Open Data Portal. See `ep_documents_and_open_data` for machine-readable access.


## The EPRS blog (epthinktank.eu)

The EPRS blog (`https://epthinktank.eu/`) publishes shorter commentary pieces, infographics, data visualisations, and video explainers that complement the formal publication catalogue. Blog posts are not formal EPRS publications in the citation sense but are authoritative: they are written by EPRS researchers and reviewed internally.

The blog is useful for:

- **Topical commentary** on fast-moving policy developments, published within 24-48 hours of a vote, Commission adoption, or Council conclusion
- **Data stories** that visualise EPRS research findings for a broader audience
- **Event summaries** after committee hearings or STOA panel events
- **Infographics** on legislative procedures, institutional structures, and policy comparisons (these are frequently shared by MEPs' communications teams)

The blog does not carry PE numbers and should not be treated as equivalent to formal EPRS publications in official citations. In practice, blog posts often appear before the formal Briefing on the same topic and serve as a first-read alert.

Blog categories are aligned with EP committee areas and can be browsed by topic. The blog also carries the EP "EU Fact of the Week" series and short explainers for the general public.


## EPRS vs Commission JRC vs Council research

Users regularly ask how EPRS output differs from that of other major EU institutional research bodies. The key distinctions are:

| Feature | EPRS | JRC (Joint Research Centre) | Council Secretariat research |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent institution | European Parliament | European Commission | Council of the EU |
| Independence | Independent of political groups | Arm of the Commission | Serves the rotating Presidency |
| Publication policy | Fully public; all documents on Think Tank | Mostly public (JRC Publications Repository) | Often restricted; not systematically published |
| Audience | MEPs, committees, public | Commission DGs, Member States, public | Presidency, Member State delegations |
| Methodological focus | Policy analysis, legal comparison, impact appraisal, foresight | Scientific and technical analysis; economic modelling | Legal and procedural briefings; political notes |
| Citation in legislation | EPRS studies cited in EP resolutions and reports | JRC studies cited in Commission proposals and IAs | Not typically cited in public texts |
| Type of evidence | Secondary research, legal analysis, expert review | Primary scientific research, modelling, datasets | Analysis of draft texts and positions |

For technical scientific questions (chemical safety, agricultural emissions modelling, AI risk classification thresholds) JRC is the primary source. For legal and policy questions about what the EU is doing or should do, EPRS is the primary source. See `jrc_capitalism_sustainability_democracy` for JRC output on economic governance.

Council Secretariat research is largely not public: it circulates as restricted "I" notes, working-party backgrounds, and Presidency compromise texts within the Council infrastructure. Where Council outputs are public, they appear via `data.consilium.europa.eu` and the Council's open-data portal.


## How Brubru users should cite EPRS

The standard citation format for EPRS publications is:

> [Author surname, initial.] (or [EPRS] where no named author), *[Full title of publication]*, [Publication type (Briefing / In-Depth Analysis / Study etc.)], PE [number], European Parliamentary Research Service, European Parliament, [Month Year]. Available at: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/PE[number-without-dot]

Example:

> Zoltan Balogh, *Artificial Intelligence Act: European Added Value Assessment*, European Added Value Assessment, PE 740.248, European Parliamentary Research Service, European Parliament, March 2023. Available at: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/PE740248

Key rules:

1. The PE number is mandatory: it is the stable identifier used by the EP's own reference systems and by academic citation managers.
2. The contributing unit (EAVA, MERS, IMPA, etc.) is a useful addition but not formally part of the standard citation.
3. Blog posts at epthinktank.eu are cited as: *[Title]*, EPRS Blog, European Parliamentary Research Service, [Date]. URL: [post URL]. Do not cite them with a PE number (they do not have one).
4. EU Fact Sheets on the EU are cited by sheet title + edition year: *[Sheet title]*, Fact Sheets on the EU, European Parliamentary Research Service, [Year]. URL: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home`


## Practical search tips by contributing unit

**Finding MERS "EU Legislation in Progress" for a specific file**
Search the Think Tank for the proposal's short title plus "legislation in progress" or "EU legislation". Filter by Source = MERS and type = Briefing. There will typically be one Briefing per dossier, updated multiple times; the most recent update is the live version.

**Finding IMPA initial appraisals for a Commission proposal**
Search for the proposal title plus "initial appraisal". Filter by Source = IMPA. These appear 4-8 weeks after the Commission's formal adoption. If you know the COM reference, include it (e.g. "COM(2021) 206").

**Finding EAVA studies behind an EP own-initiative**
Go to the Think Tank advanced search, select Source = EAVA. EAVA studies are linked from the OEIL procedure file for the corresponding INI or REGI report; check the "Documents" tab in OEIL for a link to the EP Think Tank.

**Finding EGOV deep dives for euro-area governance**
Filter Source = EGOV. EGOV publishes ahead of ECOFIN/Eurogroup meetings and ahead of key Semester milestones (Country Reports in February, CSRs in May). Search by country name or by instrument (SGP, MIP, ESM, Banking Union) for targeted results.

**Finding STOA / Scientific Foresight reports on emerging technology**
Filter Source = SOP (or search "STOA"). The STOA Panel publishes a work programme at the start of each mandate; major studies take 12-18 months and are indexed under large PE numbers.

**Finding Policy Department studies for a specific committee**
In the advanced search, filter Source by the relevant POLDEP (e.g. POLDEP C for LIBE/AFCO matters). The study title always names the commissioning committee. Policy Department studies for AFET/DROI/INTA are filed under POLDEP EXPO.

**Finding EU Fact Sheets by policy area**
The Fact Sheets on the EU are not in the main Think Tank catalogue in the same way as other publications. The canonical entry point is `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/factsheets/en/home`. Sheets are organised in chapters (e.g. Chapter 4 on Internal Market, Chapter 6 on Agriculture). See also the planned guide `ep_factsheets_database`.

For the broader EP document infrastructure, including the Open Data Portal and the EP archives, see `ep_documents_and_open_data`. For the Legislative Train tracker maintained by EPRS, see `legislative_train_observatory`. For the EP's committee and plenary output more broadly, see `european_parliament_structure` and `european_parliament_powers`.
