## Why this matters

The ICCU is the **adjudication pillar** of the Ukraine-Russia compensation architecture endorsed by 46 CoE Member States at Reykjavik in May 2023. Without a claims commission:

- The Register of Damage (RD4U, operational since 2 April 2024) can collect evidence but cannot deliver binding decisions
- Frozen Russian assets (approximately EUR 260 billion EU-side, majority at Euroclear Brussels) cannot be legally channelled to victims
- The EU's **ERA loans** (EUR 18.1 billion to Ukraine in 2024, backed by Russia's **Extraordinary Revenues** -- see `iran_strait_hormuz_eu_response` for 23 April 2026 EUCO Cyprus discussion) need a credible end-state compensation mechanism

EP consent by simple majority (Rule 114 RoP) is the final EU step. Once consent is given:

- The Council adopts the Decision and the EU deposits its instrument of accession at The Hague
- The Convention enters into force when 12 parties have ratified (including 3 EU Member States)
- Member States that want individual participation must ratify under national procedures in parallel (mixed agreement)

## Scope of claims

The Convention covers claims for:

1. **Death, injury, physical or mental harm** to natural persons (including forcibly transferred children -- see `ukraine_children_deportation`)
2. **Damage to or loss of property** (real estate, movable, intellectual property)
3. **Environmental damage** and damage to cultural heritage
4. **State-to-state claims** by Ukraine (infrastructure destruction, occupation costs)
5. **Forcibly displaced persons and refugees** (costs to host states to be settled between states and the Compensation Fund)

The Commission will apply **international humanitarian law and international human rights law** as applicable standards, and will interpret damage broadly, consistent with ICJ jurisprudence.

## Political sensitivities

- **Russia is not party** and will not participate -- the Commission will proceed under default rules of international arbitration, with Russia's liability presumed from UN General Assembly Resolution ES-11/5 (14 November 2022, establishing RD4U) and the ICJ provisional measures orders
- Some EU Member States (notably Hungary until April 2026 elections, Slovakia under Fico) historically dragged feet on the compensation architecture -- with Magyar's accession to power in Budapest (April 2026, see `hungary_election_2026_magyar`), Hungarian obstruction lifts, simplifying the EP consent + Council conclusion
- The US position: the Biden administration signed the Reykjavik declaration but the Trump administration's stance is uncertain; US non-participation does not block EU accession
- **Russian frozen sovereign assets** (G7/EU position): the ICCU does not itself determine asset-sourcing for the Compensation Fund -- that decision sits with the G7 + EU and is politically locked in via the ERA (Extraordinary Revenue Acceleration) loans mechanism
