# How the EU Sets Fishing Opportunities — TACs, Quotas and the Annual Council Cycle

## QUICK FACTS

- **PUBLISHED (OJ C-series, week to 27 August 2026)**: the plenary resolution of **10 March 2026** on **fisheries management approaches for safeguarding sensitive species, tackling invasive species and benefiting local economies** was published as **P10_TA(2026)0067** - CELEX `52026IP0067`. An own-initiative resolution: it does not bind the Council's Article 43(3) TAC decision, and is the kind of text the Parliament uses to make its views heard on a file it cannot vote on.
- **LATEST (Monday 24 August 2026)**: the Commission tabled **COM 52026PC0437**, a Proposal for a Council Regulation **fixing the fishing opportunities for certain fish stocks in the Baltic Sea for 2027**, and amending Regulation (EU) 2026/249 as regards certain fishing opportunities in other waters. This is the opening move of the 2027 fishing-opportunities round. Announced the same day by the Commission newsroom ("Commission proposes fishing opportunities for 2027 in the Baltic Sea").
- **The single most misunderstood fact**: fishing opportunities are fixed by the **Council alone**, under **Article 43(3) TFEU**, on a Commission proposal. **The European Parliament has no vote.** It is not the ordinary legislative procedure. PECH may hold exchanges of views and the EP adopts own-initiative resolutions on the policy, but the TAC regulation itself is a Council act.

- **Current multiannual TAC regulation**: **Council Regulation (EU) 2026/249** of 26 January 2026, fixing fishing opportunities **for 2026, 2027 and 2028** for certain stocks in Union waters and, for Union vessels, in certain non-Union waters — CELEX `32026R0249`. ELI: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/eli/reg/2026/249/oj
- **Baltic multiannual plan**: **Regulation (EU) 2016/1139** — cod, herring and sprat in the Baltic Sea — CELEX `32016R1139`. ELI: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/eli/reg/2016/1139/oj
- **Annual policy framing**: Commission Communication **52026DC0271**, "Sustainable fishing in the EU — state of play and orientations for 2027" (5 June 2026), with Staff Working Document 52026SC0145. The orientations Communication always precedes and frames the autumn proposals.

- **Scientific basis**: ICES (International Council for the Exploration of the Sea) advice, plus STECF (Scientific, Technical and Economic Committee for Fisheries) for EU-specific evaluation.

## What a TAC actually is

A **total allowable catch (TAC)** is the maximum quantity of a given stock that may be caught in a
given area in a given period, expressed in tonnes. It is set per stock and per sea area, not per
species globally: the same species in two sea basins is two different management units with two
different TACs.

Each TAC is then divided among Member States as national **quotas**. The division is not
renegotiated each year. It follows **relative stability**, a fixed allocation key per stock derived
from historical catch patterns and settled in 1983. This is why the annual negotiation is about the
size of the cake and almost never about the slices: changing a relative-stability key requires
unanimity in practice and is politically radioactive.

Member States may then swap quota with one another during the year, and may allocate their national
quota to their fleets by whatever national method they choose (Article 16(6) CFP), which is why two
Member States with the same quota can run entirely different fisheries.

## Who decides, and how

| Step | Actor | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scientific advice | ICES, then STECF | Stock-by-stock advice, usually published from late May onwards |
| 2. Policy framing | Commission (DG MARE) | Annual "state of play and orientations" Communication |
| 3. Proposal | Commission | Proposal for a **Council** Regulation (a `PC` document, e.g. 52026PC0437) |
| 4. Preparation | Council working party on Internal and External Fisheries Policy, then Coreper / SCA | — |
| 5. Adoption | **Council alone**, qualified majority, **Article 43(3) TFEU** | Council Regulation, directly applicable |

The European Parliament is consulted on the multiannual plans (which ARE ordinary legislative
procedure, Article 43(2) TFEU) but **not** on the annual fixing of opportunities. Getting this
distinction right matters: a multiannual plan such as Regulation (EU) 2016/1139 is co-decided; the
TAC regulation that operates inside it is not.

## The annual calendar

The cycle is deliberately staggered by sea basin, because the scientific advice and the external
negotiations arrive at different times.

| Basin | Proposal tabled | Agreed at |
|---|---|---|
| **Baltic Sea** | late August / early September | **October AGRIFISH** |
| **Atlantic and North Sea**, deep-sea stocks | autumn | **December AGRIFISH** |
| **Mediterranean and Black Sea** | autumn | **December AGRIFISH** |

The December Council is the long one: it is routinely an overnight negotiation, because it settles
the great majority of EU TACs at once and lands after the EU-UK, EU-Norway and coastal-state
consultations have concluded.

**Shift worth noting**: Regulation (EU) 2026/249 fixes opportunities for **2026, 2027 and 2028** for
certain stocks. Multiannual fixing for stable stocks reduces the annual bargaining load, and it is
why the 2027 Baltic proposal *amends* 2026/249 for certain other waters rather than replacing it.

## The two constraints every TAC must respect

**Maximum sustainable yield (MSY).** Article 2(2) CFP requires exploitation rates that restore and
maintain stocks above biomass levels capable of producing MSY, and required this to be achieved by
2020 for all stocks. Where the scientific advice says a stock is below safe biological limits, the
Commission proposal will normally track the ICES advice down, and the political argument is about
how fast.

**The landing obligation.** Article 15 CFP requires that catches of regulated species be landed and
counted against quota rather than discarded at sea. TACs were uplifted when it was phased in to
account for the fish that previously went over the side. Enforcement of it remains the weakest part
of the system, and successive DG MARE evaluations have said so.

## What tends to be contested

- **Baltic cod and herring**: several stocks have been under directed-fishery closures or
  near-zero TACs for years; the argument is over by-catch allowances and the socio-economic
  support that accompanies them.
- **Mediterranean effort regime**: the Mediterranean is managed primarily through **fishing-effort**
  limits (days at sea) rather than TACs, which makes it structurally different from Atlantic
  management.
- **Shared and straddling stocks**: TACs for stocks shared with the UK, Norway, the Faroes and
  coastal states cannot be fixed unilaterally; the EU fixes provisional TACs in January where
  consultations have not concluded.
- **Deep-sea stocks**: fixed for two years at a time under a separate regulation.
