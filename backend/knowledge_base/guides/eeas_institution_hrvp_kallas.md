## Legal basis and creation

The EEAS rests on **Article 27(3) TEU**, introduced by the Lisbon Treaty. That paragraph establishes the service as an institution that assists the HR/VP and that works in cooperation with Member State diplomatic services. It explicitly states that the EEAS shall comprise officials from relevant departments of the General Secretariat of the Council and of the Commission, as well as staff seconded from national diplomatic services.

The founding instrument, **Council Decision 2010/427/EU** of 26 July 2010 (amended by Decision (EU, Euratom) 2015/444), fleshes out the organisation, staffing rules, budget status, and legal personality. The EEAS is legally distinct from both the Council and the Commission: it has its own section of the EU budget, its own establishment plan, and its own legal personality for operational contracts. Member States contribute approximately one-third of EEAS posts at the level of AD5-AD12 (seconded national diplomats); EU officials fill the remainder.

## The HR/VP double-hat

The HR/VP is the single most important innovation of the Lisbon Treaty in external affairs. Under **Article 18 TEU**, the HR/VP is appointed by the European Council acting by qualified majority with the agreement of the Commission President, and takes part in the work of the European Council. The double-hat means the HR/VP simultaneously:

- **Chairs the Foreign Affairs Council (FAC)** in all its configurations (General, Trade, Defence), which no other Council is chaired by a non-presidency official;
- **Sits as a Vice-President of the Commission**, ensuring coherence between CFSP and other EU external instruments (trade, development, neighbourhood, sanctions, enlargement).

HR/VP Kaja Kallas, in office since 1 December 2024, is the third HR/VP (after Baroness Ashton 2009-2014, Federica Mogherini 2014-2019, and Josep Borrell 2019-2024). As a former Estonian Prime Minister with direct experience of Russia's neighbourhood pressure, her appointment reflected von der Leyen II's focus on the security and defence dimension of EU external action.

The HR/VP submits proposals to the Council for CFSP Decisions (Article 28 TEU), for CSDP mission mandates (Article 43 TEU), for EUSR appointments (Article 33 TEU), and co-proposes sanctions with the Commission (Article 215 TFEU). All require Council unanimity in the Foreign Affairs Council.

## Organisational structure

### Geographic Managing Directorates

The five geographic Managing Directorates form the backbone of EEAS analytical and operational work on third countries. Each is headed by a Managing Director and covered by country desks that monitor political, security, human-rights, and economic developments:

- **MD-AFRICA**: Sub-Saharan Africa, African Union relations, Horn of Africa security, Sahel crisis management
- **MD-AMERICAS**: United States, Canada, Latin America, Caribbean; transatlantic dialogue including EU-US Trade and Technology Council (TTC) coordination
- **MD-ASIAPAC**: China, India, ASEAN, Pacific islands, Indo-Pacific strategy implementation; also covers Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Republic of Korea
- **MD-EURCA**: Eastern Partnership countries (Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus -- the last two under restricted engagement), Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan), Western Balkans in coordination with DG ENEST (see `dg_enest_overview`)
- **MD-MENA**: Middle East Peace Process, Gulf states, North Africa, Syria reconstruction diplomacy; coordinates closely with Commission DG MENA (see `dg_mena_overview`)

### Thematic and cross-cutting departments

**INTCEN** (EU Intelligence and Situation Centre): the EU's classified intelligence-analysis hub, located within EEAS. INTCEN produces strategic assessments from intelligence shared voluntarily by Member State services. It does not conduct its own collection. Outputs feed into hybrid-threat analysis, including work on Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference (FIMI).

**EUMS** (EU Military Staff): provides military expertise for CSDP missions, including the Military Planning and Conduct Capability (MPCC) for non-executive military missions. See `eu_csdp_missions_2026` for the full mission portfolio.

**CPCC** (Civilian Planning and Conduct Capability): plans and conducts EU civilian CSDP missions (EUAM, EUCAP, EUBAM, EUMM series). The Civilian Operations Commander sits within CPCC.

**MD-PSD / SECDEFPOL**: strategic and policy direction for the Common Security and Defence Policy, including the Strategic Compass implementation, PESCO, the European Defence Agency relationship, and disarmament/non-proliferation.

**MD-GLOBAL / Values and Multilateral Relations**: manages EU positions in multilateral fora (UN, G7/G20, OSCE, Council of Europe, WTO), human-rights policy, and democracy support including EU Election Observation Missions.

## EU Delegations and EU Ambassadors

EU Delegations function as the EU's embassies to third countries and international organisations. The Head of Delegation carries the title of EU Ambassador and holds the rank of the accrediting diplomatic mission's head for the purposes of host-state protocol. Diplomatic status derives from bilateral host-state agreements (typically a Headquarters Agreement or Exchange of Letters), not from the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations directly -- though many such agreements track Vienna Convention protections.

EU Delegations serve multiple masters: they implement EEAS political instructions from Brussels, manage Commission-funded development and neighbourhood programmes (in close coordination with DG INTPA, DG ENEST, or DG MENA), represent the EU in international organisations, and report political and economic intelligence back to HQ.

Delegation Heads are appointed by the HR/VP and their deployment is subject to agreement of the host country. In practice, a significant share of Heads of Delegation (approximately one-third) are seconded Member State diplomats rather than EEAS career officials, reflecting the Council Decision 2010/427/EU requirement on staffing balance.

## Diplomatic corps and rotation

EEAS staff rotate between Brussels HQ and Delegations posts, typically on three-to-four year assignment cycles. Seconded national diplomats return to their national foreign services after their EEAS assignment. The EEAS maintains dedicated training programmes under the **European Diplomatic Programme** (EDP) to build a common EU diplomatic culture, available to national officials as well as EU staff.

---

## EU Special Representatives

EUSRs are senior diplomats tasked by Council Decision under **Article 33 TEU** with a specific geographic or thematic mandate. They are distinct from Heads of Delegation (who are resident in-country), operating as mobile political envoys with direct access to heads of government and foreign ministers in their assigned regions. As of mid-2025, 11 EUSRs are active: regions include Bosnia and Herzegovina, Central Asia, Horn of Africa, Human Rights, Kosovo, Middle East Peace Process, Sahel, South Caucasus and Georgia, Belgrade-Pristina Dialogue / Western Balkans, Gulf Region, and Great Lakes. For full roster and mandates, see `eu_special_representatives`.

## Cooperation with Member State diplomatic services

Article 27(3) TEU and Article 35 TEU oblige the EEAS and Member State missions to cooperate and share information. In practice, coordination occurs through:

- **Political and Security Committee (PSC)**: Council preparatory body composed of ambassadors from all Member States, meeting two-to-three times per week in Brussels; chaired by an EEAS official (the PSC Chair is appointed by the HR/VP)
- **COREU/Cortesy network**: secure encrypted communications network linking all Member State foreign ministries and EEAS
- **Country coordination groups**: regular meeting of EU heads of mission in-country with the EU Delegation Head
- **Team Europe** approach: coordination between Commission, EEAS, Member States, and European Development Finance Institutions on Global Gateway project delivery (see `global_gateway_strategy`)
