# EU Cardiovascular Health: the Safe Hearts Plan and the proposed Council Recommendation on health checks (September 2026)

## QUICK FACTS

- **What happened**: on **28 September 2026** the Commission adopted a **proposal for a Council Recommendation on cardiovascular health checks** ("an EU approach to early detection and screening"), reference **COM(2026) 481**, and launched the **first annual EU Screening Week (28 September to 4 October 2026)**. Sources: Commission press release **IP/26/1859** and Q&A **QANDA/26/1860**, both 28 Sep 2026.
- **Status: PROPOSAL, NOT ADOPTED.** It is a Council Recommendation (non-binding once adopted, no Parliament co-decision). The Council has not adopted it yet. Do NOT say Member States are now obliged to screen.
- **Why**: cardiovascular diseases are the **EU's number one killer**: **1.7 million deaths a year**, around **62 million people** directly affected, and a cost to the EU economy of **more than EUR 282 billion a year**, of which **EUR 47 billion is lost productivity**. **80%** of cardiovascular diseases can be prevented by tackling risk factors (high blood pressure, cholesterol, high blood sugar, obesity). **Fewer than half** of Member States run cardiovascular health checks today, and their approaches differ.
- **The Safe Hearts Plan**: adopted by the Commission in **December 2025** as part of a broader health package; its target is to **reduce premature cardiovascular deaths by 25% by 2035**. The Recommendation is a step towards that target.
- **Who should be checked (the proposal's age bands)**:
  - **Under 35**: at least **one** health check (blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol); more individual screening with a family history of premature cardiovascular disease or sudden cardiac death.
  - **35 to 64**: systematic checks **at least every five years**, more often depending on risk, with a wider set of tests (lifestyle, respiratory function, reproductive health, mental health, kidney health, body mass index, waist measures, medical and family history).
  - **65 and over**: checks **every three to five years**, possibly with electrocardiogram and pulse checks for heart rate and rhythm.
  - Member States may adapt the bands to national or regional context, health inequalities and gender differences.
- **Delivery ideas in the proposal**: checks in primary and community settings such as pharmacies and community centres, and **mobile screening units** (marketplaces, shopping centres) to reach rural and underserved areas; data-driven and AI-based tools allowed **only with proper human oversight** and in line with EU law.

## EU money and projects around it (as stated by the Commission, 28 Sep 2026)

| Initiative | What it is | Funding |
|---|---|---|
| European Cardiovascular Health Data and AI Network (**CHAIN**) | Safe Hearts flagship on data and digitalisation, led by the European Society of Cardiology; builds on the European Health Data Space | about **EUR 20 million**, EU4Health |
| Joint Action on Lifelong Prevention | New joint action to guide and build capacity to implement the Safe Hearts Plan | EU4Health |
| OECD and WHO technical support | Two new EU-funded projects for Member States | **EUR 2 million each** |
| LENSES | Stakeholder-led health literacy and awareness project, due by end 2027 | **EUR 1.6 million**, EU4Health |
| Past support | EU4Health for cardiovascular disease and diabetes, incl. Joint Actions JA Prevent NCD and JACARDI | **more than EUR 160 million** |
| "Know Your Numbers" campaign | Awareness campaign launched on World Heart Day, 29 September 2026, running through 2027 | Commission |

National examples the Commission cites under the Recovery and Resilience Facility: Bulgaria (six diagnosis and treatment centres in Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna and Pleven), Croatia (TeleCordis telecardiology), Czechia (Brno Centre for Cardiovascular and Transplant Medicine), Greece (**Prolamvano**, checks offered to 5.5 million people aged 30 to 70).

## Evidence base

OECD's EU-funded report on the State of Cardiovascular Health in the EU, WHO reports, European Society of Cardiology recommendations, the JACARDI screening guide, and the Commission's spring 2026 call for evidence.
