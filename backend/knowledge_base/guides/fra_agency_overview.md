# FRA: EU Agency for Fundamental Rights

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name**: European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights (FRA). Seat: **Vienna, Austria** (Rennweg 12).
- **Founding regulation**: Council Regulation (EC) No 168/2007 of 15 February 2007, published OJ L 53/1 (22 February 2007), entered into force 23 February 2007. Repealed the 1997 European Monitoring Centre on Racism and Xenophobia (EUMC) with effect from 1 March 2007: FRA is EUMC's direct successor with a broadened mandate.
- **Mandate extension**: Council Regulation (EU) 2022/555 of 5 April 2022 (OJ L 108, 7 April 2022; entered into force 27 April 2022) amended the founding regulation to align FRA's mandate with the Lisbon Treaty: it added the ability for FRA to undertake **own-initiative work on police and judicial cooperation in criminal matters**, and replaced the old multiannual-framework model with **annual and multi-annual programming**.
- **Director**: Sirpa Rautio (Finland), in post since March 2024.
- **Governance**: **Management Board** (one independent expert per Member State + 2 European Commission representatives + 1 Council of Europe expert; sets priorities, approves budget, monitors work) → **Executive Board** (Board Chair + Vice-Chair + 2 further Board members + 1 Commission representative; prepares Board decisions, advises the Director) → **Scientific Committee** (11 independent experts safeguarding the scientific quality of FRA's output).
- **What FRA does NOT do**: it does **not** handle individual complaints and has **no binding legal powers**: it produces evidence, comparative data and expert opinions for EU institutions and Member States when they implement EU law. It is not a court and not an ombudsman.
- **Flagship survey programme**: EU-MIDIS (I and II: European Union Minorities and Discrimination Survey), the EU LGBTIQ Survey, the Roma Survey, the EU-wide survey on violence against women, the Fundamental Rights Survey, and surveys on Jewish people's experiences of antisemitism.
- **Reference tools**: **Charterpedia** (article-by-article guide to the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights: official explanations, case law, national constitutional provisions, international law, FRA analysis: originally compiled by the EP's LIBE Committee, maintained by FRA since 2009); the **Fundamental Rights Report** (FRA's own annual flagship publication, distinct from the European Parliament's annual Fundamental Rights Report resolution); the EU Fundamental Rights Information System (EFRIS); case-law and hate-crime databases.
- **Franet**: FRA's multidisciplinary research network, running since 2011, with one contracted National Focal Point per Member State (plus observer states) supplying comparable socio-legal data for FRA's comparative reports.
- **Fundamental Rights Platform (FRP)**: FRA's structured civil-society cooperation channel, mandated by the founding regulation itself. Free, open registration; over 1,000 participating civil-society organisations from EU Member States and FRA's observer states (North Macedonia, Serbia, Albania). Not a membership body.
- **Cooperation partners**: works closely with the Council of Europe (one of its Management Board seats is reserved for a Council of Europe expert), the European Ombudsman, EU equality bodies, and national human rights institutions.

FRA is the EU's dedicated **evidence and expertise agency** on fundamental rights. It does not adjudicate cases or issue binding rulings; its influence works through data, comparative reporting and advice that EU institutions and Member States draw on when they legislate, implement or evaluate EU law that touches fundamental rights.

## What FRA covers

FRA organises its substantive work under six broad themes:
1. Asylum, migration and borders
2. Data protection, privacy and new technologies
3. Equality, non-discrimination and racism
4. Justice, victims' rights and judicial cooperation (added scope under the 2022 mandate extension)
5. Support for human rights systems and defenders
6. Environmental and social sustainability

## The survey programme

FRA's large-scale EU-wide surveys are the single most-cited empirical evidence base in EU equality and non-discrimination debates:

| Survey | Focus |
|---|---|
| EU-MIDIS (I: 2008, II: 2016) | Discrimination and victimisation experiences of immigrants and ethnic minorities, ~25,000+ respondents across the EU |
| Roma Survey | Living conditions, poverty, housing, education, health of Roma communities |
| EU LGBTIQ Survey | Discrimination and hate-motivated violence against LGBTIQ people |
| Violence against women survey | EU-wide prevalence data on gender-based violence |
| Fundamental Rights Survey | General-population survey across the full range of Charter rights |
| Antisemitism/Jewish people's experiences surveys | Discrimination and hate crime experienced by Jewish communities |

These surveys are repeated periodically to allow trend analysis and are widely cited in Commission impact assessments, European Parliament reports and Council conclusions on equality, non-discrimination and criminal-justice files.

## Charterpedia and the EU Charter

Charterpedia is FRA's article-by-article reference tool for the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, covering all seven Charter titles (dignity, freedoms, equality, solidarity, citizens' rights, justice, general provisions). For each article it compiles the official explanations, relevant CJEU and ECtHR case law, corresponding national constitutional provisions, and international-law parallels. It is the standard first stop for anyone checking how a Charter right has been interpreted in practice: see also `eu_charter_fundamental_rights.md` for the Charter itself.

## Franet and the Fundamental Rights Platform

**Franet** supplies FRA with comparable national data: a single contracted research organisation per Member State (National Focal Point) feeds in thematic reports and monitoring data that FRA aggregates into EU-wide comparative analysis. This is how FRA can publish 27-country comparisons without running its own national field offices.

**The Fundamental Rights Platform (FRP)** is FRA's civil-society dialogue mechanism, written directly into the founding regulation as the Agency's tool for "structured and fruitful dialogue" with civil society. Registration is free and open to any fundamental-rights organisation active in the EU or in FRA's observer states; the FRP holds an Annual General Meeting and maintains a platform library and working groups.

## Why this matters for policy work

- FRA opinions and reports are frequently cited as evidence in European Parliament committee reports and Commission impact assessments on equality, non-discrimination, data protection, migration and criminal-justice files.
- Because the 2022 mandate extension lets FRA work on police and judicial cooperation on its own initiative, expect its evidence base to expand further into criminal-justice and security-sector fundamental-rights monitoring.
- FRA data (especially EU-MIDIS, the Roma Survey and the LGBTIQ Survey) is the default empirical reference for advocacy positions arguing for stronger EU equality legislation.
- FRA is advisory only: it cannot rule on individual complaints. Users looking for individual redress mechanisms should be pointed to the European Ombudsman (`european_ombudsman.md`) for maladministration complaints against EU institutions, or to national equality bodies / courts for rights violations by Member States.
