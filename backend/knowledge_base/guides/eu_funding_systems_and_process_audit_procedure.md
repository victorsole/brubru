# Systems and Process Audit Procedure

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is:** Audit of a beneficiary's internal control systems and processes in managing Horizon Europe or Euratom grants; assesses risk classification (low, medium, high) based on control tests and transaction testing
- **Who it applies to:** Large recurring participants meeting Article 24.4 criteria of the Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreement (documented cost accounting practices OR formalised internal control system, plus history of 150+ actions in Horizon 2020/Euratom and ongoing participation in 3+ HE actions)
- **Cost control focus:** Time recording practices, procurement processes, intellectual property management, financial controls, segregation of duties
- **Differs from CFS:** CFS (Certificate on Financial Statements) certifies accuracy of reported costs for individual grants; SPA certifies the organisation's systems and internal controls framework
- **Differs from cost audit:** Cost audit tests individual project transactions; SPA tests the systems that prevent errors across all grants
- **Key outcome:** Risk rating determines audit burden: low-risk participants get less frequent ex-post audits and higher CFS threshold (EUR 725,000 vs EUR 430,000)
- **Process:** Letter of announcement > verification visit > draft report > contradictory procedure > final report; typically 6-12 months
- **Deliverable:** Formal SPA report with risk classification, findings (if any), and recommendations for strengthening internal controls
- **Follow-up:** High or medium-risk findings require corrective action; re-audit may be requested within 3 years

## What is a Systems and Process Audit

A Systems and Process Audit (SPA) provides an independent opinion on whether a beneficiary's system of internal control effectively manages Horizon Europe and Euratom grant funding.

The SPA methodology combines:

- **Control tests:** Examination of documented policies, procedures, and control mechanisms (how time is recorded, how procurement is governed, who approves expenses, segregation of duties)
- **Transaction tests:** Sampling of actual transactions across multiple grant agreements to verify controls are working in practice
- **Risk assessment:** Aggregation of findings into one of three risk classifications: low, medium, or high

The audit focuses on the organisation's capacity to manage grant funds correctly, not the accuracy of individual project costs (that is the role of a cost-statement audit or CFS).

### Eligibility and Benefits

Only participants meeting Article 24.4 of the Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreement can request an SPA. The key benefit is risk-based audit relief:

- **Low-risk classification:** Leads to less frequent or less in-depth ex-post audits and a higher threshold for submitting a Certificate on Financial Statements (EUR 725,000 instead of EUR 430,000)
- **Medium or high-risk classification:** Standard HE and Euratom audit rules continue to apply; more frequent audits and lower CFS thresholds

## Difference vs Cost-Statement Audit and CFS

### Systems and Process Audit (SPA)

- **Scope:** Examines the organisation's control framework and processes across all grants
- **Focus:** Are systems in place to prevent errors? Do they work in practice?
- **Result:** Risk classification (low/medium/high)
- **Beneficiary:** Organisation-level; awarded once and covers all ongoing and future grants (unless status changes)
- **Granularity:** Sampling of transactions; aggregate risk assessment

### Cost-Statement Audit (Individual Grant)

- **Scope:** Examines reported costs within a single grant or a consortium member's share
- **Focus:** Were costs actually incurred? Are they eligible and accurately reported?
- **Result:** Cost certification or qualified opinion; can lead to recovery if errors found
- **Beneficiary:** Individual grant or beneficiary role in a specific grant
- **Granularity:** Detailed line-item review of expenses

### Certificate on Financial Statements (CFS)

- **Scope:** Certifies that reported costs in a specific grant are accurate
- **Trigger:** Required if EU contribution exceeds EUR 430,000 (or EUR 725,000 for low-risk SPA participants)
- **Requirement:** Submitted by an independent external auditor before final project closure
- **Outcome:** Assurance that financial statements are fairly presented

An SPA does not replace the CFS; it determines whether the CFS threshold is lower or higher.

## Areas Examined in a Systems and Process Audit

### 1. Internal Control Framework

- Organisational structure and segregation of duties
- Formal policies and procedures documented and approved in writing
- Governance arrangements (approval authorities, decision-making chains)
- Risk identification and mitigation measures
- Monitoring and self-assessment mechanisms

### 2. Time Recording

- Systems and tools used to record staff time on projects
- How timesheets are reviewed and approved
- Segregation between grant-eligible and ineligible hours
- Controls to prevent double-charging or understaffing claims
- Documentation and audit trails

### 3. Procurement

- Procurement policy and approval thresholds
- Conflict-of-interest screening
- Competition principles (single vs. multi-bid procurement)
- Price reasonableness checks
- Documentation of procurement decisions

### 4. Intellectual Property Management

- Ownership and background IP documentation
- Licensing agreements and co-ownership arrangements
- Disclosure procedures for foreground IP generated in grants
- Access rights for auditors and the European Commission
- IP transfer or exploitation controls

### 5. Financial Controls

- Segregation of duties in payment authorisation
- Invoice authentication and duplicate-payment checks
- Reconciliation procedures (bank accounts, ledgers, project claims)
- Cost allocation methods (direct vs. indirect, internal vs. external)
- Documentation and evidence retention

### 6. Subcontracting and Third-Party Funding

- Subcontractor selection and approval procedures
- Cost estimation and benchmarking
- Written contracts referencing EU grant terms
- Monitoring of subcontractor performance
- Controls over financial support to third parties

## The SPA Audit Procedure

### Step 1: Request Submission and Assessment

The participant submits a formal request to DG RTD (RTD-CAS-HE-SPA@ec.europa.eu) including:

- Completed SPA request template (available on the Funding & Tenders Portal)
- Supporting documentation: cost accounting practices, internal control documentation, list of participation history
- Signed statement confirming compliance with Article 24.4 criteria

DG RTD assesses compliance with eligibility criteria and completeness of documentation. If information is missing, the participant has 15 days to submit details. Incomplete or non-compliant requests are rejected.

### Step 2: Letter of Announcement and Scoping

If the request is accepted, DG RTD contacts the participant to schedule the audit. A Letter of Announcement (LoA) is issued, setting out:

- Audit objectives and scope (which internal controls will be examined)
- Timeframe and resource requirements
- List of documents and staff interviews needed
- Preliminary assessment of risk areas

The participant prepares evidence and schedules the verification visit.

### Step 3: Fieldwork and Verification Visit

The audit team visits the participant's premises to:

- Interview key staff (finance, HR, procurement, grants management)
- Examine policy documents, procedures, and approval records
- Review transaction samples (time records, invoices, procurement files, subcontractor agreements)
- Test whether controls are documented and operating as described
- Identify gaps, inconsistencies, or evidence of control failures

Fieldwork typically spans 2-4 weeks depending on organisation size and complexity.

### Step 4: Draft Report and Contradictory Procedure

The audit team prepares a draft SPA report including:

- Description of controls examined
- Findings (areas of concern, control gaps, transaction errors if found)
- Recommendations for strengthening internal controls
- Preliminary risk classification

The draft is shared with the participant in a "contradictory procedure" (usually 30-45 days). The participant can:

- Provide additional evidence or clarification
- Dispute findings with supporting documentation
- Propose corrective actions to address weaknesses

DG RTD considers the participant's response before finalising the report.

### Step 5: Final SPA Report and Risk Classification

The final SPA report is issued with a formal risk classification:

- **Low risk:** Controls are effective; minimal audit burden
- **Medium risk:** Controls exist but with minor gaps; standard audit procedures apply
- **High risk:** Control gaps or failures identified; intensive audits apply; corrective action plan required

The participant receives the final report and, if classified as medium or high risk, a formal notification of findings and a timeline for submitting a corrective action plan.

### Step 6: Follow-Up and Re-Assessment

For medium or high-risk classifications, DG RTD may:

- Request a revised control procedures document or policy update
- Conduct a follow-up audit within 12-36 months to verify corrective actions
- Require monitoring reports on the implementation of recommendations
- Downgrade the classification if controls have been strengthened

Low-risk participants are typically reassessed only if there is a material change in operations or a significant audit finding in an ex-post audit of one of their grants.

## Outcomes and Implications

### Low-Risk Participants Benefit From:

- Reduced frequency of ex-post audits (not audited on every grant)
- Reduced depth of ex-post audits where they do occur
- Higher CFS threshold: EUR 725,000 (instead of EUR 430,000)
- Faster contract establishment; granting authority may waive certain pre-financing controls
- Positive signal to auditors and funding programmes about organisational maturity

### Medium or High-Risk Participants:

- Standard HE and Euratom audit rules apply
- CFS threshold remains at EUR 430,000
- May be subject to more frequent ex-post audits
- Require corrective action plans for identified gaps
- May be ineligible for certain benefits (e.g. simplified cost reporting options)

### No Guarantee of Zero Audits:

An SPA is not an audit-free pass. The European Court of Auditors, OLAF, and internal auditors retain independent rights to audit any beneficiary at any time.
