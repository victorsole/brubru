# Interreg (European Territorial Cooperation)

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Interreg, the European Territorial Cooperation (ETC) goal of Cohesion Policy
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2021/1059 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 June 2021 on specific provisions for the European territorial cooperation goal (Interreg) supported by the European Regional Development Fund and external financing instruments (OJ L 231, 30.6.2021, p. 94), CELEX 32021R1059
- Funded mainly by: the European Regional Development Fund (ERDF), topped up by external financing instruments (IPA III for pre-accession partners, NDICI-Global Europe for neighbourhood/other third countries) where non-EU partners take part
- Budget 2021-2027: **EUR 8.05 billion** in ERDF resources (2018 prices), split across four strands
- **Four strands and their shares of the EUR 8.05bn**: Strand A cross-border, EUR 5.81bn (72.2%); Strand B transnational, EUR 1.47bn (18.2%); Strand C interregional, EUR 0.49bn (6.1%); Strand D outermost regions, EUR 0.28bn (3.5%)
- Strand A (cross-border): cooperation between adjacent NUTS 3 regions either side of an internal or external EU land or maritime border
- Strand B (transnational): cooperation across larger multi-country areas, e.g. Baltic Sea, Danube, Alpine Space, Mediterranean, Atlantic Area, North Sea, Adriatic-Ionian
- Strand C (interregional): knowledge-sharing and capacity-building rather than territorial adjacency, delivered through **INTERREG EUROPE** (regional policy learning), **URBACT** (sustainable urban development networks), **ESPON** (European territorial research/evidence base) and **INTERACT** (programme support/coordination for all Interreg programmes)
- Strand D (outermost regions): cooperation and integration of the EU's outermost regions with their neighbouring third countries and territories
- Interreg brand covers **60+ cooperation programmes**, each with its own Managing Authority and Joint Secretariat, distinct from mainstream national/regional ERDF programmes
- Legal vehicle for joint governance: **European Grouping of Territorial Cooperation (EGTC)**, established under Regulation (EC) No 1082/2006 as amended, giving cross-border partnerships their own legal personality
- Eligibility for Strand A: generally NUTS 3 regions directly on or adjacent to the border; wider NUTS 3 regions can be included for maritime cross-border areas
- Macro-regional strategies (Baltic Sea, Danube, Alpine, Adriatic-Ionian) are policy frameworks, not funding lines, but they steer priorities within Strand B transnational programmes
- Sources: interreg.eu (portal run by INTERACT), interreg.eu/about-interreg/, EUR-Lex CELEX 32021R1059

Interreg is the operational name for the European territorial cooperation (ETC) goal of EU Cohesion Policy: the part of Cohesion Policy that funds cooperation projects between regions and organisations in different Member States (and, in some strands, neighbouring third countries), rather than funding a single Member State's own national or regional programme.

## What Interreg is

Interreg sits inside Cohesion Policy alongside the "Investment for jobs and growth" goal that funds mainstream national and regional ERDF/ESF+/Cohesion Fund programmes. Where those mainstream programmes are run by a single Member State for its own territory, Interreg programmes are jointly designed, jointly managed and jointly delivered by two or more Member States (or a Member State and a neighbouring third country). The legal basis is Regulation (EU) 2021/1059, which sits alongside the Common Provisions Regulation (EU) 2021/1060 (see `cohesion_policy_audit`) and the ERDF/Cohesion Fund Regulation (EU) 2021/1058: Interreg programmes follow the shared-management rules of the CPR but with specific derogations built into 2021/1059 to reflect their cross-border, multi-country nature (e.g. a single set of eligibility rules agreed by all participating Member States, a lead partner principle for projects, and a lead Member State/Managing Authority hosting arrangement).

The name "Interreg" has been used since the first pilot round in 1990 (Interreg I) and has run through six programming periods; the current 2021-2027 round is sometimes referred to as Interreg VI. Around EUR 8.05 billion in ERDF resources is committed for 2021-2027, topped up where relevant by IPA III (pre-accession) and NDICI-Global Europe (neighbourhood and other third countries) contributions when a programme involves partners outside the EU.

## The four strands (A/B/C/D)

**Strand A - Cross-border cooperation.** The largest strand by far (72.2% of the ERDF envelope, EUR 5.81bn). Covers cooperation between NUTS 3 regions directly adjacent to an internal EU border (e.g. France-Belgium, Germany-Poland) or an external EU border (e.g. Poland-Ukraine, Romania-Moldova, Spain-Morocco). Typical projects: joint healthcare services, cross-border transport links, joint emergency response, labour-mobility schemes, environmental protection of shared river basins or mountain ranges.

**Strand B - Transnational cooperation.** EUR 1.47bn (18.2%). Covers larger cooperation areas spanning several Member States and often non-EU neighbours, organised around macro-regional or sea-basin geographies: Baltic Sea, Danube, Alpine Space, Mediterranean, Atlantic Area, North Sea, North-West Europe, Adriatic-Ionian, Balkan-Mediterranean, Euro-MED. Typical projects: joint innovation strategies, climate adaptation planning, transport corridor coordination, joint tourism or blue-economy initiatives.

**Strand C - Interregional cooperation.** EUR 0.49bn (6.1%). Unlike A and B, this strand is not built on territorial adjacency: it funds knowledge exchange, policy learning and capacity building for regional and local authorities across the whole EU (and beyond), delivered through four dedicated programmes (see below).

**Strand D - Outermost regions' cooperation.** EUR 0.28bn (3.5%). Supports cooperation between the EU's outermost regions (e.g. the Canary Islands, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Réunion, the Azores, Madeira) and their non-EU neighbours, recognising their remoteness from the rest of the internal market.

## The interregional instruments (Strand C in detail)

Strand C is delivered through four distinct programmes, each with its own governance and thematic focus:

| Programme | Focus | What it delivers |
|---|---|---|
| **INTERREG EUROPE** | Regional policy learning | Interregional cooperation projects and policy-learning platforms helping regional/local authorities improve their own Cohesion Policy investments through experience exchange |
| **URBACT** | Sustainable urban development | Transnational networks of cities exchanging practice on integrated, participative urban policy (climate, housing, inclusion, local economy) |
| **ESPON** | Territorial research and evidence | Applied research, data and territorial evidence (maps, indicators, foresight studies) underpinning EU, national and regional territorial development policy |
| **INTERACT** | Programme support and coordination | Capacity-building, know-how sharing and coordination support across all 60+ Interreg programmes; also runs the interreg.eu information portal and keep.eu project database |

## European Groupings of Territorial Cooperation (EGTC)

An EGTC is a legal instrument (originally Regulation (EC) No 1082/2006, since amended) that lets regional and local authorities, and in some cases Member States, from different countries set up a joint body with its own legal personality to manage cross-border, transnational or interregional cooperation, including but not limited to Interreg projects. An EGTC can employ staff, hold budgets, sign contracts and be a beneficiary or even the Managing Authority of an Interreg programme, avoiding the need for each partner authority to act under its own separate national legal system. Well-known EGTCs include Eurometropolis Lille-Kortrijk-Tournai, the Grande Région/Großregion (Saar-Lor-Lux and neighbours) and the Galicia-Norte de Portugal EGTC. Not every Interreg programme uses an EGTC as its governance vehicle, but the instrument is closely associated with the Interreg family because it was created specifically to solve the legal-personality problem cross-border cooperation otherwise faces.

## How a project is funded

Each of the 60+ Interreg programmes has its own **Managing Authority** (a public body, sometimes hosted by one of the participating Member States or by an EGTC) and a **Joint Secretariat** that supports applicants and project partners day to day (calls for proposals, application guidance, monitoring, first-level control coordination). Programmes are co-designed by all participating Member States (and third countries where relevant) through a Monitoring Committee, and each programme publishes its own cooperation programme document setting thematic priorities, eligible geography and calls for proposals.

Typical funding architecture for a project:
- A **lead partner** takes overall responsibility for the project and channels ERDF funding to the other partners, who must come from at least two (usually more) of the participating countries.
- ERDF co-financing rates for Interreg programmes are generally more generous than mainstream Cohesion Policy programmes (often up to 80% of eligible costs, sometimes higher for outermost regions or specific programmes), reflecting the added complexity of multi-country delivery.
- **First-level control** (verifying expenditure) is typically decentralised to each participating country, while the Managing Authority and Certifying function retain overall assurance responsibility, consistent with the shared-management audit chain described in `cohesion_policy_audit`.

## Macro-regional strategies link

The EU's four macro-regional strategies are policy coordination frameworks, not separate funding lines, but they shape priorities within relevant Strand B transnational programmes and, in places, Strand A cross-border programmes that sit inside their geography:

- **EU Strategy for the Baltic Sea Region (EUSBSR)** - the oldest macro-regional strategy (2009); interacts closely with the Baltic Sea transnational Interreg programme. See `eu_baltic_sea_strategy`.
- **EU Strategy for the Danube Region (EUSDR)** - aligns with the Danube transnational programme.
- **EU Strategy for the Alpine Region (EUSALP)** - aligns with the Alpine Space transnational programme.
- **EU Strategy for the Adriatic and Ionian Region (EUSAIR)** - aligns with the Adriatic-Ionian transnational programme.

Macro-regional strategies do not have their own budget line; they are delivered through existing instruments, of which Interreg transnational programmes are the primary vehicle. Island and coastal geographies covered by the newer EU Islands Strategy and EU Coastal Communities Strategy (see `eu_islands_coastal_communities_strategy`) also draw on relevant Interreg maritime cross-border and transnational programmes for their blue-economy and connectivity priorities.

## Brubru engagement angles

- **Cross-border SMEs and business networks**: Interreg Strand A programmes routinely fund SME internationalisation, joint innovation vouchers, cross-border business support networks and labour-mobility services. Companies operating in a border region (e.g. France-Spain, Germany-Netherlands, Ireland-Northern Ireland) should check the relevant cross-border programme's open calls, not only their national ERDF programme.
- **Regional and local authorities**: INTERREG EUROPE and URBACT are the entry points for authorities wanting to improve their own Cohesion Policy delivery or exchange urban-policy practice, without needing a cross-border neighbour to partner with.
- **EGTC formation advice**: authorities considering joint cross-border governance (transport links, shared healthcare, joint spatial planning) can be pointed to the EGTC instrument as the legal vehicle, and to existing EGTCs as precedent.
- **Funding-map positioning**: Interreg sits within the ERDF family described in `eu_commission_funding_programmes_map`; Brubru should distinguish it clearly from mainstream national/regional ERDF programmes and from thematic EU funds (e.g. EMFAF for blue economy, Horizon Europe for research) that sometimes co-fund related activity.
- **Tenderator**: Interreg calls for proposals are programme-specific (not on TED) and published on each programme's own website via keep.eu and interreg.eu; flag this data-source gap for future Tenderator coverage of Interreg calls specifically, distinct from TED-sourced EU tenders.
