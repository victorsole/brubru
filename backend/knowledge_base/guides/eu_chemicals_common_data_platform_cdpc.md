# Common Data Platform on Chemicals and the One Substance One Assessment Reform

## QUICK FACTS
- Reform name: "One Substance, One Assessment" (OSOA), the EU's chemicals data-governance overhaul.
- Three legal acts, all signed 26 November 2025, published in OJ L on 12 December 2025, entered into force **1 January 2026**:
  1. **Regulation (EU) 2025/2455** -- establishes the Common Data Platform on Chemicals (CDPC) and a monitoring and outlook framework for chemicals. CELEX likely 32025R2455 (verify on EUR-Lex; not independently confirmed here, see note below).
  2. **Regulation (EU) 2025/2457** -- reattributes scientific/technical tasks and improves inter-agency cooperation; amends Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 (General Food Law/EFSA), Regulation (EC) No 401/2009 (EEA), Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (Medical Devices) and Regulation (EU) 2019/1021 (Persistent Organic Pollutants). CELEX likely 32025R2457 (verify).
  3. **Directive (EU) 2025/2456** -- amends Directive 2011/65/EU (RoHS) to reattribute technical tasks to ECHA. CELEX likely 32025L2456 (verify).
- Origin: Commission proposal 7 December 2023, COM(2023) 779, procedure **2023/0453(COD)**. Lead EP committee: ENVI. Rapporteur: Dimitris Tsiodras (EPP, Greece), who took over from Maria Spyraki.
- Legislative path: Council general approach 14 June 2024; EP ENVI committee vote 18 February 2025 (62 for, 4 against, 15 abstentions); EP plenary negotiating mandate 1 April 2025; trilogue political agreement 12 June 2025; final signature 26 November 2025.
- Platform operator: **ECHA** (European Chemicals Agency, Helsinki), working with EFSA, EMA, the European Environment Agency (EEA), the EU Agency for Safety and Health at Work (EU-OSHA), the Commission (including the Joint Research Centre) and Member States.
- Sectoral scope: pools hazard, use, emission, exposure and human-biomonitoring data spanning REACH, CLP, the Biocidal Products Regulation (BPR), plant protection products, food contact materials, cosmetics, the Drinking Water Directive, industrial emissions and occupational exposure legislation -- ultimately drawing on roughly 70 pieces of EU chemicals-related law.
- Absorbs and builds on **IPCheM** (Information Platform for Chemical Monitoring, run by the Joint Research Centre) and introduces a systematic, EU-wide human-biomonitoring data stream.
- FAIR principles apply: platform data must be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable; non-confidential data is freely downloadable in machine-readable form. Excluded: proprietary mixture formulations, cosmetic product notifications, internal agency working documents.
- Key implementation dates (verify against the Commission's published implementation plan once issued): agencies' joint implementation plan due 2 July 2026; first annual monitoring-and-outlook report on emerging chemical risks due 2 July 2027; core platform services targeted operational within 3 years of entry into force (around January 2029); full operability including historical data integration within 10 years (around January 2036).
- Policy anchor: implements the "one substance, one assessment" objective set out in the 2020 **Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability**, COM(2020) 667, under the European Green Deal.
- Goal stated by the Commission and co-legislators: fewer duplicate assessments across agencies and sectoral laws, faster and more consistent hazard/risk decisions, less animal testing through data reuse, and earlier detection of emerging chemical risks.

Guide for professionals working on EU chemicals policy, regulatory affairs and product compliance who need to understand how the EU is centralising chemical data across REACH, CLP and sectoral product laws, and what the Common Data Platform on Chemicals will mean for reporting obligations and data access.

## What problem OSOA is solving

Before this reform, chemical hazard, use and exposure data were scattered across separate databases run by different agencies and under different sectoral laws: REACH and CLP registrations at ECHA, pesticide data under the plant protection products framework, food contact material data linked to EFSA, cosmetic ingredient notifications, biocide data under the BPR, and environmental monitoring data in IPCheM. The same substance could be assessed multiple times, by different bodies, using different (sometimes inconsistent) datasets, with no shared, searchable repository. The Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability (2020) set "one substance, one assessment" as an explicit objective: pool the data once, assess it consistently, and make it easier for regulators, scientists, industry and the public to find what already exists before generating new studies (including new animal tests).

## The three OSOA acts

| Act | What it does | Amends / establishes |
|-----|---------------|----------------------|
| Regulation (EU) 2025/2455 | Establishes the Common Data Platform on Chemicals (CDPC) and a monitoring and outlook framework for emerging chemical risks | New freestanding regulation; absorbs IPCheM |
| Regulation (EU) 2025/2457 | Reattributes scientific and technical tasks between agencies and improves cooperation | Amends Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 (General Food Law/EFSA), Regulation (EC) No 401/2009 (EEA), Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (Medical Devices Regulation), Regulation (EU) 2019/1021 (POPs Regulation) |
| Directive (EU) 2025/2456 | Reattributes technical tasks to ECHA | Amends Directive 2011/65/EU (RoHS -- restriction of hazardous substances in electrical/electronic equipment) |

Together the three acts form a package: the Regulation on the CDPC builds the shared data infrastructure, while the companion Regulation and Directive redraw which EU agency does which piece of scientific and technical work, so that duplicated assessments (the same substance evaluated separately for REACH purposes and for food-contact or RoHS purposes, for example) can be consolidated.

## The Common Data Platform on Chemicals (CDPC)

### What it pools

The CDPC is designed as a single digital entry point drawing on three broad categories of data:

- Data that agencies and the Commission already hold because EU legislation requires it (ECHA, EEA, EFSA, EMA, EU-OSHA, national authorities).
- Chemical data generated through EU, Member State or international research and monitoring initiatives.
- Data voluntarily submitted by Member States, third countries, research institutions and other stakeholders.

Coverage spans REACH and CLP substance data, biocidal products (BPR), plant protection products, food contact materials, cosmetics, the Drinking Water Directive, industrial emissions and occupational exposure regimes, with a stated ambition of eventually linking data referenced across roughly 70 pieces of EU chemicals-related law.

### FAIR principles and access

The Regulation requires that platform data be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable (FAIR). Non-confidential data will be freely downloadable in machine-readable formats. Excluded from the platform: proprietary mixture formulations, cosmetic product notifications, and internal agency working documents.

### IPCheM and human biomonitoring

The CDPC absorbs and builds on IPCheM (the Information Platform for Chemical Monitoring, run by the Commission's Joint Research Centre), which already aggregates environmental and human-exposure monitoring data, including a human-biomonitoring module tracking chemical measurements in blood and urine. OSOA formalises a systematic, EU-wide human-biomonitoring data stream feeding into the CDPC, extending work started under the EU-wide HBM4EU initiative.

### Monitoring and outlook framework

Alongside the data platform, Regulation (EU) 2025/2455 creates a monitoring and outlook framework intended to give regulators earlier warning of emerging chemical risks, drawing on input from ECHA, EFSA, EMA and EU-OSHA. An annual report on emerging risks is expected, with the first due 2 July 2027 (verify exact date once the Commission publishes the implementation plan).

## Agency reattribution: Regulation 2025/2457 and Directive 2025/2456

The two companion acts do not touch the data platform directly. Instead they reassign which EU body performs specific scientific and technical tasks so agencies stop duplicating each other's work:

- Regulation (EU) 2025/2457 amends the General Food Law framework (Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, EFSA's founding instrument), the EEA's founding regulation (Regulation (EC) No 401/2009), the Medical Devices Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2017/745) and the POPs Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2019/1021) to redraw agency responsibilities and strengthen cooperation clauses.
- Directive (EU) 2025/2456 amends RoHS (Directive 2011/65/EU) to move certain technical assessment tasks to ECHA, consistent with ECHA's expanding role as the EU's central chemicals-science hub.

## Why it matters for stakeholders

- **Industry and regulatory affairs teams**: a single search point for what data already exists on a substance across REACH, CLP, biocides, pesticides, food contact, cosmetics and RoHS may reduce duplicate testing and study costs, but also increases transparency around what companies have already submitted elsewhere.
- **NGOs, researchers and public-health advocates**: the human-biomonitoring stream and public FAIR data access materially expand what is independently verifiable about chemical exposure in the EU.
- **Public affairs professionals**: the reattribution acts (2025/2457 and 2025/2456) shift institutional weight further toward ECHA as the EU's central chemicals-science body, alongside EFSA, EEA, EMA and EU-OSHA -- relevant to any Transparency Register engagement strategy on chemicals files.
- **Compliance teams**: the CDPC does not itself create new substantive obligations under REACH, CLP, the BPR or sectoral product laws; it is a data-infrastructure reform. Substantive duties still flow from those underlying instruments (see the REACH guide and the chemicals simplification omnibus guide for the compliance-facing changes).
