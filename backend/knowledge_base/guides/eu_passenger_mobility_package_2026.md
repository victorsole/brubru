## Overview

The Passenger Mobility Package is the Commission's first cross-modal regulatory intervention in the EU passenger-transport single market since the 2017 White Paper on the future of transport. It targets the **fragmentation of digital booking, ticketing and passenger-rights regimes** across road, rail, bus and urban modes — a fragmentation that has held back the development of integrated mobility services for EU residents and visitors despite repeated industry calls.

The Commission framing positions the package as a **competitiveness measure**: a 2024 DG MOVE assessment estimated that intra-EU passenger journeys involving more than one operator generate **35-40% lower per-trip revenue** due to ticketing friction, and that integrated bookings (where they exist on a national basis — DB Navigator, SNCF Connect, OBB Scotty) drive **2-3x higher modal switch rates** from car to rail. By imposing single-market rules on booking access + ticketing interoperability, the Commission expects to unlock cross-border passenger volumes that benefit climate goals (modal shift), industrial competitiveness (digital platforms scaling), and consumer welfare (price transparency + accessibility).

The package complements the **Sustainable and Smart Mobility Strategy** (COM(2020)789) and the **Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) revision** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1679). It also lines up with the **Climate Law 2040 target** (Reg 2026/667) by making the modal-shift assumption in the EU long-term scenario more credible.

## Component 1: Multimodal Digital Mobility Services (MDMS)

The MDMS proposal targets **Mobility-as-a-Service (MaaS) platforms** and intermediaries that offer combined journeys across modes. Expected obligations on transport providers:
- Make **real-time data** (schedules, capacity, prices, accessibility info) available via standardised APIs to authorised intermediaries on fair and non-discriminatory terms
- Allow **commercial agreements** with intermediaries on reasonable conditions — no blocking of legitimate resale or aggregation
- Provide **booking confirmation + e-ticket issuance** through intermediaries with the same legal effect as direct sales

Expected obligations on intermediaries:
- **Display transparent journey information** to consumers, including total price, cancellation rules, sustainability score, accessibility info, modal mix
- **No undue preference** for affiliated operators (parallels the DMA's anti-self-preferencing rules for gatekeepers)
- **Refund and complaint procedures** — single point of contact for the consumer, with internal cascade to operators

## Component 2: Single Digital Booking and Ticketing (SDBT)

SDBT is the **infrastructure layer** that makes MDMS work. Expected core requirements:
- **Open distribution**: carriers must publish their fares + availability + booking endpoints on terms that allow third-party platforms to integrate
- **Common ticket standard**: a machine-readable e-ticket format (likely building on the UIC ETicket and ERA TAP-TSI standards for rail; new urban/bus standards for non-rail)
- **Interoperable identifiers**: passenger journey IDs that persist across operators so claims, compensation, and accessibility services can be coordinated
- **Connection guarantee mechanism**: when a multimodal journey is sold as a single product, the seller is responsible for re-routing or refunding if a connection is missed, with cascade rights to the responsible operator
- **Accessibility**: tickets must support Person with Reduced Mobility (PRM) needs at booking time; standardised assistance-request workflow

## Component 3: Revision of Rail Passengers' Rights (Reg (EC) No 1371/2007 / Reg (EU) 2021/782)

The existing Rail Passengers' Rights Regulation (revised by Reg (EU) 2021/782, applicable from 7 June 2023) is being **updated again** to:
- Integrate the **multimodal connection guarantee** introduced by SDBT
- **Strengthen compensation thresholds** for delays (likely lowering the 60-minute threshold or adjusting compensation percentages)
- **PRM rights** — pre-notification window from 36h down to 24h; mandatory assistance at unstaffed stations via portable equipment
- **Information obligations** — real-time delay info pushed to passengers via the ticket platform
- **Force majeure scope** — clarifying coverage where extreme weather / climate events are increasingly disruptive
