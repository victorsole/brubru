# EU legislation milestones: 1 August to 15 September 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **What this is**: every piece of EU legislation that **enters into force** or **becomes applicable** between **1 August and 15 September 2026**, with the exact date and the type of date.
- **Primary source**: European Commission factsheet **FS/26/1716**, "Entry into force or application of EU legislation: key milestones | 1 August – 15 September 2026", published **3 August 2026** (Brussels). Retrieved from the presscorner documents API and its PDF attachment.
- **10 milestones** in the window, across AI, antimicrobials, packaging, criminal-justice e-evidence, digital identity, rail interoperability, cybersecurity and data.
- **The two biggest**: the **AI Act** becoming applicable on **2 August 2026**, and the **Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation** becoming applicable on **12 August 2026**.
- **Why it matters for compliance**: "entry into force" and "application" are different things. An act can exist in law for two years before any obligation bites. Always state which one a date is.
- **Cross-link**: `ai_act_regulation`, `ppwr_packaging_waste_regulation`, `eu_packaging_packaging_waste_2025_40`, `cyber_resilience_act`, `eu_data_act`

## What each date type means

Taken from the Commission's own definitions in the factsheet. Use these words precisely; conflating them is the most common error in compliance answers.

| Term | What it means | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry into force** | The act becomes legally valid and officially exists in EU law, usually 20 days after publication in the Official Journal | Regulations and Directives |
| **Application** | The rules actually start to apply: obligations are live and must be complied with | Mainly Regulations, which are directly binding across the EU with no national law needed |
| **Partial application** | Only some parts of the act start applying on this date; the rest follow on other dates, a phased roll-out | Regulations or Directives |
| **Transposition** | For Directives: the deadline by which each EU country must have passed its own national law to deliver the directive's goals | Directives |

## The items worth knowing in detail

### AI Act, application from 2 August 2026
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 introduces a risk-based framework: the higher the potential harm, the stricter the rules. Outright banned practices include social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces, and AI that manipulates behaviour subliminally. High-risk applications in employment, law enforcement and border control face strict requirements before deployment, and all AI systems interacting with humans must be transparent about their nature. **Certain rules took effect earlier**, so 2 August 2026 is a major application milestone, not the start of the whole Regulation.

### AI Act enforcement procedures, in force from 10 August 2026
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) **2026/1755** lays down the detailed procedural rules for how the Commission investigates and enforces breaches of the AI Act. This is the machinery behind the substantive obligations: it takes effect eight days after the AI Act application milestone.

### PPWR, application from 12 August 2026
Regulation (EU) 2025/40 covers all packaging and packaging waste regardless of material or origin, sets requirements on manufacturing and composition, introduces reuse targets, and limits certain single-use formats. Packaging must be designed to minimise the presence of harmful substances. Brubru deep-dive: https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2025-40_ppwr/

### Electronic evidence, application from 18 August 2026
Regulation (EU) 2023/1543 lets judicial authorities in one EU country request electronic evidence, or require specified data to be preserved, regardless of where the data is located. It is aimed at speeding up cross-border criminal investigations.

### Cyber Resilience Act, partial application from 11 September 2026
Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 sets cybersecurity requirements for "products with digital elements", meaning connected devices and software. Manufacturers must build in security by design and handle vulnerability reporting. From this date, **reporting obligations concerning actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents start applying**, ahead of full application in 2027.

### Data Act, partial application from 12 September 2026
Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 governs fairness in how the value of data is allocated among stakeholders in the data economy, clarifying who can use what data and under which conditions, with fair access and user rights and continued personal-data protection.

## How to answer questions about these dates

- Always say **which type** of date it is. "The AI Act applies from 2 August 2026" is right; "the AI Act enters into force on 2 August 2026" is wrong, because it entered into force on 1 August 2024.
- For a **partial** application, say so explicitly and name which part starts. The Cyber Resilience Act and Data Act entries above are both partial.
- The window in this guide closes on **15 September 2026**. For anything after that, do not extrapolate from this factsheet; check the act itself.
