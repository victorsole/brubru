## The Two Authorisation Pathways

### Centralised Procedure (Regulation 726/2004, via EMA)
- **Mandatory** for: biotech products, orphan medicines, HIV/AIDS, cancer, neurodegenerative diseases, diabetes, autoimmune diseases, viral diseases, advanced therapies (ATMP)
- **Optional** for: any medicine with a significant therapeutic, scientific or technical innovation
- One application, one evaluation, one authorisation valid in all EU/EEA Member States
- Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) gives scientific opinion
- Commission adopts the marketing authorisation decision
- Assessment timeline: 210 days (clock stop for applicant responses)

### National/Decentralised/Mutual Recognition (Directive 2001/83/EC)
- **National procedure**: single Member State authorisation
- **Mutual recognition procedure (MRP)**: one Member State authorises, others recognise
- **Decentralised procedure (DCP)**: simultaneous application in multiple Member States, one Reference Member State leads
- Used for: most generic medicines, well-established substances, traditional herbal medicines

Key concepts:
- **EudraVigilance**: EU database of suspected adverse drug reactions
- **Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs)**: regular safety assessments by MAHs
- **Risk Management Plans (RMPs)**: mandatory for all new marketing authorisations
- **Black triangle**: medicines under additional monitoring (inverted black triangle symbol)
- **PRAC**: Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (EMA)

## Clinical Trials (Regulation (EU) 536/2014)

- Replaced the Clinical Trials Directive 2001/20/EC
- **CTIS**: Clinical Trials Information System (single EU portal for all trial applications)
- Harmonised assessment: coordinated by one Reporting Member State
- Multinational trials with a single application
- Transparency: trial results published on CTIS within 12 months of study end
- Simplified rules for low-intervention clinical trials
- Informed consent requirements harmonised across EU

## Orphan Medicines (Regulation (EC) 141/2000)

- Disease prevalence threshold: affects not more than 5 in 10,000 persons in the EU
- Orphan designation: granted by EMA Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products (COMP)
- Incentives: 10 years market exclusivity (current), protocol assistance, fee reductions
- **Reform**: breakthrough orphans to get up to 11 years market exclusivity under the new framework

## Paediatric Medicines (Regulation (EC) 1901/2006)

- Paediatric Investigation Plans (PIPs) required for all new medicines
- EMA Paediatric Committee (PDCO) assesses PIPs
- Incentive: 6-month SPC extension for completing an agreed PIP
- Waivers available if disease does not occur in children or product is unsafe/ineffective

## Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products (Regulation (EC) 1394/2007)

Three categories:
- **Gene therapy**: delivers genes to treat/prevent disease
- **Somatic cell therapy**: manipulated cells with biological characteristics changed
- **Tissue-engineered products**: engineered cells/tissues for repair/regeneration

Centralised procedure mandatory. EMA Committee for Advanced Therapies (CAT) provides scientific assessment.

## Falsified Medicines (Directive 2011/62/EU)

- Safety features on packaging: unique identifier (2D barcode) + anti-tampering device
- European Medicines Verification System (EMVS): end-to-end verification at point of dispensing
- Strengthened supply chain controls: importers, brokers, API manufacturers
- Common EU logo for legal online pharmacies

## Penalties

Commission Regulation (EC) 658/2007 (CELEX 32007R0658): Financial penalties for non-compliance with Regulation 726/2004 obligations. EMA can impose fines on marketing authorisation holders.
