## Core Problem

Generative AI systems are trained on copyrighted material without creator consent or compensation. The report finds that GenAI providers are violating copyright through unauthorised collection of works from the internet and non-compliance with text and data mining (TDM) opt-out rights under Article 4 of Directive (EU) 2019/790.

## Seven Key Recommendations

### 1. Transparency Requirements
- Mandatory detailed disclosure of all copyright-protected content used in training
- Covers training, inference, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
- Crawlers must identify themselves to web operators
- Support for digital watermarking technologies

### 2. Rights Holder Control
- Standardised, machine-readable opt-out mechanisms for creators
- EUIPO designated as trusted intermediary to manage exclusion registries
- Restore creators' absolute right to bargaining power for remuneration

### 3. Licensing Framework
- Facilitate voluntary collective licensing agreements by sector
- Establish a functioning licensing market with fair remuneration
- Oppose flat-rate payment models that undervalue diverse content

### 4. Legal Presumptions
- Rebuttable presumptions that copyrighted works were used when transparency obligations are not met
- Shift legal costs to AI providers where rights holders prevail

### 5. Content Classification
- AI-generated content is ineligible for copyright protection
- Human-created works retain full protection
- Mandatory labelling of purely AI-generated outputs

### 6. Press Sector Protection
- Fair compensation from GenAI providers to news outlets for diverted traffic
- Extend ancillary rights for press publishers to cover inference and RAG
- Preserve media pluralism and democratic discourse

### 7. Territoriality Principle
- EU copyright law applies to GenAI models deployed in the EU regardless of where training occurred
- Non-compliant models prohibited from EU market access

## Institutional Proposals
- Establish EUIPO Copyright Knowledge Centre
- Commission to urgently assess whether existing copyright acquis covers GenAI
- Propose immediate solutions without waiting for broader copyright framework review

## Plenary Vote Highlights (10 March 2026)

Adopted with an overwhelming majority (460-71-88) during the Strasbourg plenary (9-12 March 2026). The strong vote (87% in favour) sends a clear political signal to the Commission:

- EU copyright law applies to all genAI systems on the EU market regardless of where training occurred (territoriality principle)
- Full compensation required for news outlets whose traffic/revenue is diverted by AI systems
- Mandatory itemised lists of all copyrighted works used in training datasets
- EUIPO-managed opt-out registry for rightsholders
- AI-generated content explicitly excluded from copyright protection
- Protection against dissemination of manipulated/AI-generated content

## Political Context
- Balances innovation (EU competitiveness in AI) with rights protection (creator compensation)
- Rejects waiting for comprehensive legislative reform: calls for emergency action
- Relevant intersection with AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) transparency obligations
