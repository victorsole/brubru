# New Regulation on European Standardisation: the Commission Proposal of 6 October 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** on **Tuesday 6 October 2026** the Commission adopted a **proposal for a new Regulation on European standardisation**, **COM(2026) 780 final, procedure 2026/0307 (COD)**, to **repeal and replace Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012**. Legal basis: **Article 114 TFEU**. Proposed by EVP **Séjourné**. It is a **proposal only**: Parliament and Council must still adopt it.
- **Product laws: NOT amended.** No operative article names the Cyber Resilience Act, the AI Act, the Digital Product Passport (Ecodesign Regulation) or the Construction Products Regulation; there is **no transitional clause for standards already requested under them**. They appear only in recitals and the explanatory memorandum.
- **Speed target (three figures, always name the source):** press release **six years to four**; Q&A **by one third**; the impact assessment's own table: **6.1 years today, 5.3 in a "dynamic baseline", 4.2 under the preferred option** = **21% faster than that baseline** (about 31% faster than today). The executive summary words it "by 19%, to approximately 4.2 years", but 19% belongs to Option 1 (4.3 years). Never give one figure alone.
- **Structure:** 43 articles in 7 chapters, 50 recitals, 3 annexes. **Entry into force: twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal; application six months later** (recital 50). The financial statement assumes entry into force around **2028**, so nothing applies in 2026.
- **Free access (Article 5):** European and designated standards bodies must give **"unrestricted free online access" to referenced standards**, including amendments, in all authentic languages, even after the reference is withdrawn: about **3,650 of 27,000** European standards (Q&A).
- **Faster routes (proposed):** requests carry a **delivery deadline** (Article 23; the Commission itself only "shall endeavour" to meet its own); **"harmonised standardisation deliverables"** as interim step (Article 2); the Commission can **designate other standards-development organisations** when the three European bodies decline, are silent, miss deadlines or deliver non-compliant work (Articles 26-31); **common specifications** by implementing act are the **fallback** (Article 32), **NOT the first route**.
- **Centre:** the press release says **"Expert Centre on European Standardisation"**; the proposal (Article 38) says **"Centre of Excellence on European Standardisation"**, **within the Commission**, **20 full-time equivalents** per the explanatory memorandum (mostly the existing 16.5-FTE unit plus about 4, per the impact assessment). A **"Chief Standardisation Officer"** is only a recital-45 possibility ("might be led by"), not law.
- **Participation (Article 18, Annex II):** the Commission designates **four European stakeholder organisations** (SMEs, consumers, environment, social) by implementing act, at least every four years; **none is named**. **Annex II criteria:** non-governmental, non-profit, statutory objectives in the field, and a mandate from national non-profit bodies **in at least two thirds of the Member States**; consumer, environment and social bodies must also be **independent of industry and business interests** (the SME body has no such test). Funding is **permissive only ("may")**.
- **International (Article 35):** use **ISO, IEC, ITU standards where they exist or are imminent**, unless a Commission request bars it; a request may limit participation to Union entities **not controlled by a third country**.
- **Annexes:** **Annex I** = CEN, CENELEC, ETSI; **Annex II** = the stakeholder criteria above; **Annex III** = correlation table to 1025/2012, which contains errors (it cites a non-existent "Article 44").
- **Penalties:** none. Enforcement: key performance indicators (Article 13), possible withdrawal of recognition, fallback bodies or common specifications, safeguard procedure (Article 33).

## Why it matters
Harmonised standards give businesses a **presumption of conformity** with product laws (CRA, AI Act, ESPR/DPP, Construction Products Regulation, radio equipment and others). Delays in standards leave companies without a clear route to compliance; the proposal tries to shorten that gap without ending the public-private model run by CEN, CENELEC and ETSI.

## Main articles (as proposed)
- **Article 2:** a new term, **"referenced standard"**: a harmonised European standard, a harmonised standardisation deliverable, or a standard in support of Union legislation or policies. A **common specification** is a technical specification adopted by the Commission that gives a presumption of conformity.
- **Articles 3-4:** principles (openness, transparency, consensus, technological neutrality, inclusiveness); participant lists published per requested standard.
- **Article 6-7:** use of digital tools and machine-readable formats (the bodies "shall endeavour"); the Commission builds an **EU Standards Platform** (referenced standards, common specifications, access information, request status), planned for 2028 or 2029 in the financial statement.
- **Article 9:** an annual Union work programme, draft published with a one-month comment window.
- **Articles 10-16:** recognition of the European organisations (Annex I, amended by delegated act); national standardisation bodies appointed by Member States, at least one per Member State in each European organisation; only national bodies' representatives vote; annual KPIs on speed, quality, inclusiveness, cooperation and international influence (targets set by implementing act); annual reports due 31 January.
- **Article 22-25:** the Commission consults the European organisations first (reply within **one month**); requests by implementing act; the Commission "shall endeavour" to adopt a request within 12 months of the organisations' reply; it assesses delivered standards within **six months** and "shall endeavour" to publish the reference within **three months** of a positive assessment; partial compliance allows a reference with restrictions.
- **Article 32:** common specifications by implementing act (examination procedure) only on defined conditions; repealed or amended once a covering standard is referenced.
- **Article 33:** safeguard: a Member State, the Parliament or the Commission can flag a deficient standard; no objection within one month means the Commission decides within six months, an objection within twelve.
- **Article 41-42:** report on implementation five years after entry into force and every five years; **Regulation 1025/2012 repealed**; Article 48 of the General Product Safety Regulation repealed.

## Details behind the quick facts
- **Annex II, more:** the environment body must also work actively for environmental sustainability. The impact assessment refers to the four bodies that exist today (SBS, ANEC, ECOS, ETUC); the proposal names none.
- **Annex III:** cites an "Article 44" although the proposal ends at Article 43, and several old articles of Regulation 1025/2012 have no counterpart listed; Article 42(1) makes the table the reading key for references to the repealed Regulation, so say the table has errors.
- **Centre and officer:** the impact assessment makes the Chief Standardisation Officer part of the preferred option and the financial statement budgets **1 FTE for "leading the Centre"**; the legal text only mentions the possibility in recital 45.
- **Free access, more:** the Commission presents Article 5 as following Court of Justice rulings; the impact assessment says the Court left questions open (cases T-581/24 and T-53/25 are pending). It costs only the harmonised standards (about EUR 30 million a year of lost sales for the standards bodies); recital 12 allows limits to the self-standing necessary parts and protection against AI mass extraction.

## Impact assessment (staff working document SWD(2026) 786, read in full on 7 October 2026)
**Package:** the proposal is accompanied by SEC(2026) 780, **SWD(2026) 785** (subsidiarity grid), **SWD(2026) 786** (impact assessment, 197 pages) and **SWD(2026) 787** (executive summary). The evaluation of Regulation 1025/2012 is SWD(2025) 170 (summary 171).
**Problem:** the average harmonised standard takes about **6.1 years** (policy phase 1.7, delivery 3.2, publication 1.2). AI Act standards are delayed by at least two years.
**Options:** (1) modernised system run by the European organisations; (2) **preferred**: European-organisation-centred system plus alternative standards bodies, a central free-access repository, the Centre and a Chief Standardisation Officer; (3) an open Commission-managed system. The assessment rates Options 1 and 2 as similarly efficient and says Option 2 adds about EUR 66.7 million a year of benefit for EUR 44.9 million of extra cost (net EUR 21.8 million over Option 1).
**Quantified benefits (Option 2), all "indicative orders of magnitude" in the document's own words:** about **EUR 294.7 million a year** of compliance savings, measured against the 5.3-year baseline (EUR 52 million from alternative standards bodies; the factsheet rounds to EUR 300 million), plus about EUR 30 million a year from free access. Assumptions: EUR 3,700 per standard used, 10-year horizon, only about 7% of firms assumed to benefit, at most 10% of requests going to alternative bodies. SMEs would capture **78%** (EUR 253 million), a ratio taken from an earlier assessment based on 10 SME and 100 large-firm respondents.
**Costs (Option 2):** industry about EUR 10.5 million a year; **the European and national standards bodies about EUR 32.9 million a year**, mainly up to EUR 30 million of lost standards sales (against about EUR 6 million of assumed compensation); the Commission **EUR 1.5 million a year (7.5 FTE)**, of which the explanatory memorandum says **5.5 FTE are internal redeployment and 2 are additional external staff**.
**Stakeholders:** the European and national standards bodies oppose Option 2; in the open consultation 37% (196 of 588) agreed and 49% (288 of 588) disagreed with letting other organisations respond to requests.
**Scrutiny:** the Regulatory Scrutiny Board gave an **"overall positive" opinion on 19 June 2026** with five recommendations (stakeholder participation and quality, a redefined specific objective, more detail on costs, a societal benefit-cost basis, limitations). The 2025 evaluation of Regulation 1025/2012 (it found the Regulation moderately effective at speeding up delivery) had received a **negative** Board opinion in January 2025.
**Figures that do not agree inside the package** (say so if asked): the explanatory memorandum gives **EUR 183 million for 2028-2034** and the financial statement **EUR 177.575 million** (EUR 176.297 million operational plus EUR 1.278 million administrative); the impact assessment contains neither figure and we found no derivation of EUR 183 million. The national-body cost of EUR 2.9 million is **not** in conflict with EUR 1.9 million: it is EUR 1.9 million (inclusiveness) plus EUR 1.0 million (transparency registers). The financial statement cites the KPI article as Article 11 although it is Article 13. Requests: examination procedure applies to requests to designated bodies, and the Commission only "shall endeavour" to meet its own deadlines (Article 23(5)), so the time savings depend on deadlines holding.

## FAQ
**Is the Standardisation Regulation already in force?** No. It is a proposal adopted on 6 October 2026; Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 stays in force until a new regulation is adopted and applies.
**Does it change my harmonised standard under the CRA or the AI Act?** The text does not amend those laws or add a transition for them. Check each law's own standardisation request.
**What is a common specification?** A technical specification adopted by the Commission as fallback when no timely harmonised standard exists; it gives a presumption of conformity.
**Is access to standards free?** The proposal requires free online access to referenced standards (Article 5).
**What is the Expert Centre?** The press release's name for the Centre of Excellence on European Standardisation (Article 38), inside the Commission.
**Who would the four stakeholder organisations be?** The proposal names none; it sets criteria (Annex II). The impact assessment refers to the four bodies that exist today (SBS, ANEC, ECOS and ETUC).
**Is the 2025 evaluation positive?** It found the Regulation only moderately effective at speeding up delivery, and the Regulatory Scrutiny Board's first opinion on it (15 January 2025) was negative.
