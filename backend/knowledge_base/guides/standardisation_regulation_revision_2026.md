# New Regulation on European Standardisation: the Commission Proposal of 6 October 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** on **Tuesday 6 October 2026** (Strasbourg) the Commission adopted a **proposal for a new Regulation on European standardisation**, **COM(2026) 780 final, procedure 2026/0307 (COD)**, to **repeal and replace Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012**. Legal basis: **Article 114 TFEU**. Proposed by EVP **Séjourné** (Prosperity and Industrial Strategy). Press release and Q&A of 6 October 2026. It is a **proposal only**: Parliament and Council must still adopt it.
- **Speed target:** the Commission says the average time to develop a harmonised standard would fall **from six years to four** (press release); the **impact assessment summary** says **by 19%, from 6 years to approximately 4.2 years**. Quote both with their source, never one figure alone.
- **Structure (proposal text):** 43 articles in 7 chapters, 50 recitals, 3 annexes. **Entry into force: the twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal; application: six months later** (recital 50). The financial statement assumes entry into force around **2028**, so nothing applies in 2026.
- **Free access to standards (Article 5):** the European standardisation organisations and designated standards-development organisations must give **"unrestricted free online access" to referenced standards**, including amendments, in all authentic languages, even after the reference is withdrawn. This codifies the case law of the Court of Justice; recital 12 allows limits to the self-standing necessary parts and protection against AI mass extraction.
- **Faster routes (all proposed):** Commission requests to the European standardisation organisations (CEN, CENELEC, ETSI) must carry a **delivery deadline** (Article 23); **"harmonised standardisation deliverables"** can be an interim step towards a harmonised standard (Article 2); the Commission can **designate other standards-development organisations** for urgent or specialised needs when the three European bodies decline, are silent, miss deadlines or deliver non-compliant work (Articles 26-31); and **common specifications** (adopted by the Commission by implementing act) are the **targeted fallback** (Article 32). Common specifications are **NOT the first route**: they apply only after the European organisations did not reply, refused, or the response was inadequate, or a reference was withdrawn.
- **Centre:** the press release calls it the **"Expert Centre on European Standardisation"**; the **proposal text (Article 38) calls it the "Centre of Excellence on European Standardisation"**, placed **within the Commission**, with **a total of 20 full-time equivalents** according to the explanatory memorandum. Do not state a seat outside the Commission, and do not state a head's title as law: a "Chief Standardisation Officer" appears only in recital 45 ("might be led by").
- **Participation:** the Commission designates **four European stakeholder organisations** (SMEs, consumers, environment, social) by implementing act, at least every four years (Article 18); the standards bodies must enable representation of SMEs, consumers, environmental and social stakeholders, research and innovation (including open source) and public authorities (Article 17). Funding for them is **permissive only ("may")**: no fixed amount or percentage is set.
- **International:** standards bodies must use **international standards where they exist or are imminent** (ISO, IEC, ITU), unless the Commission bars it in a request as ineffective or inappropriate (Article 35). A request may limit participation to entities established in the Union **not controlled by a third country**.
- **Product laws: NOT amended.** No operative article names the Cyber Resilience Act, the AI Act, the Digital Product Passport (Ecodesign Regulation) or the Construction Products Regulation, and there is **no transitional clause for standards already requested under them**. They appear only in recitals and the explanatory memorandum (standards underpin the AI Act and the CRA; the proposal "will help in accelerating the development of standards in these areas").
- **Penalties:** none. Enforcement works through key performance indicators (Article 13), possible withdrawal of a body's recognition, fallback to other standards bodies or common specifications, and a safeguard procedure for deficient standards (Article 33).

## Why it matters
Harmonised standards give businesses a **presumption of conformity** with product laws (CRA, AI Act, ESPR/DPP, Construction Products Regulation, radio equipment and others). Delays in standards leave companies without a clear route to compliance; the proposal tries to shorten that gap without ending the public-private model run by CEN, CENELEC and ETSI.

## Main articles (as proposed)
- **Article 2:** a new term, **"referenced standard"**: a harmonised European standard, a harmonised standardisation deliverable, or a standard in support of Union legislation or policies. A **common specification** is a technical specification adopted by the Commission that gives a presumption of conformity.
- **Articles 3-4:** principles (openness, transparency, consensus, technological neutrality, inclusiveness); participant lists published per requested standard.
- **Article 6-7:** use of digital tools and machine-readable formats (the bodies "shall endeavour"); the Commission builds an **EU Standards Platform** (referenced standards, common specifications, access information, request status), planned for 2028 or 2029 in the financial statement.
- **Article 9:** an annual Union work programme, draft published with a one-month comment window.
- **Articles 10-16:** recognition of the European organisations (Annex I, amended by delegated act); national standardisation bodies appointed by Member States, at least one per Member State in each European organisation; only national bodies' representatives vote; annual KPIs on speed, quality, inclusiveness, cooperation and international influence (targets set by implementing act); annual reports due 31 January.
- **Article 22-25:** the Commission consults the European organisations first (reply within **one month**); requests by implementing act; the Commission "shall endeavour" to adopt a request within 12 months of the organisations' reply; it assesses delivered standards within **six months** and "shall endeavour" to publish the reference within **three months** of a positive assessment; partial compliance allows a reference with restrictions.
- **Article 32:** common specifications by implementing act (examination procedure) only on defined conditions; repealed or amended once a covering standard is referenced.
- **Article 33:** safeguard: a Member State, the Parliament or the Commission can flag a deficient standard; no objection within one month means the Commission decides within six months, an objection within twelve.
- **Article 41-42:** report on implementation five years after entry into force and every five years; **Regulation 1025/2012 repealed**; Article 48 of the General Product Safety Regulation repealed.

## Impact assessment (summary)
Options: (1) modernised monopoly of the European organisations; (2) **European-organisation-centred system with flexibilities** (preferred): alternative standards bodies, free-access repository, Centre of Excellence, targeted divergence from international standards; (3) an open Commission-managed system. Quantified benefits, verbatim: about **EUR 295 million a year** in compliance savings, of which **EUR 52 million** from alternative standards bodies, plus **EUR 30 million a year** from free access; SMEs would capture **78%** (EUR 253 million a year). Costs: Commission **EUR 1.5 million a year** (7.5 extra FTE); national bodies about EUR 2.9 million a year.
**Figures that do not agree inside the package** (say so if asked): the Centre and budget lines differ between the explanatory memorandum (EUR 183 million for 2028-2034) and the financial statement (EUR 177.575 million); the national-body inclusiveness cost is EUR 1.9 million in the memorandum and EUR 2.9 million in the impact assessment; the financial statement cites the KPI article as Article 11 although it is Article 13.

## FAQ
**Is the Standardisation Regulation already in force?** No. It is a proposal adopted on 6 October 2026; Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 stays in force until a new regulation is adopted and applies.
**Does it change my harmonised standard under the CRA or the AI Act?** The text does not amend those laws or add a transition for them. Check each law's own standardisation request.
**What is a common specification?** A technical specification adopted by the Commission as fallback when no timely harmonised standard exists; it gives a presumption of conformity.
**Is access to standards free?** The proposal requires free online access to referenced standards (Article 5).
**What is the Expert Centre?** The press release's name for the Centre of Excellence on European Standardisation (Article 38), inside the Commission.
