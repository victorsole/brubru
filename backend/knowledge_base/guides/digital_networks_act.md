## Overview

The Digital Networks Act is the most ambitious overhaul of EU telecoms regulation in over 20 years. Published on 21 January 2026, it replaces the Directive-based European Electronic Communications Code (EECC) with a directly applicable Regulation, ensuring uniform rules across all 27 Member States. The proposal addresses five core areas: copper-to-fibre transition, spectrum harmonisation, satellite authorisation, network resilience/cybersecurity, and market governance.

## Main Provisions

### 1. Copper Switch-Off (mandatory by 2035)
- Structured transition from legacy copper to fibre-to-the-home (FTTH)
- Before 31 December 2035: Member States must require copper switch-off in designated areas where two conditions are met: (i) at least 95% of premises passed by fibre, (ii) affordable retail fibre services available
- After 2035: switch-off in all remaining areas, completed by end of 2039
- Consumer safeguards: Member States must inform end users about timeline and alternative services
- Spain and Norway have already switched off copper networks

### 2. Spectrum Harmonisation
- Spectrum usage rights granted for **unlimited duration by default**, subject to regular reviews
- Limited-duration licences: minimum **40 years** for harmonised bands
- Commission adopts EU-level spectrum strategy and **binding roadmaps**
- **Mandatory spectrum single market procedure** replaces voluntary peer review
- Commission gains **veto power** on national draft spectrum measures
- Spectrum sharing promoted as default under "use it or share it" principle
- Commission may set binding recommendations on spectrum fees and auction reserve prices

### 3. Satellite Authorisation (centralised EU regime)
- Single EU-level authorisation for satellite networks and services
- Commission manages all stages: allocation, licensing, enforcement
- Supported by Radio Spectrum Policy Body (RSPB) and Office for Digital Networks (ODN)
- Authorisations granted for **unlimited duration** in principle
- Commission may limit authorisations in bands with spectrum scarcity
- **Fines up to 5% of annual global turnover** for breaches
- Shared spectrum between terrestrial and satellite only with primary rights holder agreement

### 4. Network Resilience and Cybersecurity
- Part II of the DNA: dedicated resilience and preparedness framework
- Obligations to ensure networks remain operational during crises, natural disasters, force majeure
- Emergency communications and public warning systems continuity
- BEREC adopts "Union preparedness plan" for digital infrastructures
- Aligned with proposed Cybersecurity Act revision (CSA2, published 20 January 2026)
- **High-risk supplier (HRS) phase-out**: Commission can designate and restrict ICT components from HRS for mobile, fixed, and satellite networks
- Mobile networks (5G): **3 years** to phase out HRS components from critical network
- Estimated cost of removing Huawei/ZTE equipment: EUR 3.4-4.3 billion/year over 3 years (approx. EUR 0.25 cents/month per mobile user)

### 5. Big Tech Contribution / Ecosystem Conciliation
- **Voluntary conciliation mechanism** (not mandatory "fair share" payments)
- Telecom operators and other digital ecosystem undertakings (e.g. large traffic generators) can request NRA-organised conciliation meetings
- Disputes over IP interconnection or commercial arrangements addressed through this facility
- BEREC adopts guidelines on ecosystem cooperation
- Commission reviews mechanism 3 years after DNA becomes applicable

### 6. Net Neutrality
- Principles retained: equal and non-discriminatory treatment of internet traffic
- Commission empowered to adopt implementing acts on conditions for "optimised services" (network slicing) for business-to-business industrial applications
- General internet access quality must not be degraded
- BEREC retains role in open internet guidelines

### 7. Universal Service Obligation (USO)
- Streamlined: affordable (i) adequate internet access and (ii) voice communications at fixed location
- Legacy services removed: payphones, public directories
- BEREC defines "adequate broadband" within 12 months of DNA entering into force
- Financing: no sector-specific designation, relies on general State aid rules (SGEI)
- Commission reviews USO scope by 30 June 2034

### 8. EU Single-Passport General Authorisation
- Replaces 27 national authorisation processes with single-passport procedure
- Providers notify one NRA, which forwards to ODN, which notifies all relevant NRAs
- Fully harmonised conditions across all Member States ("maximum list" approach)
- Covers all providers except satellite systems (separate EU-level regime)

### 9. Market Analysis and SMP Regulation
- Three-criteria test for significant market power (SMP) preserved
- New **sequential approach to remedies**: NRAs must first check existing regulation, competition law, and commercial agreements before imposing ex ante remedies
- **EU-harmonised access product** as default remedy across all Member States (FTTH unbundling, virtual unbundled local access)
- Commission veto expanded to cover SMP designation and regulatory remedies
- Symmetric regulation: identical access rules for all operators regardless of market power where justified
- In copper switch-off areas, fibre operators must connect nearby households on end-user request

### 10. Governance Reform
- Radio Spectrum Policy Group (RSPG) elevated to **Radio Spectrum Policy Body (RSPB)**
- BEREC office renamed **Office for Digital Networks (ODN)**
- ODN functions: central notification database, geolocation database for spectrum sharing, fraud intelligence, annual single market reports
- ODN funded by: satellite spectrum authorisation fees, EU numbering authorisation fees, Member State contributions
- BEREC mandate broadened to cover resilience, spectrum, and satellite matters

## End-User Protection
- Maximum harmonisation for contracts, bundles, switching
- Coordinated fight against fraud: ODN collects cross-border fraud information
- BEREC issues anti-fraud guidelines, including blocking specific numbers
- ePrivacy provisions on calling line identification and malicious call blocking incorporated directly into DNA
- Emergency services access via number-independent services (e.g. WhatsApp) now mandated

## Parliament's Prior Position

EP report on European technological sovereignty supports:
- Simplified, harmonised telecoms framework via the DNA
- Copper switch-off: "attainable and planned" transition, providers prepared logistically and financially
- Mobile: remove administrative barriers, support 5G/6G public-private investment
- Spectrum: enhanced coordination, longer licences, 6GHz band for 6G
- Satellite: common licence conditions, clear rules for satellite constellations

## Council's Prior Position

- No specific copper switch-off deadline; Member State specificities must be considered
- Spectrum: national competence, proposals must not create delays for spectrum awards
- More open to EU-level satellite authorisation but taking national circumstances into account
- Network cost contribution: market functions well, thorough impact assessment needed before intervention
