# Digital Networks Act

## QUICK FACTS
Full name: Proposal for a Regulation establishing a framework for digital networks (Digital Networks Act)
Status: Commission proposal (expected Q2 2026)
Type: Regulation (directly applicable)
Legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (internal market)
Context: Part of the Competitiveness Compass and Single Market Strategy
Responsible Commissioner: Henna Virkkunen (Tech Sovereignty, Security, Democracy)
Responsible DG: DG CNECT (Communications Networks, Content and Technology)
EPRS briefing: "Digital Networks Act legislation" (19 March 2026, EU Legislation in Progress, by Stefano De Luca)
Current framework: European Electronic Communications Code (Directive (EU) 2018/1972, CELEX: 32018L1972)
Replaces/amends: Consolidates and harmonises the EECC framework into a directly applicable Regulation

## Overview

The proposed Digital Networks Act seeks to establish a more integrated and harmonised EU framework for electronic communications, in support of the EU's digital transformation and competitiveness goals. It moves from the current Directive-based approach (European Electronic Communications Code, transposed differently across 27 Member States) to a directly applicable Regulation.

## Key Policy Areas

### 1. Infrastructure Networks
- Harmonised rules for deployment and maintenance of fixed and mobile network infrastructure
- Streamlined permitting for network rollout (fibre, 5G, future 6G)
- Cross-border coordination for transnational infrastructure projects

### 2. Spectrum Management
- More harmonised approach to radio spectrum allocation across Member States
- Longer licence durations and coordinated renewal procedures
- Spectrum sharing mechanisms for more efficient use

### 3. Satellite Authorisation
- Simplified EU-wide authorisation for satellite communication services
- Supports the EU's connectivity ambitions (IRIS2 secure satellite constellation)

### 4. Network Resilience and Cybersecurity
- Strengthened resilience requirements for critical network infrastructure
- Alignment with NIS2 Directive obligations
- Enhanced cross-border incident response

### 5. Consumer Protection
- Updated consumer rights for digital communication services
- Transparency on network quality and coverage
- Portability and switching provisions

### 6. Cross-Border Market Access
- Removal of barriers to cross-border provision of electronic communication services
- Single market principles applied to digital network operators

## Political Context

- The Draghi Competitiveness Report (September 2024) identified telecoms fragmentation as a drag on EU digital competitiveness
- The EU has 27 separate national regulators (NRAs) for telecoms, compared to a single FCC in the US
- European telecoms operators have called for consolidation and regulatory simplification
- The proposal is part of the broader "One Europe, One Market" agenda discussed at the European Council (19-20 March 2026)
