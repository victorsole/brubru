## Overview

The Critical Raw Materials Act is the EU's framework law for securing the supply of the non-energy, non-agricultural raw materials that underpin the green transition, the digital transition, and defence and aerospace applications. Its general objective, set out in Article 1(1), is to improve the functioning of the internal market by establishing a framework to ensure the Union's access to a secure, resilient and sustainable supply of critical raw materials, including by fostering efficiency and circularity throughout the value chain.

The CRMA does this through three strands of measures (Article 1(2)):

1. **Lowering the risk of supply disruptions** - by identifying and supporting Strategic Projects that increase Union extraction, processing and recycling capacity and diversify imports, and by incentivising technological progress and resource efficiency to moderate demand growth.
2. **Improving the Union's ability to monitor and mitigate supply risk** - through a supply-risk monitoring dashboard, periodic stress tests, coordination of Member States' strategic stocks, and mandatory risk assessments for large companies that manufacture strategic technologies.
3. **Ensuring free movement while raising circularity and sustainability** - free movement of compliant critical raw materials and products containing them, alongside new circularity duties (notably for permanent magnets) and a framework for recognising sustainability certification schemes and declaring environmental footprint.

**Brubru deep-dive explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers):** https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2024-1252_crma/index.html

The CRMA sits alongside the Chips Act and the Net-Zero Industry Act in the EU's open strategic autonomy agenda, and grew out of the Commission's 2020 Action Plan on Critical Raw Materials (recital 64) and the 2021 EU principles for sustainable raw materials (recital 17). Unlike ESPR or the Batteries Regulation, the CRMA regulates the upstream supply chain (extraction, processing, recycling, stockpiling, monitoring) rather than end-products; its main product-facing rules are narrow and concern permanent-magnet labelling, recycled content and critical-raw-material environmental footprint declarations (Articles 28 to 34).

## Strategic versus critical raw materials (Annexes I and II)

The CRMA works with two overlapping lists, both maintained by the Commission via delegated act (Articles 3 and 4) and reviewed by 24 May 2027 and at least every three years thereafter, or at any time on a reasoned request from the European Critical Raw Materials Board following monitoring or stress testing (Article 3(3), Article 4(3)):

- **Strategic raw materials** (Annex I, Section 1) are raw materials of high strategic importance for strategic technologies underpinning the green and digital transitions or defence/aerospace applications, characterised by a potentially significant gap between global supply and projected demand and by difficulty in increasing production (recital 6). Only strategic raw materials are subject to the Article 5 capacity and diversification benchmarks, the Strategic Project mechanism, joint purchasing, strategic stock reporting and company risk-assessment duties.
- **Critical raw materials** (Annex II, Section 1) is the wider list: it automatically includes every strategic raw material, plus any other raw material that reaches or exceeds the threshold of 1 for supply risk and 2.8 for economic importance, calculated per the Annex II, Section 2 methodology (Article 4(2)). The measures on the single point of contact, planning, exploration, monitoring, circularity and sustainability apply to all critical raw materials, not only the strategic subset (recital 7).

References to strategic and critical raw materials cover the entire value chain, including unprocessed form and at all processing stages, with an explicit clarification for the aluminium value chain (bauxite - alumina - aluminium) (recital 8). By-product raw materials (for example, those recovered from ferrous scrap) are eligible on the same basis as primary-extracted materials.

## The 2030 benchmarks (Article 5)

Article 5(1) sets out the Union-level capacity and diversification benchmarks that the Commission and Member States must work towards for strategic raw materials, to be approached or reached by 2030:

- **Extraction:** Union extraction capacity capable of extracting the ores, minerals or concentrates needed to produce **at least 10%** of the Union's annual consumption of strategic raw materials, to the extent possible given the Union's reserves (Article 5(1)(a)(i)).
- **Processing:** Union processing capacity, including all intermediate processing steps, capable of producing **at least 40%** of the Union's annual consumption of strategic raw materials (Article 5(1)(a)(ii)).
- **Recycling:** Union recycling capacity capable of producing **at least 25%** of the Union's annual consumption of strategic raw materials, and capable of recycling significantly increasing amounts of each strategic raw material from waste (Article 5(1)(a)(iii)).
- **Diversification (single-country dependency cap):** by 2030, no third country should account for **more than 65%** of the Union's annual consumption of any strategic raw material at any relevant stage of processing (Article 5(1)(b)).

By 1 January 2027 the Commission must adopt a delegated act supplementing this framework with **Union recycling capacity benchmarks** expressed as a share of the strategic raw materials available in specific waste streams, where sufficient data exists under the Batteries Regulation, the End-of-Life Vehicles Directive, the Waste Framework Directive or the WEEE Directive (Article 5(3)). Alongside the numerical benchmarks, the Commission and Member States must undertake efforts to incentivise technological progress and resource efficiency to moderate the expected increase in Union consumption of critical raw materials below a reference projection that the Commission must publish by 24 November 2026 (Article 5(2), Article 44(1)). Progress towards the benchmarks is monitored and reported by the Commission from 24 May 2027, at least every three years (Article 44(2)).

## Strategic Projects and streamlined permitting (Articles 6 to 14)

**Recognition criteria (Article 6):** the Commission recognises a raw material project as a Strategic Project - in the Union or in a third country/overseas country or territory (OCT) - if it makes a meaningful contribution to the security of the Union's supply of strategic raw materials, is technically feasible within a reasonable timeframe, is implemented sustainably (environmental and social impacts, human and labour rights, transparent business practices), and either delivers cross-border benefits beyond the Member State concerned (for projects in the Union) or is mutually beneficial to the Union and the third country (for projects in emerging markets/developing economies). Projects in extraction, processing or recycling of strategic raw materials, or in production of relevant substitute materials, all qualify.

**Application and decision process (Article 7):** project promoters apply to the Commission through a single template (due by 24 November 2024), on an open call with regular cut-off dates set at least four times a year (the first no later than 24 August 2024). The Commission notifies applicants within 30 days whether the application is complete, shares it with the Board, and - taking the Board's opinion into account - adopts its recognition decision within **90 days** of acknowledging completeness, extendable once by up to 90 days in exceptional cases (Article 7(9)-(10)). A Member State can veto recognition of a project on its own territory by objecting with reasons; a third-country government can likewise block recognition of a project on its territory.

**Priority status and permitting deadlines (Articles 10 to 12):** Strategic Projects in the Union are considered to serve the public interest or public health and safety for the purposes of the Habitats Directive, the Water Framework Directive and the Birds Directive, subject to the usual conditions in those instruments, and receive the highest national-significance status available under national law plus urgent handling in dispute-resolution and judicial procedures. The permit-granting process itself is capped at:

- **27 months for Strategic Projects involving extraction** (Article 11(1)(a)).
- **15 months for Strategic Projects involving only processing or recycling** (Article 11(1)(b)).
- Shorter derogated caps of **24 months (extraction)** and **12 months (processing/recycling)** apply where the project was already in a national permitting process before being recognised as Strategic, or where it is an extension of an already-permitted Strategic Project (Article 11(2)).
- Member States may extend these caps in exceptional cases by a maximum of **6 months (extraction)** or **3 months (processing/recycling)** (Article 11(4)).
- The time needed for the project promoter to prepare the environmental impact assessment report is excluded from these clocks (Article 11(8)).

**Reporting duty (Article 8):** every two years after recognition, project promoters must report to the Commission on implementation progress, delays and financing progress; recognition can be withdrawn if criteria are no longer met (Article 7(11)-(13)).

## Monitoring, stress tests and stockpiling (Articles 20 to 24)

**Monitoring dashboard (Article 20):** the Commission must monitor supply risk for critical raw materials - trade flows, demand/supply, supply concentration, production capacities, price volatility, and bottlenecks - and publish a continuously updated, free-access monitoring dashboard, supported by national authorities in the dedicated Board subgroup.

**Stress tests (Article 20(3)):** the Commission, working with Member States, must ensure a stress test is carried out for each strategic raw material supply chain **at least every three years**, or sooner if monitoring detects a significant increase in supply risk.

**Strategic stocks (Articles 22 to 23):** Member States must report annually (from 24 May 2026, per Article 45) on the state of their strategic stocks - levels, five-year trend, and release/allocation rules - though the Regulation explicitly does not create any obligation on Member States or companies to hold or release strategic stocks (Article 23(8)). Every two years from 24 May 2026, the Commission shares with the Board a draft benchmark for a safe level of Union strategic stocks and a comparison against existing stocks, and may issue non-binding opinions to Member States on increasing convergence (Article 23(1)-(3)).

**Company risk preparedness (Article 24):** by 24 May 2025, and within 12 months of any update to the strategic raw materials list, Member States must identify the **large companies** (more than 500 employees and net worldwide turnover above EUR 150 million, per the Article 2(29) definition) manufacturing strategic technologies on their territory that use strategic raw materials - covering batteries, hydrogen equipment, renewable energy generation, aircraft, traction motors, heat pumps, data transmission/storage equipment, mobile devices, additive manufacturing, robotics, drones, rocket launchers, satellites and advanced chips. Those large companies must, **at least every three years**, carry out a supply-chain risk assessment (mapping origins, analysing supply-affecting factors, assessing vulnerabilities) and take mitigation efforts where significant vulnerabilities are found. Member States may require the resulting report to be presented to the company's board of directors, but the report itself is not made public.

## Circularity and recycling of critical raw materials (Articles 26 to 29)

**National circularity programmes (Article 26):** within two years of the Commission's implementing act on recovery-potential products/waste streams (due by 24 May 2025), each Member State must adopt national programmes to moderate demand, promote reuse/repair, increase collection and sorting of high-recovery-potential waste (including metal scraps), increase use of secondary critical raw materials, and support skills for the circular value chain.

**Extractive waste recovery (Article 27):** operators of extractive waste facilities must submit, by **24 November 2026**, a preliminary economic assessment study on the potential recovery of critical raw materials from stored and newly generated extractive waste, unless they can demonstrate no technically recoverable critical raw materials are present. Member States must set up a database of closed (including abandoned) extractive waste facilities by 24 November 2026, populated with quantities/concentrations data by 24 May 2027, to surface the recovery potential of historic mining sites.

**Permanent magnets (Articles 28 to 29):** the CRMA's most product-facing circularity rules target permanent magnets in magnetic resonance imaging devices, wind energy generators, industrial robots, motor vehicles, light means of transport, cooling generators, heat pumps, electric motors, automatic washing machines, tumble driers, microwaves, vacuum cleaners and dishwashers.
- From two years after the entry into force of the Commission's labelling-format implementing act (due 24 November 2026), products in scope must be labelled to disclose whether they incorporate permanent magnets and, if so, of which type (neodymium-iron-boron, samarium-cobalt, aluminium-nickel-cobalt or ferrite) (Article 28).
- The same products must carry a data carrier giving access to information on the magnets' weight, location, chemical composition, coatings/glues/additives, and safe-removal instructions, kept available for the product's typical lifetime plus 10 years (Article 28(4), (7)).
- By **24 May 2027** (or two years from the relevant delegated act, whichever is later), for magnets exceeding 0.2 kg total weight, operators must publicly disclose the share of neodymium, dysprosium, praseodymium, terbium, boron, samarium, nickel and cobalt recovered from post-consumer waste present in the magnet (Article 29(1)). A subsequent delegated act, due by 31 December 2031 at the latest, will set binding **minimum recycled-content shares** (Article 29(3)).
- Products designed primarily for defence or space applications are exempt (Article 28(9), Article 29(5)).

## Joint purchasing (Article 25)

The Commission must set up and operate a demand-aggregation system, modelled on the gas joint-purchasing precedent (Council Regulation (EU) 2022/2576), allowing interested Union undertakings consuming strategic raw materials (unprocessed or processed) to pool demand and seek matching offers from suppliers. Before launching the system for any given strategic raw material, the Commission must assess, after consulting the Board, the likely market impact to avoid disproportionately affecting internal-market competition (Article 25(2)). Participating undertakings may jointly negotiate prices and terms, subject to Union competition law; entities targeted by Article 215 TFEU restrictive measures are excluded from participation (Article 25(5)-(6)). The Commission contracts a service provider to run the system, with full audit access and EU-based data storage (Article 25(7)-(10)).

## Sustainability and recognised schemes (Articles 30 to 31)

**Recognised certification schemes (Article 30):** governments, industry associations and other scheme owners can apply for Commission recognition of their sustainability certification scheme against the criteria in Annex IV, covering value-chain stages, project life-cycle stages and sustainability dimensions/environmental risk categories. Recognition is reassessed at least every three years, and can be withdrawn if deficiencies are not remedied within up to 12 months. Participation in a recognised scheme is one of the evidentiary routes a Strategic Project applicant can rely on to demonstrate sustainable implementation (recital 59). The Commission maintains a public register of recognised schemes.

**Environmental footprint declaration (Article 31):** the Commission may, following a prioritisation report due by 24 November 2026 and a necessity/proportionality assessment for each prioritised material, adopt delegated acts setting calculation and verification rules for the environmental footprint of specific critical raw materials, identifying at least three relevant impact categories including greenhouse gas emissions. Once such rules exist for a given critical raw material type, anyone placing that raw material (unprocessed, processed or recycled - but not as incorporated in intermediate/final products) on the market must make available a free-access environmental footprint declaration, including the extraction/processing/recycling country and region, the calculated footprint, and the performance class the material corresponds to once performance classes are established by a further delegated act (Article 31(6)-(8)).

## Governance: the CRMA Board (Articles 35 to 37)

The **European Critical Raw Materials Board** is established by Article 35 to advise the Commission and carry out the tasks assigned to it throughout the Regulation. It is chaired by the Commission, composed of one high-level representative per Member State (only Member States have voting rights, one vote each), with the European Parliament invited as an observer and industry, SMEs, civil society, academia, trade unions, local/regional authorities, third countries, OCTs and relevant EU agencies invitable as observers on a case-by-case basis (Article 36). The Board meets on a minimum statutory cadence:

- **Every three months** to assess Strategic Project applications (Article 36(5)(a)).
- **Every six months** to develop monitoring under Chapter 4 (Article 36(5)(b)).
- **At least once a year** to discuss exploration-obligation progress (Article 36(5)(c)).

The Board must establish at least six **standing subgroups**: financing of Strategic Projects, public knowledge/acceptance, national exploration-programme coordination, circularity/resource efficiency/substitution, monitoring and stress-testing support, and strategic-stock coordination (Article 36(8)). Separately, the Board periodically discusses **Strategic Partnerships** with third countries - their contribution to security of supply, consistency with Member States' bilateral cooperation, and criteria for prioritising future partners - without prejudice to the Council's Treaty prerogatives over international agreements (Article 37).

## Delegated and implementing powers

The delegations under Articles 3(2), 4(2), 5(3), 6(2), 28(12), 29(2)-(3), 31(1) and (8), and 34(1) run for **eight years from 24 June 2024**, tacitly renewable unless Parliament or Council object at least three months before expiry; either institution can revoke at any time, and either can object within two months (extendable by two more) of notification of an individual delegated act (Article 38). Comitology implementing acts (single-application templates, recognition criteria for certification schemes, reporting formats, permanent-magnet labelling format) run through either the advisory procedure or the examination procedure depending on the empowering Article, per Article 39 and Regulation (EU) No 182/2011.

## Penalties and evaluation

Member States must lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties for infringements by **24 November 2026**, and notify the Commission of those rules (Article 47). The Commission must evaluate the whole Regulation and report to Parliament, Council and the European Economic and Social Committee by **24 May 2028**, covering, among other things, the case for maximum environmental-footprint thresholds, 2040/2050 benchmarks, consistency with EU environmental law given the Strategic Project public-interest status, and the joint-purchasing system's competition impact (Article 48).

## Glossary

- **Critical raw material** - a raw material on the Annex II list: automatically includes every strategic raw material, plus any raw material meeting the Annex II, Section 2 supply-risk and economic-importance thresholds (Article 4).
- **Strategic raw material** - a raw material on the narrower Annex I list, selected for its strategic importance to green/digital/defence/aerospace technologies, high forecast demand growth, and difficulty of increasing production (Article 3).
- **Strategic Project** - a raw material project (in the Union, a third country or an OCT) formally recognised by the Commission as meeting the Article 6 criteria, unlocking streamlined permitting, financing support and public-interest status (Articles 6-8).
- **Benchmark** - the 2030 Union-level capacity (extraction/processing/recycling) and diversification (single-country dependency) targets set in Article 5.
- **Single point of contact** - the authority or authorities each Member State must establish or designate by 24 February 2025 to coordinate the entire permit-granting process for a critical raw material project (Article 9).
- **Permit-granting process** - the full sequence of permits, assessments and authorisations for a critical raw material project, running from acknowledgement of a complete application to the comprehensive decision (Article 2(18)).
- **Joint purchasing / demand aggregation** - the Commission-run system letting undertakings pool demand for strategic raw materials and match it against supplier offers (Article 25).
- **Stress test** - the Commission's periodic assessment of a strategic raw material supply chain's vulnerability to disruption, covering extraction/processing/recycling location, market structure, and diversification options (Article 20(3)).
- **Recognised scheme** - a sustainability certification scheme for critical raw materials formally recognised by the Commission against the Annex IV criteria (Article 30).
- **Strategic stock** - a quantity of raw material stored by a public or private operator to be released in the event of a supply disruption; reporting on strategic stocks is mandatory, holding or releasing them is not (Article 2(28), Articles 22-23).
- **Large company** - for the purposes of Article 24, a company with more than 500 employees on average and net worldwide turnover exceeding EUR 150 million in its most recent financial statements (Article 2(29)).
- **Strategic Partnership** - a non-binding commitment between the Union and a third country or OCT to cooperate on the raw materials value chain (Article 2(63)).
