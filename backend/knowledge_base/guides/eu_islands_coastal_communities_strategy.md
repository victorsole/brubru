## Overview

On 10 June 2026 the Commission adopted its first-ever pair of dedicated strategies for islands and coastal communities — a long-standing ask of insular and coastal regions, anchored in the Article 174 TFEU commitment to territorial cohesion. Together the two Communications cover a large share of the Union's people and geography: roughly 17 million islanders across more than 4,000 inhabited islands in 16 Member States, and 95 million people (21% of the EU population) living on or near the 70,000 km of EU coastline across 22 Member States.

The strategies were formally presented on 26 June 2026 in Cyprus — itself one of the three island Member States, alongside Ireland and Malta — in cooperation with the Cyprus Council Presidency, with the coastal dimension showcased in Paphos.

## What the strategies do

The Islands Strategy is built around four pillars — economic development; connectivity; competitiveness and innovation; and governance as a horizontal enabler. The economic ambition is centred on a dynamic, competitive and diversified **blue economy**: new business models such as pescatourism, the bioeconomy and offshore renewable energy, with an emphasis on innovation and high-quality jobs. The Coastal Communities Strategy applies a parallel logic to the wider coastline.

Crucially, both are **delivery-oriented but non-binding**. Rather than create new legislation, they invite Member States to embed targeted island and coastal measures in their **future National and Regional Partnership Plans** — the post-2027 cohesion-policy delivery instrument under negotiation as part of the MFF 2028-2034 (see `mff_2028_2034`). They also set up a regular dialogue between the EU institutions and island/coastal stakeholders, plus technical support (capacity building, exchange of good practices).

**Status caveat:** these are Commission Communications (strategies), not legislative acts. There is no COD/CNS procedure, rapporteur or CELEX for a "law" here; the operational follow-through will run through cohesion programming and any sectoral instruments the strategies trigger.
