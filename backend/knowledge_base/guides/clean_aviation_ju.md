# Clean Aviation Joint Undertaking and the Net-Zero Aviation R&I Track

## QUICK FACTS
- Body: Clean Aviation Joint Undertaking (Clean Aviation JU / CAJU), a European public-private partnership under Horizon Europe
- Legal basis: Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085 of 19 November 2021 establishing the Joint Undertakings under Horizon Europe (the "Single Basic Act", CELEX 32021R2085), Title II sets out Clean Aviation-specific provisions
- Lineage: Clean Sky (2008-2017, FP7) -> Clean Sky 2 (2014-2024, Horizon 2020) -> Clean Aviation (2022-2031, Horizon Europe)
- Budget: EUR 1.7 billion EU/Horizon Europe funding + no less than EUR 2.4 billion private/industry in-kind and cash contributions = EUR 4.1 billion aggregate over the 2021-2027 MFF period, activities running to end-2031
- Mission: integrate and demonstrate disruptive aircraft technologies to cut net greenhouse gas emissions, targeting up to 30% net CO2 reduction versus 2020-generation technology (rising toward up to 90% when combined with Sustainable Aviation Fuels, and towards zero in-flight CO2 for hydrogen combustion/fuel-cell propulsion), with entry-into-service of the resulting aircraft from 2035 and full civil fleet replacement by 2050
- Three technology thrusts: (1) ultra-efficient short-medium-range (SMR) aircraft, (2) hybrid-electric regional aircraft, (3) hydrogen-powered aircraft
- Two-phase programme: Phase 1 (2022 to approximately 2025/2026) matured technology building blocks; Phase 2 (from approximately 2025/2026 to 2030/2031) validates the most promising technologies at higher Technology Readiness Levels via major ground and flight demonstrators, run across successive competitive calls (Call 1-Call 4 launched to date). Exact phase-boundary and call dates vary slightly across Clean Aviation's own published materials -- verify against the live work programme before quoting a specific year.
- Confirmed private members/aerospace primes: Airbus SE, Dassault Aviation SA, GKN Aerospace, GE Avio S.r.l. (Avio Aero), Industria de Turbo Propulsores S.A.U. (ITP Aero), ATR G.I.E., Collins Aerospace Ireland, plus a wider base of SMEs, mid-caps and research institutions/universities (28 founding members at launch, growing associated-member base). Safran, Leonardo, Rolls-Royce and MTU are widely reported as participants in Clean Aviation-funded projects but their formal JU membership status should be checked against the live members list before citing.
- Governance: Governing Board (Commission + private members), Executive Director, Scientific Committee and States Representatives Group; administratively hosted alongside other Horizon Europe JUs in Brussels
- Adjacent EU instruments: ReFuelEU Aviation (SAF blending mandates), EU ETS for aviation (allowance auctioning), SESAR 3 JU (air traffic management digitalisation), EASA (certification pathway for novel propulsion), Alliance for Zero-Emission Aviation / AZEA Roadmap (DG DEFIS-convened industry alliance linking hydrogen/battery-electric deployment to Clean Aviation R&I outputs)
- Key sources: clean-aviation.eu/about-us/who-we-are, clean-aviation.eu, EUR-Lex CELEX 32021R2085

## Why This Matters for Brubru Users

Sustainable aviation policy has two distinct legs that are frequently conflated. The **regulatory leg** -- ReFuelEU Aviation's SAF blending mandates and the EU ETS aviation allowance phase-out -- sets binding obligations on today's fleet using today's fuel and airframe technology. The **research and innovation leg** -- Clean Aviation -- funds the aircraft and propulsion systems that do not exist yet: hydrogen-powered airframes, hybrid-electric regional aircraft, and ultra-efficient short-medium-range designs targeting entry-into-service from 2035. Brubru's `aviation_transport_policy` guide is policy-shaped and covers the regulatory leg in depth (ReFuelEU, ETS, EU261, Single European Sky). This guide fills the R&I gap: it is where hydrogen aircraft demonstrators, ultra-efficient wing/engine architectures, and the Horizon Europe funding vehicle behind them live. Anyone asking about long-term aviation decarbonisation technology roadmaps, EU aerospace R&I funding, or hydrogen/hybrid-electric aircraft programmes should be routed here rather than to the regulatory guide.

## What Clean Aviation Is

Clean Aviation JU is one of nine institutionalised European Partnerships (Joint Undertakings) established by the Single Basic Act, Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085 (CELEX 32021R2085), adopted 19 November 2021. Joint Undertakings are EU bodies with legal personality that pool Commission/Horizon Europe funding with binding industry co-investment commitments to de-risk pre-competitive research in strategic sectors. Clean Aviation sits within Horizon Europe Pillar II, Cluster 5 (Climate, Energy and Mobility).

It succeeds two earlier aeronautics research partnerships:
- **Clean Sky** (2008-2017, funded under FP7) -- first-generation green aircraft technology demonstrators
- **Clean Sky 2** (2014-2024, funded under Horizon 2020) -- larger-scale integrated technology demonstrators, overlapping in transition with Clean Aviation's launch
- **Clean Aviation** (2022-2031, funded under Horizon Europe) -- the current programme, explicitly reoriented around the EU's 2050 climate-neutrality target and the aviation sector's "Destination 2050" industry roadmap

## Budget and Structure

Clean Aviation's aggregate resources are approximately EUR 4.1 billion: EUR 1.7 billion in EU/Horizon Europe public funding matched by no less than EUR 2.4 billion in private-sector contributions (cash and in-kind) from members. This 40/60 public/private split is the standard co-investment ratio used across Horizon Europe institutionalised partnerships. Governance runs through a Governing Board with Commission and private-member representation, supported by an Executive Director's office, a Scientific Committee providing independent technical advice, and a States Representatives Group giving Member States an advisory voice.

## Mission and CO2 Target

Clean Aviation's stated mission is to integrate and demonstrate disruptive aircraft technologies that decrease net greenhouse gas emissions by no less than 30% compared to 2020-generation technology, as a stepping stone toward the sector's 2050 climate-neutrality goal (up to 90% reduction achievable in combination with Sustainable Aviation Fuels; near-zero in-flight CO2 for hydrogen-fuelled propulsion). The programme frames success as enabling 75% of the world's civil aviation fleet to be replaced with next-generation aircraft by 2050, with the first disruptive products reaching entry-into-service around 2035.

## Three Technology Thrusts

1. **Ultra-efficient short-medium-range (SMR) aircraft** -- new wing, engine and airframe architectures for the single-aisle segment that dominates EU intra-European traffic, aiming for step-change fuel-burn reductions ahead of a 2035 entry-into-service.
2. **Hybrid-electric regional aircraft** -- an advanced thermal engine coupled to an electric motor/generator, targeting at least 20% CO2 reduction at aircraft level for the regional segment; for a 2035 entry-into-service, hybridisation is expected to rely on battery electric-energy storage, with fuel-cell-based concepts developed under the hydrogen thrust instead.
3. **Hydrogen-powered aircraft** -- concepts combining hybrid-electric propulsion with hydrogen (fuel cells or direct H2 combustion) as the power source, targeting up to 90% lower emissions while meeting ICAO noise rules; matured through Phase 2 ground and flight demonstrators.

Flagship demonstrator projects funded under these thrusts include HERACLES and DEMETRA (hybrid-electric regional propulsion) and FASTER-H2 (hydrogen-powered short/medium-haul propulsion).

## Two-Phase Programme

- **Phase 1** (2022 onward, technology building blocks): 28 projects launched with more than EUR 800 million of EU funding, maturing core technology bricks (structures, propulsion sub-systems, systems architecture) at lower Technology Readiness Levels.
- **Phase 2** (from around 2025/2026, technology validation): validates the most promising Phase 1 technologies at higher Technology Readiness Level via major ground and flight demonstrators, delivered through successive competitive calls for proposals (multiple calls launched, including hybrid-electric ground demonstrator projects). Published Clean Aviation materials are not fully consistent on the exact Phase 1/Phase 2 boundary year (2025 vs 2026) -- check the live work programme and budget document before citing a specific transition date.

## Members

Clean Aviation counts a founding cohort of 28 private members at launch (aerospace primes, mid-caps, SMEs and research institutions/universities), since expanded through waves of associated members. Confirmed on Clean Aviation's own members listing: Airbus SE, Dassault Aviation SA, GKN Aerospace, GE Avio S.r.l. (Avio Aero), Industria de Turbo Propulsores S.A.U. (ITP Aero), ATR G.I.E. and Collins Aerospace Ireland, alongside national aerospace research centres (for example CIRA in Italy) and a broad base of specialised SMEs across Member States. Other major European aeroengine and systems manufacturers are widely reported as active in Clean Aviation-funded consortia; their formal JU membership tier should be verified against the current members list before being asserted as fact.

## Adjacent EU Instruments

- **ReFuelEU Aviation** (Regulation (EU) 2023/2405) -- the regulatory-leg SAF blending mandate that Clean Aviation's technology outputs (more fuel-efficient and hydrogen-capable airframes) are designed to complement, not substitute for
- **EU ETS for aviation** -- carbon-pricing leg driving near-term airline behaviour while Clean Aviation funds the long-term technology shift
- **SESAR 3 Joint Undertaking** -- the sister Horizon Europe partnership for air traffic management modernisation and digitalisation of European airspace (Single European Sky digital layer); aircraft-side (Clean Aviation) and airspace-side (SESAR 3) partnerships are designed to be complementary
- **EASA** -- certification authority whose airworthiness and novel-propulsion certification pathways determine whether Clean Aviation demonstrator technologies can actually enter commercial service by the 2035 target
- **Alliance for Zero-Emission Aviation (AZEA)** -- DG DEFIS-convened industry alliance and roadmap (published 20 April 2026) that links Clean Aviation's hydrogen and hybrid-electric R&I outputs to a commercial deployment timeline, plus dual-use defence-innovation and Industrial Accelerator Act considerations
- **CountEmissionsEU** -- harmonised transport-emissions measurement methodology (COM(2023)441) relevant once Clean Aviation aircraft enter service and need standardised emissions accounting
