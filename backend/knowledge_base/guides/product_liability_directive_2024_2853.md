# Product Liability Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/2853)

## QUICK FACTS

- Full name: Directive (EU) 2024/2853 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2024 on liability for defective products and repealing Council Directive 85/374/EEC
- CELEX: 32024L2853
- OJ reference: OJ L, 2024/2853, 18.11.2024
- Repeals: Council Directive 85/374/EEC (the original Product Liability Directive of 1985)
- Type: Directive (Member-State transposition required)
- Procedure: 2022/0302(COD), ordinary legislative procedure
- Commission proposal: COM(2022) 495, 28 September 2022
- Lead DG: DG JUST (Justice and Consumers)
- Responsible Commissioner (proposal): Didier Reynders (Justice 2019-2024); current Commissioner: Michael McGrath (Justice and Consumers, Ireland)
- EP lead committees: IMCO + JURI
- Transposition deadline: 9 December 2026
- Application date: applies to products placed on the market or put into service from 9 December 2026 onwards (existing claims continue under the 1985 Directive)
- **CORRIGENDUM (7 May 2026)**: OJ:L_202690364 -- corrigendum to Directive (EU) 2024/2853 published in the Official Journal -- technical corrections to the consolidated text. Cross-reference any compliance work to the consolidated post-corrigendum text.

## What changed vs the 1985 Directive

The 2024/2853 recast modernises EU product-liability rules to fit the digital age. Headline changes:

1. **Software is a product** -- the new directive explicitly covers software (including AI systems, embedded software, firmware) as a "product" subject to no-fault liability. The 1985 Directive's product definition (movables) is now widened.
2. **AI and machine-learning** -- if an AI system causes damage that is the result of a defect (e.g. a self-driving system, an AI medical-diagnosis tool, a financial-advice agent), the producer is liable on a no-fault basis. This complements the (separate) AI Liability Directive proposal track and the AI Act.
3. **Digital services** -- related digital services that are integrated with a product (e.g. cloud connectivity for an IoT device, OTA software updates) are part of the product for liability purposes.
4. **Cybersecurity defects** -- a product is defective if it fails to meet safety expectations under cybersecurity-related EU law (e.g. RED Cyber, Cyber Resilience Act once applicable).
5. **Substantially modified products** -- if a third party substantially modifies a product (including via firmware update), they can be held liable as a producer.
6. **Disclosure of evidence** -- claimants can ask courts to order producers to disclose internal documentation about the product (subject to safeguards for trade secrets).
7. **Burden of proof eased** -- in technically complex cases (notably AI), the burden of proof is alleviated where claimants face excessive difficulty proving the defect.
8. **Loss of data** -- non-material damage including loss of data is now compensable.
9. **Liability cap removed** -- no upper limit on per-product liability; the 70-million-euro Member-State cap of 85/374/EEC is gone.
10. **Threshold removed** -- the 500-euro lower threshold of 85/374/EEC is gone; small claims are admissible.

## Why the corrigendum matters

The 7 May 2026 corrigendum (OJ:L_202690364) corrects technical errors in the consolidated text published in OJ L, 2024/2853, 18.11.2024. Member States preparing transposition (deadline 9 December 2026) and producers preparing internal compliance must work from the **consolidated post-corrigendum text** -- not the original publication. The corrigendum does not change the substantive scope of the Directive.
