# Attacks on Commercial Shipping in the Black Sea: What the EU Has Said (6-22 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **UPDATE (Thursday 22 October 2026): the European Parliament adopted resolution 2026/2933(RSP), P10_TA(2026)0365, by 432 votes to 52 with 88 abstentions.** Parliament, NOT the Commission or the Council, holds Russia responsible for the deadly strikes on civilian merchant vessels off Bulgaria and Romania and for repeated violations of Romanian airspace, and reads them as a pattern of escalation of the war against Ukraine into the western Black Sea. Parliament asks the EU and its Member States to **consider deploying an EU naval operation in the Black Sea**, asks NATO to consider a Black Sea initiative, asks for more EU support to Romania and Bulgaria for early detection and tracking of hostile drones, commends the Romanian and Bulgarian rescue services, and criticises the Bulgarian government's "ambiguous handling" and "weak diplomatic response", calling on Prime Minister Rumen Radev to act and to stop delaying Bulgaria's participation in NATO's anti-drone initiative. The Commission's own line of 6 October (circumstances still being established) has not been updated in a formal statement: the attribution to Russia is Parliament's. Source: EP press release of 22 October 2026 (20261018IPR48267); the resolution text was not yet available in English when written.
- **What the EU said (all on 6 October 2026, official social-media posts, NOT a formal Commission or Council statement):** Commission President von der Leyen: "The situation in the Black Sea is increasingly concerning. Commercial ships and innocent crews are being targeted, with lives already lost at sea. Today, two commercial vessels were hit in **Bulgaria's economic zone** in Black Sea." Then: "This comes just one day after a Turkish ship carrying Ukrainian grain was struck and sunk off the Romanian coast. While exact circumstances of these incidents are still being established, the developments are very concerning." And: "Attacks on civilian shipping are unacceptable. We stand in full solidarity with our Member States on the Eastern flank." Commissioner for Agriculture and Food **Christophe Hansen** (the current Agriculture Commissioner, in office since December 2024): "Weaponising food and disrupting agricultural trade has consequences far beyond the battlefield: putting pressure on food prices and availability, and the risk of pushing millions more into food insecurity."
- **What the EU has NOT said:** no vessel names, flags, casualty numbers, attribution of the attacks, or EU measures. The only official casualty wording is "lives already lost at sea". Do not state who attacked the ships.
- **Press reports (NOT official, unconfirmed):** media describe a grain-carrying Turkish ship sinking on 5 October and two more vessels hit on 6 October, with crew dead, missing or rescued; outlets differ on locations and on who fired. Do NOT use any vessel name, casualty figure or attribution from press as established.
- **No EU action announced yet:** neither the Commission nor the Council had announced sanctions, naval measures or a legal response to these incidents when this guide was written.
- **Related, earlier, official proposal (State of the Union, 16 September 2026, before these attacks):** von der Leyen proposed "Europe's own Article 4 - or an **Emergency Security Protocol**": "When triggered by one Member State, all Member States would convene. To coordinate a European response. To deter further escalation. And to mitigate the impact"; said "we will set out our ideas to make a **European Security Council** a reality"; and said she will propose, with the High Representative, "a new **European security strategy**". No official document links these proposals to the Black Sea incidents, and the strategy's date is not confirmed.
- **Why it matters:** the Black Sea is the export route for Ukrainian grain and a Member State coast (Romania, Bulgaria) is involved; the incidents add to the hybrid-threat debate and to food-security concerns ahead of the European Council.

## FAQ
**Has the EU blamed Russia?** Not in any statement read: the Commission says the circumstances are still being established.
**Is NATO Article 4 invoked?** Not according to the reports: Bulgaria's prime minister is quoted as seeing no grounds for Article 4 or 5. The Commission's "Emergency Security Protocol" is a separate EU idea, not NATO's Article 4.
