# EU-Canada Relations - CETA, SPA, and the 2026 Parliament Recommendation

## QUICK FACTS

- **LATEST (26 August 2026 - CARNEY TO ADDRESS THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT)**: the Canadian Prime Minister's Office confirmed that **Mark Carney has accepted President Roberta Metsola's invitation to address the European Parliament in Strasbourg**, and will attend **von der Leyen's State of the Union address on 16 September 2026** as guest of honour. The invitation was extended in **May 2026**. **Hedge the date of his own speech**: the PMO statement did **not** confirm the trip date, and **17 September is press expectation, not a confirmed EP agenda item** (Canadian Press, 26 Aug). The 16 September attendance is the firm part. Context: an escalating Canada-US trade war after negotiations broke down on 21 August, and Carney's stated strategy of doubling Canada's non-US exports. Sources: PMO statement via Canadian Press / Global News, 26 Aug 2026; Agence Europe. **Not yet on the EP plenary agenda** - verify against doceo before citing 17 September as fixed.
- **LATEST (published OJ C-series, week to 27 August 2026)**: the European Parliament's **recommendation on enhanced EU-Canada cooperation in the current geopolitical context, including the threats to Canada's economic stability and sovereignty**, adopted at the March 2026 plenary, was published as **P10_TA(2026)0078** - CELEX `52026IP0078`. The title is unusually direct for a Parliament text: it names threats to a partner's **sovereignty**, which is the language the Parliament reserves for coercion by a third state.
- **What a "recommendation" is here, and why it matters procedurally**: this is a **recommendation to the Council and the Commission / High Representative under Article 118 of the EP Rules of Procedure**, in the field of the Common Foreign and Security Policy. It is **not legislation and binds nobody**. The Parliament has no legislative role in the CFSP: Article 24(1) TEU excludes the ordinary legislative procedure, and the Parliament's formal rights are to be consulted and informed (Article 36 TEU). A recommendation is the strongest instrument it holds in that space.
- **The trade pillar - CETA**: the **Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement**, Council Decision **2017/38** on provisional application - CELEX `32017D0038`. CETA has been **provisionally applied since 21 September 2017** and is **still not fully in force**, because ratification by every Member State parliament is incomplete. The provisions outside exclusive EU competence - above all **investment protection and the Investment Court System** - are **NOT being applied** and will not apply until the last national ratification lands.
- **Why CETA is the standing exam question**: a user asking "is CETA in force?" is usually asking the wrong question. The correct answer is two-part: **provisionally applied in the parts of EU exclusive competence since 2017**, and **not in force** as a mixed agreement. Opinion **1/17** of the Court of Justice (30 April 2019) confirmed the Investment Court System is compatible with the Treaties, which removed the legal obstacle but not the ratification one.
- **The political pillar - SPA**: the **Strategic Partnership Agreement**, signed 30 October 2016 alongside CETA, covering foreign policy, security, counter-terrorism, migration and energy. It is the framework any "enhanced cooperation" is built on and is the natural legal home for the March 2026 recommendation's asks.
- **Institutional setting**: EU-Canada Summits (leaders); the **CETA Joint Committee** for trade; the EEAS and the High Representative for the CFSP strand. Canada is a **G7 and NATO** member and a party to the **EU-Canada Green Alliance** (2021), the EU's first such alliance with any partner.

## What the March 2026 recommendation is responding to

The framing - "threats to Canada's economic stability and sovereignty" - places the text in the
same family as the Parliament's other 2025-2026 responses to economic coercion. Read it as the
Parliament positioning the EU alongside a G7 partner under external pressure, and asking the
Council and the Commission to deepen the SPA and CETA relationship as a response.

Two cautions when answering on this file:

1. **Do not describe it as a change in EU-Canada trade law.** Nothing in a Rule 118 recommendation
   alters CETA, its provisional application, or any tariff line.
2. **Do not assert the vote tally or the rapporteur** unless the doceo record has been read. The OJ
   publication carries the adopted text, not the roll call.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Calling CETA "in force". It is **provisionally applied**, and the distinction is the whole answer.
- Treating a Parliament recommendation in the CFSP as binding, or as legislation.
- Confusing the **SPA** (political) with **CETA** (trade). They were signed on the same day and are
  different agreements with different legal bases.
- Assuming an EP recommendation implies a Commission proposal will follow. It carries no such
  obligation.
