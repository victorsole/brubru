## Overview

On 17 May 2023, the European Commission proposed a comprehensive reform of the EU customs framework, replacing the existing Union Customs Code (Regulation 952/2013) with a new regulation. The proposal introduces two major innovations:

1. **EU Customs Data Hub**: a centralised platform replacing multiple legacy customs IT systems. Traders submit all customs data to a single environment, enabling real-time AI-powered risk analysis. Phased implementation 2028-2032. Potential savings: EUR 2 billion/year for Member States.

2. **EU Customs Authority**: a new EU-level authority to coordinate customs enforcement, manage the Data Hub, and harmonise risk management across Member States.

The European Parliament adopted its 1st reading position on 13 March 2024 with significant amendments. The file is currently in interinstitutional negotiations (trilogues announced December 2024), awaiting the Council's 1st reading position.

## EP Amendments (Key Changes)

The Parliament's 1st reading text (T9-0151/2024) introduced amendments to the Commission proposal on:
- Governance structure of the EU Customs Authority
- Data privacy safeguards in the Customs Data Hub
- Transition timeline for Member States
- Role of national customs authorities vs the EU Authority
- E-commerce and de minimis threshold provisions
- Interoperability with other border management systems (EES, ETIAS, SIS)

For the full amended text, see T9-0151/2024 on the EP Doceo portal.
