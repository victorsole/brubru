# Unit Cost Decision: Research Infrastructures (Horizon Europe / Euratom)

## QUICK FACTS

- Authorises use of unit costs for trans-national and virtual access to research infrastructures under Horizon Europe (2021-2027) and Euratom Programmes (2021-2025, 2026-2027)
- Simplifies cost reporting: beneficiaries can use unit costs instead of detailed cost tracking for access provision
- Unit cost = average annual access costs divided by average annual quantity of access (calculated from two years' historical data for trans-national; one year for virtual)
- Eligible direct costs include staff, maintenance, consumables, management fees, energy, software licences and computing services
- Indirect costs fixed at 25% of eligible direct costs
- Double-unit-cost protection: facilities (buildings, equipment depreciation) excluded unless specified in work programme
- Applies to any research infrastructure providing access to users; "installation" means any separately usable part of a RI
- ESFRI (European Strategy Forum on Research Infrastructures) roadmap institutions and distributed RIs both eligible
- Certified or auditable historical data required to establish unit costs
- Updated 2026: Euratom Programme extended to 2027

## Research Infrastructure access in Horizon Europe

The Horizon Europe Framework Programme aims to provide researchers and user groups with access to excellent research infrastructures needed for their work. Research Infrastructures actions cover trans-national access (physical travel to a facility) and virtual access (remote use via electronic means). The unit cost methodology was developed to simplify financial management for both beneficiaries (infrastructure operators) and the granting authority, reducing administrative burden whilst maintaining sound financial principles.

The unit cost approach was successful under Horizon 2020 and has been maintained and extended under Horizon Europe and the Euratom Programmes.

## What this unit-cost decision covers

The decision permits two types of access costs to be declared using unit costs:

- Costs of providing trans-national access
- Costs of providing virtual access

All other eligible costs (including travel and subsistence costs for users visiting the facility) must be reimbursed on the basis of eligible costs actually incurred.

Recipients can choose to declare eligible access costs in one of three ways:
1. Unit costs calculated from historical data
2. Actual eligible costs incurred
3. A combination of unit costs and actual costs (if some costs vary significantly and cannot be represented by an average)

## Types of access (trans-national, virtual, remote)

Trans-national access involves researchers or user groups physically travelling to use a research infrastructure. This access is typically costed over a two-year historical period to account for annual variations.

Virtual access refers to remote use of research infrastructure, typically delivered electronically or through digital means. Virtual access is costed over a one-year historical period because the technologies underlying installations evolve rapidly, making a single recent year more representative than a longer average.

The distinction between the two access types leads to different calculation periods: trans-national uses a two-year average, whilst virtual uses one-year data. In exceptional and duly justified cases, the granting authority may agree to a different reference period.

## Calculation methodology (per user-day, per unit of access)

Beneficiaries must identify and define a measurable unit of access specific to each installation. This unit of access is the metric that allows the access provider to measure both the access provided and total access quantity. Examples might include user-days, beam-time slots, or computing cycles, depending on the infrastructure type.

### Formula for trans-national access

Unit cost = (average annual total access costs over the past two years) divided by (average annual total quantity of access over the past two years)

Historical data must be:
- Certified or auditable
- Allocated to the concerned installation according to the access provider's usual cost accounting practices
- Exclusive of periods when the installation was out of service (maintenance, repair, downtime)

### Formula for virtual access

Unit cost = (total virtual access costs over the past year) divided by (total quantity of virtual access over the past year)

Historical data must be:
- Certified or auditable
- Allocated to the concerned installation according to the access provider's usual cost accounting practices
- Exclusive of periods when the installation was unusable

The one-year reference for virtual access reflects the fast-evolving nature of the underlying technologies.

## Eligible RIs (ESFRI-listed, distributed, national and pan-European interest)

Eligible research infrastructures are those that:
- Provide trans-national or virtual access to researchers or other users meeting the eligibility conditions set by the infrastructure
- Include large-scale facilities, distributed RIs (networks of institutions), national RIs and pan-European RIs
- Operate as one or more "installations" (where an installation is a part or service of a RI that could be used independently)

ESFRI (European Strategy Forum on Research Infrastructures) roadmap RIs are typical beneficiaries, but the eligibility criteria are not limited to ESFRI-listed facilities.

### Eligible direct costs for access provision

Personnel cost of administrative, technical and scientific staff directly assigned to the functioning of the installation and user support.

Maintenance and repair costs specifically awarded for the installation's functioning (if not capitalised), including cleaning, calibration and testing.

Consumables specifically used for the installation and user research work.

Installation management contracts (including security, insurance, quality control and certification costs) for maintaining national and EU quality, safety and security standards (if not capitalised).

Energy, power and water costs where verified as supplied exclusively for the installation and representing a major cost item.

General services specifically included in access services (library costs, shipping, etc.).

Software licences, internet connection and electronic services for data management and computing needed to provide access services.

Specific scientific services included in or needed for access provision.

### Ineligible costs

Capital investments in infrastructure (buildings, equipment rental, lease and depreciation costs) are excluded unless otherwise specified in the Work Programme, in which case only the portion used to provide access is eligible.

All other ineligible costs listed in Article 6.3 of the Model Grant Agreement are excluded.

### Indirect costs

Indirect costs are fixed at 25% of eligible direct costs. This flat rate is deemed appropriate because it ensures that indirect costs correspond only to a fraction of overall Research Infrastructure costs. Only cost items directly linked to running the RI (specific or exclusive to its operation) are included in the indirect cost calculation, preventing inflated rates.

## Documentation and reporting

Beneficiaries must:

- Specify the unit cost in the grant agreement for each installation
- Base calculations on certified or auditable historical data
- Declare the selected form of cost (unit costs, actual costs, or combination) and use it consistently throughout the action
- Maintain clear identification and specification of costs to avoid double funding
- Submit periodic reports showing units of access provided and costs incurred (every 12-18 months, plus final report within 60 days of project end)

The granting authority verifies compliance with unit cost conditions during grant preparation, implementation, or afterwards through checks, reviews or audits.

### Audit and control

The granting authority has the right to conduct ex-post audits and OLAF (European Anti-Fraud Office) and the ECA (European Court of Auditors) have independent audit rights. The audit window extends up to two years after final payment.

Unit costs reduce irregularity and fraud risk because they are calculated from historical data of typically large public research organisations whose financial statements are regularly certified and audited by external or public auditors.
