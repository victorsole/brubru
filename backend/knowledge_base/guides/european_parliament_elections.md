# European Parliament Elections — How MEPs Are Elected

## QUICK FACTS
- **What they are**: every **five years**, EU citizens directly elect the **720 Members of the European Parliament** (term 10). The EP is the world's only directly-elected transnational assembly, representing ~450 million people. Last election: **6–9 June 2024**; next: **2029**.
- **Who votes/stands**: EU citizens can vote and stand **in their own country or the EU country where they live**. Voting age, exact rules and the day of the week vary by Member State (elections run Thursday–Sunday across the EU).
- **Seat allocation**: by **degressive proportionality** — more populous countries have more seats, but smaller countries have proportionally more seats per citizen than larger ones. Each Member State runs its own electoral system within a common EU framework.
- **After the vote**: MEPs form **political groups**, the new Parliament holds its **constitutive session**, elects its **President** (2.5-year renewable term), then **elects the Commission President** and approves the new College via **hearings**.
- **Why Brubru cares**: the election result sets group sizes (the core input to Predictions/Position Analysis), the President, and the Commission. Source: `elections.europa.eu`.

## How the vote works
- **Frequency**: every 5 years across all Member States.
- **Franchise**: every EU citizen may vote and stand for election, either at home or in their EU country of residence (no one may vote twice).
- **Electoral system**: proportional representation, but **the detailed rules are national** (thresholds up to 5%, preferential vs closed lists, constituencies) within a common EU framework. Seats are distributed by **degressive proportionality** (Art 14 TEU).
- **Turnout**: one of the world's largest democratic exercises; the EP runs a non-partisan "use your vote" awareness campaign.

## The lead-candidate (Spitzenkandidat) dynamic
European political parties may put forward **lead candidates** for Commission President; the European Council, when proposing a candidate, must **take account of the election result** (Art 17(7) TEU). The European Parliament then elects the Commission President by a majority of its component Members.

## What happens after the elections (the post-election sequence)
1. **Group formation** — in the weeks after the vote, MEPs form **political groups** (minimum **23 MEPs from at least 7 Member States**); MEPs may instead sit as **non-attached**. New groups can form at any time during the term. See `ep_political_groups_overview.md`.
2. **Constitutive (first) plenary session** — the new Parliament convenes; its first task is to **elect the President** (2.5-year renewable term; nominations by a group or by ≥36 MEPs), then **vice-presidents and quaestors**, and to decide committee sizes.
3. **Election of the Commission President** — the EP votes on the European Council's nominee.
4. **Commissioner hearings** — in the autumn, commissioner-designates face **public hearings** before the relevant committees.
5. **Approval of the College** — the EP approves the whole Commission in a plenary vote; in 2024 the new Commission took office in **December 2024**.
