## What the CRMA does

The CRMA is the EU's strategic-autonomy response to a structural finding: the green and digital transitions require **vastly more raw materials** (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths, gallium, germanium, silicon, magnesium, copper, aluminium, etc.), and the EU is **structurally dependent on a small number of third countries** (often single-supplier — China for >90% of rare-earth processing; DRC for >70% of cobalt; Chile + Australia for lithium).

The CRMA sets **strategic targets** for the EU's supply chain by 2030 and creates **fast-track instruments** to meet them.

## The CRMA's 2030 benchmarks (Article 5)

For **Strategic Raw Materials** (Annex I list — currently 17 substances including lithium, cobalt, nickel, manganese, rare earths, gallium, germanium, magnesium, silicon, copper, aluminium):

| Stage | EU 2030 target | 2024 baseline |
|---|---|---|
| **Extraction** | At least **10%** of EU annual consumption | Single-digit % for most strategic materials |
| **Processing** | At least **40%** of EU annual consumption | Often below 5% (rare earths: ~0%) |
| **Recycling** | At least **25%** of EU annual consumption from secondary sources | 5-15% depending on material |
| **Diversification** | No more than **65%** of EU annual consumption from a single third country | Currently many materials >80% single-source |

For **Critical Raw Materials** (Annex II list — wider, includes phosphate rock, bauxite, etc.): qualitative monitoring obligation but not the same numerical targets.

## The Strategic Project mechanism (Articles 6-12)

A flagship CRMA tool: the Commission designates **Strategic Projects** anywhere in the EU value chain (extraction, processing, recycling, substitution) that benefit from:
- **Streamlined permitting** — single point of contact + maximum permitting period of **27 months** for extraction projects, **15 months** for processing/recycling projects (or 7 months for projects on existing brownfield sites)
- **Easier financing access** — InvestEU + Innovation Fund + EIB + Just Transition Fund eligibility flags
- **Recognition as a project of overriding public interest** under Habitats Directive Article 6(4) and Water Framework Directive Article 4(7)
- **Priority status in EU industrial alliances** (European Raw Materials Alliance, European Battery Alliance, etc.)
- **First mover support** for projects in strategic-value-chain segments where the EU has no current capacity (notably rare-earth processing, certain magnet manufacturing)

The first **Strategic Project list** was adopted by the Commission in March 2025 — 47 projects across 13 Member States. Annual update cycles are now established.

## Joint Purchasing System (Article 26-28)

The CRMA enables **voluntary joint purchasing** of strategic raw materials by EU companies, with a Commission-led platform supporting demand aggregation. The instrument is modelled on the gas joint purchasing system (AggregateEU) and is intended to give SME consumers price-and-quality leverage against global commodity-trader oligopolies.

## Reporting obligations on companies

Large EU companies (>500 employees, revenue >EUR 150 million annual) that produce **strategic technologies** (batteries, EV motors, electrolysers, wind turbines, solar panels, semiconductors, robotics) must:
- Conduct **supply-chain risk assessments** every 3 years
- Identify dependencies on strategic raw materials
- Report exposure to Member State + Commission

This is the CRMA's **due-diligence-lite** track, separate from but complementary to:
- **CSDDD** (Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive) — Reg-wide due diligence
- **Forced Labour Regulation** (EU) 2024/3015 — import-side bans
- **Conflict Minerals Regulation** (EU) 2017/821 — narrow scope on 3TG

## Monitoring and stress-testing

The Commission's **EU Critical Materials Board** (CRMA Article 35) coordinates Member States + industry on:
- Annual joint **risk monitoring**
- **Stress tests** of supply-chain disruptions (real or simulated)
- **Strategic stockpiling** coordination (Member State competence but harmonised approach)

## Recyclable raw materials interaction (relevant to Eurostat 21 May 2026 data)

The CRMA Article 27 mandates a **25% recycling-content target** for strategic raw materials by 2030. The EU's net-import dependence on secondary raw materials (scrap metals, e-waste, paper-for-recycling) is a structural drag on this target. Eurostat's 21 May 2026 data showing **net imports up +7.8%** signals:
- More EU recyclable-materials demand is being met by **imports** rather than EU-domestic collection
- Sources of imported scrap include US, UK, Switzerland, Russia (until 2022 sanctions), Asia
- The interaction with the **Waste Shipment Regulation (EU) 2024/1157** is critical — the new regime limits exports to non-OECD countries and may reduce some of this flow

This is the policy backdrop for the **Circular Economy Act 2026** negotiation (separate file) aiming to lift EU domestic recycling capacity.
