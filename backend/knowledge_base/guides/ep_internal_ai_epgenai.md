# European Parliament — Internal AI Use and the EPGenAI Hub

## QUICK FACTS
- **What this is**: how the European Parliament itself uses generative AI internally — the **EPGenAI Hub** platform being rolled out to MEPs and staff, and the internal rules governing AI use in parliamentary work.
- **LATEST (Tuesday 21 July 2026 — EPGENAI HUB REVEALED)**: the Parliament is preparing an internal generative-AI platform, the **EPGenAI Hub**, giving MEPs and staff access to models from **Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral**. It is **currently in testing** and **could begin rolling out as early as September 2026**. It has already been demonstrated to MEPs and staff at internal workshops. Source: POLITICO, "The European Parliament's answer to its AI worries: More AI", 21 July 2026.
- **Two hosting tiers** (this is the design's defining feature): users choose between
  - models **operated securely within Parliament's own infrastructure** — Meta's **Llama**, OpenAI's **GPT-OSS**, and **Mistral Small**; and
  - **more powerful externally hosted services** — OpenAI's **ChatGPT** and Anthropic's **Claude Sonnet**.
- **Stated purpose**: a **safer alternative to the public AI tools already being used for legislative work**. The premise is that MEPs and staff are already using consumer AI tools; the Hub is an attempt to bring that usage inside a governed perimeter rather than to introduce AI use for the first time.
- **Staff rules**: parliamentary employees are subject to **separate internal AI guidelines dated April 2024**, which are **already due to be redrafted**. They require staff to label AI-generated work with an **"AI-assisted" disclaimer** and **prohibit entering sensitive information into public generative-AI systems**. The guidelines warn that inaccuracies produced by the technology risk **creating or spreading misleading information, or material that misrepresents the Parliament**.

## What is and is not established
Treat the following as the boundary of what is currently on the record:
- **Established**: the platform exists in beta and has been reviewed; the four model vendors; the split between locally-operated and externally-hosted models; the September 2026 earliest-rollout expectation; the existence and content of the April 2024 staff guidelines and the fact they are being redrafted; that workshops have been held.
- **NOT established** (do not assert): how the Hub retrieves or grounds information; whether it is connected to Parliament's own document stores (doceo, OEIL, committee documents); any accuracy, evaluation or benchmark figures; procurement value or contract terms; whether MEPs' own use (as opposed to staff use) is bound by the April 2024 guidelines; any official Parliament confirmation of the rollout date. The September date is attributed to two people familiar with the plans, granted anonymity — it is a plan, not a commitment.

## Why this matters for EU policy professionals
- **Institutional AI governance is becoming self-referential.** The Parliament that co-legislated the AI Act is now procuring and governing genAI for its own legislative work. The internal guidelines predate the AI Act's main application dates and are being redrafted against them — the redraft is the thing to watch.
- **Transparency of AI-assisted legislative work.** The "AI-assisted" labelling requirement is a live precedent for how AI involvement in legislative drafting might be disclosed. It currently binds staff, not Members.
- **Data-sovereignty design.** Running Llama, GPT-OSS and Mistral Small inside Parliament's own infrastructure, while routing heavier work to external providers, is a concrete example of the tiered approach many EU institutions and public bodies are converging on: sensitive work stays local, general work goes to the frontier model.
- **The distinction that matters analytically**: EPGenAI is a **model-access hub** — it selects which language model a user talks to. That is a different category from a **grounded legislative-intelligence system**, which retrieves from authoritative EU sources (EUR-Lex/Cellar, OEIL, doceo, the institutional calendar) and cites them. A model-access hub inherits whatever the underlying model knows; it does not, on the public record, add an EU-specific retrieval layer. Keep these two things distinct when answering questions comparing them.
