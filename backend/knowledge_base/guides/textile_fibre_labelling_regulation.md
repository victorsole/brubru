# Textile Fibre Labelling Regulation - Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011

## QUICK FACTS
- **CELEX:** 32011R1007
- **Full title:** Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 September 2011 on textile fibre names and related labelling and marking of the fibre composition of textile products and repealing Council Directive 73/44/EEC and Directives 96/73/EC and 2008/121/EC (Text with EEA relevance)
- **Common name:** Textile Fibre Labelling Regulation (or Textile Labelling Regulation). The act that harmonises textile fibre names and compulsory fibre-composition labelling across the EU.
- **Type:** Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council (directly applicable, no national transposition). Ordinary legislative procedure.
- **Legal basis:** Article 114 TFEU (internal market).
- **Adopted:** 27 September 2011, at Strasbourg.
- **OJ reference:** OJ L 272, 18 October 2011.
- **Entry into force:** the twentieth day following the 18 October 2011 OJ publication.
- **Application:** applies from 8 May 2012. The three repealed directives (73/44/EEC, 96/73/EC, 2008/121/EC) were repealed with effect from 8 May 2012; a transitional provision allowed products compliant with Directive 2008/121/EC and placed on the market before 8 May 2012 to remain available until 9 November 2014.
- **Structure:** 28 Articles in 4 Chapters (General provisions; Textile fibre names and labelling and marking requirements; Market surveillance; Final provisions), plus Annexes I to X.

The naming-and-composition foundation beneath the newer EU textile circular-economy rules, the Waste Framework Directive textile amendment (Directive (EU) 2025/1892) and the ESPR-based digital product passport for textiles. ## Overview

This is the reason a jumper's label reads "80% wool, 20% polyamide" in the same controlled vocabulary in every EU shop. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 harmonises, across the single market, the names that may be used for textile fibres and how the fibre composition of a textile product must be labelled and marked. It replaced three older directives with a single directly applicable regulation, so that technical updates (new fibre names, new methods) no longer need national transposition.

Although adopted in 2011, it already looked ahead to electronic labelling and language-independent fibre symbols, the direction the EU's digital product passport now takes for textiles. It sits beneath the textile DPP and the circular-economy rules as the layer that fixes what a fibre is called and how composition must be declared.

## Fibre names and the label

- **Controlled vocabulary (Annex I):** only the textile fibre names listed in Annex I may be used to describe fibre content. A manufacturer may apply to have a new fibre name added to Annex I (Article 6), supported by a technical file meeting the minimum requirements in Annex II. The Commission adds new names by delegated act.
- **Compulsory composition labelling:** when a textile product is made available on the market, its full fibre composition must be indicated on a label or marking that is durable, easily legible, visible and accessible (Articles 4, 5, 9, 14, 16).
- **"100 percent", "pure", "all":** these may be used only for a product composed exclusively of one fibre (Article 7).
- **Animal-origin parts:** a product containing non-textile parts of animal origin must say so with the phrase "Contains non-textile parts of animal origin" (Article 12), so consumers can make informed choices. Labelling must not be misleading.
- **Language:** the label must be in the official language or languages of the Member State where the product is made available (Article 16(3)).

## Who is responsible, and what is exempt

- **Responsibility:** the economic operator placing the product on the market is responsible for the label and its accuracy; importers and distributors have defined duties (Articles 15 and 16).
- **Exemptions and inclusive labelling:** certain products are exempt or subject only to inclusive or global labelling, as listed in the Annexes. Examples include products contracted out to persons working in their own homes or to independent firms working from supplied materials, customised products made up by self-employed tailors, and defined categories of disposable or inclusive-labelling products.

## Testing and tolerances

- **Extraneous fibres:** need not be stated up to the tolerances set in Article 19.
- **Agreed allowances:** when fibre content is determined by analysis, agreed allowances apply to the anhydrous mass of each fibre (Article 19 and Annexes VII to IX), with specific handling of wool and animal hair in carded or combed products.
- **Methods to standards:** the Commission manages the transition of the uniform analysis methods in the Regulation towards harmonised standards.
- **Market surveillance (Chapter 3):** applies under Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 and, at the time of adoption, the General Product Safety Directive 2001/95/EC.

## The 2011 law that looked ahead

Article 24 required the Commission to report by 30 September 2013 on possible new EU labelling requirements, expressly naming an origin-labelling scheme, a harmonised care-labelling system, uniform size labelling, indication of allergenic substances, and electronic labelling and language-independent symbols or codes for identifying fibres. Article 25 required a study by 30 September 2013 on any causal link between allergic reactions and chemicals used in textiles. These forward-looking clauses anticipated exactly the digital and traceability direction the ESPR digital product passport for textiles now delivers.

## Where it sits

- **This act:** the naming-and-composition foundation for textiles, in force since 2012.
- **The circular-economy layer:** the Waste Framework Directive textile amendment (Directive (EU) 2025/1892), which brings separate collection and extended producer responsibility to textiles.
- **The passport layer:** the ESPR (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) and its digital product passport, which will carry textile composition and traceability data digitally, building on the vocabulary this Regulation fixed.
