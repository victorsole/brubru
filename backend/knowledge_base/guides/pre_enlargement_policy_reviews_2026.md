# Preparing for a Wider Union: the Commission's Pre-Enlargement Policy Reviews (6 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** on **Tuesday 6 October 2026**, in Strasbourg, the European Commission adopted the Communication **"Preparing for a wider Union"** (COM(2026) 705 final) together with a Staff Working Document on the **pre-enlargement policy reviews** and a press release and Q&A (published 16:06 CEST). It follows von der Leyen's 2023 State of the Union announcement, the 2023 Granada Declaration and the **March 2024 Communication** (COM(2024) 146), and answers the **European Council's June 2024 mandate**.
- **Exactly four areas** were reviewed: **values, policies, the budget and governance.** Headline conclusion: the Union **can accommodate a larger membership through targeted adaptations within the existing Treaty framework and accession treaties**. The Commission says it "supports Treaty change where needed", but this package does **NOT** propose a Treaty amendment.
- **It does NOT fix dates:** the assessment "does not prejudge the timing or sequence of future accessions". Accession stays **merit-based**; the Council acts **unanimously** on accession (Article 49 TEU), with Parliament's consent and ratification by all contracting States.
- **Governance (qualified-majority voting):** the Commission will keep advocating **passerelle clauses** (these need unanimity to activate, and in some cases Parliament's consent and national parliaments) to move to qualified majority on **sanctions, human rights and civilian CSDP missions, measures against tax fraud, evasion and avoidance, fiscal measures in energy policy, and social protection**. It **recommends qualified majority for opening negotiating clusters and other intermediate decisions** in accession negotiations. Accession itself stays unanimous.
- **Enhanced cooperation:** needs **at least nine Member States**; the Commission will **review pending proposals** to find where it could break a persistent blockage (examples cited: the European Public Prosecutor's Office and the Ukraine Support Loan for 2026-2027).
- **New institutional safeguard clause for accession treaties:** covers **serious breaches of Article 2 TEU values and of the principle of sincere cooperation**; applies for **15 years after accession**; measures on a Commission proposal or a reasoned request of Member States, **deemed adopted unless the Council rejects them by qualified majority**; can extend, in the most serious cases, to **suspension of Council voting rights**; **complements, does NOT replace, Article 7 TEU**.
- **Three sectoral safeguards are kept** (economic, internal market, justice and home affairs). Previous treaties allowed activation for **three years** after accession; future treaties should **"significantly extend"** that period (the Communication gives no figure for the sectoral clauses: do not quote one). Also proposed: a **general pre-accession monitoring clause** (signature to accession) and, as a last resort, a **postponement clause** (decision-making arrangements "remain to be specified").
- **Ukraine agriculture:** the Commission wants **targeted arrangements that significantly limit financial support and market access for sensitive agricultural products**, while helping Ukraine regain historic third-country export markets; to be reflected in the draft common positions for **Cluster 5 (resources, agriculture and cohesion)** "at an appropriate moment".
- **Gradual integration** (earlier participation of candidates in selected EU policies): **merit-based, conditional and reversible**; the Commission will **map existing instruments**, launch **annual gradual-integration dialogues**, and fund needs from a **dedicated share of the proposed Global Europe instrument**. Priorities: Single Market (including Conformity Assessment agreements), energy, transport and digital networks (TEN-T, TEN-E), security and defence, and benefits for citizens (SEPA, Roam Like at Home).
- **Next steps:** feeds the **European Council of 15-16 October 2026**; the **2026 Enlargement package** will present **indicative roadmaps for Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine** (the College's indicative agenda lists it for 28 October, to be confirmed); safeguards are to be applied **first to Montenegro's accession arrangements**.
- **Not the same as:** the **2026 Enlargement package** (country reports, due later), nor a legislative proposal: the Communication is a policy document that invites Parliament and Council to act.

## Context
Enlargement is described as a "geopolitical imperative" after Russia's war against Ukraine. The Communication and the March 2024 work programme rest on two parallel tracks from the Granada Declaration: candidates reform, the Union prepares internally. The reviews examined EU policies for the effect of a larger membership on design, implementation or financing, and found that **administrative capacity and implementation** are the most recurrent requirements, in the EU and in candidate countries.

## 1. Governance
- **Lisbon Treaty tools:** extended QMV and the ordinary legislative procedure, double-majority voting, ceilings for the Parliament, rules for the Commission, passerelle clauses and easier enhanced cooperation.
- **Safety nets for QMV:** emergency brakes (Articles 48, 82(3) and 83(3) TFEU) and the **Ioannina mechanism**; the Commission offers to discuss "appropriate and proportionate safeguards" with Parliament and Council.
- **Council practice:** the Commission asks the Council to use QMV more where the Treaties allow, to avoid unnecessary **mixed agreements**, and to apply QMV as default for non-binding arrangements with third countries.
- **CFSP flexibility:** constructive abstention and Article 31(2) TEU (QMV for defined cases).
- **Institutions:** the Treaty default is a **College of two thirds of the number of Member States**, but the European Council has kept one Commissioner per Member State (Decision (EU) 2024/2997). That decision must be reviewed before the accession of the **30th Member State**. The Parliament is capped at **750 Members plus the President** with degressive proportionality; the 2024-2029 composition leaves some room, but further enlargement needs a new allocation of seats (unanimous European Council decision on Parliament's proposal). Practical preparation: staffing, translation and interpretation, legal revision, secure digital systems, meeting facilities, agencies, delegations.

## 2. Values and the rule of law
- The **Fundamentals cluster** opens first, closes last and sets the pace. The **Rule of Law Report** already involves **Albania, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia**; the Commission will extend it to other candidates as they are ready.
- **EPPO:** now part of the acquis, so new Member States must participate from accession (preparing prosecutors, delegated prosecutors, criminal-law alignment).
- **Article 7 TEU:** high thresholds (four-fifths, unanimity) and no timeframes limit it; the Commission wants to explore **timeframes and better follow-up**, possibly through interinstitutional arrangements within the existing Treaties.
- **Charter and non-discrimination:** the Commission will assess whether the general passerelle in **Article 48(7) TEU** could help with Article 19(1) TFEU anti-discrimination measures.
- **Democratic resilience:** candidates are associated with the **European Democracy Shield** and the European Centre for Democratic Resilience.

## 3. Policies (principal sectoral conclusions)
Freedom, security and justice (external borders, common information systems); Single Market (uniform application, market surveillance, customs, competition, state aid; **transitional measures on free movement of workers** may be envisaged as before); competitiveness and inclusive growth (the **Customs Union** poses particular challenges); decarbonised economy and connectivity; cohesion, agriculture and rural development, food and fisheries; external relations, security and defence (**alignment with CFSP and sanctions "remains insufficient among certain enlargement countries"**); administrative policies and anti-fraud.

## 4. Budget
- Main response is the **2028-2034 MFF proposal** (COM(2025) 571), including a **revision mechanism when a new Member State joins** (Article 11); candidates are funded before accession under the proposed **Global Europe** instrument (COM(2025) 551; grants, loans, guarantees, technical assistance) and after accession under **National and Regional Partnership Plans** (COM(2025) 565) and programmes such as the European Competitiveness Fund.
- **Montenegro's financial package** (COM(2026) 334, 30 June 2026) is the first application: at accession the **Global Europe plan becomes the unallocated component** of the National and Regional Partnership Plan, and **additional envelopes for cohesion, the CAP, home affairs and Interreg** are added; additional commitment appropriations avoid cutting funding for existing Member States.
- Objective-based financing (Ukraine Facility, Reform and Growth Facilities for the Western Balkans and Moldova) stays the main vehicle; payments are **suspended or reduced** if conditions or financial-control standards are not met.

## 5. Gradual integration
- Built on the **2020 revised enlargement methodology**; pathways differ between the Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Areas (Ukraine, Moldova) and the Stabilisation and Association Agreements (Western Balkans).
- **Sensitive sectors** (semiconductors, quantum, biotechnology, AI, space): participation depends on programme rules and security requirements, with alignment on investment screening, export controls, sanctions and technology protection.
- **Citizens' benefits:** **Albania, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Serbia** have started participating in **SEPA** schemes; **Ukraine and Moldova joined Roam Like at Home on 1 January 2026**; the **Council authorised negotiations in June 2026** to extend it to the Western Balkans.
- **Institutions:** observer participation in agencies, networks and expert groups; information, consultation and observer arrangements after the **final Intergovernmental Conference**; parliaments of acceding countries may be invited as observers after signature. Pre-accession participation **cannot confer College membership or decision-making rights**.
- **Reversibility:** access can be suspended or withdrawn, proportionately and by sector, with conditions stated in advance.

## 6. Safeguards in future accession treaties (Part IV)
Principles: clear grounds and periods, reasoned decisions on objective evidence, judicial scrutiny, regular review, proportionality. Components: three sectoral safeguards with a **longer activation period**; general monitoring clause; **postponement clause** as last resort; **institutional safeguard** (15 years, reverse-QMV adoption, can suspend Council voting rights); **Schengen**: full application still needs a **separate Council decision**, now with explicit consideration of rule-of-law conditions; a **declaration** that a new Member State would not, for a limited period, oppose Council and European Council decisions on future enlargements linked to **bilateral disputes**; transitional arrangements (free movement of workers and services, agriculture, sensitive products, funding, common systems), proportionate and time-limited. The Commission will submit proposals for Union positions under **Chapters 33, 34 and 35** and the sectoral chapters, starting with Montenegro.

## What the Staff Working Document adds (SWD(2026) 705, 149 pages)
General verdict: policies are "fit for enlargement" with adjustments; the gaps lie in candidates' preparedness and administrative capacity, in the budget, and in unanimity decisions. The areas most affected are **agriculture (the CAP), cohesion policy, foreign-policy unanimity and languages**. The analysis is **scenario-neutral on timing**.
- **Size of the enlarged Union (document's "EU36" scenario against today's EU27, 2021 data):** area **+21.8%**, population **+15.1%**, total GDP **+4.8%**, GDP per head **-8.9%** (average **91.1** against EU27 = 100). For comparison the document gives the 2004 round at **-8.0%** (population +19.2%, area +22.2%) and the 2007 round at **-3.4%**.
- **Agriculture ("likely to be one of the most impacted"):** with Ukraine, the document estimates EU soy and sunflower output could rise **by up to 150%**, maize **by 50%** and wheat **by 28%**. Options named: phasing-in, "effective safeguard mechanisms for sensitive products" and a "specific treatment" for Ukraine; the 2028-2034 MFF proposal already carries mandatory degressivity and capping, a crisis mechanism and a doubled agricultural reserve renamed the **"Unity Safety Net"** (the current reserve is at least EUR 450 million a year).
- **Schengen:** new members join only after a separate Council decision; the delay in lifting border checks was **three years for Austria, more than nine for Croatia and 18 for Bulgaria and Romania**. The document proposes a **new merit-based Schengen process** under which advanced candidates could enter the Schengen evaluation and monitoring mechanism before accession.
- **Workers:** transitional measures on free movement were used in the last three enlargement rounds and "can be expected" again.
- **Languages:** legal revision and publication of the acquis needs about **2.5 years** of lead time; interpreter recruitment starts about **18 months** before accession; the Maltese and Irish precedents are discussed as options for new languages.
- **Fisheries:** "minimal impact"; the Common Fisheries Policy can be extended. **Environment:** strengthen pre-accession support rather than change policy; transitional measures may be needed in accession treaties.
- **Public opinion (Eurobarometer 564, early 2025):** **56%** of EU respondents favour further enlargement, **38%** are against.
- **Institutions' offices:** the target of cutting **275,000 m2** of office space by 2030 may need adjusting.
- **Cover numbering:** the Communication is COM(2026) 705; the Staff Working Document's own cover reads SWD(2026) 705 (not 706).

## FAQ
**Did the Commission propose to change the Treaties?** No. It says the Union can prepare within the existing Treaties and accession treaties, and supports Treaty change where needed.
**Is there a date for the next enlargement?** No. The package explicitly does not prejudge timing or sequence.
**What is the 15-year clause?** The new institutional safeguard for serious breaches of Article 2 TEU values and sincere cooperation, applicable for 15 years after accession.
**Can a candidate join the Single Market before membership?** The Communication envisages deeper, conditional and reversible participation in selected policies and asks how candidates that comply with Single Market legislation could take part in relevant parts of it; it does not offer full Single Market membership.
**Which countries are named for roadmaps?** Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine, provided they keep up the pace of reforms.
**Where do the numbers and sector detail come from?** The Staff Working Document SWD(2026) 705 (149 pages), summarised below.
