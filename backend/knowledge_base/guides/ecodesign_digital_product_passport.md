## Overview

The **Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR)**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, is the EU's framework for setting sustainability requirements for virtually all physical products on the single market. It replaces the 2009 Ecodesign Directive (2009/125/EC), which was limited to energy-related products. The ESPR introduces the **Digital Product Passport (DPP)** -- a digital identity card for products, components, and materials that stores sustainability, circularity, and compliance information accessible via QR codes or RFID tags.

## Key Measures

### 1. Ecodesign Requirements (via Delegated Acts)
Product-specific or horizontal requirements covering: durability, reusability, upgradability, reparability, energy/resource efficiency, recycled content, carbon/environmental footprint, substances of concern, waste reduction, and sustainability information.

### 2. Digital Product Passport (DPP)
- Digital record for each product unit storing sustainability and compliance data
- Accessible electronically by consumers, manufacturers, authorities, and customs
- Information varies by product: technical performance, material origins, repair activities, recycling capabilities, lifecycle environmental impacts
- Enables automatic customs checks on product existence and authenticity
- **DPP Registry:** Centralised EU registry, operational from 19 July 2026
- **Technical standards:** GS1 Digital Link URI syntax emerging as de facto standard; CEN/CENELEC harmonised standards expected
- **Commission consultation on DPP data management:** Launched 9 April 2025 (DG GROW), deadline 1 July 2025. Focused on data storage by service providers and certification scheme for DPP service providers.

### 3. Destruction of Unsold Consumer Products
Ban on destruction of unsold textiles and footwear (delegated act adopted 9 February 2026). Large companies must disclose volumes of unsold products destroyed.

### 4. Green Public Procurement
Mandatory minimum green public procurement criteria for product groups covered by ESPR delegated acts.

### 5. EPREL Database
The European Product Registry for Energy Labelling (EPREL) is the existing EU product database for energy-labelled products. It serves as the precursor and technical reference for the broader DPP registry infrastructure. EPREL stores energy labels and product fiches accessible to consumers and market surveillance authorities.

## Working Plan 2025-2030

The first ESPR and Energy Labelling Working Plan was adopted on 16 April 2025 (SWD(2025)130). It prioritises product categories for delegated acts:

| Wave | Product Groups | Expected Delegated Act |

| First (2025-2026) | Iron and steel, Aluminium, Textiles and apparel, Furniture, Mattresses, Tyres | 2026 |

## DPP in Other EU Legislation

The DPP concept is not limited to ESPR. Three EU regulations already mandate product passports:

| Legislation | CELEX | DPP Requirement | Deadline |

| ESPR (EU) 2024/1781 | 32024R1781 | DPP for all product categories via delegated acts | 2027 onwards |

## EPRS Study: DPP for Textiles

The European Parliamentary Research Service published a study on the DPP for the textile sector (PE 757.808, June 2024). Key findings: a DPP could enhance textile industry traceability, circularity, and transparency, benefiting producers, supply-chain tiers, regulatory authorities, sorters, recyclers, and consumers. The study proposes a three-phase deployment scenario.
