## Overview

The **Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR)**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, is the EU's framework for setting sustainability requirements for virtually all physical products on the single market. It replaces the 2009 Ecodesign Directive (2009/125/EC), which was limited to energy-related products. The ESPR introduces the **Digital Product Passport (DPP)** -- a digital identity card for products, components, and materials that stores sustainability, circularity, and compliance information accessible via QR codes or RFID tags.

- **Reference:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1781
- **CELEX:** 32024R1781
- **OJ:** OJ L, 2024/1781, 28.6.2024
- **Procedure:** 2022/0095(COD)
- **Legal basis:** Article 114 TFEU (internal market)
- **Responsible DG:** DG Environment (ecodesign requirements), DG GROW (DPP technical infrastructure, consultation on data management)
- **Entry into force:** 18 July 2024
- **Replaces:** Directive 2009/125/EC (Ecodesign Directive)

## Key Measures

### 1. Ecodesign Requirements (via Delegated Acts)
Product-specific or horizontal requirements covering: durability, reusability, upgradability, reparability, energy/resource efficiency, recycled content, carbon/environmental footprint, substances of concern, waste reduction, and sustainability information.

### 2. Digital Product Passport (DPP)
- Digital record for each product unit storing sustainability and compliance data
- Accessible electronically by consumers, manufacturers, authorities, and customs
- Information varies by product: technical performance, material origins, repair activities, recycling capabilities, lifecycle environmental impacts
- Enables automatic customs checks on product existence and authenticity
- **DPP Registry:** Centralised EU registry, operational from 19 July 2026
- **Technical standards:** GS1 Digital Link URI syntax emerging as de facto standard; CEN/CENELEC harmonised standards expected
- **Commission consultation on DPP data management:** Launched 9 April 2025 (DG GROW), deadline 1 July 2025. Focused on data storage by service providers and certification scheme for DPP service providers.

### 3. Destruction of Unsold Consumer Products
Ban on destruction of unsold textiles and footwear (delegated act adopted 9 February 2026). Large companies must disclose volumes of unsold products destroyed.

### 4. Green Public Procurement
Mandatory minimum green public procurement criteria for product groups covered by ESPR delegated acts.

### 5. EPREL Database
The European Product Registry for Energy Labelling (EPREL) is the existing EU product database for energy-labelled products. It serves as the precursor and technical reference for the broader DPP registry infrastructure. EPREL stores energy labels and product fiches accessible to consumers and market surveillance authorities.

## What a delegated act must specify for the DPP (Article 9(2))

The ESPR itself creates no DPP for any product. **Article 9(1)** makes the passport a condition of market access -- a covered product "can only be placed on the market or put into service if a digital product passport is available" -- but the substance always arrives through a **delegated act adopted under Article 4**. For each product group, that delegated act must specify:

| # | What the delegated act fixes | Article 9(2) point |
|---|------------------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | The data to be included in the passport, drawn from **Annex III** | (a) |
| 2 | One or more **data carriers** to be used | (b) |
| 3 | The **layout** of the data carrier and where it is positioned | (c) |
| 4 | Whether the passport sits at **model, batch or item** level, and how those levels are defined | (d) |
| 5 | How the passport is made accessible to customers **before** they are bound by a contract, including in distance selling | (e) |
| 6 | **Which actors have access** to which data | (f) |
| 7 | **Which actors create or update** the passport, and what they may write | (g) |

Article 9(1) also sets the standing data-quality duty: the data "shall be accurate, complete and up to date".

This table is the practical specification any DPP platform has to build against. Until the delegated act for a product group exists, the obligations for that group are not yet fixed.

## The DPP Registry: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778

**ESPR Article 13** requires the Commission to set up and manage a registry storing, as a minimum, the **unique identifiers** -- plus the **commodity code** for products going into the customs procedure "release for free circulation". The economic operator uploads that data (Art. 13(4)); the registry then automatically returns a **unique registration identifier** (Art. 13(5)). That communication is explicitly **not proof of compliance** with the ESPR or any other Union law -- a distinction worth keeping in any compliance advice.

The Commission is the **data controller** for the registry under Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 (Art. 13(3)). The Commission, competent national authorities and customs authorities have access (Art. 13(6)).

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 (16 July 2026) fills that frame in. Its **Article 1** is the key to scope, because it lists exactly which legislation pulls a product into the registry:

| Products caught by the registry | Legal hook |
|--------------------------------|------------|
| Products covered by ESPR delegated acts | Art. 4, Reg. (EU) 2024/1781 |
| Batteries | Art. 77, Reg. (EU) 2023/1542 |
| Construction products | Art. 76, Reg. (EU) 2024/3110 |
| Toys | Art. 19, Reg. (EU) 2025/2509 |
| Detergents and end-user surfactants | Art. 21, Reg. (EU) 2026/405 |
| Any other product under Union law requiring a DPP and its registration | catch-all, Art. 1(1)(f) |

The implementing arrangements themselves cover: access management; the verification process for economic operators and value chain actors; the technical set-up including the **semantic repository**, the log system for data exchange models and software release management; registering and storing unique identifiers and commodity codes; **granularity level (model, batch, item)**; statuses of registered data; traceability data across granularity levels; update and deletion of registration data; personal data processing; anti-fraud measures; technical audits; and availability of the registry.

## The public web portal (Article 14) and customs (Article 15)

Two consequences of the DPP that are easy to miss because they sit outside the Registry:

- **Article 14 -- public web portal.** The Commission must set up and manage a **publicly accessible web portal** letting stakeholders **search for and compare** data in digital product passports, consistent with the access rights set in the relevant delegated act. This is a separate surface from the Registry.
- **Article 15 -- customs.** Anyone placing a covered product under "release for free circulation" must provide customs with the product's **unique registration identifier**, from the moment the Registry is operational. Customs may release the goods only after verifying that the identifier and commodity code match what is stored in the Registry. That verification becomes electronic and automatic once the Registry is interconnected with **EU CSW-CERTEX** (the EU Customs Single Window, Reg. (EU) 2022/2399) -- an interconnection due **within four years of the entry into force of the Article 13(5) implementing act**, which is Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778.

For importers, Article 15 is the point at which the DPP stops being a documentation exercise and becomes a border condition.

## Ecodesign Forum and Member States Expert Group (Articles 19-20)

**Article 19** requires the Commission to establish an **Ecodesign Forum** as an expert group, with balanced participation of experts designated by Member States and of all interested parties -- industry including SMEs and craft industry, social enterprises, trade unions, traders, retailers, importers, consumer and environmental organisations, circular-economy actors, European standardisation organisations and researchers.

The Forum contributes in particular to:

1. preparing ecodesign requirements;
2. preparing working plans;
3. examining the effectiveness of established market surveillance mechanisms;
4. assessing self-regulation measures;
5. assessing the prohibition of destruction of unsold consumer products beyond those listed in Annex VII.

**Article 20** requires the Commission to establish a **Member States Expert Group** as a **subgroup of the Ecodesign Forum**, composed of experts designated by the Member States.

This is the formal consultation channel through which ESPR delegated acts are prepared, so it is where a sector first sees the shape of its future requirements. Recital material also records that the Commission should give financial support to SME representatives, in particular microenterprises, so they can participate effectively in the Forum.

## The battery passport: 71 data points

The battery passport is the only digital product passport with its data content already fixed in law, which makes it the worked example for every sector that follows. It carries **71 data points**, set out in **Annex XIII of the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542** and mandatory from **18 February 2027**.

Annex XIII splits them by who may read them, and that split is the part operators most often get wrong: a battery passport is not one public record but four access tiers over the same product.

| Annex XIII point | Data points | Who may read them |
|------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | 28 | the general public |
| 2 | 5 | notified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the Commission |
| 3 | 1 | persons with a legitimate interest, and the Commission |
| 4 | 20 | persons with a legitimate interest, and the Commission (performance and durability data) |

The remaining points are dynamic values recorded over the battery's life rather than fixed at placing on the market.

What the 71 cover, in order: identification and manufacturer details (points 1 to 9); physical characteristics, weight, capacity, chemistry, hazardous substances and usable extinguishing agent (10 to 14); critical raw materials, carbon footprint declaration and label, responsible sourcing, and the recycled-content shares for cobalt, lithium and nickel plus renewable content (15 to 24); electrochemical performance and durability, rated capacity, voltages, power capability, expected lifetime, temperature range, warranty period and round-trip efficiency (25 to 39); marking, EU declaration of conformity, prevention and management of waste batteries, dismantling and repair information, part numbers and exploded diagrams (40 to 50); and the state-of-health series, capacity fade, power fade, internal resistance, negative events and periodically recorded information (51 to 71).

**Applicability varies by battery type.** Not every data point applies to every battery: the Annex marks each against the battery categories it binds (LMT, industrial above 2 kWh, and electric-vehicle batteries), and a passport is complete only against its own type. In Brubru's own dataset, **13 of the 71 carry an ambiguous applicability reading** and should be checked against the Annex text before an operator relies on them.

Brubru serves these as data, one row per point with its legal source and applicability, at `/api/v2/dpp/data-points`, filterable by category and battery type.

## Working Plan 2025-2030

**Article 18** governs the working plan: it must cover at least three years, be regularly updated, be presented to the European Parliament in draft before adoption, and the Commission must report to Parliament and Council annually on progress. The Commission consults the Ecodesign Forum and the Member States Expert Group on it (Art. 18(3) and Art. 19).

Article 18(5) sets the **statutory** priority list for the first working plan, which was due by **19 April 2025**: (a) iron and steel; (b) aluminium; (c) **textiles, in particular garments and footwear**; (d) furniture, including mattresses; (e) tyres; (f) detergents; (g) paints; (h) lubricants; (i) chemicals; (j) energy-related products for first-time requirements or review of measures under Directive 2009/125/EC; and (k) ICT products and other electronics. If the Commission drops one of these or adds another, it must justify that in the working plan.

Article 18(6) carries a separate obligation on **cement**: where the Construction Products Regulation lacks adequate environmental and carbon footprint requirements, the Commission shall set ecodesign requirements for cement in a delegated act **not earlier than 31 December 2028 and not later than 1 January 2030**.

The first ESPR and Energy Labelling Working Plan was adopted on 16 April 2025 (SWD(2025)130). It prioritises product categories for delegated acts:

| Wave | Product Groups | Expected Delegated Act |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| First (2025-2026) | Iron and steel, Aluminium, Textiles and apparel, Furniture, Mattresses, Tyres | 2026 |
| First (2025-2026) | 16 energy-related products (carried over from 2009 Directive) | 2025-2026 |
| Second (2027) | Electronics (smartphones, tablets, laptops), Detergents, Paints, Lubricants | 2027-2028 |
| Third (2028-2030) | Remaining product categories | 2028-2030 |

## DPP in Other EU Legislation

The DPP concept is not limited to ESPR. Several EU instruments mandate product passports in their own right, and Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 Article 1 is the authoritative list of those that feed the **same** registry:

| Legislation | CELEX | DPP requirement | Registry hook | Deadline |
|-------------|-------|-----------------|---------------|----------|
| Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 | 32023R1542 | Battery passport for EV and industrial batteries >2 kWh | Art. 77 | 18 February 2027 |
| Construction Products Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 | 32024R3110 | DPP for construction products | Art. 76 | Q2 2027 (indicative) |
| Toy Safety Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 | 32025R2509 | DPP for toys | Art. 19 | per that regulation |
| Detergents Regulation (EU) 2026/405 | 32026R0405 | DPP for detergents and end-user surfactants | Art. 21 | per that regulation |
| ESPR (EU) 2024/1781 | 32024R1781 | DPP for product groups via delegated acts | Art. 4 | 2027 onwards |
| PPWR (EU) 2025/40 | 32025R0040 | DPP for packaging | not listed in 2026/1778 Art. 1 | 2028-2029 |

Note the distinction: the first five are named in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 Article 1 as feeding the **central DPP registry**. Packaging under the PPWR is not in that list, so do not assume registry registration for packaging on the same basis; Art. 1(1)(f) leaves the door open for other Union law to be added.

## EPRS Study: DPP for Textiles

The European Parliamentary Research Service published a study on the DPP for the textile sector (PE 757.808, June 2024). Key findings: a DPP could enhance textile industry traceability, circularity, and transparency, benefiting producers, supply-chain tiers, regulatory authorities, sorters, recyclers, and consumers. The study proposes a three-phase deployment scenario.
