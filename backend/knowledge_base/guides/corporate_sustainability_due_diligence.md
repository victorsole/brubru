## Overview

The **Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)**, Directive (EU) 2024/1760, requires large companies to identify, prevent, mitigate, and account for adverse human rights and environmental impacts in their own operations, those of their subsidiaries, and those of their business partners throughout the value chain. It also mandates that companies adopt and implement transition plans aligned with the Paris Agreement to limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius.

The directive establishes a horizontal, cross-sectoral framework for corporate due diligence, complementing existing sector-specific rules (such as the Conflict Minerals Regulation and the Deforestation Regulation) and sustainability reporting obligations under the CSRD.

## Scope and Thresholds

### EU Companies

| Phase | Application Date | Employee Threshold | Turnover Threshold |
|-------|-----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Phase 1 | 26 July 2027 | >5,000 employees | >EUR 1,500M net worldwide turnover |
| Phase 2 | 26 July 2028 | >3,000 employees | >EUR 900M net worldwide turnover |
| Phase 3 (full) | 26 July 2029 | >1,000 employees | >EUR 450M net worldwide turnover |

### Non-EU Companies

Non-EU companies are subject to the directive if they meet EU turnover thresholds corresponding to each phase:

| Phase | Application Date | EU Turnover Threshold |
|-------|------------------|-----------------------|
| Phase 1 | 26 July 2027 | >EUR 1,500M net EU turnover |
| Phase 2 | 26 July 2028 | >EUR 900M net EU turnover |
| Phase 3 (full) | 26 July 2029 | >EUR 450M net EU turnover |

### Franchise and Licensing Arrangements

Companies whose business model is based on franchising or licensing are in scope if:
- Worldwide royalties exceed EUR 80M, AND
- Net EU turnover exceeds EUR 22.5M

### Financial Sector

The financial sector is included but with tailored rules. Financial undertakings (banks, insurers, asset managers) must apply due diligence to their own operations and upstream value chain. The downstream (investment/lending) side is subject to a review clause -- the Commission shall assess whether to extend due diligence obligations to downstream financial services by 26 July 2026.

## Key Obligations

### 1. Due Diligence Policy
Companies must adopt and put into effect a due diligence policy containing:
- A description of the company's approach to due diligence (including long-term)
- A code of conduct for employees and subsidiaries
- A description of the processes to implement due diligence, including measures to verify compliance with the code of conduct and extend it to business partners

### 2. Identifying and Assessing Adverse Impacts
- Map own operations, subsidiaries, and business partners in the value chain
- Identify and assess actual and potential adverse human rights and environmental impacts
- Prioritise identified impacts based on severity and likelihood

### 3. Prevention, Mitigation, and Remediation
- Take appropriate measures to prevent potential adverse impacts
- Where prevention is not possible, adequately mitigate impacts
- Bring actual adverse impacts to an end
- Provide remediation where the company has caused or contributed to an actual adverse impact

### 4. Stakeholder Engagement
- Consult affected stakeholders and their representatives when identifying, preventing, mitigating, and remediating adverse impacts
- Enable meaningful engagement, including with trade unions, civil society, and indigenous peoples

### 5. Complaints Mechanism
- Establish and maintain a complaints procedure accessible to:
  - Persons affected or with reasonable grounds to believe they might be affected
  - Trade unions and civil society organisations active in the areas concerned
  - Business partners in the value chain

### 6. Monitoring
- Periodically assess own operations, subsidiaries, and business partners
- Verify adequacy and effectiveness of due diligence measures
- Conduct assessments at least every 12 months and whenever there are reasonable grounds to believe new risks may have arisen

### 7. Reporting and Communication
- Publish an annual statement on due diligence on the company's website
- For companies subject to CSRD reporting, this obligation is fulfilled through the sustainability report under Article 19a/29a of the Accounting Directive

### 8. Climate Transition Plan
- Adopt and put into effect a transition plan for climate change mitigation
- Plan must ensure the company's business model and strategy are compatible with:
  - The transition to a sustainable economy
  - The Paris Agreement goal of limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius
- Include time-bound targets for 2030 and five-year steps up to 2050
- Include a description of decarbonisation levers and key actions planned

## Adverse Impacts Covered

### Human Rights
Impacts on internationally recognised human rights as set out in:
- The International Bill of Human Rights (UDHR + ICCPR + ICESCR)
- ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work
- Additional instruments listed in Part I, Section 1 of the Annex

Examples: forced labour, child labour, unsafe working conditions, exploitation, inadequate living wages, interference with freedom of association.

### Environmental Impacts
Impacts defined by reference to international environmental instruments listed in Part I, Section 2 and Part II of the Annex:
- Biodiversity loss and ecosystem degradation (CBD)
- Pollution and contamination (Minamata Convention, Stockholm Convention, Basel Convention)
- Climate change (UNFCCC, Paris Agreement)
- Deforestation and land degradation

## Enforcement and Penalties

### Administrative Supervision
- Member States must designate one or more supervisory authorities
- Authorities may initiate investigations, conduct inspections, and order cessation of infringements
- Supervisory authorities cooperate across Member States via the European Network of Supervisory Authorities

### Financial Penalties
- Up to **5% of net worldwide turnover** of the company
- Must be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive
- When deciding on penalties, authorities consider: severity and duration, remediation measures taken, investments made, collaboration with the supervisory authority, and previous infringements

### Civil Liability
- Companies are liable for damages caused to natural or legal persons by failure to comply with due diligence obligations (Articles 7 and 8)
- Applies where the company intentionally or negligently failed to prevent, mitigate, or bring to an end adverse impacts
- Right to full compensation, including lost profits
- Limitation period: minimum 5 years from when the damage becomes known or ought reasonably to have become known
- Access to justice: reasonable costs, injunctive relief available, representative actions under Directive (EU) 2020/1828

### Naming and Shaming
- Supervisory authorities may publish their findings and decisions, identifying the company concerned

## Key Changes from Commission Proposal

The final text was significantly narrowed from the Commission's original February 2022 proposal:

- **Higher thresholds**: Original proposal covered companies with >500 employees and >EUR 150M turnover. Final text raised this to >1,000 employees and >EUR 450M turnover
- **Financial sector**: Downstream due diligence for financial services deferred to review clause rather than included from the outset
- **Phased application**: Three-phase roll-out (2027-2029) instead of immediate application
- **Civil liability**: Scope refined to focus on failures under Articles 7 and 8 (prevention and mitigation)
- **Directors' duties**: Provisions on directors' duty of care for sustainability removed in final negotiations
- **Value chain**: Scope refined to focus on "chain of activities" rather than the broadest value chain concept

## Transposition Status (as of March 2026)

Member States have until 26 July 2026 to transpose the directive into national law. As of March 2026, transposition is ongoing across the EU-27. Some Member States (notably France and Germany) already have national due diligence laws:

- **France**: Loi de vigilance (2017) -- covers companies with >5,000 employees in France or >10,000 worldwide
- **Germany**: Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz (LkSG, 2023) -- covers companies with >1,000 employees
- **Netherlands**: Wet verantwoord en duurzaam internationaal ondernemen (pending)

These national laws will need to be aligned with the CSDDD by the transposition deadline.

## Practical Implications for Public Affairs

- **Corporate compliance teams**: Must map value chains, establish due diligence processes, set up complaints mechanisms, and adopt climate transition plans before the applicable phase date
- **Trade associations**: Sector-specific guidance and collective action on supply chain mapping are key deliverables
- **Civil society organisations**: Gain standing under the complaints mechanism and can bring representative actions for civil liability
- **SMEs**: Not directly in scope but affected as business partners of in-scope companies; the directive requires that due diligence requirements passed to SMEs be proportionate
- **Member State transposition**: Advocacy opportunities during national transposition to influence implementation details, designation of supervisory authorities, and penalty regimes
