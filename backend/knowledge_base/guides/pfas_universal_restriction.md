# The Universal PFAS Restriction Proposal (REACH "Forever Chemicals" Restriction)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Status as of July 2026: STILL A PROPOSAL, NOT ADOPTED.** No PFAS group restriction is in force under REACH. Do not describe it as a ban, a law, or in force.
- What it is: a proposed EU-wide restriction under the REACH Regulation covering per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) as a single chemical group, roughly 10,000 individual substances, rather than substance-by-substance as REACH normally proceeds.
- Legal basis: Article 69(4) and Annex XV of the REACH Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (CELEX 32006R1907). If adopted, it would add a new entry to REACH Annex XVII (the list of restricted substances).
- Dossier submitters: five national competent authorities acting jointly: **Germany (BAuA)**, **the Netherlands (RIVM)**, **Denmark (Danish Environmental Protection Agency)**, **Norway (Norwegian Environment Agency)**, **Sweden (KEMI)**. Norway participates as an EEA/EFTA state with full REACH obligations, not as an EU member state.
- Submitted to ECHA: **13 January 2023**. ECHA published the Annex XV restriction report and supporting documents: **7 February 2023**.
- Public consultation on the original proposal: **22 March to 25 September 2023** (six months). It drew a **record 5,642 comments from roughly 4,400 organisations and individuals**, the highest number ECHA has ever received on a restriction dossier (the previous record, the microplastics restriction, drew under 500).
- Who assesses it: ECHA's two scientific committees, the **Committee for Risk Assessment (RAC)** and the **Committee for Socio-Economic Analysis (SEAC)**, working sector by sector.
- RAC adopted its **final opinion on 2-3 March 2026**: it concludes existing measures are insufficient and an EU-wide restriction is needed, with any derogations paired with risk-management measures (PFAS management plans, supply-chain communication, labelling, emissions reporting).
- SEAC adopted its **draft opinion on 10 March 2026**: it supports a restriction with **targeted, use-specific derogations** where alternatives are not yet available.
- ECHA published both opinions and opened a further **60-day consultation on SEAC's draft opinion**: 26 March to 25 May 2026, drawing 3,511 comments from over 3,200 organisations and 250 individuals (Germany, Japan and Belgium the largest sources).
- Next steps: ECHA aims to deliver the final RAC and SEAC opinions to the European Commission by the end of 2026. **The Commission's own decision, taken with EU member states via the REACH comitology (regulatory committee) procedure, is not expected before 2027.**
- Official ECHA hub: [echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/perfluoroalkyl-chemicals-pfas](https://echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/perfluoroalkyl-chemicals-pfas)

## This is different from other EU PFAS measures already in force
This guide covers the single universal REACH restriction dossier described above. Three other PFAS measures are separate legal instruments and should not be confused with it:

1. **Drinking water and surface water limits** under the recast Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184/EU (PFAS monitoring mandatory from 12 January 2026) and the Water Framework Directive's Environmental Quality Standards. These are already in force. Covered in full in `water_pollution_pfas`, not repeated here.
2. **PFHxA restriction**, Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/2462 of 19 September 2024 (CELEX [32024R2462](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R2462)), REACH Annex XVII entry 79. **Already adopted and in force.** Restricts undecafluorohexanoic acid (PFHxA), its salts and related substances above 25 ppb (PFHxA and salts) or 1,000 ppb (related substances) in textiles, footwear, cosmetics, food-contact paper and cardboard, general-public mixtures, and firefighting foam used for training, testing, public fire services and civil aviation. Transition periods run to October 2026 and October 2027 depending on the product category.
3. **PFAS in firefighting foams restriction**, Commission Regulation (EU) 2025/1988 of 2 October 2025 (CELEX [32025R1988](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1988)), REACH Annex XVII entry 82. **Already adopted and in force.** Bans the placing on the market and use of firefighting foams containing PFAS at or above 1 mg/L (sum of all PFAS) from **23 October 2030**, with earlier phased deadlines by sector (18 months for municipal fire brigades and training, up to 10 years for Seveso-III establishments and offshore installations). This restriction started life as a 2020 Commission mandate to ECHA under Article 69(1) REACH, predating and running on a separate legal track from the 2023 universal PFAS dossier.

Both of these adopted measures show how narrower, faster REACH restrictions can move ahead of the much larger universal dossier while it is still under scientific committee review.

## What counts as PFAS in the dossier
The submitting authorities use the OECD-aligned structural definition: any substance containing at least one fully fluorinated methyl (CF3-) or methylene (-CF2-) carbon atom, with no hydrogen, chlorine, bromine or iodine attached. This structural, group-based definition is what pulls in an estimated 10,000+ individual substances, from well-known ones like PFOA and PFOS (already restricted individually as Persistent Organic Pollutants) to fluoropolymers, refrigerant gases, and countless industrial and consumer-product PFAS that have never been individually assessed.

## How the assessment is structured
Because a single restriction covering 10,000+ substances across the entire EU economy is unprecedented in scale, RAC and SEAC split their review into sectors rather than assessing the dossier as one block. The 2023 proposal originally identified **14 use sectors** plus PFAS manufacturing: consumer mixtures and articles, cosmetics, ski wax, metal plating and manufacture of metal products, textiles/upholstery/leather/apparel/carpets, food-contact materials and packaging, petroleum and mining, fluorinated gases, medical devices, transport, electronics and semiconductors, energy, construction products, and lubricants.

During the 2023 consultation, stakeholders flagged **8 further sectors** not named in the original dossier: printing applications, sealing applications, machinery applications, other medical applications, military applications, explosives, technical textiles, and broader industrial uses. The dossier submitters folded these into an updated **Draft Background Document (Version 14, published 20 August 2025)** incorporating all 5,600+ original comments. ECHA confirmed on 27 August 2025 that RAC and SEAC would **not** run standalone sector-specific evaluations of these 8 additional sectors, since doing so would push the timeline well past 2026; instead they remain in scope and are addressed through horizontal, group-wide risk-management measures (PFAS management plans, supply-chain communication, emissions reporting) rather than sector-tailored derogations.

Electronics and semiconductors drew the single largest share of comments in the 2026 SEAC consultation (12.7% of submissions), reflecting industry concern about the availability of PFAS-free alternatives in chip fabrication.

## The industry and health debate
**Industry position:** chemical and electronics manufacturers, represented by groups such as Plastics Europe's Fluoropolymer Group, argue the group-based approach is too blunt for a chemical family with widely varying persistence, toxicity and use profiles. Their central asks are: exempt fluoropolymers (which some argue behave as "polymers of low concern" distinct from other PFAS), lengthen transition periods, and expand use-specific derogations, particularly for semiconductors, medical devices, renewable-energy components and defence applications where alternatives are said not yet to exist at the required performance level. Industry also warns of competitiveness and reshoring risk if the EU restricts PFAS faster than other jurisdictions.

**Health and environmental position:** NGOs including the European Environmental Bureau, ChemSec, HEAL and CHEM Trust argue the restriction should not be watered down by broad derogations, pointing to PFAS persistence (they do not break down in the environment, hence "forever chemicals"), bioaccumulation in blood and soil, and rising remediation and health costs. A January 2026 Commission-commissioned study estimated EU-wide PFAS-related health costs at roughly EUR 39.5 billion a year, with cumulative costs reaching EUR 440 billion by 2050 under a business-as-usual scenario (see `water_pollution_pfas` for the full water-pollution cost breakdown).

RAC's March 2026 final opinion sided with the case for an EU-wide restriction in principle; the live fight for the rest of 2026 and into the 2027 Commission decision is over the scope of derogations and transition periods, not over whether a restriction happens at all.
