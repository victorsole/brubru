## Overview

The European Health Data Space is the EU's first sector-specific common data space under the European Data Strategy. Its primary goal is to give individuals effective control over their electronic health records across borders while enabling a trusted framework for researchers, innovators, and policymakers to access health data for public interest purposes.

The EHDS was negotiated over nearly three years, with the main political controversies centring on: the scope of opt-out rights for secondary use, the role of industry (pharmaceutical companies, health tech firms) as eligible secondary use data users, and the mandatory data-sharing obligations placed on health data holders including private hospitals and health insurers.

The EHDS sits alongside the Data Governance Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/868), the Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854), and the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) as part of the broader EU data and digital governance architecture.

## Pillar 1: Primary Use (MyHealth@EU)

Primary use concerns the patient's own health data: the right to access, share, and control it across borders. Key provisions:

- Citizens can access their electronic health record (EHR) through national patient portals connected to MyHealth@EU
- Cross-border sharing: when a patient consults a doctor in another Member State, the treating professional can access relevant EHR data (patient summary, ePrescription, lab results, medical images) with patient consent
- Priority data categories for cross-border exchange: patient summaries, ePrescriptions, dispensations, medical images and image reports, laboratory results, hospital discharge reports
- MyHealth@EU is the central infrastructure maintained by the Commission; it went live for ePrescriptions and patient summaries in 11 Member States by 2025
- Patients can request corrections to their EHR and restrict access to specific data elements
- Healthcare providers must use the European EHR exchange format (EEHRxF); compliance timeline: 2027-2029 by data category

## Pillar 2: Secondary Use (HealthData@EU)

Secondary use covers access to health data by third parties for research, innovation, policy, statistics, or education. Key provisions:

- Each Member State must designate a Health Data Access Body (HDAB) responsible for handling data access applications
- HDABs grant access through secure processing environments (no raw data leaves the environment; results only)
- Eligible secondary use purposes: scientific research (public and private), development of products and services, policy-making, statistics, training of AI/algorithms, patient safety surveillance
- Applicants include: academic researchers, pharmaceutical companies, health tech companies, public authorities, international organisations
- Opt-out: Member States must establish mechanisms for individuals to opt out of secondary use for most purposes. Opt-out does not apply to public health emergency uses.
- Genetic and genomic data: initially excluded from mandatory secondary use sharing; inclusion subject to a Commission review by March 2029
- Cross-border: HealthData@EU connects national HDABs. Multi-country studies can be coordinated through a single access point.

## Relationship with GDPR and Other Legislation

The EHDS is lex specialis with respect to Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) and Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 (EU institutions GDPR). It clarifies and extends derogations under GDPR Article 9(2)(h) and (i) for health data processing. The legal bases for secondary use are GDPR Article 6(1)(e) (public task) or Article 9(2)(j) (scientific research), combined with EHDS-specific safeguards.

The EHDS also intersects with the AI Act: health data sets made available through HealthData@EU may be used to train AI systems under the AI Act's general-purpose AI provisions, subject to prohibited use restrictions.
