# Revision of EU Social Security Coordination: Regulation (EU) 2026/2359, published 27 October 2026, in force 1 November 2026

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: the regulation amending **Regulation (EC) No 883/2004** on the coordination of social security systems (CELEX 32004R0883) and its implementing **Regulation (EC) No 987/2009** (CELEX 32009R0987). These rules decide **which ONE country's social security law applies** to people who live or work across EU borders, and how benefits (unemployment, sickness, long-term care, family) are paid between countries.
- **Procedure**: **2016/0397(COD)**, Commission proposal **COM(2016) 815** of 13 December 2016. Lead committee: EMPL (Employment and Social Affairs); rapporteur **Gabriele Bischoff (S&D)**. Shadows: Jeroen Lenaers (EPP), Marie Dauchy (PfE), Francesco Torselli (ECR), Jana Toom (Renew), Gordan Bosanac (Greens/EFA), Rudi Kennes (The Left).
- **Status (read in the Official Journal, 27 October 2026): PUBLISHED as Regulation (EU) 2026/2359 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 7 October 2026 (OJ L, 2026/2359, 27 October 2026; CELEX 32026R2359; ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/2359/oj ). In force? NOT YET as of 27 October 2026: Article 3 says it enters into force on the first day of the month following publication, so on Sunday 1 November 2026.** Its rules apply in steps, set by the amended Article 91 of Regulation 883/2004 and Article 97 of Regulation 987/2009: a first block applies from **1 November 2026** (in 883/2004: Articles 11(5), 50(2), 72(ea), 75a, 86, 87a(2), 87b, 88, 88a, 88b, 88c and Annexes I to IV, VI and VIII to XI; in 987/2009: Articles 1(2), 2(2a), (5) and (6), 3(3), 19(2) and (3), 26(4), 43(3), 65, 66(2), 68(2), 75(1), (3) and (4), 85a, 86, 86a, 87(4) and (6), 87a, 89(3), 92, 93, 94a, 96(1) and (1a) and Annex 1); a second block applies from **2 November 2028** (in 883/2004: Article 1 points (i)(1)(ii), (va) and (vb), Articles 3(1)(a), 9(1), 11(2), 12, 13(4a) and (5), 19(1), 20(1), 30, 32(3), 33a, 34, 60a, 61, 62, 63, 64(1)(c) and (3), 64a, 65, 65a, 68(2) and (2a), 68b and Annexes XII and XIII; in 987/2009: Articles 5(2) to (5), 14 (parts), 15, 16 (parts), 19a, 20(3) and (4), 25(1) and (3), 31(2), 32(1), 54(1), 54a, 55 (parts), 56 (parts), 56a, 67(3) and (7), 70, 72(1), 73, 77, 78 (parts), 79 to 82, 84, 85(1) and (1a) and Annexes 5 and 6); **Article 64 of 987/2009 applies from 1 January 2027**. Luxembourg keeps the pre-2026 rules for its former frontier workers (Articles 65, 65a and 86 of 883/2004; Articles 56 and 70 and Annex 5 of 987/2009) **until 2 November 2031**, extendable by notification to 2 November 2033. Parliament's position: 7 July 2026 (T10-0239/2026, 511 to 87, 61 abstentions); Council approval 28 September 2026. Two Commission statements published with it in OJ C: **C/2026/5549** on control, activation and job placement of unemployed persons exporting benefits (the Commission will propose EESSI data-exchange enhancements so that standardised fields confirming active job-seeking are operational by the date of application) and **C/2026/5553** on seasonal workers. **What was read**: Article 3 of the Regulation, the amended Articles 91 and 97, and the two statements; the substantive amendments were NOT re-read from the OJ text (the section below rests on the OEIL summary of Parliament's position). Brubru's EU Law Comply has no cluster for this act yet (27 October 2026).
- **Why it matters**: the file took **almost ten years**. Parliament debated and voted on it in April 2019 without the procedure closing, then resumed it in October 2019 and again in November 2024; EMPL approved the finally agreed text on 6 May 2026 (OEIL key events).
- **Do NOT confuse it with ESSPASS.** The **European Social Security Pass** is a SEPARATE Commission **proposal** of 15 September 2026 (part of the Fair Labour Mobility Package, `COM(2026) 980`), which also amends 883/2004 and 987/2009 to digitalise documents. ESSPASS is NOT law; this revision IS published law (in force 1 November 2026). See `fair_labour_mobility_package`.

## What changes (from Parliament's adopted text, OEIL summary of T10-0239/2026)

**One applicable law.** A person is subject to the legislation of a single Member State only. People receiving cash benefits because of their work are treated as still pursuing that activity (NOT for invalidity, old-age or survivors' pensions, accident-at-work or occupational-disease pensions, or long-term care cash benefits).

**Posted workers (the A1 rules).**
- A worker sent by their employer to another Member State stays under the home country's law if the foreseeable posting is **no longer than 24 months** and they do not replace another posted worker.
- **Anti-fraud condition**: the worker must have been **affiliated to the home country's social security for at least three months** before the posting.
- Someone working in one Member State while receiving unemployment benefit from another is covered by the Member State paying the benefit.

**Prior notification.** Working in a Member State other than the competent one must be **notified in advance** to the competent institution, with an attestation. **Exemption: business trips and short postings of up to three days, but NOT in the construction sector**, which is always covered.

**Work in two or more Member States.** To locate an undertaking's real seat, authorities look at where essential decisions are taken and central administration is carried out, using factors such as **turnover**, where **general meetings** are held and the habitual nature of the activity. The target is **letterbox companies**.

**Unemployment benefits.**
- A jobseeker can **export unemployment benefit for six months** from the date they leave the employment services of the Member State they left, extendable up to the end of their entitlement.
- **Cross-border (frontier) workers**: after an **uninterrupted 22 weeks** of employment, self-employment or insurance in the Member State of work (not residence), **that Member State pays** the unemployment benefit.

**Long-term care.** A stable legal framework with a **definition and list of long-term care benefits**, coordinated broadly like sickness benefits. Insured people and their families staying in another Member State receive the benefits in kind needed on medical or care grounds, paid for by the competent institution.

**Family benefits.** A clearer line between **family benefits in cash that replace income** when a parent stops or reduces work to raise a child, and other family benefits, meant to remove disincentives to sharing childcare.

**Digitalisation and cooperation.** Employers or the persons concerned must be able to **request the determination of applicable legislation fully online**. Member States should share information promptly to detect errors and fraud.
