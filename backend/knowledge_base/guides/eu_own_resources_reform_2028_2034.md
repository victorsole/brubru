# EU Own Resources Reform: how the EU budget is financed and the new resources debate

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: "Own resources" are the EU's own revenue -- the money that finances the EU budget directly, as distinct from national contributions negotiated line by line
- Legal basis: Article 311 TFEU (own resources system) + Article 312 TFEU (MFF ceilings); the system is set out in a Council Decision requiring **unanimity in Council, EP consent, and ratification by all 27 Member States** under their own constitutional requirements
- Current legal act: **Council Decision (EU, Euratom) 2020/2053** of 14 December 2020 "on the system of own resources of the European Union", repealing Decision 2014/335/EU, Euratom (CELEX 32020D2053)
- Own resources ceilings under the current Decision: **1.40% of EU GNI for payment appropriations, 1.46% of EU GNI for commitment appropriations** (raised from 1.20%/1.26% mainly to reflect the UK's withdrawal), plus a temporary headroom increase of 0.6 percentage points reserved exclusively to guarantee NextGenerationEU (NGEU) borrowing
- Four existing own resources: (1) **Traditional Own Resources (TOR)** -- mainly customs duties on imports from outside the EU, with a share (currently 20%) retained by Member States as collection costs; (2) **VAT-based resource** -- a uniform 0.30% call rate on each Member State's harmonised VAT base, capped at 50% of that state's GNI; (3) **GNI-based resource** -- the balancing, residual resource applying a uniform rate to national GNI, by far the largest single source (over 70% of EU revenue); (4) **Plastic packaging waste own resource** -- EUR 0.80 per kilogram of non-recycled plastic packaging waste, in force since 1 January 2021, with lump-sum rebates for lower-income Member States
- Reform driver: the EU must start repaying the principal and interest on the EUR 800 billion+ **NextGenerationEU (NGEU)** borrowing (Recovery and Resilience Facility) between **2028 and 2058**, without crowding out policy spending or overloading the GNI resource, which already falls disproportionately on a handful of net contributors
- New own resources proposed by the Commission as part of the **MFF 2028-2034 package (COM(2025) 570/571 final, 16 July 2025)**, expected to generate roughly **EUR 58.2 billion per year**: an **ETS-based resource** (30% of ETS 1 auctioning revenue, c. EUR 9.6 billion/year), a **tobacco excise duty resource (TEDOR)** (15% call rate on manufactured tobacco, c. EUR 11.2 billion/year), an **e-waste own resource** (new levy on electronic waste, c. EUR 15.0 billion/year), a **corporate resource (CORE)** (annual lump sum on companies with net turnover above EUR 100 million, c. EUR 6.8 billion/year), and a **CBAM-based resource** (75% of Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism revenue, c. EUR 1.4 billion/year); plus an estimated EUR 14.3 billion/year from adjustments to existing resources (cutting the TOR collection-cost retention from 20% to 10%, and raising the plastics call rate to EUR 1/kg from 2028)
- This is the **second-generation** own resources push: an earlier December 2021 Commission proposal (ETS + CBAM + a since-abandoned "BEFIT" corporate-tax-base resource) stalled in Council; the July 2025 package supersedes and widens it
- Procedure: any new own resources decision needs Council unanimity, EP consultation (not co-decision) and ratification by all 27 national parliaments -- a high bar historically taking 2-3 years even after political agreement
- Status as of writing: under negotiation alongside the MFF Regulation itself; Council remains divided, especially on CORE (opposed by low-corporate-tax and non-eurozone states) and TEDOR (opposed by tobacco-growing and low-excise Member States). Verify the live negotiating state before citing a "final" own resources basket.

Own resources are the plumbing behind every EU spending line -- without agreement on how the money comes in, the MFF 2028-2034 ceilings agreed by BUDG and Council cannot be financed, and the NGEU repayment bill falls back onto Member States' GNI contributions.

## Why This Reform Exists

Since 2021, the EU has borrowed on capital markets to fund NextGenerationEU (formally the Recovery and Resilience Facility), the EUR 800 billion-plus post-pandemic recovery instrument. That debt is real EU-level debt, guaranteed by the own resources ceiling itself, and it has to be repaid: principal and interest from the EU budget between 2028 and 2058. The MFF 2028-2034 proposal factors in EUR 149.3 billion of NGEU debt-servicing within its EUR 1.763 trillion total (see `mff_2028_2034`).

Two options exist to cover this: (a) raise GNI-based national contributions further, which is politically toxic for net-contributor Member States, or (b) create new EU-level revenue streams that do not require an equivalent political fight every year. The Commission has consistently preferred option (b), tying new own resources to policies the EU already runs for other reasons -- emissions trading, carbon border pricing, the single market for tobacco and electronic goods -- so that a portion of revenue the EU's own policies generate flows back to the EU budget rather than only to national treasuries.

## The Existing Own Resources System

| Resource | Mechanism | Share of EU revenue (approx.) | Legal basis |
|----------|-----------|-------------------------------|-------------|
| Traditional Own Resources (TOR) | Customs duties on non-EU imports; Member States retain a collection-cost share (currently 20%) | Roughly 10-15% | Decision 2020/2053, Article 2(1)(a) |
| VAT-based resource | Uniform 0.30% call rate on the harmonised VAT base, capped at 50% of national GNI | Roughly 10-12% | Decision 2020/2053, Article 2(1)(b) |
| GNI-based resource | Balancing/residual resource: uniform rate on national GNI, calculated to cover whatever TOR and VAT do not | Over 70% (the dominant source) | Decision 2020/2053, Article 2(1)(c) |
| Plastic packaging waste resource | EUR 0.80/kg on non-recycled plastic packaging waste, with rebates for lower-income states | Small but growing | Decision 2020/2053, Article 2(1)(d), in force since 1 Jan 2021 |

The GNI resource's dominance is precisely the problem the reform seeks to address: it makes the EU budget look, and function, like a set of national subscriptions rather than genuine EU-level revenue, and it concentrates political friction on a handful of large net-contributor Member States (Germany, the Netherlands, Austria, Sweden, Denmark among the historical "frugal" bloc).

## The New Own Resources on the Table (MFF 2028-2034 Package)

| Proposed own resource | Mechanism | Estimated annual revenue | Political sensitivity |
|------------------------|-----------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| ETS-based resource | 30% of Emissions Trading System (ETS 1) auctioning revenue to the EU budget | c. EUR 9.6 billion | Moderate -- interacts with Member States' own ETS revenue use (many earmark it for national climate spending) |
| CBAM-based resource | 75% of Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism revenue to the EU budget | c. EUR 1.4 billion | Low revenue but symbolically links a new own resource directly to a flagship EU policy (see `cbam_carbon_border_adjustment_mechanism`) |
| Tobacco excise duty resource (TEDOR) | 15% call rate on the excise duty Member States already levy on manufactured tobacco | c. EUR 11.2 billion | High -- opposed by tobacco-growing and low-excise Member States, seen as taxing a national excise base |
| E-waste own resource | New EU-level levy tied to electronic waste generation | c. EUR 15.0 billion | Moderate -- largest single new resource by value, limited prior EU precedent for a waste-stream-based levy of this kind |
| Corporate resource (CORE) | Annual lump-sum contribution from companies with net turnover above EUR 100 million | c. EUR 6.8 billion | High -- read by some Member States (and business groups) as a step toward EU-level corporate taxation; successor to the shelved BEFIT-linked resource from the 2021 proposal |

Additional revenue (c. EUR 14.3 billion/year) is expected from technical adjustments to existing resources rather than new instruments: reducing the TOR collection-cost retention rate from 20% to 10% (more customs revenue flows to the EU budget, less stays with national customs administrations), and raising the plastics own resource call rate to EUR 1/kg from 2028.

## Procedure and the Ratification Bottleneck

Unlike ordinary EU legislation, an Own Resources Decision is adopted under a special procedure set out in Article 311 TFEU:

1. The Commission proposes a Council Decision (part of the July 2025 MFF package, alongside COM(2025) 570 and 571 final).
2. The European Parliament is **consulted** (not a co-legislator; no amendment power, similar to the MFF Regulation's consent-only role but even lighter here -- consultation, not consent).
3. The Council must agree **unanimously**.
4. Every one of the 27 Member States must then **ratify the Decision domestically** in accordance with its own constitutional requirements -- a parliamentary vote in most Member States, sometimes requiring supermajorities or referenda depending on national constitutional law.

This ratification step is historically the slowest part of the process: the 2020/2053 Decision was agreed by the European Council in July 2020, formally adopted by Council in December 2020, but did not enter into force until **1 June 2021** once the last national ratification landed -- and even then only after a scramble, because NGEU borrowing could not begin until the Decision (which raises the own resources ceiling to allow it) was in force. Any new own resources decision tied to MFF 2028-2034 faces the same ratification bottleneck, meaning political agreement in Council (targeted for end-2026) does not equal money flowing to the EU budget on day one of the new MFF period.

## Political Fault Lines

- **Net contributors vs net beneficiaries**: net-contributor Member States broadly favour new own resources if they reduce pressure on GNI contributions; some net beneficiaries worry new resources could be used to justify lower overall MFF ambition.
- **Sectoral resistance**: tobacco-producing and low-excise Member States resist TEDOR; low-corporate-tax jurisdictions resist CORE as a precedent for EU-level corporate taxation; Member States that already earmark national ETS revenue for domestic climate transition resist ceding a share to the EU budget.
- **Sovereignty concerns**: several governments frame CORE and TEDOR as creeping EU taxation competence, an area where the Treaties preserve near-total national sovereignty (tax matters generally require unanimity under Article 113 TFEU); the Commission counters that own resources are budget financing, not tax harmonisation, since Member States retain full control over their own excise and corporate tax rates.
- **NGEU precedent fatigue**: some Member States that reluctantly accepted joint borrowing for NGEU as a one-off pandemic response are wary of normalising it, and by extension wary of a permanent revenue stream (like CORE) that resembles an EU-level tax base.
