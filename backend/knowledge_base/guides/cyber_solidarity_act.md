# EU Cyber Solidarity Act (Regulation (EU) 2025/38)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Brubru deep-dive explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers):** https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2025-38_cybersolidarity/index.html
- Regulation (EU) 2025/38 of 19 December 2024 "laying down measures to strengthen solidarity and capacities in the Union to detect, prepare for and respond to cyber threats and incidents and amending Regulation (EU) 2021/694 (Cyber Solidarity Act)". CELEX 32025R0038. OJ L, 2025/38, 15.1.2025. **In force since 4 February 2025** (Cellar; Commission policy page).
- Legal basis: **Article 173(3) and Article 322(1), point (a), TFEU** (industry and budget rules), NOT Article 114. Procedure 2023/0109(COD), proposal COM(2023)0209 of 18 April 2023, ITRE rapporteur Lina Gálvez (S&D). Parliament first reading 24 April 2024 (T9-0355/2024); Council adoption 2 December 2024.
- 26 articles in five chapters, plus one Annex that rewrites the cybersecurity parts of Annexes I and II of the Digital Europe Programme (DEP) Regulation (EU) 2021/694.
- **Three pillars**: (1) the **European Cybersecurity Alert System**, a voluntary pan-European network of National Cyber Hubs and Cross-Border Cyber Hubs (Chapter II); (2) the **Cybersecurity Emergency Mechanism**, with preparedness testing, other preparedness actions, the **EU Cybersecurity Reserve** and mutual assistance (Chapter III); (3) the **European Cybersecurity Incident Review Mechanism** run by ENISA (Chapter IV).
- It is a **funding and solidarity instrument**: all actions are financed under Specific Objective 3 of the DEP and implemented mainly through the ECCC (Bucharest), with the Reserve run by the Commission and ENISA. It imposes **no new obligations on companies**; participation in the Alert System and in preparedness testing is voluntary.
- **EU Cybersecurity Reserve**: response services from **trusted managed security service providers** (Article 17 lists 11 selection criteria), deployable on request in all Member States, for EU institutions (via CERT-EU) and for DEP-associated third countries. The contracting authority must answer requests from Member State and EU users **within 48 hours** (Article 16(1)). Support covers response and **initial** recovery only. Under a contribution agreement signed in 2025, the Commission entrusted ENISA with operating the Reserve; ENISA is its contracting authority (ENISA). ENISA reports that Moldova is so far the only eligible DEP-associated third country and that the Reserve was first deployed there in September 2025.
- Funding rates (Article 9): National Cyber Hub tools up to **50 %** of acquisition and **50 %** of operating costs; Cross-Border Cyber Hub (Hosting Consortium of at least **three** Member States) up to **75 %** of acquisition and **50 %** of operating costs. A Member State whose hub has not joined a Cross-Border Cyber Hub within **2 years** loses further support under that Chapter.
- Key deadlines: ENISA interoperability guidelines for Cross-Border Cyber Hubs **by 5 February 2026** (Article 6(4)); first Commission evaluation **by 5 February 2027**, then at least every **4 years** (Article 25); delegation of power on Reserve services for **5 years from 5 February 2025** (Article 23(2)).
- Corrigenda: C1 of 24 January 2025 (statement reference, C/2025/310); one of 15 October 2025 concerns the Hungarian version only; one of 6 November 2025 adds Specific Objective 6 to the amended Article 12(6) of the DEP Regulation. The consolidated text CELEX 02025R0038-20250115 includes only C1.
- Companion act adopted the same day: Regulation (EU) 2025/37 amending the Cybersecurity Act to add managed security services to EU certification. Once such a scheme exists, Reserve providers must be certified under it within 2 years of its date of application (Article 17(2)(j)).

## Structure and key numbers (read from the text)

### Chapter I: general provisions (Articles 1 and 2)
- Article 1: subject matter (the three pillars), general objectives (competitiveness, technological sovereignty, open strategic autonomy, skills) and specific objectives (detection and situational awareness; preparedness and response including for DEP-associated third countries; incident review). Actions complement the CSIRTs network, EU-CyCLONe and the NIS Cooperation Group (Article 1(4)). National security remains the sole responsibility of each Member State (Article 1(5)). Confidential information is shared only as relevant and proportionate (Article 1(6)).
- Article 2: 17 definitions, most pointing to NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555): significant cybersecurity incident (NIS2 Article 23(3)), large-scale cybersecurity incident (NIS2 Article 6(7)), managed security service provider (NIS2 Article 6(40)). New terms: Cross-Border Cyber Hub, Hosting Consortium, large-scale-equivalent cybersecurity incident (a major incident for EU institutions under Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2023/2841, or an incident beyond a DEP-associated third country's capacity), DEP-associated third country, contracting authority, trusted managed security service providers.

### Chapter II: European Cybersecurity Alert System (Articles 3 to 9)
- Article 3: a pan-European network of National and Cross-Border Cyber Hubs joining **on a voluntary basis**, to pool and analyse threat data, produce cyber threat intelligence, issue alerts and develop tools such as AI and data analytics. Funded by the DEP, Specific Objective 3.
- Article 4: a participating Member State designates one **National Cyber Hub**, a single entity under its authority (a CSIRT, cyber crisis authority, competent authority or another entity). It may cooperate with private entities and request telemetry, sensor and logging data where Union and national law allow; recital 14 says hubs are neither obliged nor empowered to enforce such requests.
- Article 5: at least three Member States form a **Hosting Consortium** and a **Cross-Border Cyber Hub** by written consortium agreement; other Member States may join; the ECCC keeps ownership of jointly procured tools.
- Articles 6 and 7: information sharing within and between Cross-Border Cyber Hubs, cooperation agreements between hubs, ENISA interoperability guidelines by 5 February 2026; close cooperation with the CSIRTs network; early warnings of potential or ongoing large-scale incidents through EU-CyCLONe and the CSIRTs network.
- Article 8: security of the network; sharing with non-public entities must not harm Union or Member State security interests.
- Article 9: ECCC calls for expressions of interest, joint procurement and grants (rates above); hosting and usage agreements; ECCC mapping of tools at least every 2 years.

### Chapter III: Cybersecurity Emergency Mechanism (Articles 10 to 20)
- Article 10: established to improve resilience and, in a spirit of solidarity, to prepare for and mitigate the short-term impact of significant, large-scale and large-scale-equivalent incidents; actions for Member States are on request and complementary. Implemented mainly through the ECCC; the Reserve by the Commission and ENISA.
- Article 11: four types of action: coordinated preparedness testing (Article 12), other preparedness (Article 13), Reserve response (Article 14), mutual assistance (Article 18).
- Article 12: **voluntary** coordinated preparedness testing (for example penetration testing and threat assessment) of entities in sectors of high criticality (NIS2 Annex I); the Commission identifies sectors after consulting the NIS Cooperation Group, EU-CyCLONe and ENISA; common risk scenarios developed by the NIS Cooperation Group with the Commission, the High Representative and ENISA; support mainly as grants.
- Article 13: other preparedness actions (vulnerability and risk monitoring, exercises, training), on request, mainly grants.
- Article 14: **EU Cybersecurity Reserve**: users are (a) Member States' cyber crisis management authorities and CSIRTs, (b) CERT-EU, (c) competent authorities of DEP-associated third countries. May include pre-committed services convertible into preparedness services if unused. Commission has overall responsibility and sets priorities with the NIS Cooperation Group, reviewed every 2 years; the Commission **shall entrust** operation and administration to ENISA in full or in part (Article 14(5)); ENISA maps needed services at least every 2 years; delegated acts may specify the types and number of response services (Article 14(7)).
- Article 15: requests go through the NIS2 single point of contact (Member States), directly from CERT-EU, or through a third country's single point of contact; requests must describe the entity, impact, service requested and mitigation already taken; ENISA prepares a request template.
- Article 16: response within **48 hours** for Member State and EU users; assessment criteria include scale and severity, type of entity (priority for essential entities), impact, cross-border spillover and the user's own measures; on a tie, Member State users go first; services provided under specific agreements with liability conditions, on ENISA templates; the Commission, ENISA and users bear **no contractual liability** for damage to third parties; users report within **2 months** after support ends; the contracting authority reports to the NIS Cooperation Group at least twice a year.
- Article 17: procurement principles and **11 criteria** for trusted providers, including integrity and competence, protection of classified information, transparent governance, security clearance where a Member State requires it, secure IT, hardware and software free of known exploitable vulnerabilities and compliant with the Cyber Resilience Act, relevant experience, short deployment time, language capability, certification under a future managed-security-services scheme within 2 years, and conversion conditions for unused services.
- Article 18: grants for technical **mutual assistance** from one Member State to another (including NIS2 Article 11(3)(f)); costs may be eligible before the grant application in duly justified cases (amended DEP Article 19).
- Article 19: support to **DEP-associated third countries** only where their DEP association agreement provides for it; the country reports on its cyber resilience within 3 months and yearly; the Commission assesses three criteria at least yearly; the **Council** authorises support by implementing act for at most one year (renewable, may cap support per request at no less than 75 days), as a rule within eight weeks of the Commission proposal.
- Article 20: coordination with the Union Civil Protection Mechanism (Decision No 1313/2013/EU) and the IPCR Arrangements (Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1993).

### Chapter IV: European Cybersecurity Incident Review Mechanism (Article 21)
- At the request of the Commission or EU-CyCLONe, ENISA, with the CSIRTs network and the approval of the Member States concerned, reviews a significant or large-scale incident and delivers an **incident review report** (causes, known exploitable vulnerabilities, lessons learned, recommendations where appropriate) to EU-CyCLONe, the CSIRTs network, the Member States concerned and the Commission. The report must not include details of actively exploited vulnerabilities that remain unpatched. ENISA may publish a public version.

### Chapter V: final provisions (Articles 22 to 26)
- Article 22: amends the DEP Regulation: new operational objectives (Alert System; Emergency Mechanism and Reserve), new budget split (Specific Objective 2, AI: EUR 1 760 806 000; Specific Objective 3, Cybersecurity and Trust: EUR 1 372 020 000; Specific Objective 4, Advanced Digital Skills: EUR 482 640 000), automatic carry-over of unused Reserve and mutual-assistance appropriations to the following year, derogations allowing third-country-controlled entities established in the Union in Alert System and Reserve actions where EU supply is at real risk and the security risk is proportionate, procurement on behalf of DEP-associated third countries and EU bodies, conflict rule (new Article 16a), direct grants.
- Article 23: delegated acts (5 years from 5 February 2025, tacitly extended). Article 24: DEP Coordination Committee. Article 25: evaluation by 5 February 2027 and every 4 years. Article 26: entry into force on the twentieth day after publication (4 February 2025).

## Implementation (primary sources)
- Commission policy page (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-solidarity): in force 4 February 2025; in a first phase launched in November 2022, two consortia of cross-border Security Operations Centres (now Cyber Hubs) were selected under the DEP.
- ENISA: the Commission entrusted ENISA with administering and operating the EU Cybersecurity Reserve under a contribution agreement signed in 2025, about EUR 36 million over three years; ENISA is the contracting authority. Moldova is so far the only eligible DEP-associated third country; first deployment there in September 2025.
