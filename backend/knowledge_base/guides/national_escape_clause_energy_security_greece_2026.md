# National escape clause extended to energy-security spending: Greece, Council Recommendation of 9 October 2026

## QUICK FACTS
- **What:** **Council Recommendation of 9 October 2026** allowing **Greece** to deviate from its maximum growth rates of net expenditure under **Regulation (EU) 2024/1263** (the reformed fiscal rules), Article 26 (**national escape clause**). Published in the **Official Journal on 16 October 2026, C/2026/5419**. It supersedes Recommendation C/2025/3965.
- **What is new:** the clause, first activated for Greece on **8 July 2025** for **defence** spending, now also covers **energy-security measures** decided after **28 February 2026** that cut dependence on imported fossil fuels.
- **The caps (the Council's figures, for ALL Member States using the energy flexibility):** the overall cap of **1.5% of GDP** stays unchanged and covers defence AND energy together; energy flexibility may not exceed **0.3% of GDP per year**, and **0.6% of GDP cumulatively by end-2028**.
- **Greece's request:** submitted **6 August 2026**, citing the repercussions of **military action in the Persian Gulf** on Europe's energy security. Greece's initial list of measures: photovoltaic panels, clean energy storage, heat pumps and water heaters, energy upgrades of public buildings and infrastructure, and railway improvements. The Commission's preliminary view is that these **could** fall within scope; eligibility is assessed later.
- **Reporting:** Greece reports **twice a year** (spring and autumn) the measures it considers eligible and their budgetary impact; the Commission checks them in its regular fiscal surveillance.
- **Policy basis (named in the recitals, not read here):** Commission communication of **3 June 2026** extending the clause's scope to energy security, and a Commission **Notice of 18 August 2026** with guidance.
- **Not read:** the operative points after recital (12) and the footnotes; no euro amount for Greece is stated in what was read.
