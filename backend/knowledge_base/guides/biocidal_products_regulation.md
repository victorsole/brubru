# Biocidal Products Regulation (BPR)

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Regulation (EU) No 528/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 22 May 2012 concerning the making available on the market and use of biocidal products
- CELEX: 32012R0528 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32012R0528)
- Type: Regulation, directly applicable in all Member States
- Entry into force / application: entered into force 17 July 2012, applied from 1 September 2013 (replaced the earlier Biocidal Products Directive 98/8/EC)
- Legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (internal market)
- Scope: biocidal products (disinfectants, preservatives, pest control products such as rodenticides and insecticides) and treated articles (everyday goods treated with a biocidal active substance, e.g. antibacterial textiles, treated paints, impregnated wood)
- Classification: Annex V splits biocidal products into **22 product-types grouped into four main groups**: Main Group 1 Disinfectants (product-types 1-5), Main Group 2 Preservatives (product-types 6-13), Main Group 3 Pest control (product-types 14-20), Main Group 4 Other biocidal products (product-types 21-22, antifouling and embalming/taxidermist fluids)
- Two-step system: (1) EU-level approval of the **active substance** for a given product-type, evaluated by a Member State competent authority and opined on by ECHA's Biocidal Products Committee (BPC), decided by the Commission; (2) national, Union, or mutual-recognition **authorisation of the finished biocidal product**, which may only contain approved active substances for that product-type
- Key agency: ECHA (European Chemicals Agency, Helsinki), which manages the process and hosts the Biocidal Products Committee (BPC)
- IT platform: R4BP 3 (Register for Biocidal Products), the mandatory submission portal for all active-substance and product applications (https://r4bp.echa.europa.eu)
- Legacy backlog: the "Review Programme" re-evaluating all active substances that were already on the market when the Biocidal Products Directive entered into force in 2000 (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 1062/2014, CELEX 32014R1062); still not complete as of 2026, more than a decade after the BPR's application date
- 2026 fix for the backlog: Regulation (EU) 2026/1165 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 May 2026 (CELEX 32026R1165), published in the Official Journal on 26 May 2026, extends the Article 95 data-protection periods for active substances still stuck in the Review Programme from 31 December 2025 to 31 December 2030, so suppliers are not exposed to free-riding while ECHA finishes the backlog. Originating proposal: COM(2025) 1020, CELEX 52025PC1020, presented 16 December 2025 as part of the Commission's Food and Feed Safety simplification package; EP rapporteur Pierfrancesco Maran (S&D, Italy, ENVI committee); EP adopted its first-reading position 591 votes to 54 (8 abstentions); Council formally adopted at first reading 11 May 2026
- Wider 2025-2026 context: BPR simplification also sits inside "Omnibus VI" (the chemicals simplification omnibus expected Q2-Q3 2026), which targets REACH, CLP, BPR and POPs together for procedural streamlining. See `omnibus_vi_chemicals_simplification`
- Official portal: https://echa.europa.eu/regulations/biocidal-products-regulation and https://health.ec.europa.eu/biocides/biocidal-products_en

Guide for professionals working on EU chemicals and product-safety compliance, covering the Biocidal Products Regulation: its two-step authorisation architecture, ECHA's operational role, the ongoing Review Programme backlog, treated-articles obligations, and the 2025-2026 simplification agenda.

## What counts as a "biocidal product"

Article 3 BPR defines a biocidal product as any substance or mixture, in the form in which it is supplied to the user, consisting of, containing, or generating one or more active substances, with the intention of destroying, deterring, rendering harmless, preventing the action of, or otherwise exerting a controlling effect on any harmful organism by any means other than mere physical or mechanical action. In practice this covers a very wide everyday market: hand sanitisers and surface disinfectants, wood preservatives, antifouling paints for boat hulls, rodenticides and insecticides, water-treatment biocides, preservatives added to paints, cosmetics and industrial fluids to stop microbial spoilage, and repellents.

**Treated articles** are a distinct, narrower category: everyday objects or materials (textiles, paints, plastics, footwear, construction materials) that have been treated with, or incorporate, a biocidal product primarily to protect the article itself (e.g. an antibacterial chopping board), not to control organisms as their main function. Treated articles are not biocidal products in their own right but are still regulated under BPR Article 58 (see below).

| **2. Preservatives** | PT6-13 | PT6 in-can preservatives, PT7 film preservatives, PT8 wood preservatives, PT9 fibre/leather/rubber/polymerised-materials preservatives, PT10 masonry preservatives, PT11 preservatives for liquid-cooling and processing systems, PT12 slimicides, PT13 metalworking-fluid preservatives |
| **3. Pest control** | PT14-20 | PT14 rodenticides, PT15 avicides, PT16 molluscicides, PT17 piscicides, PT18 insecticides/acaricides, PT19 repellents and attractants, PT20 control of other vertebrates |
| **4. Other biocidal products** | PT21-22 | PT21 antifouling products, PT22 embalming and taxidermist fluids |

A single active substance may be approved for more than one product-type, and each approval (and each downstream product authorisation) is product-type specific. This granularity is one reason the Review Programme backlog is so large: the same substance can require multiple, separately evaluated approvals.

## The two-step system

### Step 1: EU-level approval of the active substance

Before any biocidal product containing it can be authorised, the active substance itself must be approved by the Commission for the relevant product-type(s). The process:

1. An applicant (or, for legacy substances, a "participant") submits a dossier via R4BP 3 to an evaluating Member State competent authority (eCA)
2. The eCA carries out a scientific risk assessment and prepares a Competent Authority Report (CAR)
3. ECHA's Biocidal Products Committee (BPC), one of ECHA's five scientific committees, reviews the CAR and delivers an opinion
4. The Commission decides, via implementing act, whether to approve the active substance for that product-type, and under what conditions (approval periods typically run up to 10 years, renewable)

Approved active substances and their conditions are listed in the Union List of Approved Active Substances published by ECHA.

### Step 2: national, Union, or mutual-recognition authorisation of the finished product

Once its active substance(s) are approved, a finished biocidal product still needs its own market authorisation, following one of three routes:

- **National authorisation**: granted by a single Member State's competent authority, valid in that Member State only
- **Mutual recognition**: a product already authorised in one Member State can be recognised in others, either sequentially (after the first national authorisation) or in parallel (simultaneous applications across several Member States)
- **Union authorisation**: introduced as an option for most product-types by BPR, a single authorisation valid across the entire EU/EEA (plus Switzerland by bilateral arrangement), granted by the Commission after an ECHA/BPC opinion, avoiding the need for country-by-country applications. Especially useful for products marketed EU-wide with similar conditions of use

All applications, whichever route, are submitted and tracked through R4BP 3.

## ECHA and the Biocidal Products Committee (BPC)

ECHA (the European Chemicals Agency, Helsinki) runs the operational machinery of the BPR on behalf of the Commission and Member States. Its Biocidal Products Committee (BPC) is the scientific body that delivers opinions on active-substance approval, renewal and Union authorisation dossiers, alongside ECHA's other committees (RAC, SEAC, MSC) which handle REACH and CLP work. ECHA also runs a dedicated biocides enforcement subgroup (BPRS) under its cross-legislation Forum for Exchange of Information on Enforcement. For ECHA's full institutional role and committee structure, see `echa_agency_overview`.

## R4BP 3: the Register for Biocidal Products

R4BP 3 (https://r4bp.echa.europa.eu) is the mandatory IT platform for essentially all interactions under the BPR: active-substance applications, product authorisation applications (national, mutual recognition, Union), renewals, changes, and simple product notifications. Companies and competent authorities both use R4BP 3, alongside IUCLID (the shared REACH/CLP/biocides dossier-format tool) and the SPC (Summary of Product Characteristics) editor for the standardised product-characteristics document required for every authorised biocidal product.

Article 95 of the BPR additionally requires an "Article 95 list": active-substance suppliers and product suppliers whose substances participate in, or have completed, the Review Programme (or a recognised third-party dossier) must be listed by ECHA before their biocidal products can be placed on the market. Being on, or being excluded from, the Article 95 list is a hard market-access gate, independent of product authorisation itself.

## The Review Programme and its backlog

The Review Programme, set out in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) No 1062/2014 (CELEX 32014R1062), is the systematic re-evaluation of all active substance/product-type combinations that were already on the EU market when the original Biocidal Products Directive (98/8/EC) took effect around 2000. Substances in the programme benefit from transitional market access (BPR Article 89) while their EU-level approval decision is pending, subject to national rules.

More than a decade after BPR became applicable, the Review Programme is still not finished: many active substance/product-type combinations remain under evaluation. The knock-on effects are: (a) product authorisation cannot conclude until the underlying active-substance approval is decided, so delays cascade downstream, and (b) suppliers whose data was due to lose statutory protection (Article 95(5), originally 31 December 2025) faced a "free-rider" problem, where competitors could reference their studies at no cost while the review itself was still stuck in the queue. This is precisely what Regulation (EU) 2026/1165 addresses (see QUICK FACTS), extending protection to 31 December 2030 and creating a compensation mechanism for data owners.

## Treated articles (Article 58)

Article 58 BPR applies exclusively to treated articles that are not themselves biocidal products. Core obligations:

- The active substance(s) used to treat the article must be approved (or under review with a positive outcome expected) for the relevant product-type in at least one Member State or at Union level
- Labelling is mandatory whenever the treated article makes a biocidal-property claim (e.g. "antibacterial") or when specific conditions attached to the active substance's approval require it. Labels must use the approved BPR names for the active substances involved, as published in the substance's approval regulation
- Manufacturers and importers of treated articles bear responsibility for verifying that the active substances used comply with BPR conditions, including in imported goods
- On request, suppliers of a treated article must provide consumers with information on the biocidal treatment within 45 days, free of charge

This is a frequent compliance blind spot for companies outside the traditional chemicals sector (textiles, furniture, electronics, packaging) who treat a product for anti-microbial or anti-odour purposes without realising Article 58 applies.

## The 2025-2026 simplification and revision context

Two distinct but related simplification threads are running through the BPR in 2025-2026:

1. **The targeted data-protection fix**: COM(2025) 1020, adopted as Regulation (EU) 2026/1165 (see QUICK FACTS), a narrow, urgent amendment purely extending Article 95 data-protection deadlines to 2030 so the Review Programme backlog does not strip legacy suppliers of protection mid-review. This moved through co-decision in under six months (December 2025 proposal to May 2026 adoption), reflecting cross-institutional agreement on the urgency
2. **Omnibus VI, the broader chemicals simplification package**: expected as a Commission proposal in Q2-Q3 2026, targeting REACH, CLP, BPR and the POPs Regulation together for procedural and administrative streamlining (digital-first submissions, a single ECHA data platform, reduced duplicative reporting, faster authorisation/restriction timelines). BPR-specific expectations inside Omnibus VI include reducing overlap with REACH and simplifying authorisation for demonstrably low-risk active substances. Full detail: `omnibus_vi_chemicals_simplification`

An Implementation Dialogue on the BPR was also held with stakeholders (businesses and civil society) on 15 July 2025, feeding both simplification threads.

## Why it matters to a policy professional

- **Market access gate**: no biocidal product can legally be sold or used in the EU without both an approved active substance and an authorised product, so BPR status directly determines whether a company can place a product on the market
- **Cross-sectoral reach**: BPR affects far more sectors than "chemicals" as conventionally understood, disinfectants, construction materials, textiles, cosmetics adjacent products, water treatment, agri-inputs, marine coatings
- **Review Programme delay risk**: any company relying on a legacy active substance still stuck in the Review Programme faces genuine authorisation-timing uncertainty; the 2026 data-protection extension buys time but does not fix the underlying evaluation backlog
- **Treated-articles exposure**: companies outside the chemicals sector that make marketing claims (antibacterial, anti-odour, self-cleaning) on treated goods carry BPR obligations they may not track through their usual regulatory-affairs function
- **Watch Omnibus VI closely**: it will determine whether biocides compliance gets materially lighter-touch, or whether the changes are mostly procedural (digital submissions) with substantive risk assessment unchanged
