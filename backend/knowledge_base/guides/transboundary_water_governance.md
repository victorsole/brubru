# Transboundary Water Governance (EU + Global)

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU and international governance of transboundary water resources -- shared rivers, aquifers, lakes -- under conditions of structural water scarcity ("water bankruptcy"), drought-flood compounding, and water-energy-food-ecosystem nexus
- **EPRS Briefing (27 April 2026)**: "Transboundary water governance: Competition and cooperation in a 'water bankruptcy' era". Ref: **EPRS_BRI(2026)785730**. URL: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/EPRS_BRI(2026)785730. Key thesis: water scarcity is a structural issue, not a temporary crisis; sustained pressure from rising demand + environmental degradation + climate change requires reframed governance from sovereignty-based to nexus-based cooperation
- **Council ART Paper (April 2026)**: "**Bridging Water Extremes -- Can floods alleviate droughts?**" -- Analysis and Research Team (Council of the EU) paper on managing flood + drought risks together. URL: https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/documents-publications/council-research-papers/. Frames water extremes as a defining EU strategic challenge.
- **EU Water Resilience Strategy** (Commission Communication COM(2025) 280, 4 June 2025): EU framework integrating WFD, Floods Directive, drought management, water-energy-food nexus, transboundary cooperation
- **EU instruments**: Water Framework Directive (WFD) 2000/60/EC, Floods Directive 2007/60/EC, Groundwater Directive 2006/118/EC, Nitrates Directive 91/676/EEC, Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive (recast 2024/3019), Drinking Water Directive (recast 2020/2184)
- **International instruments**: UN Watercourses Convention 1997 (EU not party), UNECE Water Convention 1992 (EU + 28 MS parties)
- **Global hot spots referenced in EPRS_BRI 785730**: Mekong (China-MRC tensions), Nile (GERD dispute), Indus (Pakistan-India), Tigris-Euphrates (Türkiye-Syria-Iraq), Rio Grande (US-Mexico), Aral Sea basin (Central Asia)
- **EU transboundary basins**: Rhine, Danube, Meuse, Elbe, Oder, Sava, Drava, Po, Ebro (EU-only); Bug-Vistula, Niemen (EU-Belarus-Russia); Drin (Albania-North Macedonia-Kosovo-Montenegro -- enlargement candidates)

## Why this matters

Europe consumes water faster than it is replenished in many basins. Climate change is accelerating two simultaneous trends -- more frequent floods AND deeper droughts -- and Member States increasingly face the **water-bankruptcy** condition: structural over-allocation of finite water resources to agriculture, industry, urban use, and environmental flows.

Transboundary water governance is therefore both:

1. **An internal EU coherence problem**: Member States sharing river basins (Danube has 19 riparian states including non-EU; Rhine has 9; Sava has 4 with 3 non-EU candidates) face coordination challenges, especially when upstream-downstream interests diverge during droughts (e.g. France-Spain over Tagus, Slovakia-Hungary over Gabčíkovo)
2. **An external geopolitical problem**: water scarcity drives migration (Sahel-Mediterranean corridor), conflict (Nile, Tigris-Euphrates), and economic disruption (Mekong-China relations)

The EPRS + ART pairing on 27 April 2026 signals **water moving up the strategic agenda** at the institutional level -- both EP research arm and Council research arm produced major papers in the same week.

## EU Policy Framework

### Water Framework Directive (WFD) 2000/60/EC
- River Basin Management Plans (RBMPs) -- 6-year cycles, 4th cycle (2027-2033) under preparation
- Status objectives: "good ecological + chemical status" -- weakened in 2027 review under enforcement realism debates
- Transboundary basin coordination: international river basin districts (Rhine, Danube, Sava, Meuse, Elbe, Oder)

### Floods Directive 2007/60/EC
- Flood Risk Management Plans (FRMPs) -- coordinated with WFD cycles
- Preliminary Flood Risk Assessment (PFRA) + Flood Hazard + Risk Maps + FRMPs

### Drought management
- **No standalone EU drought directive** -- a long-running gap
- Commission's Drought Communication COM(2007) 414 -- ageing, replaced by 2025 Water Resilience Strategy
- Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) eco-schemes include water-saving practices but voluntary

### Recent legislation
- **Water Resilience Strategy** -- COM(2025) 280, 4 June 2025
- **Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive recast** -- Directive (EU) 2024/3019 (energy neutrality + reuse)
- **Water Reuse Regulation** -- Regulation (EU) 2020/741 (agricultural reuse standards)
- **Drinking Water Directive recast** -- Directive (EU) 2020/2184

## International Cooperation

### UNECE Water Convention 1992
- EU + 28 MS parties; opened to global accession 2016
- Implementation Committee in Geneva
- Joint bodies for transboundary basins (Rhine: ICPR; Danube: ICPDR; Sava: ISRBC)

### UN 2030 SDG 6 (Water and Sanitation)
- Target 6.5: integrated water resources management at all levels including through transboundary cooperation
- 2025 stocktake: many basins still without operational arrangements

### Climate Adaptation Strategy 2021
- COM(2021) 82 -- water-resilient societies as Action 4
- Joint with EU Strategy for Adaptation to Climate Change
