# EU Pillar Two: 15% Global Minimum Corporate Tax (Directive 2022/2523)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Directive**: Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523 of 14 December 2022 ("the Pillar Two Directive" / "Minimum Tax Directive"). CELEX **32022L2523**.
- Entered into force 23 December 2022. Member State transposition deadline: 31 December 2023.
- Implements the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework's **GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion) Model Rules** into EU law -- the EU translation of BEPS Pillar Two.
- Sets a **15% effective minimum tax rate** on large multinational enterprise (MNE) groups and large-scale purely domestic groups with **consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million** in at least 2 of the preceding 4 fiscal years.
- Three charging mechanisms:
  - **IIR (Income Inclusion Rule)**: applicable for fiscal years starting on or after **31 December 2023** (FY2024 for calendar-year groups). The parent entity's Member State collects the top-up tax on low-taxed subsidiaries.
  - **UTPR (Undertaxed Profits Rule)**: applicable for fiscal years starting on or after **31 December 2024** (FY2025). Backstop mechanism -- other group entities' jurisdictions collect residual top-up tax if the parent jurisdiction does not apply IIR.
  - **QDMTT (Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax)**: an optional domestic top-up levy that lets a Member State collect the top-up tax itself before it flows to another jurisdiction under IIR/UTPR. Widely adopted: 22 of 27 EU Member States had IIR and QDMTT operating in 2025 (Tax Foundation Pillar Two tracker); verify the current count before citing a precise figure.
- **Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE / "carve-out")**: reduces the GloBE tax base by a formulaic percentage of eligible payroll costs and the carrying value of tangible assets, phasing down over a 10-year transition period (higher percentages in 2023, falling toward a 5% floor by 2033). Genuine economic activity is shielded from top-up tax.
- **Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour**: a temporary simplified test using existing Country-by-Country Reporting and financial-statement data to exclude low-risk jurisdictions from full GloBE computations. Available for fiscal years beginning on or before 31 December 2026 (broadly "through 2026").
- **GloBE Information Return (GIR)**: the standardised compliance return. Ordinary filing deadline is 15 months after fiscal year-end; the **first-year deadline is extended to 18 months**, meaning calendar-year groups' FY2024 GIR is due **30 June 2026** -- the first major EU/OECD-wide Pillar Two compliance deadline.
- **US interaction ("side-by-side")**: the G7 announced a political agreement in June 2025 proposing that US-parented groups be exempted from IIR/UTPR in recognition of the US's own GILTI and BEAT minimum-tax regimes. The OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework issued administrative guidance on the side-by-side safe harbour in January 2026, framed as effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2026. This arrangement was **not universally endorsed** by Inclusive Framework members as of the guidance's release and remains politically contested -- treat any claim of final, unanimous adoption as unverified and check the latest OECD/Inclusive Framework status before asserting it.
- Lead DG: **DG TAXUD** (Taxation and Customs Union). Commissioner: **Wopke Hoekstra** (Climate, Net Zero and Clean Growth), working alongside **Maria Luís Albuquerque** (Financial Services) on corporate tax dossiers.
- Council configuration: **ECOFIN**. Adopted under **Article 115 TFEU** (direct taxation affecting the internal market) -- unanimity required, a rare achievement for EU direct-tax legislation.
- **Review clause**: Directive Article 56 requires a Commission review of implementation and effectiveness; this is the substantive backdrop to the ECON own-initiative report covered in `eu_corporate_tax_policy_2026`.

## Why the Directive Exists

The OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework agreed the "Two-Pillar Solution" to international tax challenges in October 2021: Pillar One reallocates taxing rights over the largest, most profitable MNEs; Pillar Two sets a global floor on corporate tax competition. The EU, needing all 27 Member States to legislate the OECD Model Rules coherently (several Member States, notably Ireland, Hungary, Cyprus, Luxembourg, historically used low headline or effective rates as an investment lever), adopted Directive (EU) 2022/2523 to transpose Pillar Two uniformly across the single market and avoid a patchwork of 27 divergent implementations. Political agreement required overcoming a Hungarian veto in mid-2022 before unanimous Council adoption in December 2022.

## How the 15% Floor Works

1. **Scope test**: does the MNE group (or large domestic group) have consolidated revenue >= EUR 750 million in at least 2 of the last 4 years? If yes, every constituent entity in every jurisdiction is in scope.
2. **Jurisdictional effective tax rate (ETR) calculation**: for each jurisdiction where the group operates, GloBE income and covered taxes are aggregated to compute a jurisdictional ETR.
3. **Top-up tax**: if the jurisdictional ETR is below 15%, a top-up tax equal to the shortfall applies to the excess profit (GloBE income minus the SBIE carve-out).
4. **Collection mechanism**: QDMTT (if the low-tax jurisdiction has one) collects first; otherwise IIR (parent jurisdiction) collects; UTPR is the final backstop allocated across other group jurisdictions by a formula based on employees and tangible assets.
5. **De minimis exclusion**: jurisdictions where the group's average revenue is below EUR 10 million and average GloBE income is below EUR 1 million can be excluded from top-up tax computation.

## Compliance Timeline for Business Users

- **FY2024** (fiscal years starting on or after 31 Dec 2023): IIR applies; QDMTT applies in adopting Member States.
- **FY2025** (fiscal years starting on or after 31 Dec 2024): UTPR also applies.
- **30 June 2026**: first GIR due for calendar-year groups' FY2024 (18-month extended first-year deadline), plus domestic top-up tax returns per Member State QDMTT rules. Non-calendar-year groups compute their own 18-month window from fiscal year-end.
- **Ongoing**: subsequent GIRs due 15 months after each fiscal year-end (i.e. FY2025 GIR generally due around 30 June 2027 for a March year-end, adjust per fiscal calendar).
- **Central filing**: a Qualified International Agreement can allow the GIR to be filed once with the Ultimate Parent Entity's tax administration and automatically exchanged with other jurisdictions, avoiding duplicate local filings -- confirm central-filing agreement status for the relevant jurisdictions.

## US Divergence and the "Side-by-Side" Question

The United States never enacted the OECD Model Rules domestically; it retained its pre-existing GILTI (Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income, restructured as NCTI -- Net CFC Tested Income -- under the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act) and BEAT (Base Erosion and Anti-Abuse Tax) regimes, which differ technically from GloBE (worldwide blending versus jurisdictional blending; and a minimum rate historically below 15%). This created friction: without an accommodation, US-parented groups would face IIR/UTPR top-up tax in every EU Member State where their US minimum-tax exposure fell short of GloBE's jurisdictional 15% test.

The G7's June 2025 "side-by-side" proposal would exempt US-parented groups from IIR/UTPR on the basis that GILTI/BEAT already deliver comparable outcomes, in exchange for the US not retaliating against other countries' Pillar Two regimes (a threat raised via a since-shelved Section 899 "revenge tax" proposal in the US). The OECD Inclusive Framework's January 2026 administrative guidance operationalises a side-by-side safe harbour effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2026, but adoption requires each jurisdiction's own domestic implementation and the guidance was contested by a number of Inclusive Framework members at release. **Do not assert this is settled EU law** -- the Pillar Two Directive itself has not been amended to codify a blanket US exemption; any Member State or EU-level implementation of the side-by-side safe harbour should be verified against the latest Commission/Council/OECD material before being presented as current practice.

## Interaction With Adjacent EU Tax Files

- **BEFIT (Business in Europe: Framework for Income Taxation)**, COM(2023) 532: proposes a common EU tax base and formulary apportionment for groups above the same EUR 750 million threshold as Pillar Two. Stalled in Council on unanimity grounds; conceptually a "second layer" on top of Pillar Two rather than a replacement.
- **DAC9** (9th amendment to the Directive on Administrative Cooperation): establishes the EU legal mechanism for automatic exchange of GloBE Information Returns between Member State tax administrations, mirroring the OECD's multilateral GIR exchange framework.
- **Unshell Directive (ATAD III)**, COM(2021) 565: targets shell entities used for tax avoidance; progressing slowly but conceptually adjacent (both aim to prevent profit-shifting to low-substance jurisdictions).
- **Public country-by-country reporting**, Directive (EU) 2021/2101: separate disclosure obligation (effective from June 2024) that increases transparency around where large groups book profit and pay tax, complementing but distinct from Pillar Two's tax-collection mechanism.
- **ECON own-initiative report (Kollár, PE781.467)**: the EP's 2026 stocktaking of Pillar Two implementation experience, BEFIT's stalled status, and the US divergence -- see `eu_corporate_tax_policy_2026` for the political and procedural detail this guide does not duplicate.
