## Common Fisheries Policy (CFP) Framework

The CFP is the EU's framework for managing fisheries and aquaculture, first established in 1983 and most recently reformed in 2013. It gives the EU exclusive competence over the conservation of marine biological resources and shared competence over the wider fisheries sector.

### Three Pillars of the 2013 CFP Reform

**1. Sustainability and MSY:** All fish stocks must be exploited at levels consistent with maximum sustainable yield (MSY). The regulation set a target of achieving MSY by 2015 where possible, and by 2020 at the latest. Scientific advice from ICES (International Council for the Exploration of the Sea) and STECF (Scientific, Technical and Economic Committee for Fisheries) underpins annual TAC (Total Allowable Catch) and quota decisions.

**2. Landing obligation (discard ban):** Article 15 of Regulation 1380/2013 introduced a phased obligation to land all catches of regulated species, ending the practice of discarding unwanted fish at sea. Fully applicable since 1 January 2019 for all quota species. De minimis exemptions allow discarding up to 5% in certain circumstances.

**3. Regionalisation:** Articles 18-19 allow Member States with a direct management interest in a fishery to develop joint recommendations for conservation measures, which the Commission can adopt as delegated acts. This decentralises decision-making to regional Advisory Councils (ACs) and groups of Member States.

### Multiannual Plans (MAPs)

The CFP operates through multiannual plans for specific sea basins:

| Plan | Regulation | CELEX | Sea Basin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baltic fisheries | Regulation (EU) 2016/1139 | 32016R1139 | Baltic Sea |
| North Sea demersal | Regulation (EU) 2018/973 | 32018R0973 | North Sea |
| Western Waters | Regulation (EU) 2019/472 | 32019R0472 | Atlantic |
| Western Mediterranean demersal | Regulation (EU) 2019/1022 | 32019R1022 | Mediterranean |

MAPs set fishing mortality ranges (FMSY ranges), safeguard reference points (Bpa, Blim), and management measures including effort regimes. Annual fishing opportunities (TACs and quotas) are set by the Council each December based on Commission proposals informed by ICES/STECF advice.

## Fisheries Control Regulation (2023/2842)

### Overview

The Fisheries Control Regulation recast modernises the EU's fisheries monitoring, control, and surveillance system. Adopted as Regulation (EU) 2023/2842 on 22 November 2023 (published OJ L, 20.12.2023), it amends the original Control Regulation (EC) No 1224/2009, the IUU Regulation (EC) No 1005/2008, and the external fleet regulation (EU) 2017/2403. The implementing and delegated acts (Commission Implementing Regulation 2025/2196 and Commission Delegated Regulation 2025/1766) entered into force on 10 January 2026.

### Key Reforms

| Reform | Detail |
|---|---|
| **CATCH system** | Fully digital catch certification IT system replacing paper-based catch certificates. Mandatory from 10 January 2026 for EU importers and Member State authorities |
| **Electronic logbooks** | Mandatory for all commercial fishing vessels (previously only >12m). Electronic transmission of catch data, transshipment and landing declarations |
| **Vessel tracking (VMS)** | Extended to all vessels >7m (previously >12m). Position data transmitted at least every 2 hours (transitional until July 2027), then tighter frequency |
| **Remote Electronic Monitoring (REM)** | CCTV cameras mandatory for vessels >=18m identified as high risk of non-compliance. CCTV data not live-streamed. Phased from 2028 |
| **Unique Haul Identifier** | Digital traceability from catch to retail across the entire supply chain, lot-by-lot tracking |
| **Recreational fisheries** | Catch reporting for recreational catches of certain stocks (first time) |
| **Margin of tolerance** | Maintained at 10% per species in the fishing logbook |
| **Point system** | Harmonised across all Member States. Points applied to licence holders and skippers for serious infringements. Over 18 points: automatic licence suspension |
| **Serious infringements** | Include: document falsification, engine tampering, obstructing officials, use of prohibited gear, forced labour |
| **IUU catch certificates** | Enhanced catch certificate requirements: vessel IMO number, fishing gear, precise catch dates, landing/transshipment details. Processing statement mandatory for all imported processed products from 10 January 2026 |

## Small-Scale and Coastal Fisheries

Commissioner Kadis presented the Commission's approach to small-scale and coastal fisheries at the AGRIFISH Council on 30 March 2026, emphasising the implementation dialogue with Member States. Small-scale coastal fisheries (vessels <12m, no towed gear) account for ~75% of the EU fishing fleet by number but a smaller share of catches. The new control rules extend digital reporting and tracking obligations to these vessels for the first time.

The Commission's "Pact for Fisheries and Oceans" aims to enforce the CFP with the help of Member States and stakeholders. Proposals also aim to make the industry more appealing to younger workers and encourage cleaner energy use in the sector.

## Technical Measures Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2019/1241 (CELEX: 32019R1241) establishes rules on fishing gear, practices, and the protection of marine ecosystems. It covers:

- Prohibited fishing gear and methods (e.g., driftnets, poisons, explosives)
- Minimum mesh sizes and selectivity requirements for towed and static gear
- Minimum conservation reference sizes (replacing minimum landing sizes)
- Protection of sensitive species (marine mammals, seabirds, sharks) and habitats
- Closed or restricted areas for juvenile and spawning protection
- Regionalisation: Member States can adopt regional technical measures via joint recommendations

The regulation replaced 6 older regulations (894/97, 850/98, 2549/2000, 254/2002, 812/2004, 2187/2005) with a single framework.

## External Dimension: Sustainable Fisheries Partnership Agreements (SFPAs)

The EU maintains bilateral fishing agreements with third countries, primarily in Africa and the Pacific. SFPAs allow EU vessels to fish surplus stocks in partner countries' waters under strict conditions: scientific sustainability, fair access fees, sectoral support, transparency, and compliance with IUU rules.

### Types of SFPAs

- **Tuna agreements:** Access to migratory tuna and tuna-like species along African coasts, the Indian Ocean, and the Pacific
- **Mixed agreements:** Access to various demersal and pelagic stocks, cephalopods, and shrimp in a partner country's EEZ

### Northern Agreements

The EU also has reciprocal fisheries arrangements with Norway, Iceland, and the Faroe Islands for joint management of shared stocks in the Northeast Atlantic. These are distinct from SFPAs and operate on a reciprocal access basis.

### Regional Fisheries Management Organisations (RFMOs)

The EU participates in all major RFMOs that manage shared stocks in international waters. A new proposal COM(2025)195 (procedure 2025/0106(COD)) aims to implement RFMO measures into EU law.

## EU Maritime, Fisheries and Aquaculture Fund (EMFAF)

Regulation (EU) 2021/1139 (CELEX: 32021R1139) provides EUR 6.1 billion (2021-2027) for:
- Sustainable fisheries (fleet modernisation, selectivity, energy efficiency)
- Sustainable aquaculture
- Blue economy and maritime policy
- Data collection and control
- Community-led local development (CLLD) for fishing communities

## Emergency Support to Farmers (March 2026)

On 30 March 2026, the Commission allocated EUR 21.5 million in emergency support to farmers in Bulgaria, Estonia, and Hungary. While primarily an agricultural measure, it was announced alongside Commissioner Kadis's fisheries speeches at the AGRIFISH Council.
