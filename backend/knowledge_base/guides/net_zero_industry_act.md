## Article 25 — the procurement regime, as the Commission reads it

### Scope
Article 25 applies to procurement procedures within the scope of **Directive 2014/23/EU** (concessions), **Directive 2014/24/EU** (public sector) and **Directive 2014/25/EU** (utilities), where the contract has a net-zero technology listed in **Article 4(1)(a) to (k)** as part of its subject matter — and to **works contracts and works concessions** including those technologies. The scope **mirrors the procurement directives**: their thresholds and exclusions carry across. Article 25 does not create a procurement regime of its own.

### A. Environmental sustainability — Article 25(1)
Contracting authorities must include minimum environmental-sustainability requirements for the technologies procured, **as established by implementing acts**. Because the obligation depends on such an act, **Article 25(1) applies from the date of application of the relevant implementing act**.

The first such act is **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/718 of 20 March 2026**, laying down **minimum mandatory requirements for the recyclability of wind turbine blades**, which **applies from 30 June 2026**. Article 25(1) does not by itself impose obligations on technologies for which no implementing act yet exists — a point worth stating plainly, because the natural assumption is that the whole of Article 25 bites at once. It does not.

### B. Additional non-price requirements — Article 25(3)
These apply **only to works contracts and works concessions** including an Article 4(1)(a)-(k) technology. Three requirements:
1. a **special condition on social or employment-related considerations**;
2. a requirement to **demonstrate compliance with applicable cybersecurity requirements** in national and EU law — the Commission encourages national authorities to follow the specifications in **Article 5 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1176** (which formally governs Article 26 auctions) where possible;
3. a **specific contractual obligation to deliver on time**.

### C. Resilience — Article 25(7)
**High supply dependency** is defined by two alternative situations:
- more than **50%** of EU supply of a net-zero technology final product or main specific component comes from a **single non-EU country**; **or**
- more than **40%** of EU supply comes from a single non-EU country **and** that share has risen on average by at least **10 percentage points across two consecutive years**.

Where that dependency exists, two ceilings apply against the **dominant source of supply**:
- **Article 25(7)(a):** no more than **50% of the value of the net-zero technology final product** may originate from the dominant source. This is measured as a share of **the cost to the contracting authority**, and is expressly **not** the sum of the costs of the main specific components.
- **Article 25(7)(b):** no more than **50% of the value of the main specific components** may originate from the dominant source. This applies to the **aggregate value of all** main specific components, **not to each component individually** — a distinction that decides real tenders.

The **main specific components** of each technology are listed in **Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1178 of 23 May 2025**.

**Article 25(7)(d)** provides for a **proportionate charge** where the resilience requirements are not met.

### D. Exceptions
| Situation | Provision |
|---|---|
| Single supplier (monopoly) | Article 25(9)(a) |
| No suitable tender in a similar former procedure | Article 25(9)(b) |
| No suitable tender in the same procedure | Article 25(11) |
| Disproportionate cost differences or technical incompatibility | Article 25(9)(c) and Article 25(10) |
| International agreements (e.g. the WTO Government Procurement Agreement) | Article 25(8) |

### Ownership neutrality
Recital 81 requires the sustainability and resilience requirements to be applied so as to ensure **fair and equal competition regardless of ownership structure**. Public undertakings participating in Article 26 renewable energy auctions are assessed on the same basis.

## What this means in practice
A contracting authority buying, say, rooftop solar PV within the procurement directives' thresholds must ask three separate questions: whether an **implementing act** sets environmental-sustainability minimums for that technology yet; whether the contract is a **works** contract, which alone triggers the Article 25(3) trio; and whether the EU has a **high supply dependency** for that product or its main specific components, which alone triggers the two 50% ceilings. The Commission's own worked example: for rooftop solar PV, the contract can allocate **at most 50% of the total cost** of the PV systems to systems from the dominant source of supply.

## Common confusions to avoid
- **Article 25 is procurement; Article 26 is renewable energy auctions.** They share vocabulary (sustainability, resilience, cybersecurity) and are different regimes.
- **The guidance is not law.** It is a Communication and says so. Cite the Regulation for obligations and the Communication only for the Commission's interpretation.
- **Article 25(1) is not in force for every technology.** It bites technology by technology, as implementing acts appear.
- **The 50% ceilings are conditional**, not default: they apply only where a high supply dependency exists.
- **The 25(7)(b) ceiling is aggregate**, across all main specific components, not per component.
