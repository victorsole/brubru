# daa and Others, C-857/24 (15 October 2026): Dublin airport's annual passenger cap may limit airline slots

## QUICK FACTS
- **Court of Justice, judgment of 15 October 2026, Case C-857/24, daa and Others** (preliminary reference from the **High Court of Ireland**; press release No 149/26). Interprets the **Slot Regulation, Council Regulation (EEC) No 95/93** on common rules for the allocation of slots at EU airports, as amended.
- **The facts:** planning consents for Dublin airport's terminals, granted in **2007 and 2008**, cap traffic at **32 million passengers a year**. In 2024 the operator forecast the cap could be reached or exceeded, so the **Irish Aviation Authority** set **seasonal passenger caps**. The airport operator (daa) and several airlines challenged those decisions.
- **The ruling:** the coordination parameters must reflect **all capacity actually available** at a coordinated airport. Constraints may be **legal**, including a **limit in a development consent** that restricts operational capacity and binds the operator. It does not matter whether the planning rule is old or the policy behind it has changed. On the facts given by the Irish court, the Dublin limit **must be taken into account**.
- **Historic slots ("grandfather rights"):** priority for airlines that regularly used a series of slots does **not** guarantee automatic renewal; it depends on available capacity. A passenger limit may therefore **exceptionally** mean some historic slots are not reallocated.
- **What was decided, and by whom:** the Court of Justice interprets EU law; the **High Court of Ireland** decides the dispute itself.
