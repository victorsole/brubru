# SoHO Regulation: Substances of Human Origin (Regulation (EU) 2024/1938)

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is:** A single, directly-applicable EU Regulation setting harmonised quality, safety and traceability standards for substances of human origin (SoHO) intended for human application. It replaces two older directives with one binding regulation, closing gaps exposed by the covid-19 pandemic (donor shortages, uneven national oversight, gaps for newer substances like breast milk and microbiota).
- **Full title:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1938 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on standards of quality and safety for substances of human origin intended for human application and repealing Directives 2002/98/EC and 2004/23/EC.
- **CELEX number:** 32024R1938. Full text: [eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1938/oj/eng](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1938/oj/eng) or by CELEX query: [eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1938](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1938). A corrigendum was published 30 January 2026 (CELEX 32024R1938R(03)); always check for the latest consolidated version on EUR-Lex.
- **Legal basis:** Article 168(4)(a) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (public health, common safety concerns in blood, tissues and cells).
- **Adopted:** 13 June 2024 by the European Parliament and the Council (ordinary legislative procedure), following a Commission proposal of 14 July 2022.
- **Published:** Official Journal L series, 2024/1938, 17 July 2024.
- **Entered into force:** 6 August 2024 (20 days after publication).
- **Applies from:** 7 August 2027 for its main obligations (three years after entry into force), the same date on which Directive 2002/98/EC (the "Blood Directive") and Directive 2004/23/EC (the "Tissues and Cells Directive") are repealed. A small number of provisions apply one year later still, so operators must check the article-by-article transition timeline rather than assume a single cut-over date.
- **Scope (SoHO):** blood and blood components, tissues, cells (including reproductive cells/gametes used in medically assisted reproduction), human breast milk, intestinal microbiota, and other substances collected from the human body intended for human application, plus "SoHO preparations" derived from processing them.
- **Explicitly excluded:** solid organs. Recital 26 of the Regulation states that solid organs are excluded from the definition of SoHO and remain governed separately by Directive 2010/53/EU on standards of quality and safety of human organs intended for transplantation.
- **Who runs it:** each Member State designates a SoHO national authority and one or more SoHO competent authorities; EU-level coordination runs through the SoHO Coordination Board (SCB), the EU SoHO Platform (hosted by the European Commission, DG SANTE), and technical guidance from the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) and the Council of Europe's European Directorate for the Quality of Medicines & HealthCare (EDQM).
- **Official Commission portal:** [health.ec.europa.eu/blood-tissues-cells-and-organs/soho-regulation_en](https://health.ec.europa.eu/blood-tissues-cells-and-organs/soho-regulation_en).

## What the Regulation does

The SoHO Regulation is the concluding building block of a wider revision of the EU's blood, tissues and cells framework. It replaces two directives, transposed differently across 27 Member States, with a single directly-applicable Regulation, removing the national transposition variance that had produced uneven safety and quality standards and made it harder to share SoHO across borders in emergencies.

It responds to five weaknesses identified in the pre-existing framework: insufficient patient protection against new and emerging risks (accelerated by covid-19); insufficient donor protection, including for offspring conceived through medically assisted reproduction; inconsistent oversight practices between Member States; limited capacity to deal with scientific and technical innovation (new therapies, new processing techniques); and a weak EU-wide capacity to guarantee continuity of supply of critical SoHO during shortages or crises.

## Scope: what counts as a "substance of human origin"

A SoHO is defined broadly as any substance collected from the human body, whether or not it contains cells, and whether those cells are living or not, together with any "SoHO preparation" resulting from processing such a substance. Covered categories include:

- Blood and blood components (the former Blood Directive scope)
- Tissues and cells, including reproductive cells/gametes used in medically assisted reproduction (the former Tissues and Cells Directive scope)
- Human breast milk
- Intestinal (faecal) microbiota
- Other substances collected from the human body intended for human application, and SoHO preparations derived from processing

**Solid organs are not covered.** They remain regulated separately under Directive 2010/53/EU on standards of quality and safety of human organs intended for transplantation. A policy professional working across the wider "human biological material" space needs both instruments: the SoHO Regulation for blood/tissues/cells/other substances, and the 2010 Organs Directive for transplantable organs.

The Regulation also intersects with the medicines framework: where a SoHO undergoes substantial manipulation and is intended to treat, prevent or diagnose disease through a pharmacological, immunological or metabolic action, it can cross into advanced therapy medicinal product (ATMP) territory under Regulation (EC) 1394/2007, where the European Medicines Agency's Committee for Advanced Therapies (CAT) has classification authority. The SoHO Coordination Board is tasked with improving cross-sector coherence on these regulatory borderline questions between the SoHO framework, the medicines framework and the medical devices framework. See `advanced_therapy_medicinal_products_regulation` and `medical_devices_regulation` for the adjacent frameworks, and `ema_human_regulatory_overview` for EMA's general regulatory role.

## Governance architecture

### SoHO national authority and SoHO competent authorities (Member State level)

Each Member State designates a **SoHO national authority** and one or more **SoHO competent authorities**, which authorise SoHO establishments, register SoHO entities, run inspections, and handle vigilance (adverse reaction and adverse event reporting). A national authority can also be a competent authority. Germany's Paul-Ehrlich-Institut, for example, is designated as both. Every SoHO establishment is a SoHO entity, but not every SoHO entity is a SoHO establishment (the Regulation distinguishes entities that only apply SoHO to patients, such as clinics and dentists, from establishments that process, store, release, import or export SoHO).

### SoHO Coordination Board (SCB)

The SCB is the central EU-level coordination body, established under Articles 68 to 69 of the Regulation. It is composed of two permanent members and two alternate members from each Member State, drawn from the national SoHO authority (and, if the Member State chooses, from the ministry of health or another relevant authority). It is co-chaired by the European Commission and a Member State representative, with the Commission providing the secretariat and technical, scientific and logistical support. The SCB may invite experts and observers, including professional societies.

The SCB's tasks include: promoting coordination between Member States; endorsing best practices for supervisory functions and compensation conditions; providing indicative criteria for what counts as "critical SoHO" and "critical SoHO entities"; advising on whether the Regulation applies to a borderline case; and exchanging good practice with the expert bodies (ECDC, EDQM) and with EMA on regulatory borderline questions. It is supported by six topic-specific working groups covering authorisation, registration, traceability, supply, inspections and regulatory questions.

### EU SoHO Platform

Referred to in Articles 73 and 74 of the Regulation, the EU SoHO Platform is a digital platform hosted and maintained by the European Commission to exchange information on SoHO activities across the EU. It has a public part and a restricted-access part. The public part gives access to: the registration and authorisation status of SoHO entities; the list of national authorities; SCB membership, rules of procedure, agenda and minutes; the list of approved SoHO clinical studies and authorised SoHO preparations; the annual Union SoHO activity report and annual Union SoHO vigilance report; and best practices documented by the SCB. The restricted part supports exchanges between the Commission and national authorities, between national authorities and the SCB, between national authorities and the ECDC, and between SoHO entities and their competent authority.

The technical specifications for the Platform were laid down in **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1467 of 18 July 2025** (OJ L, 2025/1467, 21 July 2025): [eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1467](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32025R1467) (verify this CELEX independently before citing, as implementing acts publish on a separate numbering track from the parent Regulation).

### ECDC and EMA support roles

**ECDC** (see `ecdc_agency_overview`) is tasked with developing authoritative technical guidelines on preventing transmission of communicable diseases through SoHO (donor selection criteria, infectious-disease testing standards), working alongside the Council of Europe's EDQM, which covers the broader quality and safety guidance historically used by the blood, tissue and cell sector. In infectious-disease outbreaks with SoHO relevance, ECDC also publishes rapid risk assessments with recommendations to maintain microbial safety. Making this technical guidance the primary route to meeting EU safety and quality standards (rather than a rigid annex to the Regulation itself) lets standards keep pace with new scientific evidence without a legislative amendment each time.

**EMA**'s role under the SoHO framework itself is narrower and mostly borderline-related: through its Committee for Advanced Therapies (CAT), it retains classification authority when a processed SoHO product crosses into ATMP territory under Regulation (EC) 1394/2007. The SCB is expected to liaise with EMA on these regulatory borderline questions rather than EMA taking a direct implementation role comparable to ECDC's or EDQM's.

## Supply-continuity and donor-protection provisions

Recognising that shortages of critical SoHO (for example specific blood types, certain tissues, cord blood) can directly harm patients, the Regulation requires SoHO entities to alert their competent authority when a significant shortage of critical SoHO supply is anticipated, specifying the underlying reasons, the foreseeable effects on recipients, and possible mitigation measures (Article 64 sets out what counts as a "significant" shortage). Member States must identify which SoHO entities work with critical SoHO (those whose insufficient supply would cause serious harm or risk of serious harm to recipients), and those entities must have documented emergency plans. SoHO entities also report annual activity data at EU level to help track donation rates and anticipate shortages.

On donor protection, the Regulation reaffirms the principle of voluntary, unpaid donation, clarifies the definition of "compensation" and introduces a principle of financial neutrality (donation must not be a source of financial gain), while promoting a diverse, broad donor base and a strong public/non-profit sector to build resilience. It strengthens follow-up requirements after donation of certain SoHO types, in some cases over longer periods, so adverse reactions can be monitored and reported, extending protection to donors, recipients and offspring from medically assisted reproduction.

## Transition timeline

- **13 June 2024:** Adopted by the European Parliament and Council.
- **17 July 2024:** Published in the Official Journal (OJ L, 2024/1938).
- **6 August 2024:** Entered into force (20 days after publication).
- **18 July 2025:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1467 on the technical specifications of the EU SoHO Platform adopted.
- **16 March 2026:** SoHO Coordination Board adopted an information paper for national/regional awareness-raising ahead of application.
- **7 August 2027:** Main application date. Directive 2002/98/EC (Blood Directive) and Directive 2004/23/EC (Tissues and Cells Directive) are repealed on this date, and the bulk of the Regulation's obligations become directly applicable in all Member States. Previously authorised tissue establishments are deemed authorised under the new Regulation, with those already on the EU coding platform automatically transposed to the EU SoHO Platform.
- Certain provisions apply one year after the main date, so operators should check the article-specific timeline in the Regulation rather than assume 7 August 2027 covers everything.

Always verify the current transition status against the consolidated EUR-Lex text before asserting a compliance deadline to a user, since implementing and delegated acts continue to be adopted in the run-up to 2027.

## Why it matters to a policy professional

This is a rare example of the EU replacing two directives with one directly-applicable regulation in the health space, meaning Member States lose the transposition flexibility they had before and must apply the same rules on the same schedule. For clients in blood establishments, tissue banks, fertility clinics, biobanks, hospitals, cosmetics/biotech firms sourcing human-derived material, or breast milk banks, the compliance clock started running from entry into force (6 August 2024) even though the main obligations only bite from 7 August 2027, so registration, authorisation and traceability system work needs to start well ahead of the deadline. It is also a useful example of the EU building bespoke digital coordination infrastructure (the EU SoHO Platform) directly into a Regulation rather than leaving coordination to informal Member State cooperation, a pattern comparable to other EU digital coordination tools; see `ehds_european_health_data_space` for the parallel EU-level health-data exchange infrastructure being built for a different purpose (secondary use of electronic health data rather than SoHO entity/donation tracking), and note the potential overlap in vigilance and traceability data as both frameworks mature.
