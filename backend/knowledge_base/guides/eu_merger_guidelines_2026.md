## What is new — the headline shifts vs 2004/2008

| Theme | New / changed |
|---|---|
| **Innovation shield** (¶192) | **NEW**: explicit safe harbour for transactions involving small innovative companies / start-ups / R&D projects with dynamic competitive potential — 5 alternative criteria, with **DMA gatekeeper carve-out** |
| **Loss of investment and expansion competition** (Section II.B.3) | **NEW** standalone harm theory (separate from loss of head-to-head competition) |
| **Loss of innovation competition** (Section II.B.4) | Expanded into 3 sub-categories: **specific innovation** (¶180-185), **general innovation** (¶186-191), and **innovation shield** (¶192) |
| **Defence readiness** (¶9, ¶15(c)(iii)) | **NEW** explicit procompetitive consideration; mergers strengthening EU defence industrial base welcomed |
| **Resilience of supply chains** | **NEW** as procompetitive factor and as part of non-price competition parameter |
| **Privacy + sustainability** | Added to non-price parameters of competition (¶20) |
| **Scale-enhancing mergers** (¶15) | Explicitly welcomed in 3 categories: (a) innovation/tech progress, (b) market integration / global scale, (c) security / resilience |
| **Part III — Article 21(4) legitimate interests** | **Major expansion**: codified guidance on Member State power to block / condition mergers on public security, media plurality, prudential rules, "other public interests" |

## Structure (3 parts, 399 paragraphs)

### Part I — Introduction and Guiding Principles (¶1-51)

- **Role of EU merger control** — internal market, competitiveness, innovation, investment, resilience, defence readiness
- **Guiding Principles**:
  - **Parameters of competition** (¶20): price + non-price (output, quality, choice including media/cultural diversity, capacity, investment, **innovation**, **privacy**, **sustainability**, **resilience including security of supply**)
  - **Burden of proof**: Commission proves anticompetitive effects without quantifying consumer harm (¶22); merging parties prove efficiencies (¶24)
  - **Theories of harm and benefit** (¶23): articulated and substantiated; describe how merger modifies competitive constraints
  - **Evidence**: case law tests; "sufficiently cogent and consistent body of evidence"
  - **Causality + Counterfactual** (¶37-44): pre-merger conditions are usual benchmark; 3 adjustment scenarios — (i) future market changes unrelated to merger, (ii) non-representative market conditions (war/trade conflict/health emergency etc.), (iii) alternative mergers/agreements available absent the notified one
  - **Failing firm** (¶45-51): 3 cumulative criteria — (1) firm forced out of market in near future absent merger, (2) no less anticompetitive alternative, (3) assets would inevitably exit market or accrue to acquirer; **failing division** rules at ¶50

### Part II — Competitive Assessment (¶52-357)

**A. Market Power** (¶55 onward)
- Structural indicators: large market shares, high concentration
- Other indicators: low price sensitivity, high profit margins, high barriers to competition
- Dynamic competitive potential (forward-looking adjustment of static market power assessment)
- Countervailing factors: dynamic entry/expansion, out-of-market constraints, countervailing buyer power
- Dominance and other types of market power

**B. Anticompetitive Effects** (8 categories)
1. Loss of head-to-head competition (closeness, important competitive force, specific market aspects)
2. **Loss of investment and expansion competition** *(NEW standalone)*
3. **Loss of innovation competition** — specific (¶180-185, including killer/reverse-killer acquisitions), general (¶186-191), **innovation shield** (¶192)
4. Loss of potential competition (3 cumulative conditions, ¶194)
5. Foreclosure (ability + incentive + effect on competition)
6. Entrenchment of a dominant position
7. Coordination (terms, monitoring/deterrence, disruption)
8. Other (access to commercially sensitive information, portfolio effects)

**C. Benefits / Efficiencies** (¶ from p.74)
- Direct efficiencies: taxonomy, verifiability, merger specificity, benefit to consumers
- Dynamic efficiencies (¶82+): same four-prong test
- **Balancing benefit and harm**: symmetric, asymmetric, across consumer groups/markets

### Part III — Measures to Protect Legitimate Interests (¶358-399)

Codifies Article 21(4) EUMR — Member State power to take "appropriate measures" to protect legitimate interests other than competition.

**A. Substantive assessment**
- Member State measures: legislative + regulatory + administrative, including omissions to act and de facto binding measures
- **Recognised interests**:
  - **Public security** (¶368-371) — strict interpretation; cannot serve purely economic ends; sensitive sectors / classified state secrets carve-out
  - **Media plurality** (¶372-374) — number, range, variety of persons controlling media enterprises; explicit link to **European Media Freedom Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/1083, Articles 22 and 23)**
  - **Prudential rules** (¶375-376) — banking, insurance, financial services; harmonised framework reduces standalone Member State scope
- **Other public interests** (¶377): water distribution (Lyonnaise des Eaux/Northumbrian Water M.567), consumer protection (case-by-case)
- **General principles**: proportionality (4 cumulative criteria), non-discrimination (Article 18 TFEU), legal certainty, primacy of EU law
- **Compatibility with other EU law**: FDI Regulation (EU) 2019/452, EMFA, ECB / EU prudential authorities

**B. Procedural framework**
- Notification + standstill obligation; recognised-interest exception narrow
- 25-working-day Commission decision after notification complete
- Decisions: **interim** (suspend pending review) / **positive** (compatible) / **negative** (withdraw)
- Article 258 TFEU infringement if Member State fails to withdraw incompatible measure

## Innovation shield — full criteria (¶192)

Transactions involving a **small innovative company / start-up / R&D project with dynamic competitive potential** are **not** found to give rise to a SIEC (across loss-of-innovation, potential-competition, entrenchment and foreclosure theories) where ANY of these conditions hold:

- a) **No overlap** — neither party is or plans to be active in the same/related market or innovation space
- b) **R&D project + existing activities overlap**: combined market share **≤ 40 %** AND ≥ 3 other independent firms with similar R&D potential. *Start-up exception*: even if criteria not met, shield applies if **acquirer is not the largest firm in the relevant market AND not a DMA gatekeeper**
- c) **R&D-to-R&D overlap**: ≥ 3 other independent firms with similar R&D potential
- d) **R&D capabilities overlap**: combined ≤ **25 %** in innovation space AND ≤ 25 % at industry level
- e) **Vertical / closely-related overlap**: combined market share ≤ 40 % in upstream/downstream/related market. *Start-up exception*: shield still applies if acquirer is not largest firm AND not a DMA gatekeeper

The DMA gatekeeper exclusion (¶192(b) and (e), footnotes 278 and 281) is significant: **Apple, Alphabet, Amazon, ByteDance, Meta, Microsoft cannot benefit from the start-up exception** because they are designated gatekeepers under Reg. (EU) 2022/1925 Article 2.

## Why this matters for Brubru users

1. **Tech / digital advisors** — innovation shield + DMA gatekeeper carve-out fundamentally re-shapes the killer-acquisition debate. Big-tech start-up acquisitions face stricter scrutiny than any other acquirer profile.
2. **Pharma / agrochem advisors** — loss of innovation competition (specific + general) gets dedicated guidance with Dow/DuPont and Bayer/Monsanto as anchors.
3. **Defence / dual-use industrial base** — defence readiness is now an explicit procompetitive factor, helping mergers in EDIP / SAFE perimeter.
4. **Member State competition authorities and ministries** — Part III codifies what Article 21(4) measures will and won't survive Commission scrutiny. Critical for Italy (Banco BPM), Spain, and other Member States with active screening regimes.
5. **Media-merger advisors** — media plurality interest now explicitly tied to European Media Freedom Act review.
6. **Banking-merger advisors** — prudential rules section reflects post-Banking-Union harmonisation; standalone Member State scope significantly narrowed.
7. **All M&A advisors** — efficiency claims still bear merging-party burden, but dynamic efficiencies + scale-enhancing rationale (innovation, integration, security) get explicit positive recognition for the first time at this scale.
