# Unit Cost Decision: SME Owners and Natural Persons not Receiving a Salary

## QUICK FACTS
- Applies to SME owner-founders and sole-traders who do not receive a salary from their own company but provide work to EU-funded actions
- Also covers natural persons (not employees) who contribute to EU actions but do not receive a salary
- Unit cost is a fixed daily rate set by Commission Decision, designed to bypass the "no payroll, no cost" barrier in actual-cost budgeting
- Current base rate: EUR 9,271 per day-equivalent (as of 16 December 2025, Decision C(2024) 5328 final)
- Rate increased 46% above the Marie Sklodowska-Curie postdoctoral fellowship living allowance to reflect managerial responsibility of SME owners
- Daily rate is multiplied by a country-specific correction coefficient (same as used for MSCA postdoctoral fellowships) and divided by 18 working days to calculate the hourly or daily sub-unit
- Beneficiaries can declare up to 215 day-equivalents per calendar year (not per project)
- Commonly used in Horizon Europe, Erasmus+, EIC Accelerator, EIC Accelerator for Academic Institutions, and other post-2020 programmes
- Documentation required: declaration of hours worked, self-certification of role and eligibility, time records (not full employment contracts)
- Falls under Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046 Article 181(7) (Financial Regulation) and Regulation (EU) 2021/695 (Horizon Europe)

## Who this unit cost applies to (SME owner-founders and natural persons without salary)

This unit cost mechanism is open to:

1. **SME owner-founders** who do not receive a salary from their own company but contribute personally to the implementation of the grant-funded action
2. **Sole traders** and self-employed persons engaged as beneficiaries (not subcontractors)
3. **Natural persons** (individuals) who contribute work to an action but do not receive a salary or employment contract
4. **Partner organisations in consortia** where the person holds an ownership stake and contributes hands-on work

The unit cost CANNOT be used if the person:
- Receives a normal salary or wages from the beneficiary organisation during the project period (standard personnel cost rules apply instead)
- Is employed as a regular staff member with a formal employment contract
- Is declared as a subcontractor (must use actual-cost or other simplified cost mechanisms instead)

## Why it exists (the no-payroll problem in actual-cost mode)

Under the Financial Regulation, beneficiaries can declare eligible costs. For personnel, this typically means salary and employment costs recorded in the organisation's accounts.

The problem arises with SME founders and natural persons:
- An SME founder may contribute 100 hours per month to the project but pay themselves a dividend instead of a salary, or take no personal compensation
- A natural person volunteer or co-founder contributes time but has no employment contract and no salary cost in the books
- Under strict actual-cost rules, there is no "cost" to declare, even though real work is being performed
- This gap discourages SMEs and individual innovators from participating in EU funding programmes

The unit cost mechanism solves this by allowing beneficiaries to declare a notional value for the work, based on a pre-agreed daily or hourly rate. The rate is set by Commission Decision and does not require proving the "actual cost" in the accounts.

## The unit-cost rate (daily and hourly amounts)

### Base rate (as of 16 December 2025)

The Commission Decision C(2024) 5328 final establishes the following calculation:

**Unit cost = EUR 9,271 multiplied by the country-specific correction coefficient, divided by 18 working days**

This means:
- The base unit cost per day-equivalent is EUR 9,271 ÷ 18 = approximately EUR 515.06 per day
- The country-specific correction coefficient (same as for MSCA postdoctoral living allowances under Horizon Europe) is applied based on the location of the beneficiary
- For example, if the coefficient is 100% (Belgium baseline), the daily rate is EUR 515.06. If the coefficient is 80% (some other countries), the daily rate would be EUR 412.05
- The hourly sub-unit would be the daily rate divided by standard working hours (typically 8 hours per day for a standard working week of 40 hours = 5 days)

### Automatic updates

The EUR 9,271 base is automatically updated in line with the Marie Sklodowska-Curie unit cost for postdoctoral living allowances. When the MSCA unit cost increases, the SME owner rate is recalculated as MSCA rate + 46%.

Previous versions:
- Version 1.0 (October 2020): EUR 4,880 per day-equivalent
- Version 2.0 (July 2024): EUR 8,745.40 per day-equivalent
- Version 3.0 (December 2025): EUR 9,271 per day-equivalent

### Why 46% above MSCA rates?

Research by Eurostat and Belgian national statistics (Statbel) shows that managers earn approximately 46% more than professionals and technicians in the research and development sector. Because SME founders typically occupy managerial roles, the unit cost is set 46% above the researcher living allowance to reflect this.

## Eligibility conditions (entity type, role, activity)

To use this unit cost, the following conditions must be met:

### Entity requirements
- The beneficiary must be a small or medium-sized enterprise (as defined in the Horizon Europe or relevant programme rules) OR a natural person
- The beneficiary must be a participant in the grant-funded action (either as a beneficiary or partner, not a subcontractor)

### Person requirements
- The person must not receive a salary, wages, or remuneration from the beneficiary organisation during the period of work on the action
- The person must have a clear role and responsibility within the action (founder, co-founder, principal investigator, technical lead, or similar)
- The person must contribute directly to the tasks described in the grant agreement

### Activity requirements
- Work must be carried out under the action or work programme (project scope)
- Work must be documented and traceable (time records, meeting notes, deliverables, or similar evidence)
- The activity must be eligible under the relevant funding programme (e.g. research, innovation, capacity-building, policy analysis)

### Time limits
- Maximum 215 day-equivalents per person per calendar year (even if working on multiple projects)
- The calculation is per calendar year, not per project or per funding agreement

## Documentation required (declarations, time records)

Beneficiaries must maintain and make available for audit:

### For the beneficiary (organisation level)
1. A written declaration identifying the SME owner or natural person by name, role, and contribution area
2. A statement confirming that the person does NOT receive a salary from the beneficiary during the project period
3. Copy of the relevant grant agreement or annex identifying the person and their tasks

### For the person (individual level)
1. **Time records**: A declaration of hours or days worked on the action, with dates and description of tasks
   - Does not need to be a detailed time-sheet (as for employed staff), but should be traceable
   - Weekly or monthly summary of days worked is acceptable
   - Should match the beneficiary's project records (delivery reports, meeting attendance, technical milestones)

2. **Role declaration**: A self-certification stating:
   - Name, contact details, and organisation (if any)
   - Percentage of time allocated to the action (e.g. 50% full-time = 50% of 215 day-equivalents per year)
   - Description of tasks and responsibilities
   - Confirmation that no salary or remuneration is received from the beneficiary for this work during the project period

3. **Eligibility statement**: Confirmation that the person meets the conditions (e.g. SME founder, natural person, not an employee)

### What auditors will check
- That the person is genuinely not on the beneficiary's payroll during the project period
- That the time records are consistent with project deliverables and milestones
- That the declared day-equivalents do not exceed 215 per calendar year
- That the unit cost calculation (base rate × coefficient ÷ 18) is applied correctly
- That double-funding is avoided (no person is claiming the same hours twice under different cost categories or grants)

## How to claim under MGA Article 6 (or Annex 2 of the Lump Sum Grant Agreement)

The Model Grant Agreement (MGA) for Horizon Europe contains the rules for eligibility and the calculation of unit costs.

### Step 1: Identify the cost category in the grant agreement
In the Description of the Action (Annex 1) and Estimated Budget (Annex 2), the beneficiary must:
- Clearly list the SME owner or natural person by name
- Assign them to a work package and task
- Note that their costs will be declared under "Unit costs for SME owners/natural persons not receiving a salary"

### Step 2: Calculate the eligible amount per person
At the time of the proposal:
- Estimate the number of day-equivalents the person will work (e.g. 50 days over 2 years)
- Multiply by the unit cost rate: days × (EUR 9,271 × country coefficient ÷ 18)
- Insert this as the budget line for that person

During implementation:
- The beneficiary declares actual day-equivalents worked (up to the planned amount)
- The granting authority reimburses on the basis of actual day-equivalents claimed, multiplied by the unit cost

### Step 3: Include supporting documentation in the periodic report
When submitting interim or final financial reports (typically every 12-18 months), include:
- The time records and role declarations for each person
- A reconciliation of planned vs. actual day-equivalents
- Confirmation that the 215 day-equivalent annual cap has not been exceeded

### Step 4: Final claim and closure
In the final technical and financial report (submitted within 60 days of project end), include:
- Complete time records for all SME owners and natural persons
- Final reconciliation of actual vs. claimed day-equivalents
- Signature and certification by the project coordinator and beneficiary representative

## Use in EIC Accelerator and other programmes

This unit cost mechanism is particularly important for:

### EIC Accelerator (formerly SME Instrument Phase 2)
- SME founders often have significant equity stakes but minimal salary
- The unit cost allows them to charge their own time to the grant without establishing a formal payroll position
- Common in deeptech and climate ventures where founders are principal technical contributors

### EIC Accelerator for Academic Institutions
- Researchers with ownership or leadership roles can use the unit cost
- Useful for university spin-offs where the researcher-founder has an equity stake

### Horizon Europe Research and Innovation Actions (RIAs) and Innovation Actions (IAs)
- SME partners in consortia can use it for owner-founders
- Allows SMEs to scale their participation without the administrative burden of formal employment contracts

### Other EU programmes (2021-2027)
- Erasmus+, Creative Europe, Citizens, Rights, Equality and Justice
- Customs, Tax and Fiscal Union, Consumers
- Any programme based on the Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2018/1046
