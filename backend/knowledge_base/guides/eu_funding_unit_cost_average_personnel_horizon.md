# Unit Cost Decision: Average Personnel Costs and Internally Invoiced Goods and Services (Horizon Europe and Euratom)

## QUICK FACTS

- **Who can use it:** All Horizon Europe and Euratom (2021-2025 and 2026-2027) beneficiaries
- **What it allows:** Use average personnel costs (hourly or daily rates) instead of reporting actual hourly rates for each staff member
- **Calculation basis:** Annual total personnel costs divided by annual productive hours worked
- **Key condition:** The method must reflect your organisation's usual cost accounting practices applied consistently across all funding sources
- **Internally invoiced goods/services:** In-house laboratory services, internal IT support, research equipment access, testing facilities, specialist premises, and hosting services for visiting researchers
- **Audit trail:** You must maintain records proving time worked (for staff) or units used/provided (for goods/services) so the Commission can verify later
- **Consistency requirement:** The same unit cost methodology must apply whether the action is EU-funded or funded from other sources
- **Documentation must include:** How the unit cost is calculated; what costs are covered; frequency of updates; reference period for actual data; which version applies to current reports
- **Simplification benefit:** Eliminates need to justify individual hourly rates and reduces administrative burden for both beneficiaries and Commission
- **Common audit risk:** Including ineligible costs (e.g. capital depreciation for research infrastructure when declaring virtual access) or double-counting costs in multiple categories
- **Legal basis:** Article 184(4)(c)(ii) of Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509; Articles 35-36 of Regulation (EU) 2021/695; Articles 6 and 9 of Council Regulations (Euratom) 2021/765 and 2025/1304

## What the Decision Allows

### Average Personnel Costs

You may declare staff costs using unit costs (average hourly or daily rates) for all Horizon Europe and Euratom actions, rather than reporting individual researcher names and actual hourly rates. The unit cost represents the average cost of employing a person in a given professional category within your organisation.

**Example:** A research institute calculates that its senior researchers cost an average of EUR 75 per hour (including salary, benefits, and employment contributions). Rather than reporting 820 hours for Dr Jones at EUR 75/h and 655 hours for Dr Smith at the same rate, it declares the total hours multiplied by the unit rate.

### Internally Invoiced Goods and Services

You may declare costs for internally invoiced goods and services using unit costs. These are goods or services provided within your organisation directly for the action, valued according to your usual cost accounting practices.

**Examples include:**
- Self-produced consumables (laboratory reagents, test samples)
- Use of research devices or research facilities (electron microscopes, computing clusters)
- Specialised premises for research specimens (climate-controlled storage, animal facilities)
- Standardised testing or research processes (clinical trial protocols, measurement services)
- Hosting services for visiting researchers (office space, accommodation)

### Exception for Transnational Access

You **cannot** use unit costs for internally invoiced goods and services when you are declaring costs for transnational or virtual access to research infrastructure, unless the call conditions explicitly allow it. This restriction prevents you from charging the beneficiary for your capital costs (building depreciation, equipment lease payments) since these are not eligible for access services.

## Conditions for Use: Consistency and Audit Trail

### Consistency Requirement

Your unit cost methodology must be based on your usual cost accounting practices, which means:

- The method uses objective criteria defined in your accounting system
- The same method applies across your entire organisation regardless of funding source (EU or non-EU)
- The method is applied consistently to all projects and all cost categories
- It complies with your internal financial regulations and accounting standards

### Audit Trail and Documentation

You must maintain records that the Commission can verify during checks and audits:

- **For staff costs:** Proof that the person worked the number of hours declared (timesheets, activity logs, working time records)
- **For internally invoiced goods/services:** Proof of the number of units used, provided, or produced (inventory records, service delivery logs, facility access records)

The Commission may conduct ex-post audits up to 2 years after your final payment to verify these records exist and support your unit cost declarations.

## Calculation Methodology

### Formula for Average Personnel Costs

Unit cost = Total annual personnel costs / Total annual productive hours

**Components:**

- **Total annual personnel costs:** All actual costs recorded in your accounts for a given job category (salary, benefits, employer social contributions, statutory charges)
- **Total annual productive hours:** The actual hours worked on productive activities (project work, research, teaching) excluding holidays, sick leave, administrative leave
- **Reliable proxy:** The resulting unit cost must reliably reflect the actual costs incurred; you cannot use arbitrary figures or estimates unmoored from your accounts

### Costs That Can Be Included

- Gross salaries and wages
- Employer's social contributions
- Statutory benefits and charges
- Reasonable allowances for professional development if part of your normal practice

### Updating the Unit Cost

You may adjust unit costs based on budgeted or estimated figures if they are:
- Relevant for calculating personnel costs
- Reasonable and based on objective, verifiable information
- Updated at regular intervals (typically annually)

**Reference period:** The actual data supporting the unit cost should be from your most recent accounting year (e.g. the calendar year preceding the finalisation of the calculations).

## Internally Invoiced Goods and Services: Costing Rules

### Using Actual Costs

For internally invoiced goods and services, you must use the actual costs recorded in your accounts. This includes:

- Direct costs (materials, labour directly attributable to producing the good or service)
- Indirect costs (overhead allocated via your cost-accounting drivers, such as depreciation, utilities, facility management)

**You cannot use flat-rate indirect costs** (e.g. a blanket 20% mark-up on all overheads) when calculating these unit costs.

### Co-Owned Resources

If a resource is co-owned by your organisation and a third party (e.g. a shared research facility with another institution), you may use the costs from the third party's accounts **only if:**
- The third party is named in your grant agreement (for example, as a contributor of in-kind resources)
- The costs meet all other eligibility conditions (ineligible costs are excluded)

### Preventing Double Funding

Your unit cost calculation must ensure that:
- Each cost element is counted only once
- No cost is allocated to more than one unit cost category or flat rate
- No cost is double-funded through multiple EU grants

## Documents Required

Your method documentation must clearly answer these questions:

1. **How is the unit cost calculated and what costs are included?**
   - Describe the formula or procedure (e.g. "sum of payroll for category X divided by productive hours in category X")
   - Itemise which cost categories are included (salaries, social contributions, allowances, etc.)

2. **How often is the unit cost reviewed and updated?**
   - Specify the frequency (e.g. "annually", "every two years")
   - State the process for reviewing and approving changes

3. **When are the calculations updated?**
   - Give a specific date or period (e.g. "every year at the end of February")

4. **What is the reference period for the actual data?**
   - Specify the accounting period used (e.g. "the calendar year preceding finalisation")
   - Explain how historical or projected data is handled

5. **Which unit cost version applies to project reports?**
   - Clarify whether you use the most recently calculated unit cost for all current reports, or whether older projects continue under their original unit cost
   - State whether you update costs retroactively or prospectively

## Common Audit Issues

### Ineligible Costs

Auditors frequently flag unit costs that include:
- Capital depreciation when declaring virtual or transnational access to infrastructure
- Costs that are already eligible for separate reimbursement under other budget lines
- Overhead costs forbidden by the call conditions (e.g. costs of buildings for certain action types)

### Lack of Consistent Application

If your organisation uses different unit costs for EU-funded and non-EU-funded projects (or applies the method inconsistently), the Commission will reject the costs.

### Weak Audit Trail

Insufficient timesheets, inventory records, or facility-access logs mean the Commission cannot verify that the hours or units claimed actually occurred.

### Double Funding

If you declare an item both as a unit cost (internally invoiced service) and as a separate direct cost, the entire amount becomes ineligible.

### Gaps in Documentation

If your written method does not clearly explain how the unit cost is calculated or which version applies to the current reporting period, auditors will request clarification or disallow the costs pending investigation.
