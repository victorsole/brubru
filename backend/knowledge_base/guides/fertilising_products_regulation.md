# EU Fertilising Products Regulation (FPR)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: The EU's product-safety and CE-marking law for fertilisers, soil improvers, growing media and plant biostimulants. It replaced the old "EC fertiliser" regime and, for the first time, opened the harmonised single market to recovered, recycled and organic fertilising products alongside conventional mineral ones.
- **Legal basis / CELEX**: Regulation (EU) 2019/1009 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 5 June 2019, laying down rules on the making available on the market of EU fertilising products and amending Regulations (EC) No 1069/2009 and (EC) No 1107/2009 and repealing Regulation (EC) No 2003/2003. CELEX: **32019R1009**.
- **Official text**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019R1009 (consolidated version, incorporating later amendments: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02019R1009-20251230)
- **Who runs it**: DG GROW (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs) is the lead Commission service. Enforcement is by national market surveillance authorities and accredited notified bodies designated by each Member State; the Commission maintains oversight via committee comitology and delegated/implementing acts to update the annexes.
- **Application date**: applicable **since 16 July 2022** (three years after entry into force on 15 July 2019, per Article 51). Regulation (EC) No 2003/2003 (the old "EC fertiliser" regime) was repealed on the same date, though products already placed on the market under it may continue to circulate under transitional rules.
- **Key mechanism**: harmonisation is **optional, not mandatory**. Manufacturers may choose to CE-mark a product as an "EU fertilising product" (free circulation across the whole internal market) or keep selling it as a national fertiliser under domestic law and the Treaty's mutual-recognition rules. The FPR does not ban non-CE-marked fertilisers.
- **Structure**: Product Function Categories (PFCs, what the product does) crossed with Component Material Categories (CMCs, what the product is made of). A product must fit into a PFC and be made exclusively of materials matching listed CMCs.
- **Headline safety limit**: cadmium (Cd) in phosphate (inorganic macronutrient) fertilisers capped at **60 mg/kg P2O5** since application. The Regulation carries a legally mandated review clause: **the Commission must report to the European Parliament and Council by 16 July 2026** on the feasibility of lowering the limit further, to 40 or 20 mg/kg.
- **Key amendment on the books**: Regulation (EU) 2024/2516 (adopted 18 September 2024, CELEX 32024R2516) added an option for voluntary digital labelling of EU fertilising products.
- **Not to be confused with**: `fertilisers_action_plan_2026`, the Commission's May 2026 policy Communication responding to the fertiliser price/supply crisis. The FPR is product-safety law already in force since 2022; the Action Plan is a 2026 political strategy document about affordability, decarbonisation and import dependency that references the FPR as one of its legal anchors.

## What the Regulation covers

The FPR defines a "fertilising product" broadly: any substance, mixture, micro-organism or other material applied to plants, mushrooms or their rhizosphere/mycosphere to supply nutrients or improve nutrition efficiency. This single legal concept replaces the narrower pre-2022 idea of "fertiliser" and folds in several product types that used to sit in regulatory grey zones (notably plant biostimulants, which used to be argued over between fertiliser and pesticide law).

### The seven Product Function Categories (PFCs)

A CE-marked EU fertilising product must be assigned to one of:

1. **PFC 1 – Fertilisers**: split into 1(A) organic fertilisers, 1(B) organo-mineral fertilisers, and 1(C) inorganic (mineral) fertilisers, further divided by macronutrient (straight vs compound) and micronutrient fertilisers.
2. **PFC 2 – Liming materials**: correct soil acidity (pH).
3. **PFC 3 – Soil improvers**: improve the physical, chemical or biological properties of soil (excluding nutrient supply as the primary function).
4. **PFC 4 – Growing media**: substrates other than soil in situ in which plants/mushrooms are grown (potting mixes, substrates for hydroponics, etc.).
5. **PFC 5 – Inhibitors**: nitrification, denitrification and urease inhibitors that improve the nutrient-release pattern of a fertiliser.
6. **PFC 6 – Plant biostimulants**: a genuinely new EU legal category, created by the FPR, for products that stimulate plant nutrition processes independently of nutrient content (improving nutrient-use efficiency, abiotic-stress tolerance, quality traits, or nutrient availability in soil/rhizosphere). The FPR was the first legal framework worldwide to recognise biostimulants as a distinct category, separating them definitively from Regulation (EC) 1107/2009 (plant protection products).
7. **PFC 7 – Fertilising product blends**: mixtures of two or more products from PFCs 1-6, each blended component must independently meet the requirements of its own PFC.

### The 15+ Component Material Categories (CMCs)

CMCs govern the raw materials a fertilising product may legally be made from: for example CMC 1 (virgin material substances and mixtures), CMC 3 (compost), CMC 4/5 (digestate), CMC 7 (microorganisms, with a positive list currently covering *Azotobacter spp.*, *Rhizobium spp.*, *Azospirillum spp.* and mycorrhizal fungi), CMC 10 (certain animal by-products/derived products that have reached the "end point" under Regulation (EC) 1069/2009), CMC 11 (by-products under the Waste Framework Directive), and further categories for industrial by-products, polymers, and other recovered materials. A fertilising product may consist only of materials meeting the listed CMCs, and inputs classified as waste, or as by-products that have not exited waste status, are excluded unless specifically listed. Several CMCs (1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 11) also trigger a REACH registration obligation on the underlying substances (see `reach_chemicals_regulation`).

## The circular-economy dimension

Before 2022, EU fertiliser law (Regulation (EC) 2003/2003) covered essentially only mineral fertilisers and did not provide a harmonised route to market for organic or recovered/recycled fertilising products. The FPR was designed explicitly to close that gap: by defining CMCs for compost, digestate, certain animal by-products and other recovered materials, it gives organic and recycled fertilisers, including nutrients recovered from waste streams such as struvite (recovered phosphate), the same CE-marking route to the single market that mineral fertilisers already had. This is one of the reasons the Fertilisers Action Plan (May 2026) treats the FPR as an existing legal anchor for its "circular mineral fertilisers" pillar, which aims to reduce EU dependency on imported virgin phosphate rock (see `fertilisers_action_plan_2026`).

## Cadmium in phosphate fertilisers

Phosphate rock, the main input for mineral phosphate fertilisers, naturally contains cadmium, a toxic heavy metal that accumulates in agricultural soils over time. The FPR was the first EU instrument to set a harmonised cadmium limit for CE-marked phosphate fertilisers, at **60 mg/kg P2O5**, applicable from 16 July 2022. This was a compromise: industry and Member States with phosphate-rock-dependent production (Sweden, Finland, some importers reliant on Russian/Moroccan rock) argued a stricter limit would be costly or supply-constraining; environmental and public-health advocates (backed by agencies such as France's ANSES) argued 60 mg/kg is still too permissive.

The Regulation resolves this with a built-in review clause: the Commission must submit a report to the European Parliament and Council, by **16 July 2026**, assessing the feasibility of lowering the cadmium limit to 40 mg/kg or 20 mg/kg, weighing environmental factors (soil and climatic conditions), health factors, and socioeconomic factors including security of supply. Any actual tightening of the limit requires a further legislative or delegated act; the review deadline itself does not automatically change the 60 mg/kg figure.

## Conformity assessment and notified bodies

To be legally CE-marked, an EU fertilising product must go through a conformity assessment procedure set out in Annex IV of the FPR. Annex IV lists several assessment "modules" (broadly modelled on the EU's New Legislative Framework used across other CE-marking regimes such as machinery or construction products), ranging from manufacturer self-declaration for lower-risk PFCs/CMCs to mandatory third-party involvement for higher-risk categories, particularly recovered and by-product-derived materials.

- **Notified bodies**: independent conformity assessment bodies, accredited and designated by a Member State, then notified to the European Commission and other Member States. They test and certify higher-risk products (or specific modules) before CE marking can be affixed.
- **Manufacturer obligations**: draw up an EU declaration of conformity, affix the CE mark, keep technical documentation, and ensure traceability through the supply chain (importers and distributors have parallel obligations to verify compliance).
- **Market surveillance**: national authorities police non-compliant products already on the market, including recall and withdrawal powers, coordinated at EU level for cross-border cases.

## Digital labelling amendment (2024)

Regulation (EU) 2024/2516 of the European Parliament and of the Council, adopted 18 September 2024 (CELEX 32024R2516), amended the FPR to allow **voluntary digital labelling** of EU fertilising products, letting manufacturers provide some of the mandatory label information (Annex III) via a digital format (e.g. QR code) instead of, or alongside, physical labelling, easing the compliance burden for products sold across many linguistic markets.

## Why it matters to a policy professional

- **It is the enabling law behind the EU's "circular fertilisers" push**, referenced repeatedly in the 2026 Fertilisers Action Plan as the existing legal framework that already lets recovered-nutrient and organic products reach the whole internal market once CE-marked; any expansion of that framework (new CMCs for additional recovered materials) would come as a delegated/implementing act amending the FPR annexes, not a new regulation.
- **The optional-harmonisation design matters strategically**: because national fertiliser regimes remain legal in parallel, Member States retain scope for stricter national rules (Sweden applies a stricter national cadmium limit than the EU's 60 mg/kg to mineral fertilisers sold domestically), which is a live compliance complication for pan-EU manufacturers.
- **The 16 July 2026 cadmium review deadline is squarely live now**: any Commission report or announcement on lowering the cadmium limit should be checked against this Regulation, not the Fertilisers Action Plan, since it is a distinct, pre-scheduled legal obligation under the FPR itself.
- **Biostimulants remain a fast-moving area**: harmonised standards for testing efficacy claims (soil improvers, biostimulants) are still being developed by European standardisation bodies under Commission mandate, so full practical implementation of some PFCs lags the 2022 application date.
- **Cross-cutting compliance**: manufacturers of CE-marked fertilising products must also satisfy REACH registration for qualifying component materials (`reach_chemicals_regulation`) and CLP classification/labelling obligations for hazardous substances or mixtures used as inputs (`eu_clp_regulation_classification_labelling`).
