# CJEU judgment C-70/25 (15 October 2026): a bank must refund an unauthorised payment immediately, even if it alleges the customer's gross negligence

## QUICK FACTS
- **Court of Justice, judgment of 15 October 2026, Case C-70/25** (preliminary reference from a Polish court; press release No 147/26). Interprets the **Payment Services Directive, Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2)**, Articles 73(1) and 74(1).
- **The facts:** a customer selling an item online received a fraudulent link presented as coming from the sales platform, entered her online-banking login on a site spoofing her bank, and a third party transferred **about EUR 700** from her account. She notified the bank the next day; the bank refused to refund, alleging gross negligence.
- **The ruling:** for an unauthorised payment transaction, the payment service provider must refund **immediately**, and in any event **by the end of the next business day** after noting or being notified of it, provided the customer notified without undue delay. **The ONLY exception** to that immediate refund is where the provider has **reasonable grounds to suspect fraud by the customer** and communicates them to the competent national authority.
- **Gross negligence does NOT allow refusing the refund.** The provider's view that the customer was grossly negligent (for example, by not keeping security credentials safe) does not entitle it to refuse to refund immediately.
- **Two stages:** first the refund; then, **afterwards**, the provider **may seek reimbursement** from the customer of losses caused by the customer's intentional or grossly negligent breach of obligations. **The burden of proving the gross negligence is on the provider.**
- **Why (the Court):** allowing a refusal from the outset would make the immediate-refund obligation ineffective and force the customer to sue to assert the right.
- **Earlier step:** Advocate General Rantos proposed the same answer on 5 March 2026 (see `eu_payment_fraud_consumer_redress`).
- **Not read:** the full judgment text (CURIA publishes it on the day of delivery); this guide is based on the Court's press release. The paragraph numbers of the judgment are therefore not given here.

## How to answer
Give the rule (refund by the end of the next business day; only exception: suspected fraud by the customer reported to the authority), then the second stage (the bank may later claim losses if it proves gross negligence). This is EU law under PSD2; national rules transposing it (for example Spain's Royal Decree-law 19/2018) must be read in its light.
