# EU Equality and Anti-Discrimination Framework

## QUICK FACTS
- Core Treaty basis: Article 19 TFEU (anti-discrimination), Article 21 Charter of Fundamental Rights
- Race Equality Directive: Directive 2000/43/EC (CELEX 32000L0043), prohibits racial/ethnic discrimination in employment, education, social protection, goods and services
- Employment Equality Directive: Directive 2000/78/EC (CELEX 32000L0078), prohibits discrimination on religion, disability, age, sexual orientation in employment
- Gender Equality (recast): Directive 2006/54/EC (CELEX 32006L0054)
- Horizontal Equal Treatment Directive: COM(2008)426, proposed 2 July 2008, BLOCKED in Council since 2008 (unanimity required under Article 19 TFEU). Would extend protection beyond employment to goods, services, education, social protection.
- LGBTIQ Equality Strategy 2020-2025: COM(2020)698, 12 November 2020
- LGBTIQ Equality Strategy 2026-2030: adopted October 2025, builds on 2020-2025 framework
- Gender Equality Strategy 2020-2025: COM(2020)152
- EU Anti-Racism Action Plan 2020-2025: COM(2020)565
- Strategy for the Rights of Persons with Disabilities 2021-2030: COM(2021)101
- EP resolution: P8_TA(2023)0582 calling for adoption of horizontal directive
- Responsible DG: DG JUST (Justice and Consumers)
- Responsible Commissioner: Michael McGrath (Democracy, Justice, Rule of Law)
- Key CJEU cases: C-507/18 (NH v Associazione Avvocatura LGBTI), C-356/21 (J.K. v TP S.A.)

## Recent Developments (March 2026)

### European Citizens' Initiative: Ban on Conversion Practices
**Decision (EU) 2026/770** (CELEX: 32026D0770) -- Commission Implementing Decision of 25 March 2026 registering a European Citizens' Initiative to ban conversion practices across the EU. Conversion practices (sometimes called "conversion therapy") are interventions aimed at changing, suppressing or diverting a person's sexual orientation, gender identity or gender expression. The registration means the organisers can start collecting signatures (1 million needed from at least 7 Member States within 12 months). The EP debated this topic on 25 March 2026 in the Brussels mini-plenary.

## Overview

EU equality law is a layered framework combining primary law (treaties and Charter), secondary legislation (directives), and soft-law strategies. The core principle is that all persons are equal before the law and must be protected from discrimination on grounds of sex, racial or ethnic origin, religion or belief, disability, age, and sexual orientation. The grounds are not treated uniformly: some grounds enjoy broader protection (race, sex) while others (religion, disability, age, sexual orientation) are currently limited to the employment sphere only.

Enforcement relies on national equality bodies, courts, and the European Court of Justice. The Commission monitors transposition and infringement proceedings have been opened against several Member States for inadequate implementation.

## The Blocked Horizontal Directive

COM(2008)426, tabled on 2 July 2008, would have extended protection against discrimination on grounds of religion or belief, disability, age, and sexual orientation beyond employment to: education, social protection and social security, and access to goods and services including housing.

It has been blocked in Council for over 16 years. Article 19 TFEU requires unanimity, and a small number of Member States have consistently opposed it. The European Parliament has repeatedly called for its adoption. Under the von der Leyen II Commission, Commissioner McGrath has indicated intention to revive the proposal, but no new text had been tabled as of early 2026.

Civil society organisations (ILGA-Europe, AGE Platform, EDF) consider the absence of the horizontal directive the single largest gap in EU equality law.

## LGBTIQ Equality Strategies

### Strategy 2020-2025 (COM(2020)698)
Four pillars: (1) tackling LGBTIQ discrimination in practice, (2) ensuring LGBTIQ safety, (3) building inclusive societies, (4) leading by example globally. Delivered mutual recognition of parenthood (Regulation (EU) 2022/1904 via CJEU C-490/20). Commission filed infringement against Hungary over anti-LGBTIQ legislation.

### Strategy 2026-2030
Adopted October 2025. Focuses on implementation gaps identified in the 2020-2025 mid-term review, continuation of parenthood recognition implementation, and external action.

## Gender and Disability Strategies

The Gender Equality Strategy 2020-2025 (COM(2020)152) set targets including 40% women in decision-making roles in listed companies (Women on Boards Directive, Directive (EU) 2022/2381, CELEX 32022L2381). Pay Transparency Directive (Directive (EU) 2023/970, CELEX 32023L0970) requires employers to disclose pay data and enforce equal pay audits.

The Strategy for the Rights of Persons with Disabilities 2021-2030 (COM(2021)101) builds on the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD), to which the EU is a party. The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) entered into application in June 2025.

## Key CJEU Cases

- **C-507/18 NH v Associazione Avvocatura LGBTI (2020):** public statement of unwillingness to hire an LGBTI person constitutes direct discrimination under Directive 2000/78/EC even without a concrete vacancy
- **C-356/21 J.K. v TP S.A. (2023):** refusal to renew a contract due to public support for LGBTIQ rights constitutes discrimination on grounds of sexual orientation by association
- **C-490/20 V.M.A. v Stolichna obshtina (2021):** Bulgaria obliged to issue identity documents to child whose Bulgarian parent and same-sex partner are identified as parents in Spanish birth certificate
