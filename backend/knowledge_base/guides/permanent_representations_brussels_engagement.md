## What a Permanent Representation Is

A Permanent Representation (PermRep) is the diplomatic mission of a Member State accredited to the European Union, based in Brussels. Unlike an embassy to a third country, a PermRep is not accredited to a host-state government: it is accredited to an international organisation (the EU), operating under the Protocol on Privileges and Immunities annexed to the EU Treaties.

The legal foundations are:

- **Article 16(7) TEU**: "The Council shall meet in public when it deliberates and votes on a draft legislative act." Coreper prepares the work of the Council. The Council Rules of Procedure (Decision 2009/937/EU) allocate preparatory work to Coreper, which operates through the PermReps.
- **Protocol 7 on Privileges and Immunities of the EU**: Establishes the legal status of EU institutions and their staff, and by extension the national officials seconded to PermReps for their EU-facing duties.
- **VCDR (Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, 1961)**: While technically applicable only to bilateral diplomatic relations, the EU has adopted equivalent privileges by treaty analogy for PermRep staff.

Each PermRep is staffed by officials seconded from the national foreign ministry alongside specialists seconded from line ministries (finance, justice, agriculture, transport, environment, health, and so on). A mid-sized Member State may deploy 30--80 officials; larger states considerably more.

## Internal Structure

### Permanent Representative (Ambassador)

The Permanent Representative holds the title of Ambassador and is the head of mission. They sit in the **Committee of Permanent Representatives** at the level of Ambassadors, commonly called **Coreper II**. Coreper II covers:

- Foreign affairs and external relations (RELEX, CSDP, trade)
- Economic and Financial Affairs (ECOFIN, eurozone governance)
- Justice and Home Affairs (JHA Council)
- Institutional, development, and enlargement files
- General Affairs Council preparatory work

### Deputy Permanent Representative

The Deputy Permanent Representative is a senior diplomat who sits in **Coreper I**, which covers most internal-market, social, and sectoral legislation:

- Environment (ENV Council)
- Transport, Telecommunications, Energy (TTE Council)
- Agriculture and Fisheries (AGRI Council)
- Employment, Social Policy, Health (EPSCO Council)
- Competitiveness (COMPET Council: internal market, research, industry)

In practice, Coreper I handles the majority of draft legislation by volume.

### PSC Ambassador

The Political and Security Committee (PSC) Ambassador is a separate post held by a senior diplomat at ambassadorial rank. The PSC monitors international situations relevant to CFSP and CSDP, proposes policy options to the Council, and exercises political oversight over EU crisis management operations. The PSC Ambassador does not sit in Coreper I or Coreper II.

### Sector Attaches

Below ambassadorial level, each PermRep deploys ministry-seconded **attaches** (also called counsellors or first secretaries) who lead the Member State's work in the relevant **Council Working Party**. Examples:

| Policy area | Typical Working Party | Attache origin ministry |
|---|---|---|
| Environment | Working Party on Environment | Ministry of environment |
| Financial services | Financial Services Working Party | Ministry of finance / central bank |
| Agriculture | Special Committee on Agriculture (SCA) | Ministry of agriculture |
| Justice / civil law | Civil Law Working Party | Ministry of justice |
| Transport | Working Party on Transport | Ministry of transport |
| Competition | Working Party on Competition | Ministry of economy |
| Health | Working Party on Public Health | Ministry of health |
| Digital | Working Party on Telecommunications and Information Society | Ministry of digital |

Attaches negotiate the Member State's detailed positions in Working Parties before files reach Coreper. This is where the technical text of Council general approaches and mandates is shaped. Engaging an attache early -- before Coreper meets -- is the highest-leverage moment for a public-affairs professional.

## How Decisions Flow: Capital to Council

Understanding the decision-flow allows practitioners to intervene at the right stage.

**Step 1 -- Line ministry forms a position**
When the Commission publishes a proposal, each national line ministry assesses the impact on domestic interests. The ministry drafts a negotiating mandate, often subject to coordination with the permanent secretary and the office responsible for EU affairs.

**Step 2 -- The attache receives the mandate**
The line-ministry attache at the PermRep receives the position in writing. Large Member States often have a dedicated EU-coordination function (e.g. the German Federal Government's Europe Division, France's SGAE) that aligns positions across ministries before transmitting them to Brussels.

**Step 3 -- Council Working Party negotiations**
The attache represents the Member State in the relevant Working Party. Sessions may run over several Council Presidencies. The attache tables amendments to the Commission proposal, seeks alliances with like-minded delegations, and reports back to the capital after each meeting. Texts progressively annotated with positions (bracketed text, footnotes, "I/A" point readiness indicators) are internal Council documents circulated only to delegations.

**Step 4 -- Coreper I or Coreper II**
When the Working Party reaches agreement or an impasse on a point, the file moves up to Coreper. Items where agreement is confirmed are placed on Part I of the Council agenda (adopted without debate as "A points"). Contested items land on Part II ("B points") and require ministerial discussion.

**Step 5 -- Council formation votes or endorses**
The relevant Council formation (AGRI, ENV, ECOFIN, etc.) adopts, amends, or rejects. For co-decision files (ordinary legislative procedure), the Council's position is transmitted to the European Parliament. Trilogue negotiations involve Council representatives, typically the attaches and Coreper plus Presidency officials.

## Engagement Tactics for Public-Affairs Work

### Engaging Attaches

- Attaches are the most accessible channel. They are not ministers and are not subject to the same political-communications rules. A 30-minute technical meeting is standard practice.
- Request meetings via the PermRep's general inbox or the sector attache's contact (found via the PermRep's personnel/who-is-who page and the Publications Office Who is Who directory).
- Prepare a one-page "non-paper" or "food for thought" document -- standard Council working culture. Avoid branded position papers with heavy corporate styling.
- Focus on **technical implementation** arguments: attaches need to defend positions in Working Party against other delegations. Give them specific textual alternatives to table.
- Timing: engage **before the Working Party's first substantive reading** of the article or chapter you care about. After a delegation has tabled an amendment, shifting it is harder.

### The Presidency PermRep

The Member State holding the Council Presidency (rotating six-monthly) has additional leverage: the Presidency PermRep chairs Working Parties and Coreper. The Presidency team is heavily overstretched. Engage early in the Presidency's programme; avoid the final weeks when the Presidency is scrambling to close files.

### Coalition-building via PermReps

When a single Member State's position is unlikely to block or shape an outcome, coalition letters addressed to the Presidency (co-signed by 4--8 Member States) carry significant weight. PermReps are the channel for coordinating such letters among national capitals. Working-party chairs also read informal coalition signals from delegations as indicators of a blocking minority.

### Timing Grid

| Phase | Council stage | Best action |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal published | Pre-Working Party | Brief attaches; circulate non-papers; identify allies |
| First Working Party reading | Working Party | Provide technical text; meet attache |
| General Approach building | Working Party / Coreper | Coalition letters; Presidency informal |
| General Approach agreed | Coreper | Focus shifts to EP trilogue team |
| Trilogue | Presidency + EUCO/EP | Monitor; maintain contact with Presidency PermRep |
| Final text | Council formal adoption | No material change possible |

### What the PermRep Cannot Do

PermReps implement ministerial mandates; they cannot override them. If a ministry has taken a firm public position, the PermRep attache will not deviate without instruction. In that scenario, the engagement must go back to the capital -- to the line ministry, the state secretary's cabinet, or the EU-affairs coordination unit.

## Sub-national Delegations

Several regions and federal entities maintain liaison offices in Brussels alongside but entirely separate from the 27 PermReps. They have no Coreper seat, no Council vote, and no formal role in EU legislative negotiations. Their value is informational and political:

- They monitor proceedings and feed intelligence back to regional governments.
- They facilitate contacts between regional stakeholders and Commission, EP, and PermRep officials.
- Some (notably the German Lander offices and the Belgian regional representations) have constitutional rights to be consulted by the national PermRep on files within their competence.

Key sub-national delegations in Brussels include:

- **Catalonia (Generalitat de Catalunya)**: `exteriors.gencat.cat/en/ambits-dactuacio/afers_exteriors/delegacions_govern/ue/delegacio/index.html`
- **Bavaria (Bavarian State Representation)**: `eu.bayern.de`
- **Flanders (Flemish Government Liaison Office)**: `flanders.be/en/about-flanders/flanders-in-europe`
- **Scotland House Brussels**: `scotlandhouse.eu`

For a full treatment, see the planned guide `subnational_delegations_to_eu_catalonia_lander`.

## PermRep vs Commission vs European Parliament: Where to Engage

| Aspect | PermRep / Council | Commission | European Parliament |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role | Co-legislator (national positions) | Proposes | Co-legislates (MEPs) |
| Key stage | Working Party, Coreper | Pre-proposal, consultation | Reading, trilogue |
| Best channel | Sector attache meeting; non-paper | Consultation response; expert group | MEP meeting; rapporteur |
| Timing | Before Working Party article reading | 12-week consultation window | Before lead committee vote |
| Accountability | National ministry | College of Commissioners | Voters |
| Transparency | Lower (internal Council docs) | Higher (Have Your Say) | High (public committee meetings) |
