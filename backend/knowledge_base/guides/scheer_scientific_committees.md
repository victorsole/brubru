# SCHEER and SCCS: the Commission's independent non-food scientific committees

## QUICK FACTS
- Two committees: **SCCS** (Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety) and **SCHEER** (Scientific Committee on Health, Environmental and Emerging Risks)
- Legal basis: Commission Decision (EU) 2024/1514 of 7 August 2015 on establishing Scientific Committees in the field of public health, consumer safety and the environment (CELEX 32024D1514, OJ L 2024/1514, 31 May 2024, applying since 20 June 2024), which repealed and replaced the earlier Decision 2008/721/EC (CELEX 32008D0721)
- Status: independent expert advisory bodies of the European Commission, not EU agencies. Members serve in a personal capacity, not as representatives of their governments or employers
- Secretariat: provided by the European Commission's Directorate-General for Health and Food Safety (DG SANTE), a dedicated unit shared by both committees
- Membership: up to 19 members per committee, appointed following a public call for expressions of interest, serving fixed multi-annual terms. Current term runs 2021-2026 (ending 31 December 2026); a call to renew SCCS membership for the 2027-2031 term (five years, starting 1 January 2027) was open in 2026
- SCCS remit: safety of cosmetic ingredients and other non-food consumer products (toys, textiles, clothing, personal care and household products) and services (tattooing, artificial sun tanning). Its opinions feed directly into the annexes of the Cosmetics Regulation, Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 (CELEX 32009R1223)
- SCHEER remit: health, environmental and emerging risks not covered by any other EU risk-assessment body, e.g. medical device materials, e-cigarette ingredients and emissions, nanotechnologies, antimicrobial resistance, noise, electromagnetic fields, and environmental quality standards for pollutants
- Predecessor committees (2008-2016): SCCP (Scientific Committee on Consumer Products), SCHER (Scientific Committee on Health and Environmental Risks) and SCENIHR (Scientific Committee on Emerging and Newly Identified Health Risks). SCHER and SCENIHR merged into SCHEER in April 2016; SCCP became SCCS
- Process: Commission services issue a mandate (request) to a committee, which adopts a Preliminary Opinion, opens it to public consultation (typically an eight-week window for SCCS), then adopts a Final Opinion taking the comments into account
- Official pages: https://health.ec.europa.eu/scientific-committees_en , SCCS: https://health.ec.europa.eu/scientific-committees/scientific-committee-consumer-safety-sccs_en , SCHEER: https://health.ec.europa.eu/scientific-committees/scientific-committee-health-environmental-and-emerging-risks-scheer_en
- Contact: SANTE-SCCS@ec.europa.eu and SANTE-SCHEER@ec.europa.eu

Both committees are the Commission's in-house source of independent scientific risk assessment for everything that falls outside the remit of the EU's specialised risk-assessment agencies: EFSA for food and feed, ECHA's committees for industrial chemicals under REACH and CLP, and EMA for medicines. Where a policy professional needs to know whether a cosmetic ingredient, a medical-device material, or an emerging technology such as nanomaterials or e-cigarettes has been scientifically assessed at EU level, SCCS or SCHEER is usually where that assessment sits.

## What SCCS does

The Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety assesses the health and safety risks (chemical, biological, mechanical and other physical) of non-food consumer products and related services. In practice its workload is dominated by cosmetics:

- **Cosmetic ingredients**: hair dyes, preservatives, UV filters, colorants and other substances used in cosmetic products, especially those the Cosmetics Regulation lists as restricted or provisionally authorised, where continued use depends on a positive safety opinion
- **Nanomaterials in cosmetics**: the Cosmetics Regulation requires a specific SCCS safety opinion before certain nanomaterials can be used, given their different toxicological behaviour compared with bulk-form substances
- **CMR substances** (carcinogenic, mutagenic or toxic for reproduction) proposed for use in cosmetics, which need an exceptional SCCS safety clearance under Article 15 of the Cosmetics Regulation
- Other non-food consumer products and services: toys, textiles, clothing, childcare articles, tattooing and permanent make-up inks, artificial sun tanning (sunbeds)

At the end of the risk assessment, the SCCS adopts a formal Opinion. It can also publish self-initiated Statements and, distinctively, **Notes of Guidance for the Testing of Cosmetic Ingredients and their Safety Evaluation**, the reference methodology document that industry safety assessors use when compiling a Cosmetic Product Safety Report under the Cosmetics Regulation.

## What SCHEER does

The Scientific Committee on Health, Environmental and Emerging Risks covers everything of a health or environmental nature that does not fall under another EU risk-assessment body's remit, and typically deals with broader, more complex or more novel questions than SCCS. Its published areas of activity include:

- **Medical devices**: materials used in medical devices, including devices incorporating substances of human or animal origin, and phthalates or other substances of concern in device components. SCHEER opinions here support implementation of the Medical Devices Regulation
- **E-cigarettes**: the ingredients, emissions and health effects of electronic cigarettes and refill liquids, informing the Tobacco Products Directive framework
- **Environmental risks**: pollutants in environmental media (air, water, waste, soil), Environmental Quality Standards for priority substances under the Water Framework Directive (SCHEER has issued opinions on substances such as PFAS, glyphosate, triclosan and HBCDD in this context), and life-cycle environmental assessment
- **Emerging and newly identified risks**: antimicrobial resistance, nanotechnologies, tissue engineering, blood products, fertility-reducing substances, physical hazards such as noise and electromagnetic fields, and the cumulative or synergic effects of combined risk factors ("chemical mixtures")
- **Public health determinants and non-communicable diseases**, when the Commission specifically requests it

SCHEER also runs a standing self-mandate exercise called **FORENV** ("Foresight on Emerging Environmental issues"), producing an annual position paper flagging new or evolving risks the Commission should watch, distinct from opinions issued in response to a formal Commission mandate.

## How the committees are run

- **Secretariat**: both committees are serviced by a single dedicated Commission unit within DG SANTE (Directorate for Health Systems, Medical Products and Innovation), which manages the mandate process, member appointments, plenary and working-group logistics, and publication of opinions
- **Independent experts**: members are individual scientists, not government or industry representatives, selected through a public call for expressions of interest published on the Commission's Database of Experts. Selection criteria emphasise scientific excellence, multidisciplinary expertise, and absence of conflicts of interest; each member submits an annual Declaration of Interests
- **Committee size**: up to 19 members per committee, working in English
- **Working groups**: for a specific mandate, the responsible committee typically sets up a working group (which may draw on external hearing experts beyond the 19 core members) to draft the scientific assessment, which the full plenary then adopts
- **Mandates**: Commission services (usually DG SANTE, but also DG GROW, DG ENV or others depending on the dossier) submit a formal written mandate defining the precise question the committee is asked to answer. Committees do not select their own workload except for self-initiated statements and the SCHEER FORENV exercise
- **Public consultation on preliminary opinions**: before finalising most opinions, the committee publishes a Preliminary Opinion and opens a public consultation (commonly an eight-week window for SCCS ingredient opinions) inviting scientific comments and additional data from stakeholders, industry, NGOs and the public. The Final Opinion records how the comments were addressed
- **Plain-language outputs**: for opinions of direct public relevance, the committees produce easy-to-read fact sheets and web summaries alongside the full scientific opinion
- **Coordination with other bodies**: Decision (EU) 2024/1514 requires the committees to coordinate with, and avoid overlapping the remit of, other EU risk-assessment bodies, in particular EFSA

## From opinion to regulatory action

A SCCS or SCHEER Opinion is scientific advice, not law. It does not itself change any legal text. The route from opinion to binding rule differs by sector:

- **Cosmetics (SCCS)**: a positive or restrictive SCCS opinion on an ingredient is the technical basis the Commission uses to amend the relevant annex of the Cosmetics Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009) via a Commission Implementing or Delegated act, for example adding, restricting or banning a substance in Annex II (prohibited substances), Annex III (restricted substances), Annex IV (permitted colorants), Annex V (permitted preservatives) or Annex VI (permitted UV filters). Member State experts examine the draft act in the relevant comitology committee before adoption
- **Medical devices (SCHEER)**: opinions on materials of concern feed into Commission guidance and, where relevant, amendments under the Medical Devices Regulation framework, informing notified-body assessment criteria and substance restrictions (see `medical_devices_regulation`)
- **Tobacco and e-cigarettes (SCHEER)**: opinions inform any future review of the Tobacco Products Directive and related emission or ingredient reporting requirements
- **Environmental standards (SCHEER)**: opinions on Environmental Quality Standards feed into revisions of the priority-substances list under the Water Framework Directive

Because the underlying legal instrument, not the scientific opinion, is what changes market obligations, a policy professional tracking a specific substance or product category should follow both the committee's opinion timeline and the corresponding comitology or delegated-act procedure that will actually amend the law.

## How SCCS and SCHEER differ from other EU scientific bodies

It is easy to confuse the Commission's own scientific committees with the EU's specialised risk-assessment agencies. They are legally and institutionally distinct:

| Body | What it covers | Institutional form |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **SCCS** | Non-food consumer products: cosmetic ingredients, toys, textiles, tattoo inks, sunbeds | Commission expert committee (DG SANTE secretariat), not an agency |
| **SCHEER** | Health, environmental and emerging risks not covered elsewhere: medical device materials, e-cigarettes, environmental pollutants, emerging technologies | Commission expert committee (DG SANTE secretariat), not an agency |
| **EFSA** | Food and feed safety: additives, pesticide residues, novel foods, food contact materials, animal health and welfare linked to the food chain | Independent EU regulatory agency, seat in Parma. See `efsa_agency_overview` |
| **ECHA's RAC and SEAC** | Industrial chemicals under REACH and CLP: classification, restriction, authorisation of substances of very high concern, biocides | Committees of ECHA, an independent EU regulatory agency, seat in Helsinki. See `echa_agency_overview` |
| **EMA** | Medicines: human and veterinary medicinal products, marketing authorisation, pharmacovigilance | Independent EU regulatory agency, seat in Amsterdam |

The practical dividing line: if the question is about a substance in food, ask EFSA. If it is about an industrial chemical's classification, registration or restriction under REACH/CLP, ask ECHA (RAC/SEAC). If it is about a medicine, ask EMA. If it is about a cosmetic ingredient or another non-food consumer product, ask SCCS. If it is a health, environmental or emerging risk that does not fit any of the above and no other EU body has the remit, it goes to SCHEER. Decision (EU) 2024/1514 explicitly requires SCCS and SCHEER to coordinate with EFSA to avoid duplicating work, since some substances (for example certain preservatives or UV filters) can appear in both food and cosmetic uses with separate assessments for each.
