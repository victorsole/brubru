# EU Funding & Tenders Portal Online Manual

## QUICK FACTS

- **Single entry point**: The EU Funding & Tenders Portal is the central electronic portal for all EU grants, procurements and prizes (2021-2027 and 2014-2020 programmes)
- **Public section**: General information (Funding Opportunities, Reference Documents, Calls, Topics)
- **My Area**: Secured personalised section for participants to manage proposals and grants
- **EU Login**: Required to access My Area (work e-mail address only)
- **PIC number**: Unique 9-digit Participant Identification Code issued after Participant Register registration
- **Participant Register**: Mandatory registration for all grant applicants; contains Legal Entity (PIC), LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative), bank account, financial capacity data
- **Roles and access rights**: Organisation roles (LEAR, Account Administrators) and Project roles (Coordinator Contact, Participant Contact, Signatories) control who can view and submit information
- **Submission System**: 6-step wizard (log in, select call, draft proposal, manage parties, complete forms, submit) guides applicants through proposal creation and electronic submission
- **Grant Agreement Preparation (GAP)**: After selection, 3-5 months to prepare and sign the grant agreement; targets 8 months from call deadline to signature
- **Expert Evaluator Portal**: Separate area where external evaluators assess proposals and manage contracts
- **Secure communication**: Portal Messaging Facility for all formal communications with EU services

---

## Portal Overview

The EU Funding & Tenders Portal is managed by the European Commission and used by the EU and its agencies for grants, prizes and procurements under the 2021-2027 and 2014-2020 multi-year financial frameworks.

### Portal Structure

The Portal has two main sections:

1. **Public area**: Contains general information on funding opportunities, calls for proposals, topics, work programmes, guidance documents, and reference materials. Anyone can access this section without registration.

2. **My Area**: A secured, personalised electronic workspace for proposal and grant management. Access is restricted to registered users with specific roles and access rights. Users can view non-public information on their own proposals, projects, contracts, organisations, and communications with EU services.

### Key Functions in My Area

- Register organisations in the Participant Register and obtain a PIC (Participant Identification Code)
- Update and maintain organisation and natural person data
- Create draft proposals and submit formal applications via the Submission System
- Manage project consortia and assign roles to team members
- Sign grant agreements electronically
- Submit reports, cost claims, invoices and receive payments
- Communicate with EU services via the Portal Messaging Facility
- For experts: register, sign contracts, manage evaluation tasks, submit cost claims
- For audit firms: register and manage audit engagement tasks

## Account Creation and EU Login

### Creating an EU Login Account

To access My Area and manage grants on the Portal, you must first create an EU Login account.

**Registration steps:**
1. Go to the Portal homepage and click the Register button
2. Provide your first name, last name and work e-mail address
3. Enter the displayed security code
4. You will receive a confirmation e-mail with a link to complete registration
5. Follow the link and set your password

**Important:** Your EU Login username and password are personal. Do not share them with colleagues. Keep your password secure and change it regularly.

### Accessing the Portal

- If you already have an EU Login account (including former ECAS European Commission Authentication System accounts), log in with those credentials
- Your EU Login account gives you general access to My Area but does NOT grant access to project or contract data until your specific roles are assigned by your organisation's LEAR or project coordinators

## Participant Register: Register Your Organisation

Before submitting a proposal, your organisation must be registered in the Participant Register and assigned a unique Participant Identification Code (PIC).

### Registration Requirements

To register, you will need to provide:
- **Basic administrative and legal information**: Legal form, public or private status, non-profit status (if applicable)
- **Contact details**: Address, phone, e-mail
- **Identification numbers**: VAT number, registration number, or passport number (for natural persons)
- **Supporting documents**: Proof of legal status, registration extract, articles of incorporation, official VAT documents

### Registration Process

**Step 1: Check for existing PIC**
Before creating a new registration, search the Portal Participant Register to confirm your organisation is not already registered:
- Go to How to Participate > Participant Register > Search a PIC
- Enter your organisation data with several variants to ensure no duplicate exists

**Step 2: Register your organisation**
- Go to How to Participate > Participant Register > Register your organisation
- Complete the registration form with accurate information
- You may save and resume your registration later (go to My Organisations > Continue Registration)
- Incomplete draft registrations are automatically deleted after one year

**Step 3: Receive your PIC**
Upon completing registration, you receive a 9-digit Participant Identification Code (PIC) with provisional/declared status. Use this PIC in all applications. The PIC will be formally validated at a later stage.

### Legal Entity Validation

If your proposal is selected for funding, the Central Validation Service will request validation of your organisation's registration data.

**Validation applies to:**
- Beneficiaries and Affiliated Entities (grants)
- Contractors (procurements)
- Not required for Associated Partners, Subcontractors, or other participant types

**Required supporting documents:**
- Legal entity identification form
- Official VAT document
- Registration extract and statutes or act of establishment
- Proof of legal personality and capacity to undertake legal obligations
- Proof of private/public body status, non-profit status, or international organisation status (if applicable)

**Timeline:** The Central Validation Service will contact the person who registered the organisation (the Self-registrant) with a list of required documents and a deadline for submission.

**Important:** All communications with the Central Validation Service must take place through the Portal Participant Register (My Organisations page). E-mails will NOT be accepted.

### LEAR Appointment and Validation

The **Legal Entity Appointed Representative (LEAR)** is the person formally nominated by your organisation to manage your use of the Portal and serve as your main administrative and legal contact.

**LEAR requirements:**
- Must have necessary legal authority to commit the organisation (typically CEO, rector, Director-General or equivalent)
- Must be appointed by a legal representative with decision-making authority
- In organisations requiring joint decision-making, all representatives must jointly appoint the LEAR
- Can be any member of the organisation, often from central administration

**LEAR responsibilities:**
- Managing all organisation-related data in the Participant Register
- Maintaining overview of all proposals, projects and contracts
- Managing legal and financial information
- Managing access rights at organisation level
- Appointing Legal Signatories (for grant/contract signatures) and Financial Signatories (for cost claims/invoices)

**LEAR appointment process:**
1. The Self-registrant receives notification from the Central Validation Service to appoint a LEAR
2. Go to My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Authorised users/LEAR > Add LEAR
3. The system generates an LEAR Appointment Letter including roles, duties and declaration of consent to Portal Terms and Conditions
4. The legal representative and LEAR sign the appointment letter (blue-ink or qualified electronic signature per eIDAS Regulation)
5. Both the signed appointment letter and official proof of identity (ID card or passport) of both the legal representative and LEAR are uploaded
6. Documents proving the legal representative's authority (role/function, names) are submitted
7. Upon validation of all documents and the PIC, the Central Validation Service validates the LEAR appointment
8. The LEAR receives an account activation PIN code by post or SMS

**Timeline:** LEAR validation occurs after PIC validation by the Central Validation Service.

**Important:** LEAR appointment is mandatory for all validated participants. Without a LEAR, you cannot update legal entity data or sign contracts electronically in the Portal.

### Bank Account Registration and Validation

For payment of grants, the Coordinator organisation must register and validate a bank account.

**Timeline:** You do not need to declare bank account information when first registering. The Central Validation Service will request this after your proposal is selected.

**Required information:**
- Bank account details
- Bank statement or financial identification form

The Central Validation Service will cross-check the information and validate the account.

### Financial Capacity Assessment

For certain participants, the EU must verify financial capacity before awarding grants.

**Who requires assessment:**
- All Beneficiaries and Affiliated Entities (except public bodies and international organisations)
- Individual grant amounts above EUR 60,000
- Specific programme rules may apply (check call conditions)

**Assessment criteria:**
- Liquidity, financial autonomy, solvency and profitability
- Dependency on EU funding
- Deficit and revenue history
- Ability to implement the project

**Required documents:**
- Profit and loss accounts and balance sheets for last two closed financial years
- Business plan
- Audit report from approved external auditor
- Any other documents showing financial position

**Possible outcomes:**
If financial capacity is deemed insufficient, the granting authority may require:
- Joint and several liability for all beneficiaries
- Prefinancing in instalments
- Prefinancing guarantees
- No prefinancing
- Replacement of the beneficiary
- Rejection of the entire application

## Submission Service: Apply for Funding

### Finding Funding Opportunities

**Search the Portal:**
- Go to the Portal's Search Funding & Tenders page
- Search by text (call and topic titles, descriptions, keywords, tags)
- Filter by specific EU priorities and programmes
- View calls for grants, procurements, prizes and financial instruments

**Subscribe to updates:**
- Subscribe to the Funding Opportunities RSS feed to receive notifications of new calls and topics

### Understanding Calls and Topics

**Call:** A group of topics addressing the same policy domain, open in parallel

**Topic:** A single submission opportunity with one opening and closing date. Proposals submitted to the same topic are evaluated together and compete against each other.

**Topic page contains:**
- Call conditions and legal framework
- Key documents and templates
- Standard application form and instructions
- Practical information for applicants
- Call deadline and submission link
- Call and topic updates

### Submission Procedures

**Single-stage:** You submit a full proposal by the call deadline

**Two-stage:** You submit a short outline proposal at stage 1; if invited, you submit a full proposal at stage 2

**Multiple cut-off dates:** Some topics remain continuously open with cut-off dates for evaluation rounds

### Proposal Submission Requirements

**Who can submit:**
- A contact person from the Coordinator organisation
- Exceptions exist for programmes focused on individuals (Principal Investigator in Horizon Europe ERC; Fellow in Horizon Europe MSCA)

**Prerequisites:**
- You must have an EU Login account
- Your organisation must be registered in the Participant Register and have a PIC
- All participating organisations must be registered and have a PIC

**Submission system:**
- Access the Portal Submission System via the Topic page > Start Submission
- The Submission wizard guides you step-by-step through proposal creation and submission

### 6-Step Submission Wizard

**Step 1: Log in**
- Access the Submission System using your EU Login account

**Step 2: Select the call and topic**
- Select the specific topic to which you are applying

**Step 3: Pre-register your draft proposal**
- Launch your application and receive a reference number

**Step 4: List participants and contact persons**
- Define the Coordinator organisation
- Add Partner organisations (if applicable)
- For each organisation, designate a Main Contact and additional Contact Persons (optional)
- Assign access rights (Coordinator Contact for full access; Team Member for read-only)
- Specify Affiliated Entities if applicable

**Step 5: Complete the application form**
- **Part A (Administrative)**: Structured forms with participant data, legal declarations and contact information. Programme-specific questions may be included.
- **Part B (Technical narrative)**: Description of the project, planned activities, work packages, deliverables, costs and budget. Uploaded as a PDF file.

**Step 6: Submit your proposal**
- Review all information for accuracy
- Submit the complete application to the EU
- You will receive an electronic acknowledgement of receipt with a submission reference and timestamp

### Application Form Structure

**Part A: Administrative Forms**
- Participant details (Coordinator, Partners, Affiliated Entities, Associated Partners)
- Contact persons and roles
- Legal declarations (eligibility, non-conflict, no exclusion grounds)
- Financial and organisational information
- Programme-specific questions (ethics, security, SME status, etc.)

**Part B: Technical Description**
- Project objectives and expected outcomes
- Work package descriptions with activities, deliverables and milestones
- Roles and responsibilities by beneficiary
- Project timeline and implementation plan
- Budget breakdown and justification
- Risk management
- Quality assurance
- Dissemination and exploitation plan

### SME Self-Assessment

If SME status is an eligibility criterion or provides special advantages, and no recent self-assessment (less than 2 years old) exists, the Portal will prompt you to complete the SME self-assessment wizard.

**SME self-assessment criteria:**
- Number of employees
- Annual turnover and/or balance sheet total
- Independence (ownership links to other organisations)

## Proposal Evaluation and Results

### Evaluation Process

After the call deadline, submitted proposals undergo the following evaluation steps:

**1. Admissibility and eligibility check**
- Verification that proposal format meets requirements
- Verification that applicant meets eligibility criteria (country, participant type, legal status, etc.)
- Proposals that fail this check are not evaluated further

**2. Evaluation**
- External experts assess proposals against call criteria (quality, feasibility, impact, value for money, etc.)
- Proposals are scored and ranked
- The evaluation panel produces a ranked list of proposals

**3. Special procedures** (if applicable)
- **Ethics review**: Mandatory if research raises ethics concerns
- **Security scrutiny**: Mandatory for sensitive research
- **Ownership control check**: For some programmes, verification that beneficiaries are not under foreign ownership control

**4. Results notification**
- Applicants receive a formal decision (approved, reserved, rejected)
- Rejected applicants receive evaluation summary feedback
- Approved applicants are invited to Grant Agreement Preparation (GAP)

### Grant Preparation

For approved proposals, the following steps occur:

**1. Coordinator invitation**
- The Coordinator receives an invitation to GAP via the Portal

**2. Consortium validation**
- All beneficiaries must have a validated PIC and designated LEAR
- Bank account validation for the Coordinator (if not already done)
- Financial capacity assessment for beneficiaries (if required by call conditions)

**3. Technical annex review**
- The Description of the Action (Annex 1) is reviewed against evaluation panel feedback
- The granting authority may request adjustments

**4. Budget review**
- Cost estimates are reviewed for value for money and compliance with eligible costs rules
- Budget adjustments may be requested

**5. Ethics review** (if flagged)
- If ethics concerns were identified, a detailed ethics review is conducted
- May add 2-4 weeks to GAP timeline

**6. Grant signature**
- The Model Grant Agreement (MGA) is signed electronically by the Coordinator and the granting authority
- Signature occurs via the Portal

**Grant Agreement Preparation timeline:** Standard GAP takes 3-5 months from invitation. The EU targets 8 months from call deadline to grant signature.

### Grant Signature

**Who signs:**
- The **Legal Signatory (LSIGN)** (appointed by the organisation's LEAR) signs on behalf of the Coordinator and each beneficiary
- Electronic signature via the Portal (qualified electronic signature per eIDAS Regulation)

**Grant Agreement structure:**
The Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreement (MGA) includes:
- **Core articles**: Grant duration, eligible costs (including 25 percent flat-rate indirect costs), budget, forms of funding (actual costs, lump sums, unit costs, flat-rate financing), reporting and payment schedules, liability, termination, audits
- **Annex 1**: Description of the Action (technical annex with project details, work packages, deliverables)
- **Annex 2**: Estimated budget by beneficiary and cost category

### Proposals Not Approved

If your proposal is rejected, you will receive:
- A formal decision letter
- Evaluation summary feedback explaining the evaluation panel's assessment
- Information on grounds for rejection
- Guidance on complaints and review procedures

## Grant Management

### Key Grant Management Topics

Once your grant agreement is signed, you are responsible for:

#### 1. Keeping Records
- Maintain all documents supporting costs claimed and activities implemented
- Keep originals of supporting documents permanently for controls, audits and investigations
- Retain records according to data retention rules defined in the Portal Privacy Statement

#### 2. Amendments
- Changes to project scope, budget, consortium composition, duration or other substantive elements require written amendment to the grant agreement
- Amendments are requested by the Coordinator via the Portal Messaging Facility
- The granting authority must approve major amendments before implementation
- Minor amendments may proceed with notification only

#### 3. Reporting
- Submit periodic progress reports at intervals specified in the grant agreement (typically every 12-18 months)
- Final report due within 60 days of project end date
- Reports must document activities completed, deliverables submitted, milestones achieved, financial spending and project progress
- Continuous reporting on milestones and deliverables may be required

#### 4. Payments and Financial Management
- **Pre-financing**: Up to 50 percent of the maximum grant amount, paid within 30 days of grant signature
- **Interim payments**: Based on accepted interim reports
- **Final payment**: Based on final report and cost statement
- **Forms of funding**: May be actual costs, lump sums, unit costs or flat-rate financing depending on the call
- **Certificate on Financial Statements (CFS)**: Required if EU contribution exceeds EUR 430,000
- **Indirect costs**: Funded at 25 percent flat-rate of eligible direct costs (no detailed justification required)
- **Audit and verification**: The granting authority may conduct ex-post audits; OLAF and the European Court of Auditors have independent audit rights

#### 5. Acknowledgement of EU Funding
- Acknowledge EU funding in all communications, publications, audiovisual material and website announcements about the project
- Use the official Horizon Europe logo and acknowledgement statement in communications
- Visibility requirements ensure the European Commission's role is recognised

#### 6. Dissemination and Exploitation
- Disseminate project results through publications, conferences, websites, social media and other channels
- Exploit results through commercialisation, standardisation, policy advice or social innovation
- For Horizon Europe: Mandatory open access to publications (Creative Commons Attribution licence) and FAIR data principles for research data

#### 7. Checks, Audits, Reviews and Investigations
- The EU and its audit bodies (OLAF, European Court of Auditors) have the right to conduct audits, reviews and investigations
- Audit window: Up to 2 years after final payment
- Keep all records available for inspection
- Audits may result in recovery of funds if irregularities are found

#### 8. Communication with the Granting Authority
- All formal communications take place via the Portal Messaging Facility (My Projects > Actions > Manage Project > Process Communications)
- Use this channel for requests, notifications, reports and inquiries
- Keep records of all communications

## Expert Evaluation Portal

Experts who evaluate proposals for the EU may access a separate Expert portal to:

### Expert Registration
- Register in the Portal and create an expert profile
- Provide curriculum vitae, expertise areas and conflict-of-interest declarations
- Be identified as available for evaluation assignments

### Expert Roles and Tasks
- Assess proposals assigned to you using the evaluation criteria specified in the call
- Score proposals and provide written feedback
- Participate in evaluation panel meetings (in-person or virtual)
- Maintain confidentiality of proposal information and evaluation discussions

### Expert Contracts and Payment
- Receive and sign an expert services contract electronically via the Portal
- Submit cost claims for evaluation work (fixed fees per assignment or per diem rates)
- Receive payment for expert services

### Expert Portal Access
- Log in to the Portal using your EU Login account
- Access your expert dashboard with assigned proposals and tasks
- Submit evaluations and upload supporting documents
