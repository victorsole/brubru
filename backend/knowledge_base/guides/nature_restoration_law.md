## Overview

The Nature Restoration Law (NRL) is the first EU-wide legislation specifically targeting ecosystem restoration. It operationalises the EU Biodiversity Strategy 2030 commitment to restore at least 30% of degraded ecosystems. The regulation imposes legally binding restoration targets across terrestrial, marine, freshwater, urban, agricultural, and forest ecosystems, supplementing the existing Natura 2000 conservation framework under the Habitats and Birds Directives.

The law was highly contested throughout the legislative process, narrowly surviving votes in the ENVI committee and EP plenary in July 2023 before final adoption in June 2024. Farmer protests in early 2024 intensified political pressure to weaken or scrap the regulation, and the Council vote was secured only after Belgium (holding the Council Presidency) abstained following domestic coalition disputes.

## Key Overarching Targets

- **2030:** Restoration measures in place for at least 20% of EU land areas and 20% of EU sea areas
- **2040:** Restoration measures in place for at least 60% of degraded land and sea ecosystems
- **2050:** Restoration measures in place for all ecosystems in need of restoration (100% target)
- No net loss of restored areas after restoration measures are applied

## Specific Ecosystem Obligations

### Terrestrial and Freshwater Habitats (Annex I)
- Favourable area trend for Annex I habitat types (grasslands, forests, heathlands, dunes, wetlands) by 2030, 2040, and 2050
- No deterioration of areas already in good condition
- Re-establish habitats in areas not currently hosting them

### Marine Ecosystems (Articles 5-6)
- Restoration targets for seabed habitats including seagrasses, macroalgae, and sponge/coral beds
- Apply to marine protected areas and Natura 2000 marine sites
- Timelines: 2030, 2040, 2050

### Urban Ecosystems (Article 6)
- No net loss of urban green space and urban tree canopy cover by 2030
- Increase urban green space in cities and towns by 2040 and 2050
- Applies to all EU cities, towns, and suburbs

### Agricultural Ecosystems (Article 9)
- Positive trend in farmland bird population index by 2030
- Positive trend in grassland butterfly index by 2030
- Increase share of agricultural land with high-diversity landscape features
- Rewet at least 30% of drained peatlands under agricultural use by 2030 (non-binding indicative target); at least 40% by 2040, 50% by 2050
- Member States may limit peatland rewetting obligations where food security is at risk (emergency brake mechanism)

### Forest Ecosystems (Article 10)
- Positive trend in standing deadwood, lying deadwood, forest bird index, share of uneven-aged forests, connectivity, and organic carbon stock by 2030

### Rivers and Floodplains (Article 7)
- Restore free-flowing rivers by removing barriers to longitudinal connectivity
- At least 25,000 km of rivers restored to free-flowing state by 2030
- Restore natural flooding patterns of floodplains

### Pollinators (Article 8)
- Reverse pollinator decline by 2030 (trend reversal)
- Increasing pollinator populations from 2030 onwards, measured against 2030 baseline

## National Restoration Plans (Article 12)

Member States must submit National Restoration Plans (NRPs) to the Commission by 1 September 2026. Plans must cover:

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Quantified targets | Per ecosystem type, per decade (2030/2040/2050) |
| Geographical scope | Areas designated for restoration |
| Measures planned | Specific restoration and maintenance actions |
| Financing | How measures will be funded (including EU funds) |
| Monitoring | Indicators and monitoring frequency |
| Stakeholder involvement | Consultation process description |

The Commission assesses NRPs and may request revisions. Member States must report progress every three years.

## Link to Natura 2000 and Nature Directives

The NRL builds upon but does not replace the existing framework:
- **Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC):** Establishes Natura 2000 network; requires favourable conservation status for Annex I habitats and Annex II species. The NRL adds time-bound restoration obligations beyond mere conservation.
- **Birds Directive (2009/147/EC):** Protects all wild bird species; the NRL uses bird population indices as agricultural ecosystem indicators.
- **Biodiversity Strategy 2030:** Political commitment to protect 30% of EU land and sea (30x30); the NRL is the binding legislative tool to achieve the restoration half of this strategy.
- Areas already in favourable condition under Natura 2000 must not deteriorate under the NRL.

## Controversy: Legislative History

| Stage | Event |
|-------|-------|
| Jun 2022 | Commission proposal COM(2022) 304 |
| Jul 2023 | ENVI committee rejected proposal (rejected by 1 vote); referred to plenary |
| Jul 2023 | EP plenary narrowly adopted position with EPP opposition |
| Nov 2023 | Council general approach adopted |
| Nov 2023 | Trilogue negotiations opened |
| Nov 2023 | Provisional agreement reached in trilogue |
| Feb-Mar 2024 | Farmer protests across EU; EPP pressed to reopen/reject |
| Feb 2024 | EP re-vote on mandate survived, confirming EP negotiating position |
| Jun 2024 | Belgium abstained in Council (domestic coalition dispute); 20 other Member States voted in favour |
| 17 Jun 2024 | EP plenary formal adoption |
| 17 Jun 2024 | Council formal adoption |
| 29 Jul 2024 | Published in OJ as Regulation (EU) 2024/1991 |
| 18 Aug 2024 | Entry into force |
| 1 Sep 2026 | Deadline for Member States to submit National Restoration Plans |

The regulation's passage was the most politically contested piece of EU Green Deal legislation. The EPP, which had initially supported the proposal, reversed position in 2024 under pressure from farmer organisations and campaigned for rejection. The Council vote required Belgium's abstention to achieve qualified majority, which Belgium justified on grounds of coalition disagreement between the federal government and Flemish regional authorities.

## Omnibus Simplification Package and the NRL

The Commission's Omnibus I simplification package (February 2026) targeted the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CS3D), the Taxonomy Regulation, and the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). The NRL was **not included** in Omnibus I. Commissioner Roswall confirmed that the NRL remains in force as adopted. However, political pressure from EPP and some Member States to revisit the NRL as part of competitiveness-oriented simplification efforts continues. The Commission has not proposed any amendments to the NRL as of March 2026.
