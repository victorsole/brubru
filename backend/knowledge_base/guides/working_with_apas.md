Guide to effectively engaging with Accredited Parliamentary Assistants in the European Parliament.

## Understanding APAs

Parliamentary Assistants are the operational backbone of the EP. Most have Master's degrees in European Studies, International Relations, or Law from institutions like College of Europe, Sciences Po, or Maastricht. They are ambitious, multilingual (often 3+ languages), and deeply knowledgeable about their files.

### Types of Assistants

1. **APAs (Accredited Parliamentary Assistants)**: Direct EP contracts, work in Brussels/Strasbourg/Luxembourg
2. **Local Assistants**: National contracts, work in home constituencies
3. **Specialised Service Providers**: Contracted experts for specific tasks
4. **Trainees**: Educational placements (often from Schuman programme)

## Their Role & Power

APAs decide what reaches the MEP's desk. They are:
- **Gatekeepers**: Filter incoming requests and meetings
- **Schedulers**: Manage MEP calendars (often 15-minute blocks)
- **Researchers**: Draft briefings and prepare for votes
- **Speechwriters**: Draft interventions and statements
- **Negotiators**: Sometimes represent MEPs in technical meetings
- **Crisis managers**: Handle urgent issues and media

If your email doesn't survive their filter, it's gone. Treat them accordingly.

## The Three Tests

Every request faces three quick mental checks:

1. **Ideological Test**: Does this fit the MEP's political line? Check voting records and past positions before approaching.

2. **Practical Test**: Can this actually be done? Is the timeline realistic? Are the resources available?

3. **Political Test**: Is this worth the political capital? What are the risks vs. benefits for the MEP?

If your proposal fails any test, it will be ignored or rejected.

## Best Practices

### Be Useful, Not Inspirational
- Come with a clear, executable ask
- Don't recommend an amendment - bring the text ready to file
- Don't suggest a panel - propose the date, topic, speakers, and how it fits their agenda
- Show you've done your homework

### Respect Their Time
- They work in 15-minute blocks
- A meeting that runs long kills something else
- Be concise, precise, and prepared
- Send agendas in advance
- Stick to the allotted time

### Follow Up Professionally
- Send what you promised immediately after meetings
- Reliability matters more than charm
- Brussels is full of smooth talkers who don't deliver
- Be one of the rare ones who follows through

### Never Go Around Them
- Only contact MEPs directly when you have an established relationship
- Bypassing the APA damages your credibility
- They will find out and remember

### Speak Their Language
Frame messages in terms of:
- Deadlines (committee votes, plenary dates)
- Amendments (specific text, not vague suggestions)
- Political cost/benefit (what does the MEP gain?)
- Procedural possibilities (what's actually achievable?)

Avoid:
- Mission statements
- Vague moral appeals
- Corporate jargon
- Lengthy background documents

### Protect Confidentiality
- Discretion is everything
- When trust is established, they may share sensitive information
- Never leak or misuse what they tell you
- Reputations spread fast in Brussels

### Remember the Human
- A bit of empathy goes far: "I know you're swamped, let me make this easier"
- Occasional coffee or lunch at Place Lux builds relationships
- They're often exhausted and underappreciated
- Don't add to their stress

## Meeting Preparation

When preparing for an APA meeting:

1. **Research the MEP's Position**
   - Voting record on similar files
   - Past speeches and statements
   - Political group's line
   - National context

2. **Prepare a One-Pager**
   - Clear ask at the top
   - 3-5 key points maximum
   - Specific amendment text if relevant
   - Next steps and timeline

3. **Anticipate Questions**
   - What's the political angle?
   - Who else supports this?
   - What's the counter-argument?
   - What resources are needed?

4. **Define Success**
   - What's your minimum outcome?
   - What's your ideal outcome?
   - What follow-up is needed?

## Common Mistakes

- Sending 50-page position papers (nobody reads them)
- Asking for meetings without a clear purpose
- Over-promising and under-delivering
- Being inflexible on timing or format
- Expecting instant responses (they're overloaded)
- Treating APAs as mere schedulers rather than advisors
- Going over their heads to the MEP

## Finding and Contacting Assistants

### Assistant Directory

All assistants of the European Parliament are listed publicly:

- **Full directory**: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/meps/en/assistants
- **Per-MEP page**: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/meps/en/{mep_id}/{NAME}/assistants`
  - Example: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/meps/en/257043/MARAVILLAS_ABADIA+JOVER/assistants

On each MEP's profile page, click "Assistants" to see the names of their APAs, local assistants, and service providers. Names are listed but email addresses are not published.

### Email Pattern

EP assistant emails historically followed the pattern:

```
firstname.surname@europarl.europa.eu
```

- All lowercase, no accents (e.g., "Jose" not "Jose")
- Hyphenated surnames use a hyphen: `jean-pierre.dupont@europarl.europa.eu`
- These emails were publicly listed until a few years ago; they are no longer published but the pattern still works in most cases
- If the guessed email bounces, the assistant may use a different spelling (e.g., middle initial, abbreviated first name)

### Tips

- Always address APAs by name in emails: finding their name shows you've done your research
- If you cannot find the right email, contact the MEP's general office and ask to be forwarded
- The EP switchboard (+32 2 28 42111) can also connect you to an MEP's office

## Building Long-Term Relationships

- Be consistent and reliable over time
- Provide useful information even when you don't need something
- Acknowledge their contributions
- Be honest about limitations and constraints
- Maintain contact between active requests
- Congratulate them on career moves or successes
