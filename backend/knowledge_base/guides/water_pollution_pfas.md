## What are PFAS?
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a group of over 10,000 synthetic chemicals used in non-stick coatings, food packaging, textiles, firefighting foams, and electronics. Known as "forever chemicals" because they do not break down in the environment. They accumulate in water, soil, and human blood.

## EU Regulatory Framework

### Drinking Water (Directive 2020/2184/EU)
From 12 January 2026, all Member States must systematically monitor PFAS in drinking water. Limits:
- Total PFAS: 0.5 microg/L
- Sum of 20 specific PFAS: 0.1 microg/L
When limits are exceeded: corrective action required (well closures, treatment upgrades, supply restrictions).

### Surface Water (Environmental Quality Standards)
PFOS has been a priority substance under the Water Framework Directive since 2021. Current EQS:
- Inland surface waters (annual average): 0.65 ng/L
- Transitional/coastal/territorial waters: 0.13 ng/L
- Biota (fish, wet weight): 9.1 microg/kg
The Commission proposed (October 2022) adding 24 PFAS with an EQS of 4.4 ng/L as PFOA equivalents.

### EP Vote 26 March 2026
Parliament is expected to greenlight updated rules on groundwater and surface water pollution. The legislation updates the pollutant monitoring list to include:
- PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances)
- Pharmaceuticals
- Industrial substances and pesticides
- Microplastics
- Antimicrobial resistance indicators
Parliament and Council concluded an agreement; Council adopted its first reading position. Once formally endorsed by Parliament, the new legislation will be published in the Official Journal.

## Scale of PFAS Contamination
EEA data (2018-2022):
- 51-60% of rivers exceeded PFOS Environmental Quality Standards
- 11-35% of lakes exceeded standards
- 47-100% of transitional/coastal waters exceeded EQS
- Belgium, France: 100% exceedance rates
- Netherlands: 96% of water bodies exceeded limits
- Only 14 Member States reported monitoring data in 2022

## Cost of Inaction
EC-commissioned study (January 2026):
- Annual PFAS health costs (2024): EUR 39.5 billion
- Cumulative costs by 2050 under BAU: EUR 440 billion
- Tackling PFAS releases by 2040 would save EUR 110 billion
- Water treatment costs alone: EUR 1 trillion+ without action

## PFAS Restrictions Timeline
- PFOS, PFOA, PFHxS and related substances: already banned
- Long-chain PFCAs: restricted
- PFHxA ban (2024): covers textiles, food packaging, cosmetics, firefighting foams
- PFAS firefighting foam phase-out: October 2025
- Universal PFAS restriction: under ECHA assessment, results expected end 2026
- Chemicals Industry Action Plan (July 2025)

## Zero Pollution Action Plan
Mid-term review (January 2026) found:
- Air pollution, pesticide use, antimicrobial sales: significantly reduced
- Water pollution, waste management, microplastics: stable or worsening
- Water pollution is NOT on track for 2030 targets
- 80% of companies now integrate sustainability into innovation strategies
