## Context

The Nimes airport State aid case is one of the Commission's landmark decisions on public financing of regional airports and the commercial agreements those airports sign with low-cost carriers (LCCs). It resolves a formal investigation opened in 2012 covering measures granted to two successive airport operators (the CCI and VTAN) and a series of airport services and marketing agreements concluded between those operators and Ryanair and its subsidiary AMS between 2000 and 2010.

The case applies both the 2005 Aviation Guidelines and the 2014 Aviation Guidelines (the new framework for airport operating aid). It is frequently cited for three principles: (1) the joint assessment of airport services and marketing agreements as a single economic transaction; (2) the ex-ante incremental profitability method as the benchmark Market Economy Operator test for airport-airline agreements; and (3) the conditions under which private airport management companies can be treated as public entities for State aid purposes because of the structure of their public service delegation.

---

## Parties and Background

**Airport context:** Nimes-Garons operates as a joint civilian-military aerodrome 12 km south of Nimes. Civilian traffic peaked at approximately 320,000 passengers in 2001 and declined thereafter. The TGV arrived in 2001; Air France withdrew from the Paris route the same year; Air Littoral ceased operations in 2003. From 2003 onwards Ryanair was the sole scheduled carrier. Montpellier airport is approximately 63 km away and handles roughly six times the traffic.

**CCI (2000-2006):** The Nimes-Uzes-Le Vigan CCI held a 60-year public equipment concession for the civilian airport area from 1965. The Commission treats the CCI as a public authority whose decisions are automatically imputable to the state, distinguishing it from ordinary public undertakings subject to the standard Stardust Marine imputation test. The CCI concession was terminated early and replaced by a public service delegation managed by the SMAN (a syndicate grouping the Gard Departmental Council, Nimes Metropole and Ales Cevennes agglomeration).

**VTAN (2007-2012):** Veolia Transport Aeroport de Nimes, a subsidiary of the Veolia group, managed the airport under a public service delegation (CDSP) from 1 January 2007. Despite being privately owned, VTAN's airline agreements are treated as imputable to the state because the SMAN set the traffic-development objective, funded the deficit through a flat-rate contribution, and structured the framework under which Ryanair agreements were concluded.

## The Measures

**Measures to airport operators (declared compatible):**
- CCI-Airport: exceptional operating subsidies (EUR 250,000 + EUR 600,000 + EUR 500,000 in 2005; EUR 200,000 in 2006) from public authority grantors; interest-free advances of approximately EUR 9 million from the CCI general arm.
- VTAN: flat-rate operating contributions of EUR 1.2 to 1.5 million per year from SMAN under the CDSP and three successive amendments; specific public contribution for 2011 linked to military area handover.
- Financing of sovereign tasks (aircraft rescue and firefighting, security screening, border control, wildlife hazard) through the national airport tax system: NOT State aid.

**Measures to Ryanair and AMS (declared incompatible):**
- 2001/2002 correspondence: amendment to the 2000 airport services agreement increasing marketing payments; negative incremental flows from 2002.
- 2004 correspondence: additional marketing payment amendment; negative incremental flow of EUR 100,000 to 150,000.
- 2005 agreements (CCI with Ryanair and AMS): new airport services and marketing structure; negative incremental flows throughout the term.
- 2007 VTAN-Ryanair-AMS agreement and successive amendments (November 2007, August 2008, August 2009, August 2010, November 2010): all negative incremental flows; combined annual net cost EUR 700,000 to 850,000 per period.
- NOTE: The original April 2000 agreement is NOT State aid -- the Commission found it produced positive incremental flows (recitals 453-457).

## The Commission's Assessment

### State Resources and Imputability

CCI decisions are automatically imputable to the state (the CCI is a public authority, not a commercial entity). VTAN decisions are imputable to the state through the SMAN's structural influence over the CDSP and the flat-rate contribution that funded the Ryanair relationship's net cost. State resources were transferred in both cases.

### Market Economy Operator Test

The Commission applied the MEO test through an ex-ante incremental profitability analysis:
- Only incremental revenues are counted: aeronautical charges (landing, passenger, ground handling) and non-aeronautical commercial revenues (retail, parking, food and beverage, advertising) -- single-till approach.
- Only incremental costs are counted: variable operating costs, marketing payments to AMS, and financial incentives per rotation. Fixed sunk costs (infrastructure, depreciation) are excluded.
- Benefits beyond the agreement term (brand value, tourist multiplier, repeat visitors, network effects) are excluded unless specifically quantified at the time of signing.
- An 85% load factor was applied for agreements from October 2005 onwards (reflecting the marketing effect); lower factors for earlier periods.

Result: all agreements from 2001/2002 onwards produce negative incremental values. A market economy operator would not have signed them.

### Joint Assessment of Airport and Marketing Agreements

Airport services agreements and AMS marketing services agreements signed simultaneously were assessed as single transactions. The agreements shared identical dates, identical parties, identical terms, and were explicitly cross-referenced: the AMS agreement stated it was conditional on Ryanair maintaining routes from Nimes.

### Compatibility

**Ryanair/AMS measures:** Not compatible under either the 2005 or 2014 Aviation Guidelines. The 2001/2002 and 2004 measures were assessed under the pre-2005 framework derived from Commission practice; neither concerned a genuinely new route and neither was degressive. The 2005 and later VTAN measures were assessed under the 2005 Aviation Guidelines (points 71-79): aid to airlines for new routes requires degressiveness, a business plan, a link to net traffic development on the new route, and a transparent tender. None of these criteria were met. The November 2010 amendment was directly linked to Ryanair threatening to withdraw existing routes -- a conditioning payment, not start-up aid.

**Airport operator measures:** Declared compatible under Article 107(3)(c) TFEU and the 2014 Aviation Guidelines (point 137). The airport contributes to local connectivity and tourism (EUR 100 million annual tourist spending, 2,200 jobs at the airbase). Without subsidies, operations would have ceased. Aid is proportionate to the operating funding gap; budgetary supervision by the French state prevents overcompensation. Trade distortion is limited given the airport's small size (under 700,000 passengers per year).

### SGEI Argument Rejected

France argued the airport provided a service of general economic interest. The Commission rejected this: Nimes is not geographically isolated (TGV to Paris in 3 hours; Montpellier airport 50 minutes by road), and the SGEI criteria are not met.

---

## Recovery

France was ordered to recover from Ryanair and AMS:
- All aid from the 2005 agreements onwards: Ryanair and AMS jointly and severally (indicative principal EUR 5 million to 7 million).
- Aid from the 2001/2002 and 2004 amendments: Ryanair alone (indicative principal EUR 150,000 to 300,000).
- Interest: compound, from the date aid was placed at the beneficiary's disposal.
- Deadline: recovery within four months of notification of the decision (23 July 2014).

The decision may be subject to appeal before the EU courts. Any such appeal does not suspend the recovery obligation.

## Key Takeaways for Airports, Airlines and Advisers

1. The MEO test for airport-airline agreements uses an ex-ante incremental profitability method. No contemporaneous profitability analysis almost always means the test fails.
2. Airport services and marketing agreements with the same airline must be assessed as one transaction. Structuring them separately does not prevent joint assessment.
3. Start-up aid to airlines requires degressiveness, a business plan, a transparent tender, and a link to net traffic development. Aid for existing routes or without a tender is incompatible.
4. Payments by an airport to maintain (rather than develop) routes are operating aid, not start-up aid, and are presumptively incompatible.
5. Small airports under 700,000 passengers per year can receive compatible operating aid provided all five criteria under point 137 of the 2014 Aviation Guidelines are met.
6. Sovereign task payments (rescue, security, border control) are not State aid where the financing system is non-discriminatory and prevents overcompensation.
7. Recovery falls on the airline and its marketing subsidiary, not the airport operator.
