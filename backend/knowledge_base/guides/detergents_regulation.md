## Overview

The Detergents Regulation is the EU's rulebook for detergents (cleaning products for fabrics, dishes and surfaces) and the surfactants used to make them. Adopted on 11 February 2026, it replaces Regulation (EC) No 648/2004, the 2004 Detergents Regulation. The Commission's evaluation of that Regulation found it had "achieved its objectives to a large extent" but identified weaknesses: duplicated information requirements against a chemicals framework that has changed radically since 2004, enforcement gaps in online sales, and market developments the old rules never anticipated, in particular detergents containing intentionally added micro-organisms and the growth of refill sales (recitals 2-3).

**Brubru deep-dive explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers):** https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2026-405_detergents/index.html

The Regulation sits at the intersection of chemicals regulation, consumer information and the EU's circular economy agenda. It complements rather than duplicates other EU chemicals law: it applies without prejudice to REACH (Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006), the CLP Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008) and the Biocidal Products Regulation (Regulation (EU) No 528/2012) (Article 1(2), recital 9). It also plugs detergents and end-user surfactants into the EU's wider digital product passport architecture alongside toys, construction products and batteries, and it explicitly anticipates that the horizontal Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) could bring detergents within its own scope in the medium term, in which case a single, shared digital product passport would serve both instruments (recital 47).

Two things distinguish this Regulation from its DPP-family siblings. First, it is the first EU product law to set harmonised safety rules for detergents containing living micro-organisms, an entirely new product category the 2004 Regulation could not have addressed. Second, unlike the Toy Safety Regulation or the Construction Products Regulation, it does **not** introduce CE marking or third-party notified-body conformity assessment: manufacturers self-declare compliance under the single, least-stringent conformity assessment route, Module A of Decision No 768/2008/EC (recital 19, Annex IV).

## What changed from Regulation (EC) No 648/2004

- **Detergents containing micro-organisms get their own EU-harmonised safety regime.** Chapter II Article 5 and Annex II are entirely new: strain identification via whole genome sequencing, a list of seven pathogenic species that must never be present, a ban on genetically modified micro-organisms, minimum antibiotic susceptibility, minimum shelf-life and plate-count requirements, and a mandatory strain-and-product-level risk assessment under a Commission methodology due by 23 September 2028 (Article 30(6)).
- **Refill sales are explicitly regulated for the first time.** Article 12 sets safety and information rules for detergent and end-user surfactant refill stations, tying the Regulation into the European Green Deal's and the Circular Economy Action Plan's reuse and refill targets (recitals 3, 41).
- **A digital product passport for detergents and end-user surfactants.** Chapter V is new: manufacturers must create a DPP before placing a detergent or end-user surfactant on the market, upload identifiers to a shared EU registry, and connect it to a data carrier that customs and market surveillance authorities can check.
- **Digital labelling becomes possible for the first time.** Chapter IV allows part of the mandatory labelling information (certain surfactant classes, phosphates, phosphonates, soap, and, for refill and certain dosage information, more besides) to be provided on a digital label only, subject to accessibility and availability safeguards (Articles 18-19, Annex V Part C).
- **The definition of 'detergent' is extended.** It now expressly covers products containing intentionally added micro-organisms and ancillary products that support the cleaning process or modify fabric odour when used alongside a laundry or dishwasher detergent (recital 6, Article 2(1)).
- **A general, explicit ban on animal testing for compliance purposes.** Article 7 prohibits placing on the market detergents or surfactants whose final formulation or ingredients were tested on animals to meet this Regulation's requirements, subject to a narrow Commission derogation route and a grandfather clause preserving the use of data acquired before 22 March 2026.
- **Biodegradability requirements extend beyond surfactants.** The 2004 Regulation only addressed surfactant biodegradability. This Regulation adds a staged mandate for the Commission to set future biodegradability criteria for water-soluble polymeric films used to encapsulate detergents (delegated act due 23 March 2029, applying from 23 March 2032) and for other organic substances added at 10% w/w or more (delegated act due 23 March 2031, applying from 23 March 2034) (Article 4(3)-(4), Article 30(4)).
- **Streamlined labelling to cut duplication.** Where the same information is required under multiple Union legal acts (this Regulation, CLP, the Biocidal Products Regulation, the Cosmetic Products Regulation), it need only be provided once, under the stricter rule (recital 31).
- **A modernised safeguard procedure.** Article 27 replaces the 2004 Regulation's safeguard clause with a procedure aligned to the examination procedure under Regulation (EU) No 182/2011, and Article 28 adds a new power for market surveillance authorities to act against detergents and surfactants that are technically compliant but still pose a risk to human health or the environment, mirroring the equivalent provision in the Toy Safety Regulation.
- **10-year uniform document retention.** Manufacturers, authorised representatives and importers must keep technical documentation and the digital product passport for 10 years after the detergent or surfactant is placed on the market (Articles 8(3), 9(3)(b), 10(10), 15(2)).
- **No CE marking, no notified bodies.** Unlike the Toy Safety Regulation and the Construction Products Regulation, this Regulation retains the single least-stringent conformity assessment route (Module A, internal production control) and does not introduce a CE mark or third-party notified-body assessment for detergents or surfactants.

## Scope and definitions: detergents and surfactants

**Detergent (Article 2(1)):** a substance, mixture or micro-organisms, or a combination of these, intended to clean, soak (pre-wash), rinse or bleach fabrics, dishes or surfaces; to modify the feel or odour of fabrics in processes complementing washing; or to support the cleaning process when used together with a laundry detergent or an automatic dishwasher detergent. References to "surfaces" include the surfaces of fruit and vegetables (recital 6).

**Related detergent categories:**
- **Consumer laundry detergent** - a laundry detergent placed on the market for use by non-professionals, including in public laundrettes (Article 2(2)).
- **Consumer automatic dishwasher detergent** - a detergent for automatic dishwashers placed on the market for use by non-professionals (Article 2(3)).
- **Detergent containing micro-organisms** - a detergent to which one or more micro-organisms have been intentionally added, on their own or via one of its components (Article 2(4)).
- **Industrial and institutional detergent** - a detergent placed on the market for use only outside the domestic sphere, by specialised personnel (Article 2(5)).

**Surfactant (Article 2(11)):** any organic substance or mixture, contained in or intended to be used in detergents, with surface-active properties, consisting of one or more hydrophilic and one or more hydrophobic groups capable of all four of: reducing the surface tension of water below 45 mN/m; forming spreading or adsorption monolayers at the water-air interface; forming emulsions, microemulsions or micelles; and adsorbing at water-solid interfaces.

**End-user surfactant (Article 2(12)):** a surfactant made available on the market to professional users or consumers, as distinct from a surfactant sold business-to-business for use in manufacturing detergents. This distinction matters throughout the Regulation: because surfactants are primarily sold B2B, only minimum rules apply to them (labelling information and a technical documentation obligation), while the digital product passport and ingredients data sheet obligations attach only where a surfactant is made available directly to consumers or other end-users, i.e. as an end-user surfactant (recital 7).

Article 2 sets out 40 definitions in total, covering the supply chain (manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor, economic operator), market surveillance concepts drawn from Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (recall, withdrawal, corrective action), and the DPP vocabulary shared with the ESPR (data carrier, unique product identifier, unique operator identifier, digital product passport, digital product passport service provider).

The Regulation does not affect the application of Regulations (EC) No 1907/2006 (REACH), (EC) No 1272/2008 (CLP) and (EU) No 528/2012 (Biocidal Products) (Article 1(2)).

## Biodegradability of surfactants

Article 4 and Annex I set out the Regulation's biodegradability regime, structured in four parts.

**Part A (surfactants, in force now):** surfactants, and surfactants contained in detergents, must achieve "ultimate aerobic biodegradation" (Article 2(13)), meaning the substance is broken down by micro-organisms to carbon dioxide, water and mineral salts. A surfactant meets this if it achieves either:
- **at least 60% biodegradability (mineralisation) within 28 days**, measured by one of four test methods (C.4-C CO2 evolution test, C.4-D manometric respirometry, C.4-E closed bottle test, C.4-F M.I.T.I. test, all under Commission Regulation (EC) No 440/2008); or
- **at least 70% biodegradability (mineralisation) within 28 days**, measured by one of two other methods (C.4-A DOC die-away test, C.4-B modified OECD screening test).

Pre-adaptation of test organisms is not allowed, and the "10-day window" principle does not apply to any of these methods. Testing must be carried out by laboratories that are either good-laboratory-practice compliant under Directive 2004/10/EC (or an equivalent international standard) or accredited under Regulation (EC) No 765/2008.

A narrow exemption applies to surfactants used as biocidal active substances for disinfection purposes, provided they are on the Union list of approved active substances, are in the Article 528/2012 review programme, or are constituents of disinfectants lawfully placed on the market under Article 55 of that Regulation (Article 4(2)).

**Parts B, C and D (films, other organic substances, derogations): ANNEX-ONLY, NOT YET POPULATED.** As published, Annex I Part B ("Films and polymers within films"), Part C ("Other organic substances") and Part D ("Derogations for specific organic substances") are headings only, with no substantive criteria yet set out in the text. The actual biodegradability criteria and test methods for these categories are to be introduced by future Commission delegated acts: for films and polymers within films, due by 23 March 2029 and applying from 23 March 2032 (Article 4(3)); for organic substances intentionally added at 10% w/w or more of the total mass of substances (excluding water), other than surfactants, films and polymers within films, due by 23 March 2031 and applying from 23 March 2034 (Article 4(4)). Do not cite specific percentage thresholds or test methods for Parts B, C or D as if they already exist in the Regulation; only the compliance deadlines and the delegation are fixed today.

## The digital label and the digital product passport for detergents

**Digital labelling (Chapter IV, Articles 17-20).** A physical label remains the baseline: detergents and surfactants made available in individual packaging or through refill must be accompanied by a label carrying the Annex V Part A information, plus Annex V Part B dosage information for consumer laundry detergents, consumer automatic dishwasher detergents and consumer detergents for surfaces (Article 17). Economic operators may choose to also provide a digital label, but by default all information must still be duplicated on the physical label (Article 18(1)(b)). Two carve-outs allow digital-only provision:
- **Content that may go digital-only regardless of sales channel** (Annex V Part C, point 1): anionic, cationic, amphoteric and non-ionic surfactants; phosphates; phosphonates; soap.
- **Content that may go digital-only for refill sales specifically** (Annex V Part C, point 2): the same content categories as above, but for refill sales this extends further, excluding only the fragrance allergen and preservative content information (Annex V Part A, points 1(h)(iii)-(iv)), which must always remain physically visible to protect against allergic reactions (recital 38).
- **Dosage information** for consumer laundry detergents may go digital-only if a simplified dosage grid (basic use instructions, recommended quantities for medium water hardness, washing machine load indication) still appears on the physical label (Annex V Part C, point 3).

A digital label must be searchable, accessible EU-wide regardless of the end-user's location, linked via the same data carrier as the DPP, designed with vulnerable users (including persons with disabilities) in mind, compatible with major browsers and operating systems, and available for 10 years from placing on the market, including through the creating operator's insolvency or cessation of activity (Article 19(1)). Economic operators must not track or analyse usage beyond what is strictly necessary to provide the information (Article 19(2)), and must provide the same information by other means, free of charge, on request or whenever the digital label is temporarily unavailable, including at the point of purchase (Article 19(3)). The Commission must adopt a delegated act on specific digital-labelling technical requirements by 1 October 2028 (Article 30(10)).

**The digital product passport (Chapter V, Articles 21-25).** Unlike labelling, the DPP obligation applies only to **detergents and end-user surfactants**, not to B2B-only surfactants. Before placing either on the market, the manufacturer must create a DPP that: corresponds to a specific model; states that compliance has been demonstrated; contains at least the Annex VI Part A data (trade name, unique product identifier, a colour image, manufacturer/importer/authorised representative contact details and the manufacturer's unique operator identifier, the DPP service provider hosting the back-up copy, traceability information, a sole-responsibility statement, applicable commodity codes, references to Union law complied with, the full list of intentionally added substances, and, where relevant, the taxonomic list of intentionally added micro-organisms); is accurate, complete and up to date; is available in the required Member State language(s); is accessible free of charge without registration or a password; and remains available for 10 years from placing on the market, including through insolvency or cessation of activity (Article 21(1)-(2), Article 22(c)-(d)). Annex VI Part B allows optional inclusion of further Article 17(3)-(4) labelling information. Where a detergent or end-user surfactant already falls under a DPP obligation in other Union law (in particular, if the ESPR is extended to cover detergents), a single shared DPP must be used (Article 21(6), recitals 46-48).

The data carrier connecting to the DPP must be physically present on the label, packaging or accompanying documents (for bulk transport), indelible, machine-processable, present at refill stations, carry a "Please scan for more comprehensive information on the product" statement (or equivalent), and be visible to the consumer before purchase, including in distance sales (Article 21(4)). The Commission must adopt an implementing act on the DPP's basic technical requirements, applying no earlier than 18 months after its own entry into force (Article 21(10)).

**Registry and customs interconnection.** Before placing a detergent or end-user surfactant on the market, the responsible economic operator uploads the unique product identifier and unique operator identifier to the digital product passport registry established under Article 13(1) of the ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 (Article 24(1)); the registry automatically issues a unique registration identifier, which is not itself proof of compliance (Article 24(2)). When such a product enters the Union under the "release for free circulation" customs procedure, the importer or declarant must provide that unique registration identifier; customs authorities may release the product only after verifying, at minimum, that the identifier and commodity code (against the Annex VII list) match the registry data (Article 25(2)-(3)). This verification runs electronically and automatically via the interconnection between the registry and the EU Customs Single Window Certificates Exchange System (EU CSW-CERTEX), applying **from 23 September 2029 or from the date that interconnection becomes operational, whichever is later** (Article 25(4)), the same trigger date used in the Toy Safety Regulation. It does not replace ordinary market surveillance (Article 25(7)).

## Sustainability, refill sales, and micro-organisms in detergents

**Refill sales.** Article 12 defines "refill" as the on-site operation by which a detergent or surfactant is filled into a packaging, and a "refill station" as the place, manual, automatic or semi-automatic, where this happens (Article 2(37)-(38)). The Regulation explicitly frames refill as a waste-prevention measure in line with the European Green Deal (Commission communication of 11 December 2019) and the new Circular Economy Action Plan (Commission communication of 11 March 2020), and encourages economic operators to also offer other sustainable sales forms, such as recyclable at-home refill packaging (recital 3). Where a detergent or end-user surfactant is sold through refill, the economic operator must apply risk mitigation measures, in particular preventing children from using the refill station unsupervised and training staff appropriately, and must ensure the products offered at a refill station do not react with each other in a way that could endanger human health (Article 12). Refilled products must meet the same compliance requirements as individually packaged products, and end-users must still receive, in physical form, at minimum a simplified dosage grid (for consumer laundry detergents) and information on fragrance allergens and preservatives, precisely because more content may otherwise be pushed to a digital-only label at refill points (recitals 38, 41).

**Micro-organisms in detergents.** Article 5 and Annex II establish the EU's first harmonised safety regime for this category. Every intentionally added micro-organism must: belong to or be deposited in a collection of an International Depository Authority; be identified and characterised using whole genome sequence analysis, per a methodology the Commission must adopt by 23 September 2028 (Article 30(6)); and carry full taxonomic identification (genus, species, strain name or code). Seven named pathogenic species (Escherichia coli, Streptococcus spp/Enterococcus spp, Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus cereus, Salmonella spp, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Candida albicans) must not be present in any microbial formulation. Genetically modified micro-organisms are prohibited outright. Added strains must remain susceptible to each of five major antibiotic classes (aminoglycoside, macrolide, beta-lactam, tetracycline, fluoroquinolone), tested by the European Committee on Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing disk diffusion method or an equivalent. Products must carry a standard plate count of at least 1x10^5 CFU per millilitre or gram when placed on the market, falling no lower than 1x10^4 CFU per millilitre or gram by the end of a shelf life that must be no shorter than 18 months. Safe use must be demonstrated through a strain-level and product-level risk assessment under the same forthcoming Commission methodology, covering all foreseeable use conditions, including spray formats and surfaces in contact with food. Manufacturers must substantiate any performance claims, and may not claim or suggest an antimicrobial or disinfecting effect unless the product also complies with the Biocidal Products Regulation (Article 528/2012).

**Animal testing.** Article 7 prohibits placing on the market any detergent or surfactant whose final formulation, ingredients or combination of ingredients were the subject of animal testing carried out to meet this Regulation's requirements, while allowing continued use of data acquired before 22 March 2026. The Commission may grant a narrow derogation, on its own initiative or on a reasoned request, where an ingredient is widely used with no substitutable alternative and a substantiated human health or environmental concern justifies it, after consulting the European Centre for the Validation of Alternative Methods (ECVAM) where relevant.

## Obligations of economic operators

Chapter III (Articles 8-16) sets differentiated obligations along the supply chain:

- **Manufacturers (Article 8):** ensure detergents/surfactants are designed and manufactured to comply; draw up technical documentation and carry out the Module A conformity assessment; create the digital product passport, ensure the data carrier is available, and upload identifiers to the registry, all before placing on the market; keep technical documentation and the DPP for 10 years; maintain series-production conformity and carry out risk-appropriate sample testing; comply with the labelling requirements; before placing on the market mixtures not already notified for emergency health response under CLP Article 45, submit the ingredients data sheet to Member States' appointed bodies, and keep it updated; provide corrective-action information and cooperate with authorities; keep public complaint channels available, taking into account accessibility for persons with disabilities.
- **Authorised representatives (Article 9):** act only within a written mandate, which cannot include the manufacturer's core design/compliance obligations or drawing up technical documentation; verify the DPP, registry upload, technical documentation and conformity assessment have been carried out; keep documentation and the DPP available for 10 years; where established for a non-EU manufacturer selling via distance sales, also provide/update the ingredients data sheet, keep it confidential, verify labelling compliance, and make best efforts (a document-based check, not exhaustive investigation) to verify the manufacturer's compliance evidence.
- **Importers (Article 10):** place on the market only compliant products; verify before placing on the market that conformity assessment, technical documentation, DPP, data carrier and registry upload are in order; provide and update the ingredients data sheet, keeping it confidential; ensure storage/transport does not jeopardise compliance; carry out risk-appropriate sample testing; keep the unique product identifier and technical documentation available for 10 years; verify manufacturer complaint channels exist or provide their own.
- **Distributors (Article 11):** act with due care; verify before making available that required documents, the label (and, where used, digital label and data carrier) are present and compliant; submit the ingredients data sheet to the appointed body in a new Member State where the product is first made available there, unless another operator already has; ensure storage/transport does not jeopardise compliance; cooperate on corrective action and notify authorities of non-compliance or risk.
- **Refill suppliers (Article 12):** apply risk mitigation measures at refill stations (supervised access for children, trained staff) and ensure refilled products do not react dangerously with each other.
- **Manufacturer-equivalent persons (Article 13):** importers or distributors that place a product under their own name/trademark, modify a product in a way that could affect compliance, or make a non-end-user surfactant available to end-users, assume full manufacturer obligations.
- **Packaging and repackaging (Article 14):** importers/distributors who package or repackage (outside Article 13) must mark the package "packaged by"/"repackaged by" with their own contact details, keep a specimen of the original label information for 10 years, and keep the unique product identifier available for 10 years. Making a product available through a refill station is not "packaging or repackaging" for this purpose.
- **Identification of economic operators (Article 15):** all economic operators must, on request, identify who supplied them and who they supplied, for 10 years both ways.
- **Confidentiality of the ingredients data sheet (Article 16):** appointed bodies and medical personnel must keep the ingredients data sheet confidential, using it only for emergency health response or for statistical risk-management analysis requested by a Member State, the Commission or the European Chemicals Agency.

## Conformity and CE-style requirements

Article 8(2) requires manufacturers to draw up technical documentation (Annex IV) and carry out the conformity assessment procedure set out there before placing a detergent or surfactant on the market. There is only **one** conformity assessment route under this Regulation: **Module A, internal production control**, taken from Decision No 768/2008/EC, the least stringent of the EU's harmonised modules (recital 19, Annex IV point 1). The manufacturer self-declares, on its sole responsibility, that the product conforms; there is no notified-body examination, no EU-type examination certificate, and no equivalent to the Toy Safety Regulation's Chapter VII on notification of conformity assessment bodies.

The technical documentation (Annex IV, point 2) must include: a general description of the detergent or surfactant; where applicable, test reports demonstrating compliance with Annexes I and II and the micro-organism risk assessment report; a list of test methods used; calculation results demonstrating compliance with the Annex II/III limit values; and an **ingredients data sheet**, itself part of the technical documentation, containing the product's trade name, unique formula identifier, manufacturer details and intended use; the full list of intentionally added substances (with weight-percentage banding or exact concentrations); where applicable, the taxonomic list of intentionally added micro-organisms and their standard plate count; the mixture's pH; and a specimen of the Article 17 label.

Manufacturers must also create the digital product passport for each model (Annex IV, point 4), and by creating it, assume responsibility for the product's compliance with the Regulation (Article 21(9)). Technical documentation and the DPP must remain available for 10 years after the product is placed on the market.

**There is no CE marking under this Regulation.** Compliance is demonstrated exclusively through the Module A self-declaration, the technical documentation, and the digital product passport, marking a deliberate divergence from the CE-marking, notified-body model used elsewhere in the EU's DPP-family legislation (Toy Safety Regulation, Construction Products Regulation).

## Market surveillance

Chapter VI (Articles 26-29) builds on Regulation (EU) 2019/1020. A market surveillance authority that has reason to believe a detergent or surfactant poses a risk to human health or the environment must evaluate it and, if non-compliance is found, require corrective action within a reasonable period; if the economic operator fails to act adequately, the authority takes provisional measures (prohibition, restriction, withdrawal, recall) and notifies the Commission and other Member States via the ICSMS information system referenced in Article 34(1) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (Article 26). A provisional national measure is deemed justified if no Member State or the Commission objects within three months (Article 26(8)). The Union safeguard procedure (Article 27) lets the Commission determine, by implementing act under the examination procedure, whether a contested national measure is justified. As with the Toy Safety Regulation, market surveillance authorities can act against a detergent or surfactant that is technically compliant but still poses a risk to human health or the environment (Article 28), reflecting experience under the 2004 Regulation. Formal non-compliance findings (Article 29) cover: a DPP not properly drawn up; missing or incomplete technical documentation; a missing data carrier; a missing, non-compliant, false or incomplete label; or a missing or un-updated ingredients data sheet, with escalation to restriction, prohibition, recall or withdrawal if the defect persists.

## The transitional regime and repeal of Regulation (EC) No 648/2004

Article 35 repeals Regulation (EC) No 648/2004 with effect from **23 September 2029**, the same date the bulk of the new Regulation starts applying; Annex VIII provides an article-by-article correlation table between the old Regulation and the new one. Article 36 sets a two-stage bridge, consistent with recitals 63-64:
- **Stock already in the distribution chain or in storage** at 23 September 2029, compliant with the 2004 Regulation as it applied on 22 September 2029, may continue to be made available on the market **indefinitely** (Article 36(1)).
- **Stock not yet in the distribution chain** at 23 September 2029, still compliant with the 2004 Regulation as it applied on 22 September 2029, may be placed on the market for a further year, but may not be made available on the market **after 23 September 2030** (Article 36(2)).

The animal-testing ban (Article 7) and the future biodegradability criteria for films/polymers and other organic substances (Article 4(3)-(4)) are the named exceptions to the general 23 September 2029 application date: they apply from entry into force (22 March 2026) with their own later compliance deadlines of 23 March 2032 and 23 March 2034 respectively (Article 37, second paragraph).

## What it covers: 5 themes for an infographic

1. **A digital passport for every detergent (and every end-user surfactant).** Before it reaches the shelf, manufacturers must create a digital product passport, upload it to an EU-wide registry, and connect it to a data carrier that customs and market surveillance authorities can check instantly.
2. **Digital labels enter the laundry aisle.** For the first time, part of a detergent's mandatory label, such as surfactant types, phosphates and refill dosage details, can move to a scannable digital label, while allergy-critical information stays physically printed.
3. **Micro-organisms get their own rulebook.** Living-ingredient detergents, a market Regulation (EC) No 648/2004 never anticipated, now need genome-level strain identification, a named pathogen exclusion list, and a dedicated safety risk assessment before sale.
4. **Refill goes from grey area to green law.** Detergent refill stations, one of the EU's flagship reuse measures under the Green Deal, get explicit safety rules: no unsupervised child access, trained staff, and full labelling parity with bottled products.
5. **A staged path to greener chemistry.** Beyond the surfactant biodegradability rules already in force since 2004, new EU-wide biodegradability criteria are coming for detergent-capsule films (applying 2032) and for high-concentration organic ingredients (applying 2034).

## Glossary

- **Detergent** - a substance, mixture or micro-organisms, or a combination of these, intended to clean, soak, rinse or bleach fabrics, dishes or surfaces, or to modify fabric feel/odour or support cleaning alongside a laundry/dishwasher detergent (Article 2(1)).
- **Surfactant** - an organic substance or mixture with surface-active properties, meeting the four technical criteria of Article 2(11), used in or intended for use in detergents.
- **End-user surfactant** - a surfactant made available on the market to professional users or consumers, as opposed to a surfactant sold business-to-business for use in manufacturing detergents (Article 2(12)).
- **Ultimate aerobic biodegradation** - the level of biodegradation reached when a substance is fully used by micro-organisms in the presence of oxygen, breaking down to carbon dioxide, water, mineral salts and new microbial biomass, as measured by the Annex I Part A test methods (Article 2(13)).
- **Detergent containing micro-organisms** - a detergent to which one or more micro-organisms have been intentionally added, on their own or via a component (Article 2(4)).
- **Refill / refill station** - the on-site operation of filling a detergent or surfactant into packaging for an end-user, and the place (manual, automatic or semi-automatic) where this happens (Article 2(37)-(38)).
- **Digital product passport (DPP)** - a set of data specific to a detergent or end-user surfactant model, accessible via a data carrier, demonstrating compliance with this Regulation and, potentially, other Union law (Article 2(32), Article 21).
- **Digital label** - the optional digital counterpart to a detergent's or surfactant's physical label, which may carry certain content digitally only under Annex V Part C (Article 18).
- **Ingredients data sheet** - the confidential document manufacturers must submit to Member States' appointed emergency-health-response bodies for detergents/end-user surfactants that are mixtures not otherwise required to notify under CLP Article 45 (Article 8(6), Annex IV point 2.2(e)).
- **Unique registration identifier** - the identifier automatically communicated by the digital product passport registry once an economic operator uploads a product's unique product and operator identifiers, used by customs to verify a DPP exists (Article 24(2)).
