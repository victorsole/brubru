# EU Detergents and Surfactants Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2026/405)

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is:** the recast EU framework governing detergents and surfactants placed on the internal market, replacing and repealing the 2004 Detergents Regulation.
- **Legal act:** Regulation (EU) 2026/405 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 February 2026 on detergents and surfactants, amending Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 and repealing Regulation (EC) No 648/2004. CELEX: `32026R0405`. Legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation).
- **Official text:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R0405
- **Repealed act:** Regulation (EC) No 648/2004, in force since 2004: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX:32004R0648
- **Procedure:** 2023/0124(COD), ordinary legislative procedure (co-decision). Legislative Train page: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-european-green-deal/file-revision-of-the-detergents-regulation
- **Commission proposal:** COM(2023) 217 final, tabled 28 April 2023, together with impact assessment SWD(2023) 114 and preceded by the 2019 evaluation of the old Regulation, SWD(2019) 298.
- **Key legislative dates:** EP first-reading position 27 February 2024 (P9_TA(2024)0091); Council general approach 14 June 2024; provisional political agreement in trilogue 10 June 2025 (Commission welcome press release 11 June 2025); Council position formally adopted 8 December 2025; European Parliament approval at second reading 22 January 2026; act signed by the Presidents of Parliament and Council 11 February 2026; published in the Official Journal 2 March 2026.
- **Entry into force:** 22 March 2026 (20 days after OJ publication).
- **Date of application:** 23 September 2029 (most provisions), with Regulation (EC) No 648/2004 repealed from the same date. Some biodegradability-related provisions follow separate, later timelines set by delegated acts.
- **Transition periods:** products compliant with the old 2004 Regulation placed on the market before 23 September 2029 may continue to be made available with no time limit; products placed on the market between 23 September 2029 and 22 September 2030 under the old rules may continue to be made available until 23 September 2030.
- **Who runs it:** European Commission (DG GROW/DG ENV lead), national market surveillance authorities, and the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) for scientific/technical support; enforced alongside Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance, which this Regulation also amends.
- **Why it matters:** it is the biggest overhaul of EU detergents rules in two decades, folding in digital labelling, a Digital Product Passport, an EU-wide ban on animal testing, refill-sale rules, and a first legal framework for detergents containing living micro-organisms.

## What it is and why it exists

Detergents and surfactants (laundry detergents, dishwashing products, hard-surface cleaners, industrial and institutional cleaning products, and the surfactants used as ingredients) are among the most widely used chemical product categories in the EU, sold to both consumers and professional users. Regulation (EC) No 648/2004 has governed their biodegradability, labelling and market surveillance since 2004, but the Commission's 2019 evaluation (SWD(2019) 298) found the framework outdated: overlapping and duplicative requirements with the CLP Regulation on classification, labelling and packaging of chemicals, no rules for detergents containing living micro-organisms (a fast-growing market segment), and no mechanism for digital or refill-based product information.

The revision was first flagged in October 2020 as part of the action plan accompanying the EU Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability ("Towards a Toxic-Free Environment"), and the Commission tabled its formal proposal on 28 April 2023 (COM(2023) 217 final) as a REFIT (regulatory fitness) simplification exercise: strip out redundant requirements, align with other chemicals legislation, and open the door to safe, innovative products.

## What changes compared with the 2004 Regulation

**Rules for detergents containing micro-organisms.** For the first time, the Regulation extends the definition of "detergent" to expressly cover products with intentionally added living micro-organisms (a market segment the 2004 Regulation did not anticipate). Annex II sets out dedicated safety requirements: prohibitions on genetically modified organisms, and stability, shelf-life and safety-testing obligations. Labels must disclose the presence of micro-organisms and give storage and use instructions to preserve their effectiveness and safety. The Digital Product Passport must list every intentionally added micro-organism with full taxonomic classification (genus, species, strain).

**Digital Product Passport (DPP) and digital labelling.** The Regulation introduces a mandatory Digital Product Passport, replacing the old ingredient data sheet mechanism, and a voluntary digital-labelling option that runs alongside the mandatory physical label (Article 18). Under Annex VI Part A, the DPP must contain: product identification (trade name, unique product ID, a colour image of the packaging/label); manufacturer, importer and authorised-representative contact details and a unique operator ID; the identity of the passport service provider hosting the back-up copy; traceability data; a statement that the manufacturer is solely responsible for the DPP's content; relevant customs/commodity codes under Regulation (EEC) No 2658/87; confirmation of compliance with this Regulation and other applicable EU law; the full list of intentionally added substances (as defined under the CLP Regulation) including carry-over preservatives; and the micro-organism disclosures described above. The DPP must be referenced in the product registry established under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, must be interoperable with other EU digital product passport systems, and will be checkable at EU external borders, including for e-commerce imports.

**Physical labelling, still mandatory.** The core physical-label obligations of the 2004 Regulation survive: under the new Article 17(3) and Annex V Part A, labels must show the product/trade name, a type or batch number (or other traceable identifier), the UFI (Unique Formula Identifier, "UFI:" followed by the code), manufacturer/importer name, a single EU postal address, an email address and telephone number, instructions for use, and any necessary precautions. Certain elements (traceability data, manufacturer identity, product name) must always stay on the physical label and can never be pushed to digital-only; other information (Annex V Part C) may be digital-only. Consumer laundry, automatic-dishwasher and surface-cleaning detergents must additionally carry dosage instructions (Annex V Part B), and fragrance allergens listed in Annex V Part D must appear on-label.

**Refill sales.** The Regulation formally addresses detergents and surfactants sold to end users via refill stations, a market development the 2004 text never contemplated. Refilled products must always carry a physical label (bulk/refill sales cannot rely on digital-only information, mirroring the CLP Regulation's approach to refill), plus the digital data carrier specified under Article 21(2)(h), for every package that is refilled.

**Biodegradability.** Annex I revises and extends the biodegradability criteria, and abolishes the derogation regime that previously let some surfactants avoid full "ultimate biodegradability" testing through an application process; going forward all in-scope surfactants must meet the ultimate biodegradability standard with no exemption route. The Commission is empowered to adopt delegated acts setting biodegradability criteria and test methods for the water-soluble polymeric films used in laundry pods/capsules and other films, and for organic substances intentionally added at 10% w/w or more. The Commission is also tasked with assessing the feasibility of reducing phosphorus levels in detergents.

**Animal testing ban.** The Regulation introduces an explicit, legislative prohibition: no animal testing may be carried out in connection with the production of in-scope detergents or surfactants, going further than the case-by-case restrictions under REACH and the CLP Regulation.

**Repeal of Regulation (EC) No 648/2004.** The old 2004 Regulation is repealed in full from 23 September 2029, the date the new Regulation starts to apply, subject to the transition periods described in QUICK FACTS.

## Interaction with adjacent chemicals legislation

Detergents manufacturers and importers sit at the intersection of three EU legal regimes, and the new Regulation is explicitly designed to remove overlaps between them:

- **CLP Regulation** (classification, labelling and packaging of substances and mixtures, `eu_clp_regulation_classification_labelling`): detergents and surfactants must still be classified and labelled for hazards under CLP; the new Regulation removes the previous duplication between CLP hazard labelling and the detergents-specific ingredient labelling regime, and aligns its refill and bulk-sale label rules with the parallel digital-labelling provisions the CLP Regulation itself gained through Regulation (EU) 2024/2865 (the CLP modernisation act, adopted December 2024), which for the first time allowed digital and expandable labels for chemical products generally.
- **REACH** (`reach_chemicals_regulation`): surfactants and other detergent ingredients remain subject to REACH registration, evaluation and authorisation obligations; the Detergents Regulation governs the finished-product rules (biodegradability, labelling, market access) layered on top of REACH's substance-level obligations.
- **Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781** (`ecodesign_digital_product_passport`): the detergents DPP is built to be interoperable with, and registered alongside, the horizontal Digital Product Passport infrastructure the Ecodesign Regulation is rolling out product category by product category. Detergents are one of the first non-textile, non-electronics categories to get a sector-specific DPP mandate directly in its own product legislation rather than through an Ecodesign delegated act.
- **Chemicals simplification agenda**: the Detergents Regulation revision was explicitly framed by the Commission as a REFIT/simplification exercise, and sits within the broader wave of chemicals-law streamlining that includes the Omnibus VI chemicals simplification package (`omnibus_vi_chemicals_simplification`), which targets overlapping administrative burdens across REACH, CLP and sector-specific chemical product rules.
- **Market surveillance**: the Regulation amends Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (the general market surveillance and product compliance regulation) to fold detergents and surfactants into the EU's common customs and market-surveillance checks, replacing the old "safeguard clause" procedure under the 2004 Regulation with a faster, more transparent mechanism for products presenting a risk to health or the environment.

## Who to watch

Lead Commission service: DG GROW (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs), with DG ENV (Environment) on biodegradability and environmental provisions. In the European Parliament, the file was led by the Internal Market and Consumer Protection Committee (IMCO), with Environment (ENVI) as an associated committee, notably on the biodegradability and micro-organism safety provisions. In Council, the file was handled through the Competitiveness Council (Internal Market).
