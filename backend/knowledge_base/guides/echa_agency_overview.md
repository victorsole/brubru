# ECHA: European Chemicals Agency

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Chemicals Agency (ECHA)
- Seat: Helsinki, Finland. Operational since 1 June 2007, around 600 staff
- Legal basis: established by REACH, Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 (CELEX 32006R1907)
- Status: an independent, decentralised EU regulatory agency, not a Commission department
- Executive Director: Sharon McGuinness
- Governance: Management Board, Executive Director, Secretariat, Board of Appeal
- Five committees plus a Forum: RAC (Committee for Risk Assessment), SEAC (Committee for Socio-Economic Analysis), MSC (Member State Committee), BPC (Biocidal Products Committee), and the Forum for Exchange of Information on Enforcement ("the Forum"), which runs a dedicated biocides subgroup (BPRS)
- Legislation ECHA implements or supports: REACH (Reg (EC) 1907/2006), CLP (Reg (EC) 1272/2008, CELEX 32008R1272), the Biocidal Products Regulation (Reg (EU) 528/2012, CELEX 32012R0528), the Prior Informed Consent Regulation on hazardous chemical exports/imports (Reg (EU) 649/2012, CELEX 32012R0649), and the POPs Regulation (Reg (EU) 2019/1021, CELEX 32019R1021)
- ECHA CHEM: ECHA's public chemicals database, launched January 2024 at chem.echa.europa.eu, covering REACH registration data, the Classification and Labelling Inventory and EU regulatory lists, drawing on over 100,000 REACH registration dossiers covering more than 360,000 substances
- Expanded mandate (2026): the "one substance, one assessment" (OSOA) legislative package entered into force 1 January 2026 -- Regulation (EU) 2025/2455 establishing a Common Data Platform on Chemicals, plus Regulation (EU) 2025/2457 and Directive (EU) 2025/2456 reattributing chemical-safety tasks between EU agencies. ECHA will build and manage the Common Data Platform, expected operational within three years (around 2029), with data contributions from EFSA, EEA, EU-OSHA and EMA
- Live dossier to watch: the universal PFAS restriction. RAC has adopted its final opinion; SEAC's draft opinion was open for consultation and a final SEAC opinion is expected by the end of 2026, after which both opinions go to the Commission for a restriction decision

ECHA is the EU's chemicals safety agency. Most people know it only through REACH, but its remit now spans classification and labelling, biocides, hazardous chemical trade, persistent pollutants, and a new EU-wide data infrastructure for chemical safety assessments.

## What ECHA does

ECHA manages the technical, scientific and administrative work behind the EU's chemicals legislation. It does not write the law (the Commission proposes, Parliament and Council co-legislate) but it runs the machinery that makes the law operate day to day:

- Receives and evaluates REACH registration dossiers from manufacturers and importers
- Runs the REACH Candidate List and Authorisation List for substances of very high concern (SVHCs)
- Coordinates and opines on restriction proposals (bans or limits on specific uses of a substance)
- Manages the biocidal products authorisation system under the Biocidal Products Regulation
- Maintains the Classification, Labelling and Packaging (CLP) inventory
- Supports Member States and the Commission on the Prior Informed Consent (export/import) procedure for hazardous chemicals
- Provides scientific and technical input on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)
- Coordinates Member State enforcement of chemicals law through the Forum
- Since January 2026, is building the EU's new Common Data Platform on Chemicals

ECHA is legally independent of the Commission. It is funded by a mix of EU budget contribution and industry fees (mainly REACH registration fees).

## Governance and committees

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| **Management Board** | Sets strategy, appoints the Executive Director, adopts the budget and work programme. Includes Commission, Member State and European Parliament-nominated representation |
| **Executive Director** | Sharon McGuinness. Runs day-to-day operations and represents the Agency |
| **RAC** (Committee for Risk Assessment) | Delivers ECHA's scientific opinions on health and environmental risk for restrictions, classification and biocides. Working groups cover CLH (classification/labelling harmonisation), restrictions, applications for authorisation (AfA) and dossier evaluation |
| **SEAC** (Committee for Socio-Economic Analysis) | Assesses the socio-economic costs and benefits of proposed restrictions and authorisations, working alongside RAC |
| **MSC** (Member State Committee) | Resolves disagreements between Member States on SVHC identification and other REACH decisions, seeking unanimous agreement |
| **BPC** (Biocidal Products Committee) | Delivers opinions on biocidal active substances and Union-level product authorisations |
| **Forum for Exchange of Information on Enforcement** ("the Forum") | Coordinates national enforcement of REACH, CLP and the Biocidal Products Regulation across Member States; runs a dedicated biocides enforcement subgroup (BPRS) |
| **Board of Appeal** | Hears appeals against ECHA decisions, e.g. on registration or evaluation |

Committee members typically serve three-year renewable mandates. RAC and SEAC members are nominated by Member States and appointed by the Management Board; MSC and BPC members are directly appointed by Member States.

## ECHA CHEM

ECHA CHEM (chem.echa.europa.eu) is ECHA's public chemicals database, launched in January 2024 to replace and consolidate the Agency's earlier information platform. It brings together:

- REACH registration data submitted by companies (over 100,000 dossiers)
- The Classification and Labelling Inventory
- EU regulatory lists (Candidate List, Authorisation List, restrictions)

It now covers information on more than 360,000 chemical substances and is the reference public source for checking a substance's registration, hazard classification and regulatory status.

## The Common Data Platform on Chemicals (the 2026 reform)

The "one substance, one assessment" (OSOA) reform is the biggest change to ECHA's mandate since REACH itself. The package entered into force on 1 January 2026 and consists of three acts:

- **Regulation (EU) 2025/2455**, establishing the Common Data Platform on Chemicals and an EU monitoring and outlook framework for chemicals. This is the flagship measure: a single, publicly accessible EU hub bringing together chemical safety data currently scattered across REACH, CLP, biocides, pesticides, cosmetics, food contact materials and other frameworks
- **Regulation (EU) 2025/2457**, reattributing technical and scientific tasks and improving cooperation between the EU agencies working on chemicals (ECHA, EFSA, EEA, EU-OSHA and EMA)
- **Directive (EU) 2025/2456**, reattributing specific technical tasks to ECHA

The aim, first set out in the Commission's 2020 Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability, is to stop the same substance being assessed separately, and inconsistently, under six or more different EU legal frameworks. ECHA will build and manage the Common Data Platform, with data contributions from EFSA (food and feed), EEA (environmental monitoring), EU-OSHA (occupational exposure) and EMA (medicines). The platform is expected to become operational within three years of entry into force, around 2029.

This is a significant data-governance shift for any organisation that reports chemical safety data to more than one EU agency: fewer duplicate submissions in the long run, but new formats, controlled vocabularies and transparency obligations to track as implementing measures are adopted.

## PFAS and other active dossiers

- **Universal PFAS restriction**: proposed to ECHA in 2023 by the national authorities of Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden. RAC has adopted its final opinion; SEAC's draft opinion (recognising the need for targeted derogations to keep the restriction proportionate) was open for stakeholder consultation, with a final SEAC opinion expected by the end of 2026. Both committee opinions then go to the Commission, which decides on the restriction under REACH's Annex XVII procedure
- **Bisphenol A and structurally similar bisphenols**: RAC and SEAC opinions adopted, feeding into a possible restriction on BPA and related substances
- **Animal testing phase-out**: ECHA is a named coordination partner (alongside EFSA) in the Commission's roadmap for phasing out animal testing in chemical safety assessments through New Approach Methodologies (NAMs)

## Engaging with ECHA

- **Public consultations**: RAC and SEAC opinions on restrictions and harmonised classifications go through public consultation before adoption; consultations are published on ECHA's website
- **Stakeholder observer status**: registered stakeholder organisations can request observer status at RAC, SEAC, MSC and BPC meetings, subject to ECHA's code of conduct for observers
- **Registration and notification obligations**: companies manufacturing or importing chemicals above EU thresholds interact with ECHA directly through REACH-IT and the biocides IT platform (R4BP)
