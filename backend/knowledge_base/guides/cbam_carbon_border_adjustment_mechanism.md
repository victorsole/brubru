- **WHAT CBAM IS (precision — do not mis-state)**: CBAM is **not a tax**. It is a **regulatory carbon-pricing obligation**: importers of covered goods must buy **CBAM certificates** whose price tracks the weekly average **EU ETS** allowance auction price, reflecting the embedded emissions of the imported goods. It was deliberately built as a regulatory measure, not a tax, to be **WTO-compatible** and to mirror the carbon cost EU producers already bear under the ETS. Never call it a "carbon-border tax". The **six covered sectors are: cement, iron & steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen** (do not omit fertilisers or duplicate steel).

## Overview

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) puts a carbon price on the emissions **embedded in carbon-intensive goods imported into the EU**, equivalent to the price domestic producers pay under the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). Its purpose (Article 1) is to **prevent carbon leakage** — the risk that, as the EU tightens its own carbon price, production (and its emissions) simply moves to countries with weaker climate rules, or that EU-made goods are undercut by higher-emission imports.

CBAM is the import-side complement to the EU ETS. As the EU **phases out the free emission allowances** that have historically shielded heavy industry from carbon costs (Art 10a, Dir 2003/87/EC), CBAM phases in on the import side, so imported and domestic goods face an equivalent carbon price. The combined design must never give imported goods more favourable treatment than EU goods (Recital 12), and was structured to comply with **World Trade Organization** law (Recital 15) — which is why it is a regulatory certificate obligation, not a border tax, and imposes **no quantitative limits** on imports (Recital 21).

CBAM is **directly applicable** in all Member States (it is a Regulation — no national transposition), but it is **administered jointly**: the Commission runs the central CBAM registry, the common platform for selling/repurchasing certificates, the weekly price, and risk-based review; **Member State competent authorities** authorise declarants, sell certificates, run enforcement and impose penalties; **customs authorities** police the border (only an authorised CBAM declarant may import covered goods).

## Scope — the six covered sectors (Annex I, by CN code)

CBAM applies to goods listed in Annex I **originating in a third country** when released for free circulation in the EU (Art 2). Coverage is defined by **Combined Nomenclature (CN) codes** and linked to specific greenhouse gases:

| Sector | Indicative CN coverage | Greenhouse gas(es) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cement** | 2507 00 80 (certain kaolinic clays), 2523 (clinker, Portland, aluminous, other hydraulic cements) | CO2 |
| **Electricity** | 2716 00 00 (electrical energy) | CO2 |
| **Fertilisers** | 2808 (nitric acid), 2814 (ammonia), 2834 21 (potassium nitrate), 3102 + 3105 (nitrogenous / NPK fertilisers) | CO2 **and N2O** |
| **Iron & steel** | Chapter 72 + downstream 7301–7311 (excluding ferro-alloys 7202 and ferrous scrap 7204) | CO2 |
| **Aluminium** | 7601, 7603–7608, 7609 (unwrought + selected downstream) | CO2 **and PFCs** |
| **Hydrogen** | 2804 10 (hydrogen) | CO2 |

**Annex II** lists the goods for which **only direct emissions** are counted in the initial phase (iron & steel, aluminium, certain chemicals/hydrogen) — because EU producers in those sectors receive financial compensation for indirect (electricity) carbon costs, so charging indirect emissions on imports would be asymmetric. Cement, fertilisers and electricity have **both direct and indirect emissions** counted.

**Out of scope:** consignments of negligible value (≤ €150), goods in travellers' personal luggage, goods for military use, and goods originating in the Annex III countries/territories. Organic chemicals and refinery products were **deliberately left out at adoption** (Recitals 34–35) because their embedded emissions could not yet be unambiguously allocated to individual products.

## How it works — the definitive regime (from 1 January 2026)

1. **Authorised CBAM declarant (Arts 4, 5, 17).** Only an importer (or its indirect customs representative) holding the status of **authorised CBAM declarant** may import covered goods. The status is granted by the competent authority of the Member State of establishment, recognised EU-wide, and conditional on a clean customs/tax record, financial and operational capacity, an EORI number, and (for newer entities) a guarantee.
2. **Calculate embedded emissions (Art 7, Annex IV).** Emissions are determined from **actual data** where possible, otherwise from **default values**. Methods distinguish "simple goods" from "complex goods" (whose precursors carry their own embedded emissions). Third-country **operators can register installations** in the CBAM registry (Art 10) and share verified emissions data with declarants.
3. **Verify (Art 8, Annex VI).** Declared embedded emissions must be verified by an **accredited verifier** (accreditation aligned with the EU ETS verification regime, Implementing Reg (EU) 2018/2067).
4. **Buy certificates (Arts 20–21).** Member States sell CBAM certificates on a **common central platform** at the **weekly EU ETS auction average price**. Each certificate = 1 tonne CO2e.
5. **Hold ≥80% each quarter (Art 22(2)).** At every quarter-end the declarant must hold certificates covering at least 80% of the year-to-date embedded emissions (default-value basis).
6. **Declare and surrender annually (Arts 6, 22).** By **31 May**, the declarant submits the **CBAM declaration** for the prior year and surrenders the matching number of certificates, **net of** (a) any carbon price already paid abroad (Art 9) and (b) the **free-allocation adjustment** under Art 31.
7. **Repurchase / cancellation (Arts 23–24).** A declarant can sell back excess certificates (capped at one third of prior-year purchases) at the original price; certificates left unused beyond the validity window are cancelled each 1 July without compensation.
8. **Enforcement (Arts 19, 26–27).** The Commission runs risk-based review; competent authorities impose penalties mirroring the ETS excess-emissions penalty, rising to 3–5× for non-declarant importers, and the Commission monitors **circumvention** (slightly-modified goods, artificial shipment-splitting, resource shuffling).

## The transitional period (1 October 2023 – 31 December 2025)

During the transitional phase there were **no financial obligations**. Importers (or their indirect customs representatives) filed a **quarterly CBAM report** (Art 35) within one month of each quarter-end, declaring the embedded direct and indirect emissions and any carbon price paid abroad — building the data and habits for the definitive regime. The **last** transitional report (Q4 2025) was due **31 January 2026**. This phase is now closed; the figures most queries refer to ("CBAM started in 2026") mean the **definitive, paying** regime.
