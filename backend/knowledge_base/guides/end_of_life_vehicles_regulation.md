## What the Regulation does
The ELV Regulation merges and modernises two ageing directives — the 2000 ELV Directive (2000/53/EC) and the 2005 3R type-approval Directive (2005/64/EC) — into a single directly-applicable framework covering the **whole vehicle life cycle, from design to end-of-life treatment**. Context: there are around 286 million motor vehicles on EU roads and roughly 6.5 million become waste each year.

Key obligations:
- **Circular design requirements** (Article 4, from type-approval on 1 September 2032) — 85% reusable or recyclable and 95% reusable or recoverable by mass, with supply-chain material data collected and the calculation done under the Commission methodology or, until it applies, ISO 22628:2002 with Annex III Part A
- **Recycled plastic content** (Article 6, adopted figures) — **15% of the plastic in each new vehicle type type-approved from 1 September 2032**, rising to **25% from 1 September 2036**, measured by weight from post-consumer plastic waste. **At least 20% of that target** must come from end-of-life vehicles or from parts removed during the use phase. Elastomers from tyres and thermosets other than polyurethane cushioning foams are excluded from the calculation. The Commission is to set recycled-content targets for steel and aluminium separately
- **Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport** (Article 13, from 1 September 2032) — every vehicle placed on the market carries one, free of charge, aligned and where possible integrated with the **battery passport under Article 77 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542**, the passport in Regulation (EU) 2024/1257 and passports under the **ESPR (EU) 2024/1781**. It carries the removal and replacement information, the parts containing lead, mercury, cadmium or hexavalent chromium, the recycled-content declaration, and the official spare-parts catalogue. Information already in one of those other passports is not duplicated
- **Recycled-content declaration** (Article 10) — per vehicle type, the share of recycled neodymium, dysprosium, praseodymium, terbium, samarium, nickel, cobalt and boron in e-drive permanent magnets, plus aluminium, magnesium, steel and plastics, distinguishing pre-consumer from post-consumer for plastic components over 100 grams
- **Existing recovery targets retained** — minimum **85% recyclability and 95% recoverability** by weight
- **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** strengthened and harmonised at EU level (collection, treatment, information provision)
- **Mandatory removal of parts at Authorised Treatment Facilities (ATFs)** before shredding
- **Export rules** for used vehicles from the EU to third countries (curbing illegal exports of non-roadworthy vehicles)
- **Scope, phased by vehicle category** (Article 2): cars and vans (M1, N1) from the start; **special purpose M1 and N1 from 1 September 2029**; **buses, lorries, trailers (M2, M3, N2, N3, O) and L-category two- and three-wheelers from 1 September 2031**. Excluded: small-series vehicles, vehicles for the armed services only, and vehicles for civil defence, fire and public-order services

## Sectors / who is affected
Vehicle manufacturers and importers, dismantlers and ATFs, recyclers (especially plastics and metals), material-data and compliance teams, and the automotive supply chain. The new rules drive design changes, material-data management, recycled-content sourcing, and circular-economy compliance strategies.
