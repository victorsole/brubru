# Food 2040: the six areas of action, and the Food 2030 pathways it builds on

## QUICK FACTS
- **Food 2040 has exactly SIX areas of action** (AgRI 2040, for agriculture, forestry and rural areas, has EIGHT; the strategy's shared priorities are a separate list of six). Source: COM(2026) 453 final, 8 September 2026, section 3.4, the DG RTD Food 2040 page and its pictogram.
- **The six areas:** (1) **place-based food systems**; (2) **healthy and sustainable diets**; (3) **resource efficiency and zero-pollution in food systems**; (4) **food environments and consumer empowerment**; (5) **scaling innovations, business models and fair food systems solutions**; (6) **aquatic food** (sustainable fisheries and aquaculture, blue bioeconomy).
- **What Food 2040 is:** the R&I framework for **food systems, including fisheries and aquaculture**. It builds on **Food 2030**, keeps its systemic, cross-sectoral and multi-actor approach and puts more weight on **impact, uptake, scale-up and deployment**. Scope: food processing and manufacturing, packaging, distribution and retail, food services, consumption, food waste and recycling. A framework in a Communication, NOT a law; no obligation.
- **Timing and money:** the areas guide co-creation of R&I under **Horizon Europe 2028-2034** and relevant parts of the European Competitiveness Fund, "without prejudging" the next MFF; R&I activities on the six areas from **Q3 2028** (action 18); first Food 2040 high-level conference **Q4 2028** (action 19). No budget figure.
- **Food 2030 (since 2016) had exactly 11 pathways for action:** (1) governance for food systems change; (2) urban food systems transformations; (3) food from the ocean and freshwater resources; (4) alternative proteins for dietary shifts; (5) food waste and resource-efficient food systems; (6) the microbiome world; (7) nutrition and sustainable healthy diets; (8) food safety systems of the future; (9) food systems Africa; (10) data and digital transformation; (11) zero-pollution food systems.
- **Food 2030's four cross-cutting priorities:** nutrition and health; climate and sustainability; circularity and resource efficiency; innovation and communities.
- **Why a successor:** stakeholders called Food 2030 "essential but no longer sufficient": more operational, measurable, aligned with current risks, with stronger scale-up, competitiveness, resilience, governance and territorial focus. Food 2030 funded about **EUR 770 million**; FutureFoodS (83 partners, 29 countries) is its co-funded partnership.
