# EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Reg (EU) 2025/40 — PPWR)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Regulation**: **Regulation (EU) 2025/40** of the European Parliament and of the Council on packaging and packaging waste — known as the **PPWR** (CELEX **32025R0040**, OJ L of 14 January 2025).
- **Entry into force**: 11 February 2025 (20th day after publication in the OJ).
- **General application**: **12 August 2026** (Article 70(2), with phased provisions earlier — see Compliance roadmap below).
- **Repeals**: Directive **94/62/EC** on packaging and packaging waste (CELEX 31994L0062), the original 1994 packaging instrument.
- **Type**: Regulation (directly applicable; supersedes the prior directive-level harmonisation).
- **Lead DG**: DG ENV (Environment)
- **Commissioner**: Jessika Roswall (Environment, Water Resilience and Competitive Circular Economy)
- **EP lead committee**: ENVI (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety)
- **Council configuration**: Environment Council
- **Procedure adopted via**: 2022/0396(COD) — ordinary legislative procedure (file negotiated in trilogue, political agreement March 2024, EP plenary adoption 24 April 2024, Council adoption 16 December 2024, OJ publication 14 January 2025)
- **Recital count**: 168 recitals
- **Article count**: 75 articles + 13 Annexes
- **Cross-link**: `eu_circular_economy_action_plan`, `eu_waste_shipment_regulation_diwass`, `single_use_plastics_directive`, `eu_extended_producer_responsibility`

This guide covers the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR), which replaces the 1994 Directive 94/62/EC with a directly-applicable, harmonised framework for all packaging placed on the EU market.

## Why a Regulation instead of a Directive

The 1994 framework was a directive: it left wide discretion to Member States on definitions ("recyclable", "compostable"), on EPR scheme design, on take-back requirements, and on reuse targets. Result: a patchwork of 27 packaging regimes that distorted single-market trade. The PPWR collapses that fragmentation: a Regulation is **directly applicable**, with definitions, targets, conformity routes, and labelling rules harmonised across the EU.

## What the PPWR does — five pillars

### Pillar 1 — Quantitative reduction targets (Article 43)
- Packaging waste per capita: **-5% by 2030**, **-10% by 2035**, **-15% by 2040** versus 2018 baseline
- Member States must set up monitoring programmes and report progress annually to the Commission
- Specific reduction targets for grouped packaging, transport packaging, and e-commerce packaging

### Pillar 2 — Reuse targets (Articles 26-29)
- Beverage packaging (cold drinks): reuse target of **10% by 2030**, **40% by 2040**
- Transport packaging (pallets, crates, boxes between economic operators): reuse target of **40% by 2030**, **70% by 2040**
- E-commerce transport packaging: **10% reuse by 2030**
- Take-away beverage and ready-to-eat food: reuse offering obligation
- HoReCa (hotels, restaurants, catering) packaging: specific reuse and refill obligations

### Pillar 3 — Recyclability and recycled content (Articles 6-7, 9)
- By **2030**, all packaging placed on the EU market must be **designed for recycling** following the Commission's Delegated Act on Design-for-Recycling criteria (to be adopted)
- By **2035**, packaging must also be **recycled at scale** (Article 6(5) — performance grades A, B, C with C eligible only until 2038)
- **Minimum recycled-content thresholds** for plastic packaging:
  - **30%** by 2030 for contact-sensitive packaging (PET food contact)
  - **35%** by 2030 for contact-sensitive non-PET
  - **10%** by 2030 for single-use plastic beverage bottles
  - **65%** by 2040 for the most demanding categories
- The Commission to adopt a **delegated act** by 31 December 2026 setting the calculation methodology, mass-balance rules, and chain-of-custody requirements

### Pillar 4 — Bans on specific packaging formats (Annex V)
From 1 January 2030, the following are **banned**:
- Single-use plastic packaging for unprocessed fresh fruit and vegetables (under 1.5 kg)
- Single-use plastic packaging for foods and beverages filled and consumed in cafés and restaurants (dine-in)
- Single-use single-portion packaging in HoReCa (condiments, sauces, sugar)
- Single-use plastic packaging for accommodation small toiletries (under 100 ml)
- Very lightweight plastic carrier bags (under 15 microns) — except where required for hygiene
- Grouped (multi-pack) packaging used as a marketing tool to bundle products

### Pillar 5 — Conformity assessment and Declaration of Conformity (Articles 36-37, Annex VII)
- Every packaging placed on the EU market must be accompanied by an **EU Declaration of Conformity** (DoC)
- Format of the DoC: standardised in **Annex VII** of the regulation
- Required fields:
  - Identification of the packaging type (single-use vs reusable; primary, secondary, tertiary)
  - Identification of the responsible economic operator (producer, importer, fulfilment service provider, authorised representative)
  - Declaration that the packaging meets sustainability requirements of Articles 5 to 11 (substances of concern, recyclability, minimum recycled content, minimisation, reusability where applicable)
  - Conformity assessment route used (internal production control or third-party verification)
  - Reference to harmonised standards or technical specifications used
  - Reference to the EPR registration number and the SDIB (Single Designated Information Body) of the responsible MS
  - Signature, location, date
- The DoC must be in the language(s) of the Member State where the packaging is placed on the market and must be made available to market surveillance authorities on request
- Records must be retained for **10 years** after the packaging is placed on the market (Article 37)
- A copy of the DoC must accompany the packaging or be made electronically accessible (QR code link)

## EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility) — Articles 45-51

- Every Member State must operate an EPR scheme for packaging by **1 January 2028** (PRO model possible)
- Eco-modulation of EPR fees: producers placing more recyclable / less hazardous packaging pay less; non-recyclable formats pay more
- Cross-MS EPR coordination via **EUROPEN** and **PROs Europe**; a Commission implementing act will set the harmonised fee-modulation criteria by 2027
- Producers must register with an EPR scheme in EACH MS where they place packaging — single-window registration via the Single Designated Information Body is planned for 2030

## Cross-link with the new Waste Shipment Regulation (DIWASS)

The **DIWASS platform** (Digital Information for Waste Shipments) went live on **21 May 2026** as part of the new **Waste Shipment Regulation (EU) 2024/1157**. PPWR-covered packaging that becomes waste enters the DIWASS-monitored shipment system when shipped across MS borders. The two regulations together close the post-consumer packaging loop. See guide `eu_waste_shipment_regulation_diwass`.

## Compliance roadmap (post-entry-into-force)

| Date | Obligation |
|---|---|
| 11 February 2025 | PPWR enters into force |
| 12 August 2026 | General application date — all main obligations binding |
| 31 December 2026 | Delegated Act on recycled-content calculation methodology due |
| 1 January 2028 | All MS must operate an EPR packaging scheme; reusable-packaging targets baseline starts running |
| 1 January 2030 | Single-use packaging bans (Annex V) take effect; reuse targets phase 1; recycled-content thresholds phase 1 |
| 1 January 2035 | Recyclable-at-scale grading regime fully binding |
| 1 January 2038 | Grade-C packaging no longer eligible (only A and B allowed) |
| 1 January 2040 | Reuse targets phase 2 fully binding |

## What it means for SMEs and producers

- **SMEs placing < 10 tonnes/year on the EU market**: certain reporting obligations are lighter (Article 53) but the DoC obligation still applies. The Commission to issue an SME guidance by end-2026.
- **Online marketplaces and fulfilment service providers**: due-diligence and information requirements apply (Article 35) — they must verify producer EPR registration and ensure the DoC accompanies the packaging.
- **Importers**: must hold the DoC and make it available to MS surveillance authorities; importers without an EU establishment must designate an Authorised Representative in the EU.
- **Authorised representatives**: their duties are listed in Article 34. They are the contact point for market surveillance and must be designated in writing.

## Stakeholder map

- **EUROPEN** — European Organisation for Packaging and the Environment; industry brand-owner body
- **PROs Europe** — umbrella of national EPR schemes (Der Grüne Punkt, Ecoembes, CONAI, etc.)
- **ECEPLAST / Plastics Europe** — plastic-packaging value chain
- **Zero Waste Europe** — NGO; advocates stricter reuse targets and faster bans
- **CEPI** — Confederation of European Paper Industries; promotes fibre-based substitutes
- **EUROCITIES** — represents large cities running municipal collection
- **HOTREC** — hospitality sector trade body

## Member State action map (key flashpoints)

- **Germany**: Existing Verpackungsgesetz + Zentrale Stelle is the EPR baseline most aligned with PPWR; minimal disruption
- **Spain**: Ecoembes EPR scheme well established; reuse targets are the main stretch
- **France**: AGEC law set the original templating; PPWR is broadly aligned; QR-code DoC linkage will be French preference
- **Italy**: CONAI EPR plus deposit-return systems for specific streams; PPWR reuse-target alignment is the political fight
- **Netherlands**: leading on plastic deposit-return; influential on circular procurement
- **Sweden/Denmark**: high recycling baselines; pushing for stricter recycled-content thresholds
