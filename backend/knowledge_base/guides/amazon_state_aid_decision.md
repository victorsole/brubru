## Context and Background

Amazon began European retail operations by routing all EU customer contracts through Luxembourg. By 2013, Amazon's European revenues exceeded EUR 13 billion, all booked through LuxOpCo. The structure hinged on two entities: LuxSCS (Amazon Europe Holding Technologies SCS), a Luxembourg limited partnership holding Amazon's European intellectual property, and LuxOpCo (Amazon EU S.à r.l.), the operating company employing the European workforce and running the retail and services business.

LuxSCS was owned by two US entities (Amazon Technologies Inc. and A9.com Inc.) through an intermediate US holding company. Under a Cost Sharing Agreement and Buy-In Agreement with the US entities, LuxSCS held the rights to Amazon's technology, trade names, and customer data in Europe. LuxSCS was transparent for Luxembourg tax purposes (it was not taxed in Luxembourg) and, under a US check-the-box election, treated as a corporation in the United States, where its income benefited from deferral under controlled foreign corporation rules: a classic hybrid mismatch producing effective non-taxation.

The 2003 ATR endorsed a royalty formula under which LuxOpCo paid LuxSCS: the royalty equalled LuxOpCo's actual profits minus a benchmarked routine return set at approximately [4-6]% of LuxOpCo's operating costs (OpEx), subject to a floor of 0.45% and a ceiling of 0.55% of European revenues. As LuxOpCo grew enormously, the royalty grew with it, ensuring that LuxSCS received the surplus while LuxOpCo was left with only a thin margin.

## The Commission's Legal Test

The Commission applied the four-limb Article 107(1) TFEU test:

**State resources:** The ATR caused Luxembourg to forgo tax revenue that would have been due under an arm's-length analysis. Foregone tax revenue constitutes a transfer of State resources.

**Imputability:** The ATR was a unilateral sovereign decision by the Luxembourg tax authority; it was fully imputable to the State.

**Selective advantage:** This was the core issue. The reference system was Luxembourg's general corporate income tax, which incorporates an arm's-length principle (Luxembourg Income Tax Act, Section 56 and Section 164(3)). The Commission found three methodological errors in the ATR, each of which produced a departure from arm's-length pricing:

- **Primary finding:** The ATR wrongly treated LuxSCS as the entrepreneur and LuxOpCo as the routine entity. In economic reality, LuxSCS was a shell: no employees, no real decision-making, no genuine risk management. LuxOpCo performed all value-creating functions: technology development, marketing, warehousing logistics, customer relationships. A correct functional analysis would have benchmarked LuxSCS at a low routine return and left the residual profit with LuxOpCo.

- **Secondary finding 1:** Even under the TNMM applied to LuxOpCo, using operating costs as the profit-level indicator was flawed because LuxOpCo's OpEx excluded the royalty itself; as the royalty rose, the OpEx base fell, self-reinforcing the escalation.

- **Secondary finding 2:** The 0.45% revenue floor meant that in years of strong growth, the royalty was set at a level that left LuxOpCo with less than even the benchmarked return, further compressing taxable income.

**Effect on trade and competition:** LuxOpCo competed with retailers across all EU Member States. The tax saving (approximately EUR 250 million over eight years) strengthened Amazon's competitive position relative to rivals operating on a level tax playing field.

**Compatibility:** No derogation under Article 107(2) or 107(3) TFEU applied. The advantage was not linked to investment, regional development, or services of general economic interest.

---

## The Appeal and Annulment

Both Amazon and Luxembourg challenged the Commission's decision before the General Court. The Court annulled the decision on 12 May 2021. The Court's key findings:

- The Commission failed to demonstrate to the requisite legal standard that the functional analysis of LuxSCS was incorrect. The Court found the Commission could not simply assert that LuxSCS was hollow; it had to establish that the arm's-length range produced by a correct analysis would have resulted in a materially lower royalty.
- The three methodological errors, taken individually or collectively, were not shown to produce a tax reduction relative to the arm's-length outcome that would have applied under Luxembourg tax law.
- The Commission's own alternative calculation (using LuxSCS as the tested party) contained methodological choices the Court found unjustified.

The Commission appealed to the Court of Justice (C-457/21 P). On 14 December 2023, the CJEU dismissed the appeal in full, confirming the annulment and ending the case. Luxembourg was not required to recover any amount. Amazon retained the tax advantage.

## Key Concepts for Chat Answers

**Arm's-length principle:** Intra-group transactions must be priced as they would be between independent parties acting at arm's length. National tax law (Luxembourg ITA Section 56) and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines give this standard legal force.

**Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM):** One of five OECD TP methods; benchmarks the net profit margin of the least complex party (tested party) against comparable independent companies. Correct identification of the tested party is central to the analysis.

**Hybrid mismatch:** A structural difference in how one entity is classified in two jurisdictions (transparent vs. opaque), exploited to avoid tax in both. ATAD 2 (Directive 2017/952/EU) now specifically targets such arrangements.

**Advance tax ruling / advance pricing agreement:** A binding commitment by a national tax authority on how it will tax a specific arrangement. It does not bind the Commission for State aid purposes; the Commission assesses whether the outcome endorsed is below arm's-length.

**LuxSCS:** Luxembourg limited partnership (Societe en commandite simple), no employees, held European IP. Tax-transparent in Luxembourg; treated as a corporation in the United States under check-the-box election.

**LuxOpCo:** Amazon EU S.à r.l., the EU operating entity. Employed the workforce, ran the business, signed contracts with EU customers.

## Compliance Principles (Summary)

1. IP holding entities must have genuine substance: employees, decision-making, real risk management.
2. The tested party under TNMM must be the routine entity; reversing this to place residual profits in a shell is high-risk.
3. PLIs must be internally consistent; excluding the royalty from OpEx while using OpEx as the PLI creates circularity.
4. Revenue collars on royalties require independent economic justification.
5. Buy-in valuations must reflect the IP's fair market value at transfer, including expected future returns.
6. Hybrid mismatches are now neutralised by ATAD 2 in all EU Member States.
7. ATRs do not shield arrangements from State aid review; the Commission assesses the substantive transfer pricing outcome.
8. Long-dated rulings without review clauses magnify exposure.
9. CbCR (DAC4) means high-profit, low-function jurisdictions are systematically risk-flagged by tax authorities.
10. BEPS Actions 8-10 require profit allocation to align with the location of actual value creation.
