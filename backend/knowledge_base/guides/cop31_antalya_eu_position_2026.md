# COP31 in Antalya (9-20 November 2026): arrangements and the EU's stated position

## QUICK FACTS
- **Parliament's side, adopted Thursday 22 October 2026: resolution 2026/2666(RSP), P10_TA(2026)0367, adopted by show of hands (no roll-call tally).** Parliament's mandate: **speed up the global exit from fossil fuels** (advance the COP30 roadmap on transitioning away from fossil fuels; EU to lead towards a fossil-fuel non-proliferation treaty; time-bound plans to phase out fossil-fuel subsidies and production, with the EU setting its own economy-wide timeline); **urgently increase climate finance and make it fairer** for vulnerable countries (the EU and its Member States remain the largest public providers; the gap is growing); **deliver on adaptation and loss-and-damage pledges**. Facts MEPs cite: 2025 among the hottest years on record; Europe the fastest-warming continent; the EU on track for a 54% cut by 2030 if Member States deliver existing plans, while preliminary Eurostat figures show emissions rose 1% between 2024 and 2025. The Council conclusions of 12 October remain the Member States' negotiating position; Parliament's resolution is a separate text. Source: EP press release of 22 October 2026 (20261018IPR48264); the resolution text was not yet available in English when written.
- **COP17 (UN Biodiversity Conference), Yerevan, Armenia, 19 to 30 October 2026: the Council's conclusions of 12 October set the EU position.** The EU will insist that there can be **no lowering of the ambition** of the 2022 global biodiversity framework (including the pledge to protect at least **30% of land and sea by 2030**), call for accelerated action by mainstreaming biodiversity across policies and sectors on solid scientific evidence, and for more public and private funding, including **high-integrity nature credits** (nature credits, NOT carbon credits). Minister of State Christopher O'Sullivan spoke for the Irish presidency.
- **COP31 (Antalya, 9 to 20 November 2026): the Council's conclusions of 12 October (press release, 16:03) set the EU's main negotiating position.** The Council acknowledges that the Paris Agreement's **1.5 degree goal is likely to be exceeded**, calls for urgent action to return to the 1.5 degree trajectory and names the **"overshoot-peak-decline" pathway** as the best remaining option; calls for **effective implementation of nationally determined contributions (NDCs)** and the **urgent submission of missing NDCs**; stresses delivery on the outcomes of the **first global stocktake (GST-1)**, including the **transition away from fossil fuels** and faster clean energy with clean electrification, and wants space in the negotiations for the direction of the **second global stocktake (GST-2)**; treats **climate resilience** as complementary to mitigation; and stresses that **all Member States should take part in COP31** in solidarity. Irish Minister Darragh O'Brien spoke for the presidency.
- **Three sets of Council conclusions in one week:** COP31 and COP17 on **12 October 2026**, and **climate finance on 9 October 2026**, which complements the general COP31 position. The EU and its Member States are parties to both conventions; the rotating presidency and the Commission represent the EU. The press release does not name the Council configuration; the conclusions' own text was not read in full (the summary above is the press release's).
- **When and where (UNFCCC):** the UN Climate Change Conference **COP31** is held in **Antalya, Türkiye, from 9 to 20 November 2026**. **Murat Kurum** (Türkiye) is the President of COP31; **Chris Bowen of Australia is the President of Negotiations**, leading the negotiations for the duration of the conference under the **Türkiye-Australia partnership modalities** (UNFCCC provisional agenda, document CP/2026/1). Türkiye hosts; Australia leads the negotiating room.
- **Pre-COP31 was held in the Pacific (Fiji, with Tuvalu and Australia), opening on 5 October 2026.** The EU was represented by **Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra**, who also visited **Tuvalu**. His three speeches of 7 October are published by the Commission (SPEECH/26/2085, SPEECH/26/2099, SPEECH/26/2101).
- **The EU's four priorities for COP31 (Hoekstra, SPEECH/26/2099, 7 October):** the EU calls COP31 "a COP of implementation". (1) **Close the ambition gap and stay as close as possible to 1.5 degrees:** all Parties should submit their **NDCs** and align them to a 1.5 degree pathway, and COP31 should showcase "demonstrable and scalable solutions on the ground". (2) **Means of implementation:** deliver on and defend agreed outcomes such as the **New Collective Quantified Goal on climate finance**, and ease access to finance for developing countries. (3) **Initiate a process to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of the UNFCCC regime**, to show the Paris Agreement is fit for purpose. (4) Keep **science** at the base of decisions. He called on the other **G20 members**, which with the EU account for 80% of global emissions, to cut emissions and mobilise finance.
- **Overshoot and electrification:** Hoekstra cited the **UNEP report on Limiting Overshoot** (September 2026), according to which the world is expected to cross 1.5 degrees of warming "in the next few years", and said the EU's goal of "staying as close as possible to 1.5 degrees and getting back below 1.5 remains non-negotiable" (SPEECH/26/2085). He asked for action on "clean electrification in Antalya". In Europe, he said, summer 2026 was the hottest on record "and in all likelihood the coolest of the decade we have ahead of us".
- **Energy-transition figures and EU money mentioned for the Pacific (Hoekstra, SPEECH/26/2101):** Pacific countries spend **10 to 25% of GDP on fossil fuel imports**; Europe imports **more than 80% of its gas and 95% of its oil**. The **EU-funded ElectriFI** facility approved a **EUR 4.4 million development loan for a 15 MW geothermal project on Vanua Levu, Fiji** (its first investment in the Pacific); a **EUR 25 million Global Gateway Grant-based Infrastructure Facility for Pacific Island Countries** is due to become operational in **2027**; the **EUR 15 million Pacific Regional Electrification Programme** supports smaller and more vulnerable countries.
- **Reported by the press, NOT an official statement (Climate Home News, 7 October):** Hoekstra told journalists in Fiji that the Commission had asked the UN and the COP31 organisers to give clarity on the summit's agenda and "what success would look like", and welcomed the organisers' push for electrification, saying the main effort should focus on renewables while nuclear could also be part of the solution.

## FAQ
**When is COP31 and who chairs it?** 9 to 20 November 2026 in Antalya. Murat Kurum is President of COP31; Chris Bowen of Australia leads the negotiations as President of Negotiations.
**What does the EU want from COP31?** Per Commissioner Hoekstra on 7 October: an implementation COP that closes the ambition gap (1.5 degrees, NDCs aligned to it), protects the climate-finance goal agreed at COP29, starts a process to make the UNFCCC regime more efficient and effective, and backs clean electrification.
**Has the EU agreed its mandate?** No Council conclusions on COP31 were found in the sources read on 8 October 2026, so the mandate should be described as not yet published.
