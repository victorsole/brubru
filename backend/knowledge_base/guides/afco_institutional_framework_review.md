## Overview

AFCO is the EP's committee responsible for the EU's **constitutional and institutional architecture**: treaty reform, EP internal rules, electoral law, transparency rules, EP relations with national parliaments, and the balance between the EU institutions and the Member States. Its work is typically own-initiative (INI) because treaty-level change requires IGC processes rather than ordinary legislative procedure.

The April 2026 trio of AFCO files signals a concerted AFCO review of how Union law is enforced against Member States where national authorities (governments, courts, independent regulators) are alleged to obstruct or misapply it. Article 19 TEU is the anchor because it obliges national courts to provide remedies sufficient to ensure effective judicial protection in the fields covered by Union law. Recent CJEU jurisprudence (C-64/16 Associação Sindical dos Juízes Portugueses; C-619/18 Commission v Poland; C-558/18 + C-563/18 Miasto Łowicz; C-896/19 Repubblika) has developed Article 19 TEU into the EU's main tool for scrutinising Member State judicial independence.

## Why This File Matters

### Political timing
- Hungary's 12 April 2026 election (Orbán defeated) reshuffles the rule-of-law conversation: the incoming Magyar government is signalling readiness to restore judicial independence; the Commission opened technical meetings with Magyar's team on 19 April 2026 on the EUR 19B frozen-funds file. See `hungary_election_2026_magyar.md`.
- Spain's amnesty law, Romania's judicial reform debates, and Slovakia's criminal code changes all face Article 19 TEU scrutiny.
- The Conditionality Regulation (2020/2092) and RRF horizontal enabling conditions now function as Article 19 enforcement tools alongside Article 7 TEU and infringement proceedings.

### Institutional design
- AFCO is asking: does the current mix (infringement by Commission + preliminary references by national courts + Article 7 by Council + Conditionality by Commission/Council + RRF super-milestones by Commission) deliver consistent, timely, effective enforcement? Or does it generate fragmentation, selectivity, and political paralysis?

### Stakes
- The answer drives subsequent legislative proposals: a potential "rule-of-law regulation" with binding pre-emptive mechanisms; reforms to the Conditionality Regulation scope; proposals to accelerate Article 258 TFEU procedures; EP calls for CJEU preliminary-reference training for national judges.

## Key Legal Anchors

- **Article 19(1) TEU**: "Member States shall provide remedies sufficient to ensure effective legal protection in the fields covered by Union law."
- **Article 2 TEU**: Union values (human dignity, freedom, democracy, equality, rule of law, human rights, rights of minorities)
- **Article 4(3) TEU**: Principle of sincere cooperation
- **Article 7 TEU**: Political sanction mechanism for serious and persistent breach of Article 2 values
- **Articles 258-260 TFEU**: Infringement procedure (Commission reasoned opinion → CJEU → lump-sum / penalty payment)
- **Article 267 TFEU**: Preliminary reference mechanism
- **Conditionality Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2092** (CELEX: 32020R2092): protection of Union budget against breaches of rule-of-law principles
