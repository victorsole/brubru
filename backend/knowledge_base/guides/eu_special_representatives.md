# EU Special Representatives (EUSRs)

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: Article 33 TEU (appointment by Council on HR/VP proposal)
- Appointed by: Council of the EU (Council Decision, CFSP)
- Report to: High Representative / Vice-President (HR/VP) Kaja Kallas
- Number of EUSRs: 11 (as of September 2025)
- Responsible service: European External Action Service (EEAS)
- Budget: Funded from CFSP budget line
- Mandate: Time-limited, renewable by Council Decision

## Overview

EU Special Representatives (EUSRs) are senior diplomats appointed by the Council to advance EU foreign and security policy in specific regions or countries. They serve as the political "voice" and "face" of the EU in their assigned areas, promoting peace, stability, and the rule of law. EUSRs support the work of the High Representative and operate under EEAS coordination.

EUSRs are a key instrument of the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP). Their mandates are established and renewed through Council Decisions under Article 33 TEU. Each EUSR has a specific mandate defining their objectives, scope, and reporting obligations.

## Kosovo: EU Engagement

The EU's engagement in Kosovo operates through multiple channels:

- **EUSR in Kosovo / Head of EU Office**: Ambassador Aivo Orav (Estonia). Provides political advice, ensures coherent EU action, promotes human rights, enhances EU visibility
- **EULEX Kosovo**: EU Rule of Law Mission (CSDP mission), established by Council Joint Action 2008/124/CFSP. Monitors, mentors, and advises Kosovo institutions on rule of law, judiciary, police, customs
- **Belgrade-Pristina Dialogue**: EU-facilitated normalisation dialogue. EUSR Peter Sorensen leads
- **Stabilisation and Association Agreement (SAA)**: In force since April 2016. Provides framework for political and economic reforms
- **European Reform Agenda (ERA)**: Supports Kosovo's reform priorities

Kosovo is a potential candidate for EU membership. The European Commission publishes annual enlargement reports assessing progress.

## EUSR Mandate Structure

Each EUSR mandate typically covers:
1. **Policy objectives**: promoting EU interests and values in the region
2. **Coordination**: ensuring consistency between EU instruments (CSDP missions, development aid, diplomacy)
3. **Reporting**: regular reports to HR/VP and Council working groups (e.g., COWEB for Western Balkans)
4. **Engagement**: dialogue with local authorities, civil society, international partners
5. **Duration**: typically 12-24 months, renewable

## Relevance for Advocacy

EUSRs are key interlocutors for organisations working on conflict resolution, human rights, enlargement, and regional stability. They are accessible through EEAS and often engage with civil society. For Kosovo specifically, the Belgrade-Pristina dialogue and EULEX mission create multiple entry points for policy influence on rule of law, judicial reform, and EU integration processes.
