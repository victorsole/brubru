# NDICI-Global Europe: the EU's External-Action Funding Instrument 2021-2027

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument, Global Europe (NDICI-Global Europe)
- **Legal base:** Regulation (EU) 2021/947 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 9 June 2021
- **CELEX:** 32021R0947
- **URL (EUR-Lex):** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32021R0947
- **URL (DG INTPA landing page):** https://international-partnerships.ec.europa.eu/funding-and-technical-assistance/funding-instruments/global-europe-neighbourhood-development-and-international-cooperation-instrument_en
- **Total envelope:** EUR 79.5 billion (2021-2027), the largest external-action instrument in the EU budget
- **Three pillars plus a reserve:**
  - **Geographic Programmes -- EUR 60.38 billion**, broken down as: Neighbourhood (East + South) at least EUR 19.32 billion; Sub-Saharan Africa at least EUR 29.18 billion; Asia and the Pacific EUR 8.48 billion; Americas and the Caribbean EUR 3.39 billion
  - **Thematic Programmes -- EUR 6.36 billion**, covering Human Rights and Democracy; Civil Society Organisations; Peace, Stability and Conflict Prevention; and Global Challenges (health, education, women/girls, migration, environment, climate, digital)
  - **Rapid Response Actions -- EUR 3.18 billion**, for crisis response, conflict prevention and resilience-building, deployable faster than the multi-annual programmes
  - **The Cushion (unallocated reserve) -- EUR 9.53 billion**, held back from the above totals and released to any pillar or country to respond to unforeseen circumstances, new priorities, or emerging challenges without a full budget re-negotiation
- **EFSD+ (European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus):** the blended-finance and guarantee architecture embedded inside NDICI. Unifies grants, blending and guarantees to mobilise public and private investment. Backed by the **External Action Guarantee (EAG)**, up to **EUR 53.4 billion**, which also covers guarantee operations in EU pre-accession (candidate) countries. Financial engine behind the **Global Gateway** EUR 300 billion mobilisation target.
- **Spending targets:** at least 30% of the overall envelope to climate objectives (biodiversity spending ramping to 7.5% in 2024 and 10% in 2026-2027); indicatively 10% of the envelope to migration and forced-displacement management; at least 93% of funding must meet Official Development Assistance (ODA) criteria; at least 20% to social inclusion and human development
- **Predecessors replaced (roughly ten prior instruments folded into one):** Development Cooperation Instrument (DCI); European Neighbourhood Instrument (ENI); the 11th European Development Fund (EDF) -- the first time EDF resources were brought on-budget rather than run through a separate intergovernmental fund; Instrument contributing to Stability and Peace (IcSP); European Instrument for Democracy and Human Rights (EIDHR); Partnership Instrument (PI); the original European Fund for Sustainable Development (EFSD) Regulation (EU) 2017/1601; the EU External Action Guarantee Fund, Council Regulation (EC, Euratom) No 480/2009; the EIB External Lending Mandate guarantee, Decision No 466/2014/EU; and the Instrument for Greenland/Overseas Countries and Territories cooperation. IPA (pre-accession) and humanitarian aid (DG ECHO) were deliberately kept OUTSIDE NDICI as separate instruments.
- **Governance:** programmed jointly by the European Commission (College) and the European External Action Service (EEAS), following the "lead role" split set out in the regulation -- EEAS leads on political/strategic direction for geographic programmes, the Commission (mainly DG INTPA, DG ENEST, DG MENA and the Service for Foreign Policy Instruments, FPI) leads on implementation
- **Programming instrument:** Multi-annual Indicative Programmes (MIPs), typically covering 2021-2024 and 2025-2027, per country/region and per thematic programme
- **Delivery approach:** "Team Europe" -- EU institutions, Member States and their development finance institutions (DFIs) coordinate around joint Team Europe Initiatives (TEIs) rather than each running parallel bilateral programmes
- **Responsible services:** DG INTPA (Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America-Caribbean, OCTs), DG ENEST (Eastern Neighbourhood, Western Balkans enlargement candidates via IPA III separately), DG MENA (Southern Neighbourhood, Gulf), FPI (Common Foreign and Security Policy-adjacent rapid response and thematic actions), EEAS (strategic/political steer)
- **EP lead committees:** AFET (Foreign Affairs) and DEVE (Development), often jointly; budgetary oversight via BUDG
- **Council configuration:** Foreign Affairs Council, Development configuration (FAC Dev)
- **Sister instrument:** IPA III (Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance), Regulation (EU) 2021/1529, EUR 14.2 billion, which covers EU candidate and potential-candidate countries and is explicitly excluded from NDICI's geographic scope
- **Efficiency reform:** Regulation (EU) 2026/995 of 29 April 2026 amended Regulation (EU) 2021/947 to streamline External Action Guarantee governance (faster guarantee-allocation decisions to implementing partners such as the EIB and EBRD, simplified reporting, clearer Commission-EEAS-implementing-partner accountability split) -- the most substantive NDICI change since the 2021 launch

## What NDICI-Global Europe Is

NDICI-Global Europe is the European Union's central financing instrument for external action outside the enlargement (pre-accession) and humanitarian domains. Adopted as Regulation (EU) 2021/947 on 9 June 2021 and applied retroactively from 1 January 2021, it consolidates almost all of the EU's non-humanitarian, non-enlargement external spending into a single legal and budgetary framework for the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF).

Before NDICI, EU external financing was fragmented across roughly ten separate legal instruments, each with its own regulation, committee, and reporting line: the Development Cooperation Instrument (DCI) for developing countries generally; the European Neighbourhood Instrument (ENI) for the Eastern and Southern Neighbourhood; the 11th European Development Fund (EDF) for African, Caribbean and Pacific (ACP) states, historically kept off the EU budget as an intergovernmental fund; the Instrument contributing to Stability and Peace (IcSP) for conflict prevention and crisis response; the European Instrument for Democracy and Human Rights (EIDHR); the Partnership Instrument (PI) for strategic partnerships with advanced economies; the original European Fund for Sustainable Development (EFSD) Regulation; the EU External Action Guarantee Fund; the EIB External Lending Mandate guarantee Decision; and cooperation funding for Greenland and other Overseas Countries and Territories. NDICI folded all of these into one instrument, one guarantee architecture, and (critically) brought the EDF onto the EU budget for the first time, ending the parachute of separate intergovernmental contribution keys that had made ACP funding harder to coordinate with the rest of EU external action.

Two instruments were deliberately kept outside NDICI: the Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance (IPA III), which continues to fund EU candidate and potential-candidate countries under its own Regulation (EU) 2021/1529, and humanitarian aid, which remains under DG ECHO's separate Humanitarian Aid Instrument. This split matters for Brubru users tracking a specific country: Western Balkans and Turkey funding flows through IPA III, not NDICI; a natural-disaster relief operation flows through DG ECHO, not NDICI.

## The Three Pillars and the Cushion

NDICI's EUR 79.5 billion is allocated across three structured pillars plus one flexible reserve:

1. **Geographic Programmes (EUR 60.38 billion)** are the largest pillar, split into four regional envelopes with legally guaranteed minimum floors: Neighbourhood (East and South) at least EUR 19.32 billion; Sub-Saharan Africa at least EUR 29.18 billion; Asia and the Pacific EUR 8.48 billion; and Americas and the Caribbean EUR 3.39 billion. These minimums are set in the regulation's annex and cannot be quietly reallocated away without a formal procedure.

2. **Thematic Programmes (EUR 6.36 billion)** are not country-specific. They fund cross-cutting priorities regardless of geography: Human Rights and Democracy; Civil Society Organisations; Peace, Stability and Conflict Prevention; and Global Challenges (a broad basket covering health, education, gender equality, migration, environment and climate, and digital connectivity).

3. **Rapid Response Actions (EUR 3.18 billion)** are designed for speed rather than scale: crisis response, conflict prevention, resilience-building and early recovery, deployable on shorter timelines than the multi-annual geographic and thematic envelopes.

4. **The Cushion (EUR 9.53 billion)**, formally an "unallocated reserve," sits outside the three pillars above. It exists precisely so the Commission and EEAS do not need to renegotiate the entire MFF envelope every time an unforeseen crisis (a war, a coup, a sudden migration surge, a new geopolitical priority) demands extra money in a specific country or theme. Cushion releases are one of the more useful "early signal" data points for Brubru users tracking where EU external-action priorities are shifting mid-cycle, since a Cushion allocation typically precedes a formal MIP revision.

## EFSD+ Inside NDICI

The European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (EFSD+) is not a separate pot of money but the blended-finance and guarantee mechanism embedded inside NDICI (and, since the 2026 reform, explicitly extended to cover EIB/EBRD guarantee operations in IPA III candidate countries too). EFSD+ unifies three financial tools -- grants, blending (combining EU grants with loans from development banks), and guarantees -- to de-risk and mobilise public and private investment in partner countries. It is backed by the External Action Guarantee (EAG), capped at EUR 53.4 billion, which covers losses that implementing partners (principally the EIB and the EBRD, alongside national development finance institutions) might incur on guaranteed operations.

EFSD+/EAG is the principal financial machinery behind the Global Gateway strategy's EUR 300 billion mobilisation target: Global Gateway itself is not a funding instrument but a political branding and coordination strategy, and NDICI/EFSD+ is where the money actually sits. Regulation (EU) 2026/995 (29 April 2026, OJ L 4 May 2026) reformed EAG governance to speed up guarantee-allocation decisions, simplify reporting obligations, and clarify the accountability split between the Commission, EEAS and implementing partners -- the most substantive change to NDICI since its 2021 launch, expected to shorten the pipeline for Global Gateway-aligned investment proposals once implementing acts land (expected Q3 2026).

## Geographic vs Thematic vs Rapid-Response Programming

Programming under NDICI follows two tracks that Brubru users should keep distinct:

- **Geographic programming** is bottom-up and country/region-specific: each region and, within it, each country or sub-region gets its own Multi-annual Indicative Programme setting out priority sectors, indicative allocations, and expected results, negotiated with partner governments and coordinated with Member State bilateral programmes via Team Europe.
- **Thematic programming** is top-down and horizontal: a Human Rights and Democracy action, for example, can fund a civil-society grant in any eligible country worldwide, independent of that country's geographic MIP.
- **Rapid Response Actions** bypass the multi-annual programming cycle entirely. They are activated through simplified decision-making so the EU can react to a coup, a sudden ceasefire-monitoring need, or an acute stabilisation gap within weeks rather than the year-plus lead time a full MIP revision would require.

## Multi-annual Indicative Programmes (MIPs)

MIPs are the operational backbone of NDICI. Each geographic and thematic programme has its own MIP, adopted by Commission implementing decision after consultation with the EEAS, Member States (via the NDICI Committee, a comitology body) and the European Parliament (via a structured political dialogue, not a formal veto). The current generation of MIPs runs 2021-2024 with a second-generation revision for 2025-2027, allowing priorities to be recalibrated roughly at the MFF's mid-point. MIPs set indicative financial allocations by sector and are the document Brubru users should consult to answer "how much NDICI money is earmarked for country/sector X" -- the regulation itself only sets the top-line envelopes above.

## Team Europe

"Team Europe" is the coordination doctrine under which NDICI operates in practice: rather than the Commission, EEAS, individual Member States and their bilateral development agencies (AFD, GIZ, AECID, Team Europe partners' DFIs) running parallel, uncoordinated programmes in the same country, Team Europe Initiatives (TEIs) pool EU-budget NDICI resources with Member State bilateral contributions and DFI financing around a single joint result framework. Team Europe is the delivery vehicle for both NDICI geographic programmes and Global Gateway investments, and its performance is a recurring theme in EP scrutiny of external-action effectiveness (see `global_gateway_strategy.md` for the EP's 2025/2073(INI) assessment of visibility and coordination challenges).

## Spending Targets

NDICI carries several binding or indicative spending floors written into the regulation:
- **Climate:** at least 30% of the overall envelope must contribute to climate objectives, with a biodiversity sub-target ramping from 7.5% (2024) to 10% (2026-2027)
- **Migration:** indicatively 10% of the envelope should support migration and forced-displacement management and root-cause responses
- **ODA eligibility:** at least 93% of NDICI funding must qualify as Official Development Assistance under OECD DAC criteria
- **Social inclusion and human development:** at least 20% of funding

These targets are tracked cumulatively across the 2021-2027 period, not required in every single annual budget, giving programming some year-to-year flexibility while holding the overall instrument accountable at the mid-term and final review.

## Comparison with IPA III

NDICI and IPA III (Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance, Regulation (EU) 2021/1529, EUR 14.2 billion) are structurally similar (both are MFF 2021-2027 external instruments with geographic/thematic logic, both use MIPs, both sit under the same broader "Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation" budget heading) but are legally and politically distinct. IPA III funds only EU candidate and potential-candidate countries (Western Balkans, Turkey) on an accession trajectory, with conditionality tied to accession-negotiation chapters and rule-of-law benchmarks. NDICI covers everyone else -- the Neighbourhood (East and South, which is NOT the same as enlargement), Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia-Pacific and the Americas-Caribbean -- without an accession conditionality logic. A Brubru user asking about Ukraine, Moldova or Georgia pre-candidacy support should be pointed to NDICI's Neighbourhood East envelope or the dedicated Ukraine Facility (a separate MFF instrument); once a country becomes an official candidate, its EU funding progressively migrates toward IPA III.

## Brubru Tracking Angles

- **Cushion releases** are an early signal of where EU external-action priorities are shifting mid-cycle, often preceding a formal MIP revision by months.
- **MIP revisions (2025-2027 generation)** show where regional/country allocations are being recalibrated against the original 2021-2024 baseline.
- **EFSD+/EAG guarantee decisions** (especially post-Regulation (EU) 2026/995) are the leading indicator for which Global Gateway investment proposals are about to move from pipeline to signed operation.
- **Team Europe Initiative launches** show where Member State bilateral programmes and EU-budget NDICI money are being pooled, useful for stakeholder mapping of national development agencies alongside the Commission.
- **EP scrutiny** of NDICI runs mainly through AFET/DEVE joint committee work and the annual implementation report debates, distinct from the Global Gateway-specific INI report tracked in `global_gateway_strategy.md`.
