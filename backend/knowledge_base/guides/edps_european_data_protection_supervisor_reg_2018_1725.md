# EDPS: European Data Protection Supervisor and the EU institutions data-protection regulation

## QUICK FACTS
- Body: European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) -- independent supervisory authority for the EU's own institutions, bodies, offices and agencies (EUIBAs)
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 (the "EUDPR") -- the GDPR-equivalent regime for EU institutions, replacing Regulation (EC) 45/2001; adopted 23 October 2018, applicable from 11 December 2018
- Seat: Brussels. Structure: one Supervisor (single-person authority, not a college) plus a Secretariat of specialised staff
- Current Supervisor: Wojciech Wiewiorowski, in post since 6 December 2019. His five-year term expired in December 2024; he has continued in office pending appointment of a successor. Parliament's LIBE committee has recommended Bruno Gencarelli; EU member states have favoured reappointing Wiewiorowski; both institutions heard four shortlisted candidates again in early 2026 without reaching a joint decision as of this guide. Appointment requires joint agreement of the European Parliament and the Council for a five-year term, renewable once.
- Three core functions: **Supervisor** (monitors and enforces EUIBAs' compliance, Article 58 powers), **Advisor** (Commission must consult the EDPS under Article 42(1) within 8 weeks on legislative proposals, delegated/implementing acts and recommendations with data-protection impact), **Cooperator** (provides the Secretariat to the European Data Protection Board -- EDPB -- under Article 75, and co-supervises EU large-scale IT systems)
- Enforcement powers (Article 58): investigative (audits, access to premises, data and equipment), corrective (warnings, reprimands, compliance orders, processing bans, erasure orders, administrative fines on EU institutions), plus authorisation and advisory powers
- Large-scale IT systems co-supervised with national data protection authorities via the Coordinated Supervision Committee (Article 62): Europol, Eurojust, EPPO, IMI, and (as they roll out) SIS, VIS, Eurodac, EES, ETIAS, ECRIS-TCN, CIS and their interoperability components
- AI Act role: the EDPS is the designated market surveillance authority under Article 74(9) of the AI Act for EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies that deploy AI systems, with power to investigate and fine EUIBAs for AI Act breaches
- Technology monitoring: TechDispatch (technology briefings), TechSonar (foresight trend reports) and IPEN, the Internet Privacy Engineering Network (founded 2014, brings together privacy engineers from public authorities, industry, academia and civil society)
- Not to be confused with: the EDPB (the board of all 27 national DPAs plus the EDPS, ensuring consistent GDPR application across the EU) or national DPAs (supervise private and public-sector processing inside member states under GDPR). The EDPS supervises only the EU's own institutions, bodies, offices and agencies.

The EDPS is the EU's internal data-protection watchdog: it does for the Commission, Parliament, Council, agencies and other EU bodies what national data protection authorities do for governments and companies inside member states. It is also, increasingly, a binding actor beyond classic data protection: secretariat to the EDPB, co-supervisor of major EU databases (SIS, VIS, Eurodac, Europol systems), and market surveillance authority for AI deployed by EU institutions themselves.

## Legal basis and mandate

**Regulation (EU) 2018/1725** of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 23 October 2018, on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data by the Union institutions, bodies, offices and agencies and on the free movement of such data (the EUDPR), repealed Regulation (EC) 45/2001. It mirrors the GDPR's structure and principles (lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, accountability) but applies specifically to EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies (EUIBAs) rather than to member-state public authorities or private companies.

EUIBAs covered include the European Commission, the European Parliament, the Council, the Court of Justice (administrative processing only, not judicial functions), the European Central Bank (for administrative processing), the Court of Auditors, and the full range of EU decentralised agencies (Europol, EMA, EFSA, ECHA, Frontex, eu-LISA, EIGE, EUAA, Cedefop, and others).

## Structure

The EDPS is a single-person independent authority -- unlike the College-based European Commission or the board-based EDPB -- supported by a Secretariat organised into units covering supervision, policy and consultation, technology and privacy, IT and communications, and administration.

**Appointment**: the Supervisor is appointed by joint decision of the European Parliament and the Council, following a Commission-drawn shortlist and hearings before Parliament's Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs (LIBE). Term: five years, renewable once. A separate **Assistant Supervisor** role existed until 2019 but was discontinued; the current structure is a single Supervisor.

## Three core functions

| Function | What it means | Key article |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| Supervisor | Monitors and enforces EUIBAs' compliance with the EUDPR: audits, investigations, complaint-handling, corrective measures, administrative fines | Article 58 |
| Advisor | Commission must consult the EDPS on legislative/delegated/implementing proposals and recommendations with data-protection impact; response due within 8 weeks; issues own-initiative and joint EDPS-EDPB opinions | Article 42 |
| Cooperator | Provides analytical, administrative and logistical Secretariat to the EDPB; coordinates supervision of EU large-scale IT systems with national DPAs via the Coordinated Supervision Committee | Articles 62, 75 |

### Supervisory powers (Article 58)

**Investigative powers**: order controllers/processors to provide information; carry out data-protection audits; notify alleged infringements; access all personal data and information needed for its tasks; access premises, including data-processing equipment.

**Corrective powers**: issue warnings and reprimands; order compliance with a data subject's request; order bringing processing operations into compliance; impose a temporary or permanent limitation, including a ban, on processing; order rectification, restriction or erasure of data; impose administrative fines on EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies.

**Authorisation and advisory powers**: issue opinions on prior consultations; authorise contractual clauses and administrative arrangements for international transfers; approve binding corporate rules; adopt standard data-protection clauses.

### Advisory function and joint opinions with the EDPB

Article 42(1) requires the Commission to consult the EDPS after adopting a proposal for a legislative act, a recommendation, or a proposal to the Council, and when preparing delegated or implementing acts, whenever they affect the protection of individuals' rights and freedoms in relation to personal-data processing. The EDPS must respond within 8 weeks. Where a Commission proposal is of particular importance for data protection, the Commission may also consult the EDPB (Article 42(2)), producing a **joint EDPS-EDPB opinion**. Recent joint opinions include those on the Cybersecurity Act, the AI Act, the European Biotech Act, and the 2026 Digital Omnibus package.

## Large-scale IT systems and agency co-supervision

The EDPS does not act alone on the EU's biggest data infrastructure. Together with national data protection authorities, it sits on the **Coordinated Supervision Committee (CSC)**, established under Article 62 of the EUDPR, which ensures joined-up supervision where an EU-level system draws on both EU-body processing and national competent-authority processing. National DPAs supervise the national end (police, border and administrative authorities feeding the system); the EDPS supervises the EU-body end (Europol, Eurojust, EPPO, eu-LISA, the European Commission where relevant).

Systems and bodies under this coordinated model include: **Europol**, **Eurojust**, the **European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO)**, the **Internal Market Information System (IMI)**, and -- as national connections complete -- the **Schengen Information System (SIS)**, the **Visa Information System (VIS)**, **Eurodac**, the **Entry/Exit System (EES)**, the **European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS)**, the **European Criminal Records Information System for third-country nationals (ECRIS-TCN)**, the **Customs Information System (CIS)**, and their interoperability components.

The EDPS also directly supervises agencies with operational data-processing mandates, notably **Frontex** (the European Border and Coast Guard Agency), issuing opinions, conducting on-site visits and, where necessary, formal reprimands -- for example over Frontex's 2022 move to cloud infrastructure without an adequate data-protection risk assessment, and a further reprimand over non-compliant data transmission to Europol.

## AI Act role

Where an EU institution, body, office or agency deploys an AI system falling within the AI Act's scope, the **EDPS acts as its market surveillance authority** under Article 74(9) of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), mirroring the role that national market surveillance authorities and data protection authorities play for AI systems used by member-state public bodies and private companies. This gives the EDPS investigative and fining powers specifically over EU institutions' own AI deployments -- an unusual instance of an EU body holding binding enforcement power over its sister institutions.

## Technology monitoring

- **TechDispatch**: factual briefings on individual emerging technologies, with a preliminary privacy assessment against GDPR/EUDPR principles.
- **TechSonar**: annual foresight report on technology trends relevant to privacy (recent editions have covered synthetic data, central bank digital currencies, biometric continuous authentication).
- **IPEN (Internet Privacy Engineering Network)**: founded by the EDPS in 2014, brings together privacy engineers from public authorities, industry, academia and civil society to advance the state of the art in privacy-by-design engineering.

## EDPS vs EDPB vs national DPAs

| Body | Supervises | Governing instrument | Composition |
|------|------------|----------------------|-------------|
| EDPS | EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies only | Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 | Single Supervisor + Secretariat |
| EDPB | Ensures consistent GDPR/e-Privacy application EU-wide; issues guidelines, binding decisions in cross-border disputes | GDPR Chapter VII (Articles 68-76) | Board of all national DPA heads + the EDPS; EDPS provides the Secretariat |
| National DPAs | Private-sector and national/regional public-sector processing inside each member state | GDPR (national implementing law) | One or more per member state (e.g. CNIL in France, AEPD in Spain, Garante in Italy) |

## Engaging with the EDPS

- **Prior consultation**: EUIBAs planning high-risk processing operations may be required to consult the EDPS before starting (Article 40).
- **Complaints**: individuals can lodge a complaint with the EDPS if they believe an EU institution has mishandled their personal data.
- **Public consultations and opinions**: the EDPS publishes opinions on Commission proposals; these are public documents useful for tracking the data-protection reading of a file before it reaches Parliament and Council.
- **Annual Report**: published each spring, sets out enforcement actions, opinions issued and Coordinated Supervision Committee activity for the previous year.

## Why this matters for EU policy professionals

Any organisation dealing with an EU institution, agency or large-scale EU database (visa processing, border data, competition investigations, state-aid filings, EMA submissions) is subject to EUDPR rather than GDPR for that specific interaction. The EDPS's opinions on Commission proposals are also an early, technical signal of how a file's data-protection provisions may evolve before Parliament and Council amend them -- useful for advocacy teams tracking legislation with a data or AI dimension.
