# Common European Defence Union: Institutional Design

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Institutional aspects of a Common European Defence Union (CSDP evolution, Article 42 TEU, integration options)
- Legal basis: Article 42 TEU (progressive framing of a common Union defence policy), Article 42(7) TEU (mutual assistance clause), Article 222 TFEU (solidarity clause)
- PESCO (Permanent Structured Cooperation): Article 46 TEU + Protocol No 10 + Council Decision (CFSP) 2017/2315 -- 26 Member States participate (excluding Malta since initial launch)
- White Paper on European Defence (2025): Commission framework paper on defence readiness and industrial preparedness
- ReArm Europe / SAFE: Regulation (EU) 2025/... establishing EUR 150 billion Security Action for Europe loan instrument (adopted 2025)
- European Defence Industry Programme (EDIP): Proposal COM(2024) 150 -- successor to EDF and ASAP
- Strategic Compass: Adopted March 2022 (Council), roadmap for EU security and defence to 2030
- Commissioner for Defence: Andrius Kubilius (Defence and Space portfolio, created November 2024)
- HR/VP: Kaja Kallas
- EP committee: SEDE (Subcommittee on Security and Defence), AFET lead
- AFCO own-initiative report: 2025/2212(INI) "Institutional aspects of the Common European Defence Union" -- rapporteur Salvatore De Meo (EPP, Italy), appointed 13 January 2026; draft report not yet tabled in OEIL
- Previous EP resolutions: many on European Defence Union, including 2023 Bilcik report on common security and defence policy
- Treaty change vs. Treaty-base creativity: AFCO report expected to map what can be done under current Treaties (permanent structured cooperation, enhanced cooperation, supranationalisation of procurement) vs. what would require Treaty revision (common army, Article 42 activation)
- Informal Leaders' meeting Cyprus 23-24 April 2026: defence likely on the agenda alongside competitiveness and enlargement
- Defence white paper follow-up: Commission expected to propose further defence instruments during 2026 under Kubilius portfolio

## Why This Report Matters

Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine (February 2022), the political appetite for deeper defence integration has grown, but the legal and institutional architecture remains highly fragmented. Defence decisions in the Council require unanimity; Commission competence is limited to the internal market dimension (industrial policy, procurement, dual-use goods); and most capability decisions remain intergovernmental through EDA, PESCO, or coalitions of the willing.

The AFCO INI report 2025/2212(INI) will likely assess:
- The scope of Article 42 TEU and the path to a "common Union defence"
- Role of the Commission (DG DEFIS) vs. EEAS vs. EDA
- Options for qualified majority voting in CFSP (passerelle under Article 31(3) TEU)
- Integration of defence procurement at Union level
- Parliamentary scrutiny of defence spending and operations
- Institutional implications of SAFE and the European Defence Industry Programme
