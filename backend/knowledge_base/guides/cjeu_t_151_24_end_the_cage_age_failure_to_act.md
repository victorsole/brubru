# End the Cage Age v Commission (T-151/24, 25 November 2026): the Commission failed to act on a citizens' initiative

## QUICK FACTS
- **Judgment of the General Court, Wednesday 25 November 2026, Case T-151/24, End the Cage Age v Commission** (press release No 171/26). **The General Court upheld the action for failure to act**: by not setting out a NEW timeline once its 2021 timeline had expired, the Commission failed to fulfil its obligation under the European Citizens' Initiative Regulation (EU) 2019/788, read with the principle of good administration. **It rejected the second claim** (annulment of the refusal of access to the file).
- **The initiative:** registered 2018, about 1.4 million statements of support, threshold reached in 18 Member States; asked for legislation banning cages for certain farmed species and categories.
- **The Commission's promise:** in its communication of **30 June 2021** it said it would propose, **by the end of 2023**, legislation phasing out cages for ALL species and categories covered by the initiative, with a timeline. No proposal came. In November 2023 the citizens' committee asked it to confirm and set a new timeline; on **5 February 2024** the Commission confirmed its intention but gave no new timeline.
- **The rule the Court laid down:** the obligation to set out a timeline for the measures announced **"is not limited in time"**. It lasts as long as the Commission keeps its intention to act, and requires it to **amend its communication with a new timeline when the first one has passed**. Reasons: the timeline keeps citizens informed and lets Parliament assess implementation; without it, citizens' participation in EU democratic life would be undermined.
- **Access to the file:** a right that is not limited to proceedings ending in an adverse decision, BUT it does **not apply to the preparation of acts of general application** such as an ECI communication or a possible legislative proposal.
- **What follows:** after a failure-to-act judgment the institution must take the necessary measures to comply (here: set a timeline). Appeal to the Court of Justice possible within two months and ten days (points of law only).
- **Why it matters now:** the Commission's tentative agenda SEC(2026)2586 lists "Animal welfare rules for certain farming animals" for the College meeting of **9 December 2026 (tbc, may move)**. That is a plan, NOT a proposal adopted.
- **Doctrine:** the judgment sets a continuing timeline duty on the Commission's follow-up to a successful ECI; the Commission keeps its discretion on WHETHER to legislate (the Court did not order a proposal).
