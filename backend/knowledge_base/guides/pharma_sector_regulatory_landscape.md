## Regulatory Sandbox (2026 Pharma Package)

For the first time in EU pharmaceutical law, the 2026 reform introduces regulatory sandboxes. The Commission, at the suggestion of the EMA, may establish a sandbox where a medicinal product cannot be developed and authorised under standard regulatory requirements.

**Areas envisaged:** personalised medicines, advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), AI/digital health tools, innovative antimicrobials (phage therapy).

**How it works:** controlled, supervised environment with targeted adaptations to regulatory requirements. Lessons learned become permanent regulatory rules.

**Dual sandbox issue:** Companies developing AI-enabled medicinal products face TWO separate sandbox frameworks: the pharma sandbox and the AI Act sandbox (Digital Omnibus). No formal cross-reference between them. Both may apply simultaneously.

## Compounding: Current Law vs New Directive

Pharmacy compounding is exempt from marketing authorisation under Article 3 of Directive 2001/83/EC. The 2026 reform (new Directive, provisional agreement December 2025) makes important changes:

### Magistral Formulations (individual prescriptions)
- **Current law (2001/83/EC, Art 3(1)):** A pharmacy can compound a medicine only AFTER receiving a prescription for a specific patient. No advance preparation permitted
- **New Directive:** Magistral formula products "may be prepared in duly justified cases in advance by a pharmacy serving a hospital, on the basis of the estimated medical prescriptions within that hospital for the following seven days." This is a significant practical expansion: hospitals can now maintain 7-day rolling stock of compounded medicines instead of waiting for each individual prescription

### Officinal Formulations (pharmacopoeia-based)
- **Current law (2001/83/EC, Art 3(2)):** Pharmacies can prepare officinal formulae (listed in a national or EU pharmacopoeia) and dispense them directly to patients
- **New Directive:** Retains this exemption with clarified conditions

### Key changes in the reform:
- **Partial harmonisation:** The new directive tightens and partially harmonises compounding rules across the EU (previously fully national)
- **Hospital exemption for ATMPs:** New data collection, reporting, and annual review obligations. EMA must assess whether an adapted framework is needed for less complex ATMPs under hospital exemption
- **Industrial-scale boundary:** Production disguised as "compounding" that exceeds individual patient needs triggers full GMP and marketing authorisation requirements
- **Falsified Medicines Directive:** Serialisation requirements apply to compounded products entering the supply chain
- **National variation persists:** Significant differences between Member States remain, especially between common law and civil law jurisdictions

### What DIDN'T change:
- Compounding remains fundamentally under national competence
- The Article 3 exemption from marketing authorisation is preserved
- The requirement for a medical prescription for magistral formulations is preserved
- Officinal formulations remain based on national/EU pharmacopoeia

Source: Osborne Clarke analysis (March 2026), Inside EU Life Sciences (April 2023 Commission proposal analysis), Directive compromise text published 6 March 2026

## Urban Waste Water Treatment (UWWTD Recast)

**Directive (EU) 2024/3019** (CELEX 32024L3019), adopted 27 November 2024. The most significant upgrade to EU water treatment rules in 33 years.

**Pharma impact: Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**
- Pharmaceutical and cosmetic producers must finance quaternary treatment (advanced micropollutant removal)
- 80% of quaternary treatment costs borne by pharma/cosmetics producers, proportional to micropollutant load
- Quaternary treatment = 80% removal of micropollutants required
- Plants serving 150,000+ p.e. by 2033; 10,000+ p.e. by 2039
- This is a direct "polluter pays" mechanism

Source: EPRS Briefing PE 740.240

## PFAS Impact on Pharma

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are used in pharmaceutical manufacturing (coatings, packaging, equipment). The universal PFAS restriction under REACH (~10,000 substances) is the largest restriction proposal in REACH history. ECHA assessment expected end of 2026.

Key pharma-specific PFAS issues:
- Medical device coatings and drug delivery systems may need derogations
- Active pharmaceutical ingredients containing fluorine atoms are NOT PFAS (different chemistry)
- Manufacturing process chemicals (PTFE gaskets, fluorinated solvents) ARE affected
- Drinking Water Directive PFAS monitoring entered into force 12 January 2026
- Water pollutants directive adopted 26 March 2026: PFAS + pharmaceuticals in surface water monitoring

Cost of inaction: EUR 440 billion by 2050. EUR 1 trillion+ in water treatment.

## REACH and Pharma

Regulation (EC) 1907/2006 (REACH). Key pharma interactions:
- **Active ingredients exemption:** pharma active substances exempt from REACH registration when used in medicinal products (Article 2(5)(a)), but NOT when used as intermediates or in non-medicinal applications
- **Excipients and process chemicals:** all non-active substances must be REACH-registered if above 1 tonne/year
- **SVHC authorisation:** substances on Annex XIV require authorisation for continued use
- **REACH targeted revision (2023/0234(COD)):** "One Substance, One Assessment" (OSOA) to streamline hazard assessment across ECHA, EFSA, EMA, EEA
- **Critical Medicines Act connection:** may create exemptions or fast-track for essential medicine substances

## Public Procurement

Hospital and health system pharma purchases governed by:
- Directive 2014/24/EU (general public procurement, CELEX 32014L0024)
- Directive 2014/25/EU (utilities, CELEX 32014L0025)
- Directive 2014/23/EU (concessions, CELEX 32014L0023)
- International Procurement Instrument (Regulation 2022/1031, CELEX 32022R1031): can restrict third-country bidders

Key issues: joint procurement mechanisms (COVID-19 vaccine precedent), MEAT criteria (quality/innovation vs lowest price), cross-border procurement in health.

## US-EU Pharmaceutical Trade

The EU and US are the world's two largest pharma markets. Key dynamics (2026):
- **Tariff risk:** Trump II tariff policy creates uncertainty. Pharma historically exempt but no longer guaranteed
- **Regulatory divergence:** FDA and EMA diverging on accelerated approvals, AI/digital health, biosimilar interchangeability
- **GMP MRA:** EU-US Mutual Recognition Agreement on GMP inspections (fully operational 2019). Most successful transatlantic pharma cooperation mechanism
- **China dependency:** Both EU (Critical Medicines Act) and US (BIOSECURE Act) seeking to reshore API manufacturing from China
- **Drug pricing:** US Inflation Reduction Act Medicare negotiation narrows EU-US price gap, affecting global pricing strategies

## International Regulatory Cooperation

The EU is the world's largest pharmaceutical trader. Key cooperation mechanisms:
- **ICH:** EU founding member since 1990. Harmonises registration guidelines
- **GMP MRAs:** Mutual recognition of inspections with US, Canada, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland
- **Confidentiality arrangements:** With Australia, Brazil, Canada, Japan, Korea, Switzerland, US, WHO, EDQM, PIC/S
- **WHO Listed Authority:** European Medicines Regulatory Network designated as first regional WHO Listed Authority
- **IPRP/ICMRA:** Regulatory authority forums for cooperation and strategic coordination
- **PIC/S:** EU is Associated Partner, harmonising inspection procedures

Source: https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/other-topics/international-cooperation-pharmaceuticals_en

## Critical Medicines Act (Proposed)

The Commission is preparing a Critical Medicines Act to secure EU supply of essential medicines. Key elements:
- Reduce dependency on China and India for active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs)
- Strategic stockpiling requirements
- Diversification of supply chains
- Incentives for EU-based API manufacturing
- Complements the Pharma Package supply chain resilience provisions
