## What STOA Is and Its Unique Cross-Committee Role

STOA is the European Parliament's standing advisory panel on science and technology. Unlike a standard parliamentary committee, it is a cross-committee body: its 28 MEPs are drawn from 12 different committees, giving it a horizontal reach across the full legislative spectrum from agriculture (AGRI) to civil liberties (LIBE) to industry and research (ITRE).

Its mandate is explicitly forward-looking. STOA does not draft legislation. It commissions independent scientific assessments from external experts, coordinated by the EPRS Scientific Foresight Unit, and delivers them to MEPs and committee rapporteurs as an evidence base for law-making. In practice, a STOA study on, say, automated vehicles or genomic techniques will be cited during ITRE, ENVI, or IMCO deliberations; STOA creates the common scientific factual layer across committees that would otherwise each commission competing research.

The original impulse came from a 1985 report by MEP Rolf Linkohr proposing a parliamentary equivalent of the United States' Office of Technology Assessment. After a two-year pilot it became permanent in 1988. Today it is one of the few parliamentary bodies in the world that combines:
- Original scientific foresight studies (multi-year horizon scanning, 20-50 years out)
- Short "What if..." notes on speculative but plausible technological scenarios
- A science-journalism hub
- A network linking it to national parliamentary science advisers across Europe and beyond

## The Panel and Bureau Composition

The 28 Members of the Panel are nominated by their home committees, not elected Parliament-wide, which ensures disciplinary breadth. The 12 nominating committees are: AGRI, CULT, EMPL, ENVI, IMCO, INTA, ITRE, JURI, LIBE, REGI, SANT, and TRAN. The EP Vice-President responsible for STOA sits on the Bureau ex officio but does not vote in the same way as elected Bureau members.

Bureau elections happen at the start of each parliamentary term and at the mid-term renewal (2.5-year rotation). Because Brubru cannot verify the current Chair in real-time without live EP data, users should check the Panel page at `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/stoa/en/panel` for the serving leadership.

STOA is administratively supported by the EPRS Scientific Foresight Unit, which manages procurement of external expert teams, quality control of studies, and the peer review process. The Unit is staffed by EPRS officials with scientific backgrounds, distinct from the policy analysts in other EPRS units.

## STOA Products: How to Find and Read Them

### Full Studies (EPRS_STU)
The flagship output: peer-reviewed, expert-authored assessments of science and technology impacts on policy. Typically 80-200 pages. Commissioned from universities, consultancies, or research institutes. Recent examples:
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774754**: "Expected impact of the deployment of Automated Vehicles in the EU" (29 May 2026)
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774753**: "EU civic engagement: The use of digital tools and AI to promote citizen participation in EU policymaking" (29 May 2026)
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774748**: "Tomorrow's Primary Forests: The feasibility of realising novel primary forests in the western part of Europe" (26 May 2026)
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774682**: "'Widening' Indicator: Leveraging the potential for inclusive European research and innovation" (22 May 2026)
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774725**: "eTools for regulatory simplification and consistency" (31 March 2026)
- **EPRS_STU(2026)774721**: "Academic Freedom Monitor 2025" (23 February 2026)

Find them: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/EPRS_STU(YYYY)NNNNNN`

### "What if..." At a Glance Notes (EPRS_ATA)
STOA's signature short-form product: 4-page "What if..." scientific foresight notes that pose speculative-but-grounded questions about emerging technologies. These are the most cited STOA outputs in media and policy discourse. Recent examples:
- **EPRS_ATA(2026)774746**: "What if AI data centres were put in space?" (1 April 2026)
- **EPRS_ATA(2026)774722**: "What if we could track an electron's every step?" (16 February 2026)
- **EPRS_ATA(2025)774701**: "What if generative AI is reaching its limits?" (24 November 2025)

Find them: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/EPRS_ATA(YYYY)NNNNNN`

### Briefings (EPRS_BRI)
Medium-length (typically 10-20 pages) topic briefings on specific science or technology domains:
- **EPRS_BRI(2026)774707**: "Quantum technologies: Can they boost Europe's decarbonisation?" (16 February 2026)

### Annual Reports and Newsletters
STOA publishes an annual report summarising its Panel activities, studies, and events for the year. The 2025 Annual Report was published in early 2026. Newsletters are issued periodically.

### Reading a STOA Reference Number
Format: `EPRS_[TYPE](YYYY)NNNNNN`
- `EPRS` = European Parliamentary Research Service (the institutional publisher)
- `TYPE` = STU (study) / ATA (at a glance) / BRI (briefing)
- `YYYY` = publication year
- `NNNNNN` = 6-digit document identifier

The number alone (e.g. 774746) identifies the document on EP Think Tank. The full reference confirms its type and year.

## European Science-Media Hub (ESMH)

The ESMH is an independent science-journalism platform established by STOA and hosted at `https://sciencemediahub.eu/`. Its mission: "Bringing scientists, journalists and policymakers together: communicate better sound science to all."

Key activities:
- **Articles, interviews, and media reviews** covering AI in journalism, research integrity, plant pests, wildfire resilience, children's social media use, democracy, and digital tools
- **Annual Summer School** for young science journalists and content creators, held in Brussels. The 2026 (sixth) edition took place 22-25 June with a focus on artificial intelligence and science communication
- **Interactive maps and dashboards** on 5G deployment, alternative proteins, and other technology topics
- **Science media intelligence reports** assessing media coverage quality on science topics
- **Fact-checking and misinformation analysis** on scientific claims in public discourse

ESMH operates in partnership with STOA but maintains editorial independence. It is a practical resource for tech-policy clients who want to understand how their dossiers are being covered in science media, or who want to engage science journalists directly.

## Centre for Artificial Intelligence (C4AI)

The C4AI is STOA's dedicated cluster for AI-related parliamentary technology assessment. It consolidates STOA's growing body of AI work, which now spans language models, AI data infrastructure, autonomous systems, AI in public administration, algorithmic accountability, and the intersection of AI with democracy and fundamental rights.

C4AI coordinates with the EPRS Scientific Foresight Unit to ensure STOA's AI-related outputs are sequenced alongside, and feed into, the legislative calendar. Key STOA AI-related outputs in 2025-2026 include the "What if generative AI is reaching its limits?" note (EPRS_ATA(2025)774701) and "What if AI data centres were put in space?" (EPRS_ATA(2026)774746).

The C4AI activity set is directly relevant to:

- The AI Continent Action Plan (tracked in `ai_continent_action_plan`)
- The Apply AI Strategy for the public sector (tracked in `apply_ai_strategy_public_sector`)

Committees that draw most heavily on STOA/C4AI AI outputs include ITRE, IMCO, LIBE, and JURI.

## STOA Network (EPTA)

STOA is a founding member of the **European Parliamentary Technology Assessment (EPTA)** network, established in 1990 alongside five other parliamentary technology assessment centres. EPTA's purpose is to advise parliaments on the social, economic, and environmental impacts of science and technology, and to build parliamentary technology assessment capacity across Europe.

Key partners within the STOA Network:
- **EPTA member bodies:** national parliamentary TA offices across EU member states and beyond
- **European Commission's Joint Research Centre (JRC):** the Commission's in-house scientific service; STOA coordinates to avoid duplication
- **DG Research and Innovation (DG RTD):** coordinates on Horizon Europe science policy
- **Scientific Advice Mechanism (SAM):** produces independent scientific opinions with policy recommendations for the Commission
- **European Group on Ethics in Science and New Technologies (EGE):** advises on ethical dimensions; opinions feed both STOA and Commission
- **STS Forum:** international science and technology policy dialogue (global dimension)

In practice, the STOA Network means STOA can commission studies co-produced with national parliamentary TA bodies, and that its outputs carry broader institutional legitimacy than an in-house EP publication alone.

## Annual STOA Lecture and Workshops

STOA organises an **Annual STOA Lecture**: a high-profile public event bringing a leading scientist, technologist, or policy figure to address the European Parliament on a forward-looking science and technology theme. The Annual Lecture is a flagship visibility event and frequently attractssenior MEPs and external stakeholders beyond STOA's usual audience.

In addition to the Annual Lecture, STOA runs **thematic workshops** throughout the parliamentary year. Recent examples:
- "Europe's future after embracing New Genomic Techniques" (scheduled 30 June 2026)
- Annual Summer School for science journalists (ESMH, June 2026)

Workshops are typically open to external stakeholders including NGOs, industry, and academia, and serve as an informal engagement channel between MEPs, scientists, and the policy community ahead of or during legislative processes.

## How STOA Findings Feed Into Legislative Drafting

STOA's cross-committee composition is the mechanism by which its outputs enter the legislative process. When a STOA study is published on a topic currently before a committee:
1. The responsible STOA members brief their home committees directly
2. Rapporteurs and shadow rapporteurs receive STOA publications as part of the EPRS support package
3. References to STOA studies appear in committee reports, opinions, and adopted resolutions
4. STOA workshops create informal dialogue between MEPs and scientific experts before formal amendment tabling

Importantly, STOA output does not have binding or formal co-decision status. It is an evidence resource, not a legislative actor. However, because it is produced by MEPs for MEPs, with secretariat support from EPRS, it carries more institutional authority than an external think-tank publication.

For stakeholders in tech-intensive dossiers (AI, biotech, energy transition, autonomous systems, space policy), tracking STOA output is an early-warning system: what STOA publishes in year N often shapes EP committee positions in year N+1.
