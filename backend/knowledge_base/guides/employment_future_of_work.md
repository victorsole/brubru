## Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831

### Scope and Objectives

The Directive establishes rules on:
1. **Employment status determination**: Rebuttable legal presumption of employment relationship when platform exercises control
2. **Algorithmic management transparency**: Workers' right to information about automated monitoring and decision-making systems
3. **Platform workers' rights**: Access to effective remedies, collective representation, portability of professional reputation

### Employment Status Presumption

| Criterion | Indicators of Control |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **Upper limits on remuneration** | Platform determines or sets maximum pay |
| **Supervision of performance** | Electronic monitoring of work quality, timing |
| **Restriction on work organisation** | Platform dictates working hours, dress code, appearance |
| **Restriction on client base** | Platform prevents work for other platforms or direct clients |
| **Restriction on subcontracting** | Platform prevents outsourcing tasks to substitutes |

The presumption is triggered when **at least 2 of 5 indicators** are present. Member States may add additional indicators.

### Algorithmic Management Rules

| Right | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Transparency** | Workers informed about automated monitoring/decision-making systems before work starts |
| **Human review** | Right to request human review of significant automated decisions (deactivation, account restriction) |
| **Data protection** | Platforms prohibited from processing biometric data, emotional state, or private conversations |
| **Collective information** | Workers' representatives informed about algorithmic management systems |

### Transposition

- Deadline: **2 December 2026** (Member States)
- Key implementation choices left to Member States: precise indicators, burden of proof details, enforcement mechanisms

## Right to Disconnect

### Current Status

| Level | Status |
|-------|--------|
| **EU legislation** | No directive adopted; EP resolution of 21 January 2021 calling for a directive |
| **National legislation** | France (2017), Spain (2018), Italy (2017), Belgium (2022), Portugal (2021) -- varying approaches |
| **EP position** | Right to disconnect should be a fundamental right; employers must not penalise workers for exercising it |
| **Commission** | Not included in current work programme; may be revisited in future |

### EP Resolution Key Points

- Workers should not be required to engage in work-related activities outside working hours
- Employers must implement transparent policies on disconnection
- No adverse consequences for workers exercising the right
- Minimum standards to be adapted by social partners through collective bargaining

## Youth Guarantee

### Reinforced Youth Guarantee (Council Recommendation 2020)

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Target** | All young people under 30 (extended from 25) |
| **Commitment** | Quality offer of employment, continued education, apprenticeship, or traineeship within 4 months |
| **Coverage** | All Member States |
| **Funding** | ESF+, Youth Employment Initiative, RRF |

### Quality Framework for Traineeships

- Commission proposal for a Directive on quality traineeships (March 2024)
- Key elements: fair remuneration, social protection, maximum duration (6 months, extendable to 12 for regulated professions), learning objectives, mentor assignment
- Current status: Under co-decision (EP-Council negotiation)

## ESF+ (Regulation (EU) 2021/1057)

### Programme Structure

| Strand | Managed By | Budget 2021-2027 |
|--------|-----------|------------------|
| **Shared management** | Member States (operational programmes) | EUR 88 billion |
| **EaSI (Employment and Social Innovation)** | Commission (direct management) | EUR 762 million |
| **FEAD (Food and material assistance)** | Member States | EUR 3.3 billion (integrated into ESF+) |

### Thematic Concentration

Member States must allocate minimum shares to specific objectives:
- **25%** to social inclusion
- **12.5%** to youth employment (for MS above EU average NEET rate)
- **3%** to food and material assistance (former FEAD)

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Type | Use |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Participants | Common output | Number of people supported |
| Employed upon leaving | Common result | Employment rate post-intervention |
| NEET rate | Context | Youth not in employment, education, or training |
| At-risk-of-poverty rate | Context | Social inclusion benchmark |

## Just Transition Fund (Regulation (EU) 2021/1056)

### Purpose

Support territories most affected by the transition to climate neutrality (coal, peat, oil shale regions).

### Territorial Just Transition Plans

Each beneficiary Member State must submit plans identifying:
- Territories most affected by the transition
- Economic diversification strategy
- Reskilling and upskilling needs
- Support for SMEs and start-ups
- Investments in clean energy transition

### Eligible Activities

- Productive investments in SMEs
- Research and innovation activities
- Environmental rehabilitation
- Upskilling and reskilling of workers
- Job-search assistance
- Active inclusion of jobseekers
- Investments in smart and sustainable mobility

### Budget

EUR 17.5 billion (2021-2027) in current prices.

## AI and Labour Market Impacts

### Commission Policy Framework

| Document | Key Content |
|----------|-------------|
| **AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** | High-risk AI systems in employment (Art. 6, Annex III): recruitment, performance evaluation, task allocation |
| **Platform Work Directive** | Algorithmic management rules (see above) |
| **OECD AI Principles** | Human-centred AI, transparency, accountability |
| **European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan** | Digital skills, fair working conditions in the digital age |

### High-Risk AI in Employment (AI Act)

AI systems used for:
- Recruitment and selection of candidates
- Decisions on promotion, termination
- Task allocation based on individual behaviour
- Monitoring and evaluation of workers

These are classified as **high-risk** and must comply with:
- Risk management system
- Data governance (training data quality, bias mitigation)
- Transparency and human oversight
- Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity

### Posting of Workers Directive (96/71/EC, amended by 2018/957)

- Equal pay for equal work at the same place
- Application of host Member State working conditions (minimum rates, maximum work periods, health and safety)
- Prior notification requirements in host Member State

## European Pillar of Social Rights

### 20 Principles (Three Chapters)

| Chapter | Principles |
|---------|-----------|
| **Equal opportunities and access to the labour market** | Education, training, gender equality, equal opportunities, active support to employment |
| **Fair working conditions** | Secure employment, wages, work-life balance, healthy work environment, information about conditions |
| **Social protection and inclusion** | Childcare, social protection, unemployment benefits, minimum income, old age income, health care, inclusion of people with disabilities, housing, essential services |

### Action Plan Targets (2030)

| Target | 2030 Goal | Baseline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Employment rate (20-64) | 78% | 72.4% (2019) |
| Adult learning (25-64) | 60% annually | 37% (2016) |
| Reduction in people at risk of poverty or social exclusion | -15 million (of which -5 million children) | 91 million (2019) |

## Union of Skills (2025-2026)

The Union of Skills is a flagship Commission initiative launched in 2025 to address labour shortages and keep Europe competitive. After one year (March 2026), key actions delivered include:

- Skills partnerships with industry sectors
- Reinforced support for upskilling and reskilling through ESF+
- Alignment of education/training systems with labour market needs
- Digital skills acceleration across Member States
- Recognition of qualifications and micro-credentials framework

The initiative sits under DG EMPL and connects to the European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan target of 60% adult learning participation by 2030.
