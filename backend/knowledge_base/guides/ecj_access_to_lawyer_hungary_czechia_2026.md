# Right of access to a lawyer: Court of Justice judgments against Hungary and the Czech Republic (22 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** on **Thursday 22 October 2026** the Court of Justice (Fourth Chamber) upheld in their entirety two infringement actions brought by the Commission under Article 258 TFEU: **Case C-660/24 Commission v Hungary** (CELEX 62024CJ0660) and **Case C-681/24 Commission v Czech Republic** (CELEX 62024CJ0681). CURIA press release No 152/2026.
- **The law interpreted:** **Directive 2013/48/EU** on the right of access to a lawyer in criminal proceedings and in European arrest warrant proceedings (CELEX 32013L0048). Suspects and accused persons must have access to a lawyer in such time and manner that they can exercise their rights of defence practically and effectively.
- **What the two Member States did:** both allowed, as a general rule, suspects or persons under reasonable suspicion to be **questioned without a lawyer if the lawyer did not attend within a set period**: **two hours** after being informed of the custody in **Hungary**; **48 hours** after the suspect was taken into custody in the **Czech Republic**, where questioning could also go ahead if the lawyer could not be reached. Hungary had also not transposed the Directive's rules on **waiver** of the right.
- **What the Court held:** the right of access to a lawyer includes the right to have the lawyer **present and participating effectively** when questioned, in principle **physically present**. The Directive **exhaustively** lists the grounds for a temporary derogation; a general rule tied to a waiting period is not one of them, so both national laws infringe EU law. Hungary infringed a second time by not transposing the waiver rules: a suspect who decides not to exercise the right is in a situation equivalent to a waiver, which the Directive regulates.
- **Why it matters:** the Directive exists to build mutual trust between criminal justice systems for mutual recognition (European arrest warrant). National time-limit rules on lawyer attendance exist elsewhere: this is the doctrine other Member States now have to check against.
- **What happens next:** Hungary and the Czech Republic must comply; if they do not, the Commission may bring a second action under Article 260 TFEU seeking financial penalties. No penalty was imposed in these judgments.
