# EU Media Landscape: Brussels Policy Press

- Topic: Media outlets covering EU policy, institutions, and the Brussels bubble

Guide to the key media outlets covering EU policy, institutions, and the Brussels bubble.

## Tier 1: Dedicated EU Policy Media

### POLITICO Europe (politico.eu)
- **Ownership:** Axel Springer (since 2021), originally American
- **Model:** Advertising + POLITICO Pro subscriptions + events
- **Staff:** 100+ journalists in Brussels
- **Coverage:** Breaking news, playbooks, scoops, personality-driven political coverage
- **Key products:** Morning playbooks (Brussels, London, Paris, Berlin), Pro policy verticals (Tech, Energy, Health, Trade, Agriculture, Finance, Transport, Defence)
- **Audience:** Broad Brussels bubble + international political audience
- **Founded:** 2015 (European edition)

### Contexte (contexte.com)
- **Ownership:** 100% employee-owned (no external shareholders)
- **Model:** Pure subscription (no advertising, no sponsored content, no events revenue)
- **Staff:** ~50 journalists, 22 in Brussels, 116 total employees
- **Revenue:** EUR 10.7 million (2024), up 32% year-on-year
- **Subscribers:** ~1,500 organisations, ~15,000 individual readers
- **Origin:** French (Paris-based), expanding to English-language EU coverage
- **French edition desks (8):** Pouvoirs, Energie, Transports, Environnement, Agriculture, Sante, Tech, Medias
- **EU edition (English, launched 2025-2026):**
  - Energy section (May 2025): ETS, CBAM, nuclear, natural gas, net-zero industry, European Grids Package
  - Power section (March 2026): EU institutional dynamics, competitiveness agenda, political relationships
- **Key editors (Brussels):** Jean-Sebastien Lefebvre (Editor-in-Chief), Quentin Aries (Deputy EiC)
- **Differentiator:** Granular policy intelligence, anticipatory analysis (explains "what's between the lines"), technology-journalism hybrid with legislative monitoring tools
- **Competitors say:** "Contexte is the one EU outlet that competes on depth rather than speed"

### Euractiv (euractiv.com)
- **Ownership:** Network of media companies across EU member states
- **Model:** ~85% private sector (partnerships, content sponsorship) + 15% public sector
- **Coverage:** Stakeholder-oriented, pre-legislative positioning, network journalism
- **Network:** Editions in 13+ EU languages through local media partners
- **Audience:** Policy community + civil society + think tanks
- **Differentiator:** Multilingual EU-wide network, stakeholder perspectives
- **Founded:** 1999

### The Parliament Magazine (theparliamentmagazine.eu)
- **Focus:** European Parliament and MEPs
- **Coverage:** MEP interviews, committee work, parliamentary procedure
- **Products:** Annual MEP awards, policy conferences

### EU Observer (euobserver.com)
- **Model:** Subscription + donations
- **Focus:** Independent, investigative EU journalism
- **Coverage:** Transparency, rule of law, institutional accountability
- **Founded:** 2000

## Tier 2: Wire Services & International Press with EU Bureaux

### Agence Europe (agenceurope.eu)
- **Coverage:** Daily EU institutional bulletin since 1953
- **Format:** Factual, comprehensive, institutional proceedings
- **Language:** French (primarily), English
- **Audience:** Diplomats, permanent representations, institutional insiders

### Reuters / AFP / Bloomberg / AP
- All maintain Brussels bureaux covering EU institutions
- Focus on market-moving decisions, trade, regulation, summits
- Reuters and Bloomberg especially strong on financial regulation, competition, trade

### Financial Times
- Dedicated Brussels bureau, strong on trade, competition, economic governance
- Key journalists cover ECB, Eurogroup, trade negotiations

### MLex (mlex.com, owned by LexisNexis)
- **Focus:** EU regulatory and competition law intelligence
- **Coverage:** Antitrust, state aid, mergers, trade defence, financial regulation
- **Audience:** Law firms, competition economists, compliance teams

## Brussels-Based Think Tanks with Media Operations

Many think tanks produce policy analysis that functions as quasi-journalism:
- **Bruegel** - Economic policy, fiscal governance, digital
- **CEPS (Centre for European Policy Studies)** - Broad EU policy research
- **EPC (European Policy Centre)** - EU institutional reform, migration, social policy
- **Carnegie Europe** - Foreign policy, security, defence
- **Friends of Europe** - Multi-stakeholder policy debates
- **ECIPE** - Trade and international economics
- **Egmont Institute** - Belgian/EU foreign policy

## Key Characteristics of Brussels Media Market

### Audience
- Small but influential: estimated 30,000-50,000 "Brussels bubble" professionals
- High willingness to pay for specialised intelligence
- Decision-makers who need actionable information, not general news

### Business Models
1. **Pure subscription** (Contexte, EU Observer) - No ad revenue, editorial independence
2. **Hybrid** (POLITICO, Euractiv) - Mix of free content, premium subscriptions, events, partnerships
3. **Wire service** (Agence Europe, MLex) - Institutional subscribers, factual reporting
4. **Think tank** (Bruegel, CEPS) - Research funding, grants, membership fees

### Language Dynamics
- English dominates Brussels policy media
- French remains important (Agence Europe, Contexte French edition, some EP proceedings)
- German influence growing (Table.Media expansion)
- Euractiv uniquely offers multilingual network across member states

### Accreditation
- European Commission: ~1,000 accredited journalists
- European Parliament: ~500 press passes
- Council/European Council: Rotating presidency press corps

### Key Events Covered
- European Council summits (4+ per year)
- EP plenary sessions (12 per year in Strasbourg + Brussels)
- Commission weekly college meetings (Wednesdays)
- Trilogue negotiations (informal, often leaked)
- Competitiveness Council, ECOFIN, Foreign Affairs Council, Agriculture and Fisheries Council
