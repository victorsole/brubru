# Recycling claims on plastic bottles: the CPC commitments of Coca-Cola, Nestlé and Danone (22 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** on **Wednesday 22 October 2026** the Commission announced (press release IP/26/2238) that **The Coca-Cola Company, Nestlé Waters and Danone** committed to change the recycling claims printed on their plastic bottles, after a dialogue with the Commission and the **Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) network** led by the **Swedish Consumer Agency** and the **Hungarian Competition Authority**.
- **Legal frame:** EU consumer law as enforced through the CPC Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 (CELEX 32017R2394); the claims are assessed under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC (CELEX 32005L0029). This is a **commitments dialogue, not a fine or a court case**; national authorities monitor and may enforce if the commitments are not kept.
- **The four commitments:** (1) replace labels such as "I am a bottle made from 100% recycled plastic" with **"This bottle is 100% recycled. Excluding cap and label"**; (2) replace **"Recycle me again"** with **"Recycle me"**, so consumers are not led to think the bottle was already recycled before purchase or can be recycled into another bottle; (3) make sure the label's overall impression, including combined green claims or symbols, is not misleading about the environmental benefits of plastic bottles; (4) align digital marketing on websites and social media with the same wording.
- **Timing:** the Coca-Cola and Nestlé dialogues concluded in **June 2025** and **February 2026** and some commitments (digital campaigns) are already implemented; Danone's is the latest. Origin: a **BEUC alert of November 2023** against bottling companies.
- **Next steps named by the Commission:** national authorities monitor implementation; the Commission and the CPC network may engage with bottling industry associations for a level playing field.
- **Why it matters:** it shows the CPC network applying the UCPD to environmental claims before the Empowering Consumers Directive (EU) 2024/825 rules on generic environmental claims apply (from 27 September 2026 per that Directive) and while the Green Claims Directive proposal has no adopted text.
- **Not read:** the commitment texts themselves (not published); only the Commission press release.
