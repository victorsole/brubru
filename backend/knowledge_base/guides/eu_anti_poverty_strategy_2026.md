## Context — what came before

The 2026 Anti-Poverty Strategy operationalises Principle 14 of the **European Pillar of Social Rights** (proclaimed 17 November 2017): the right to adequate minimum income that ensures a life in dignity at all stages of life. Earlier policy products it supersedes or extends:

- European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan (2021) and its **AROPE 2030 headline target**: at least 15 million fewer people at risk of poverty or social exclusion by 2030 (of which at least 5 million children)
- 2008 Active Inclusion Recommendation (2008/867/EC) — three-pillar approach: adequate income support, inclusive labour markets, access to quality services
- 2013 Council Recommendation on Investing in Children (2013/112/EU) — predecessor of the Child Guarantee
- Council Recommendation (EU) 2023/1322 on adequate minimum income (28 January 2023)
- Council Recommendation (EU) 2021/1004 establishing the European Child Guarantee (14 June 2021), CELEX **32021H1004**
- 2024 Strategic Agenda commitment to a "social Europe" delivering economic security

The strategy crowns the spring-2026 social agenda and prepares the next milestone: the **European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan 2.0**, scheduled for College adoption **22 July 2026** (per SEC(2026)2564).

## Headline architecture (from College agenda)

The strategy is structured as a Communication that:

1. **Diagnoses persistent poverty**: 94.5 million people in the EU at risk of poverty or social exclusion (AROPE) in 2024 (Eurostat SILC); 19.4% of population
2. **Sets a 2030 trajectory** consistent with the Pillar's 15 million / 5 million children targets
3. **Bundles existing instruments**: minimum income, social inclusion, in-work poverty, energy poverty, housing exclusion, child poverty
4. **Aligns Member State action** through the European Semester + EPSCO peer review + ESF+ programming
5. **Cross-references** Cohesion Policy mid-term review (March 2026), MFF 2028-2034 negotiations, Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan 2.0

The strategy is reportedly a "no fresh cash" instrument — it does not open a new financing line. Operational delivery runs through:

- **ESF+** (Regulation (EU) 2021/1057) — minimum 25% earmark for social inclusion + minimum 5% for child poverty (the Child Guarantee earmark)
- **Just Transition Fund** (Regulation (EU) 2021/1056) for transition-affected regions
- **AMIF** (Asylum, Migration and Integration Fund) for migrant integration
- **National budgets** through European Semester recommendations

## Pre-adoption press signal — "without offering fresh cash"

Politico EU (5 May 2026): the leak of the strategy ahead of College day flags that the Commission is asking Member States to "fight poverty" through better targeting of existing instruments rather than through a new EU-level transfer programme. The political subtext: cohesion-policy mid-term-review tensions + MFF 2028-2034 negotiations make it politically infeasible to announce a fresh anti-poverty financing line in spring 2026.

This positions the strategy as a **coordination instrument**, not a financing one. Member States retain primary competence under Article 153 TFEU.

## Treaty basis and competence

- **Article 153 TFEU**: shared competence on social policy; Council acts after consulting EP for combatting social exclusion (Article 153(1)(j) and (k))
- **Article 156 TFEU**: open method of coordination — Commission encourages cooperation between Member States
- **Article 9 TFEU**: horizontal social clause — Union takes account of high level of employment, adequate social protection, and combatting social exclusion in defining and implementing all policies
- **Article 175 TFEU**: economic, social and territorial cohesion — basis for Cohesion Policy delivery

## Council follow-up

Council Recommendation companion documents — the Child Guarantee strengthening and the housing exclusion Recommendation — go through EPSCO under Article 292 TFEU (recommendations) on a Commission proposal. The anti-poverty Communication itself does not require Council adoption (Communications are unilateral Commission acts).

## Why this matters

1. **First dedicated EU anti-poverty Communication** — prior products were Action Plans, Recommendations, or part of broader strategies
2. **Operational integrator** — ties together ESF+, AMIF, Cohesion Policy, Semester recommendations, EPSCO peer review
3. **Pillar 2.0 bridge** — the substantive content feeds directly into the European Pillar of Social Rights Action Plan 2.0 due 22 July 2026
4. **Member State politics** — anti-poverty competence is national; the EU lever is funding earmarks + Semester pressure + naming-and-shaming
5. **MFF 2028-2034 leverage** — the strategy's success metrics anchor the case for ESF+ successor envelope size

## Key figures (Eurostat SILC 2024 baseline)

- AROPE rate: 19.4% of EU population (94.5 million people)
- Children at risk: 24.4% of under-18s
- Severe material and social deprivation rate: 6.7%
- Housing cost overburden rate: 8.2% of households (above 40% of disposable income)
- Energy poverty: ~9% unable to keep home adequately warm (latest ESQ + LFS modules, 2024)
- AROPE 2030 target: 15 million reduction (of which 5 million children)
