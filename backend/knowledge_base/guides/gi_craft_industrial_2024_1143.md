# Geographical Indications for Craft and Industrial Products — Regulation (EU) 2024/1143

## QUICK FACTS

- **LATEST (Monday 18 May 2026)**: **First-ever geographical indication for a craft product receives EU-wide protection under Reg (EU) 2024/1143** — DG GROW (Single Market) announced the registration in the Union register, marking the operational debut of the craft/industrial GI regime that entered into force on 16 May 2024. Source: `single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/first-ever-geographical-indication-craft-name-receives-eu-wide-protection-2026-05-18_en` (18 May 2026).
- **Companion OJ-C publications (19 May 2026)**: 4 new GI applications and amendments published in OJ-C: 52026XC02777, 52026XC02785, 52026XC02789, 52026XC02804. Each is either a Member State application for registration or a Commission-approved standard amendment to a product specification.
- **Parent regulation**: **Regulation (EU) 2024/1143** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 April 2024 on geographical indications for wine, spirit drinks and agricultural products, and on traditional specialities guaranteed and optional quality terms for agricultural products + on **craft and industrial products** (new chapter), amending Regulations (EU) No 1308/2013, (EU) 2019/787 and (EU) 2019/1753 and repealing Regulation (EU) No 1151/2012.
- **CELEX**: **32024R1143**
- **EUR-Lex**: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1143`
- **Entry into force**: 13 May 2024 (general); **craft and industrial product GI regime applicable from 1 December 2025**.
- **Lead DG for craft/industrial GI**: **DG GROW** (Single Market). Agricultural / wine / spirit GIs remain with **DG AGRI**.
- **Operating registry for craft/industrial GIs**: managed by the **European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO)** in Alicante (delegated task from DG GROW under Reg 2024/1143).
- **Council configuration**: COREPER I (Internal Market) for craft/industrial track; COREPER (Special Committee on Agriculture, SCA) for the agricultural/wine/spirit track.
- **Type**: Regulation (directly applicable; full harmonisation of the EU-wide GI system across the three product families).

## Why This Regulation

Before 2024, the EU had a strong PDO/PGI/TSG system for **agricultural products, wines, and spirits** (the "traditional" GIs — Champagne, Parmigiano Reggiano, Rioja, Aceto Balsamico di Modena, etc.) but **no EU-wide protection mechanism for craft and industrial products**. National GI laws existed in France, Italy, Portugal, Czechia, Bulgaria, Croatia, Hungary, Slovakia, Romania, and a few others (typically protecting names like Carrara marble, Limoges porcelain, Bohemian crystal, Solingen blades), but the protection was patchy across the single market, and there was no EU-wide enforcement against counterfeit goods originating in or transiting through other Member States.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1143 closes that gap by:
1. Extending the GI legal regime to craft and industrial products — defined as products with **specific qualities, characteristics or reputation essentially attributable to their geographical origin** AND at least one **production step (manufacturing, processing, finishing)** taking place in the defined geographical area.
2. Delegating registry management to **EUIPO** (single point of entry for applications, examinations, publications, oppositions, registrations) — leveraging EUIPO's experience with the EU trademark + design systems.
3. Creating a **single procedure** for craft/industrial GI applications: Member State assessment → EUIPO scrutiny → publication in OJ-C → 3-month opposition window → registration with EU-wide effect.
4. Providing **strong protection** against direct or indirect commercial use of a registered GI for comparable products, against any misuse/imitation/evocation, and against any false or misleading indication of provenance.

## Structure of Reg 2024/1143

The Regulation is structured in **three pillars** corresponding to the three product families, with a common chapter on EUIPO-managed registry for the craft/industrial pillar:

| Pillar | Products | Lead DG | Registry Operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agricultural products + foodstuffs + TSG | DG AGRI | DG AGRI eAmbrosia |
| 2 | Wines + spirit drinks (single regime replacing Reg 1308/2013 + 2019/787 chapters) | DG AGRI | DG AGRI eAmbrosia |
| 3 | **Craft and industrial products** (NEW) | **DG GROW** | **EUIPO** |

## The Craft and Industrial Pillar — How It Works

### Eligibility
A craft or industrial product is eligible for GI registration if:
- It **originates in a specific place, region or country**, AND
- A **given quality, reputation or other characteristic** of the product is **essentially attributable** to its geographical origin, AND
- **At least one of the production steps** (raw material sourcing, manufacturing, processing, or finishing) takes place in the defined geographical area.

The "essentially attributable" link is the legal core: it requires a substantive causal connection between the geographical area (climate, raw materials, traditional skills, savoir-faire) and the product's quality or reputation. Mere assembly or packaging in the area is **not** enough.

### Application Procedure
1. **Producer group** in the defined geographical area drafts a **product specification** (name, geographical area boundaries, description of the product, evidence of the origin link, production methods, controls).
2. **Member State** scrutinises the application against Reg 2024/1143 requirements (typically via a national IP office or the Ministry responsible for craft/industrial products); runs a national opposition phase; transmits eligible files to EUIPO.
3. **EUIPO** examines for formal completeness and substantive admissibility, publishes the application in **OJ-C** with a **3-month EU-wide opposition window**.
4. **Opposition** can be filed by any natural or legal person established in or holding an interest in the EU, on grounds of conflict with existing GI/TM rights, genericness, or lack of origin link.
5. **Commission Implementing Decision** registers the GI (or refuses) — the GI enters the Union register and benefits from EU-wide protection.

### Scope of Protection (Article 36 of Reg 2024/1143)
A registered craft/industrial GI is protected against:
- Direct or indirect **commercial use** of the name for products not covered by the registration, in so far as those products are comparable, or in so far as use exploits the reputation of the GI.
- **Misuse, imitation, or evocation**, even if the true origin is indicated or the protected name is translated, transcribed, transliterated, or accompanied by an expression such as "style", "type", "method", "as produced in", "imitation", "flavour", "like" or similar.
- Any other **false or misleading indication** as to the provenance, origin, nature or essential qualities of the product.
- Any other practice **liable to mislead the consumer** as to the true origin.

Enforcement: ex officio by national authorities (customs + market surveillance under the Market Surveillance Regulation (EU) 2019/1020), plus civil action by producer groups and third parties with a legitimate interest.

## Cross-Reference with Other EU Quality Schemes

- **PDO / PGI** (agricultural): **same regulation 2024/1143**, different pillar; protection scope is broadly equivalent but the product type and value chain differ.
- **EU trademark** (Reg (EU) 2017/1001): a craft/industrial GI is NOT a trademark — it cannot be assigned, licensed, or held individually. It is a collective right of producers in the defined area.
- **Plant variety rights** (Reg (EC) 2100/94): unrelated; protects plant varieties not products of geographical origin.
- **Eurostars / EIC**: irrelevant; those are funding programmes, not IP regimes.

## State of Play — May 2026

- Reg 2024/1143 entered into force 13 May 2024.
- The craft/industrial pillar became applicable on **1 December 2025** (delayed entry to allow EUIPO to set up the registry).
- **First-ever craft GI registration**: 18 May 2026 (today's news).
- DG GROW + EUIPO have published implementation guides for producer groups; first application waves are concentrated in Italy, France, Portugal, Czechia.

## What Brubru Should Surface

- Always distinguish the **three pillars** of Reg 2024/1143 (agricultural / wine-spirit / craft-industrial) when answering, because the lead DG (AGRI vs GROW) and the registry operator (eAmbrosia vs EUIPO) differ.
- Always cite **Reg (EU) 2024/1143** (CELEX 32024R1143) as the single legal basis; never say "the GI Regulation" without specifying.
- For the craft/industrial pillar specifically, the registry is **EUIPO** in Alicante — not the Commission.
- The first-ever craft GI registration (18 May 2026) is a milestone; specific name and Member State to confirm via the EUIPO register once published.
