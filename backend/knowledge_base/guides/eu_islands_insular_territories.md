## New Outermost Regions Strategy (adopted 10 September 2026)

**Read the legal distinction first. The outermost regions are not "EU islands".** Two different treaty categories are involved and they must not be blurred:

| | Outermost regions | Islands in general |
|---|---|---|
| Treaty basis | Article 349 TFEU | Article 174 TFEU |
| Who is covered | Nine named territories of three Member States: French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Mayotte, Réunion, Saint-Martin (France), the Azores, Madeira (Portugal), the Canary Islands (Spain) | Around 2,400 inhabited islands across 13 coastal Member States |
| What the treaty allows | Specific measures that **tailor or adapt the application of EU law** to these territories, adopted by the Council | A cohesion principle: particular attention in EU policy. **No power to adapt or disapply EU rules** |

Most outermost regions are islands or archipelagos, but **French Guiana is on the South American mainland**, and the overwhelming majority of EU islands are **not** outermost regions. Never present a fact about one category as a fact about the other, and never cite this strategy as an islands policy.

- **What was adopted**: a Commission Communication, "A new strategic vision for the EU's outermost regions: Strengthening the Union's global reach", adopted on 10 September 2026. It is the sixth Communication of its kind, following those of 2004, 2008, 2012, 2017 and 2022.
- **Accompanying regulatory package**: the Communication comes with a regulatory package proposing targeted adaptations to EU legislation covering **agriculture, forestry, fisheries, migration and taxation**. Article 349 TFEU is the legal basis for the package. The Commission describes it as a first step in a continuous process.
- **Four objectives of the strategy**:
  1. **Harness geostrategic potential and strengthen regional integration**: better align EU internal and external policies to deepen cooperation with neighbouring countries while boosting trade, connectivity, security, defence and maritime security.
  2. **Increase competitiveness and access to the single market**: support smart specialisation where these regions have clear competitive advantages, such as the blue economy, renewable energy, biotechnology, research and space, and improve connectivity through stronger air and sea networks.
  3. **Improve resilience and preparedness**: climate adaptation, environmental challenges including the influx of sargassum, biodiversity protection, disaster response, energy autonomy, protection of critical infrastructure such as ports and submarine cables, and food security and self-sufficiency through agriculture and fisheries.
  4. **Promote social fairness and support people**: employment, access to education, social inclusion, poverty reduction, affordable housing, clean water and healthcare, and support for people's right to stay in the place they call home, particularly young people.
- **Links to other live files**: the Commission's proposal to review the EU Emissions Trading System includes provisions for these regions that take account of geographical constraints and the need to preserve territorial continuity, connectivity and access to essential services. The proposed Public Procurement Act allows certain procurement rules to be tailored to their specificities. The forthcoming European Product Act is expected to allow targeted adjustments to product requirements where these are necessary, proportionate and justified by local conditions.
- **Governance and money**: a yearly structured dialogue, a dedicated portal for the outermost regions, a new youth project, and a revamped advisory tool on EU programmes and funding instruments, all ahead of the 2028-2034 multiannual financial framework. The Commission's proposal for that framework introduces National and Regional Partnership Plans requiring Spain, France and Portugal to include specific measures for their outermost regions.
- **Next steps and stage**: the strategy itself is implemented through EU sectoral policies. The **regulatory package will be submitted to the Council for agreement by the Member States after consultation of the European Parliament**. Nothing in that package is adopted law yet, so do not describe any of its measures as being in force.

## Core Policy Instruments

### Cohesion Policy (2021-2027)
- ERDF (European Regional Development Fund): Regulation (EU) 2021/1058 (CELEX 32021R1058) -- includes specific provisions for islands under "specific territorial features"
- Cohesion Fund: Regulation (EU) 2021/1057 -- supports Member States with GNI below 90% of EU average; several island regions (Cyprus, Malta, parts of Greece/Portugal) are beneficiaries
- Territorial cooperation (Interreg): Regulation (EU) 2021/1059 -- cross-border maritime programmes (MED, NPA, South Baltic, Atlantic Area) serve island regions
- Just Transition Fund: Regulation (EU) 2021/1056 -- several island energy-transition territories (e.g. Cycladic islands, Corsica) are JTF beneficiaries

### Outermost Regions (Article 349 TFEU)
- POSEI programmes (agriculture) -- Regulation (EU) 228/2013
- Specific tax regimes: "Octroi de Mer" in French OMRs, AIEM in Canary Islands, Madeira Free Zone
- Commission strategy renewed periodically through a series of Communications (2004, 2008, 2012, 2017, 2022). **The latest is the strategy adopted on 10 September 2026**, described in full in the section above; the 2022 Communication is no longer the current one

### Clean Energy for EU Islands Initiative
- Launched 2017 at Valletta Political Declaration
- Clean Energy for EU Islands Secretariat (CE4EUISLANDS) -- supports ~60 islands in their transition
- Flagship targets: 100% renewable electricity, storage, energy communities
- Funded via Horizon Europe + LIFE + InvestEU

### Connectivity
- Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T): Regulation (EU) 2024/1679 -- revised guidelines include specific provisions for island connectivity (ferries, air routes of general economic interest)
- Public Service Obligations (PSO) in aviation: Regulation (EC) 1008/2008 Article 16 -- Member States may impose PSO on air routes to peripheral regions (islands)
- Maritime transport: Regulation (EEC) 3577/92 on maritime cabotage; State aid guidelines for maritime transport

## Recurring Policy Challenges

- **Single-market integration**: smaller markets -> territorial supply constraints, limited competition (see `eu_territorial_supply_constraints.md`), higher consumer prices
- **Energy**: reliance on imported diesel + bunker fuel for electricity generation; high exposure to oil price shocks (see `iran_strait_hormuz_eu_response.md` for 2026 Middle East dimension)
- **Connectivity**: summer-winter asymmetry of transport demand; high per-passenger cost of PSO-funded air/maritime links
- **Demographic decline**: depopulation in smaller islands (Aegean, Adriatic, some Scottish + Irish islands), ageing populations
- **Climate change**: coastal erosion, freshwater stress, Mediterranean wildfires, heatwave tourism impact
- **Fiscal pressures**: dependence on summer tourism + limited industrial diversification
