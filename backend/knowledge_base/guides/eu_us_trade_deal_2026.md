## Overview

The EU and the United States agreed on a Framework on Reciprocal, Fair and Balanced Trade on 21 August 2025. To implement the agreement, the Commission proposed two regulations on 28 August 2025:

1. **COM(2025)471** (procedure 2025/0261(COD)): Adjusts EU customs duties on US imports and opens tariff quotas for certain US goods (industrial goods, agricultural products including lobster, pork, dairy, nuts, seafood)
2. **COM(2025)472** (procedure 2025/0260(COD)): Eliminates customs duties on certain categories of US goods entirely

Both are rapporteur Bernd Lange (S&D, Germany), who is also INTA chair. The Council adopted its position on 21 November 2025, adding three elements to the COM(2025)471 proposal. However, INTA suspended work on both files on 21 January 2026 following renewed Trump tariff threats related to Greenland.

## Tariff Structure Under the Framework Agreement

### US Tariffs on EU Exports
- **Baseline**: 15% (higher of MFN or 15% combined rate)
- **Steel, aluminium, copper**: 50% (unchanged)
- **Zero tariff products**: Natural resources unavailable in the US (including cork), aircraft and aircraft parts, generic pharmaceuticals and their precursors
- **Automobiles**: Section 232 tariffs reduced when EU introduces implementing legislation
- **Ongoing Section 232 investigations**: Pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, lumber

### EU Tariffs on US Imports
- **Industrial goods**: EU proposed zero-for-zero tariffs
- **Agricultural goods**: Tariff quotas opened for specific products (lobster, pork, dairy, nuts, seafood)
- **Steel**: Tariff-rate quotas (duty-free up to quota, 25% above)
- **Aluminium**: MFN tariff 0-7.5% depending on product type

### Impact on Pharmaceuticals
- **Generic pharmaceuticals**: MFN-only treatment (no reciprocal tariff) -- effectively duty-free or very low
- **Branded/patented pharmaceuticals**: Subject to baseline 15% tariff
- **Pharmaceutical precursors and chemical ingredients**: MFN-only treatment
- **US Section 232 pharma investigation**: Ongoing; could impose additional tariffs on EU pharma exports to the US

## Political Context

### In Favour of the Deal
- Avoiding a transatlantic trade war at a time of global instability
- Securing market access for EU exporters
- Maintaining the transatlantic alliance amid the Iran crisis and Ukraine war
- Germany and other export-dependent Member States want stable US trade relations

### Against the Deal / Seeking Conditions
- Former HR/VP Josep Borrell: Called the deal "unfair" and urged EU to suspend its implementation as leverage against US positions on Iran/international law
- Progressive MEPs: Want Council conclusions calling for international law to be upheld (indirect rebuke to Trump)
- Concerns about US unilateral tariffs and extraterritorial sanctions
- Trump's "very bad future" threats to NATO allies create political resistance
- Iran war has hardened anti-US sentiment in parts of the EP

### Timing Pressure
- Politico EU reports "time runs out to avert new trade war as US patience with EU wears thin"
- EP vote expected in approximately 2 weeks (late March/early April 2026)
- European Council (19-20 March) may discuss trade as part of competitiveness agenda
- Iran crisis is consuming political bandwidth that would otherwise go to trade ratification

### Brubru User Takeaways from PE 785.679

- EU-US is still the largest bilateral trade relationship globally despite tariff turbulence -- 17.6% of EU trade, 18.7% of US trade
- Record EU surplus in goods (EUR 200bn) is a political vulnerability under Trump tariff rhetoric
- Record EU deficit in services (EUR 139bn) reflects US dominance in digital/financial services -- DSA/DMA enforcement has leverage implications
- Germany (goods) and Ireland (services) are the two most exposed Member States -- compounded in Ireland's case by pharma manufacturing + US tech subsidiaries
- Pharma and organic chemicals are the fastest-growing EU exports to US -- any US tariff on pharma (Section 232 investigation ongoing) would directly hit Ireland, Belgium, Germany
- Financial/insurance FDI dominates both directions -- US bank branches in EU and EU bank subsidiaries in US are a mutual lever
- EU tech FDI in US (10.5% of outward stock) contrasts with small US tech FDI in EU (1.7% of inward) -- asymmetric digital economy exposure
