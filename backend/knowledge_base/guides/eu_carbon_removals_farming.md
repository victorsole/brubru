- Target: Contribute to EU 2050 climate neutrality goal (European Climate Law, Reg (EU) 2021/1119) and 2040 -90% target
- Scope: 4 activity categories -- (1) permanent carbon removals (DACCS, BECCS, biochar), (2) carbon farming (soil, wetlands, agroforestry), (3) carbon storage in products (long-lived wood products, construction), (4) emission reductions from soils

## Why the CRCF Regulation

Carbon dioxide removal (CDR) is essential for the EU's climate neutrality target:

- Residual fossil emissions by 2050 estimated at ~500 Mt CO2e/year (hard-to-abate sectors: aviation, cement, agriculture)
- These must be offset by genuine, verified carbon removals
- Before CRCF, no EU-wide quality standards for carbon removal credits; private voluntary markets lacked credibility (Verra, Gold Standard controversies)
- CRCF provides **EU-certified removals** with strict monitoring, reporting, verification (MRV), and permanence requirements

## Four Activity Categories

### 1. Permanent Carbon Removals (Article 2(1)(a))
Technologies that durably store CO2 for centuries:
- **Direct Air Capture with Carbon Storage (DACCS)**: capture CO2 from ambient air, store in geological formations
- **Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS)**: biomass combustion with flue-gas CO2 capture and geological storage
- **Biochar**: pyrolysis of biomass, carbon locked into soil-applied biochar
- **Enhanced weathering**: mineralisation of basalt dust on agricultural soils

Permanence requirement: **several centuries** (typically >= 1,000 years for geological storage, >= 200 years for biochar).

### 2. Carbon Farming (Article 2(1)(b))
Land management practices that sequester CO2 in biomass or soils:
- **Afforestation / reforestation**: new forest on non-forested land
- **Peatland restoration**: rewetting drained peatlands
- **Agroforestry**: integrating trees into cropping / livestock systems
- **Cover cropping / reduced tillage**: soil organic carbon enhancement
- **Wetland restoration**

Permanence: "at least several decades", with monitoring and liability for reversals.

### 3. Carbon Storage in Products (Article 2(1)(c))
Long-lived harvested wood products and bio-based construction:
- Timber construction (CLT, glulam)
- Structural bio-based panels
- Long-lived furniture (>= 35-year lifetime)

Excludes short-lived products (paper, packaging, bioenergy).

### 4. Emission Reductions from Soil Management
Reductions of soil-based emissions (N2O, CH4) through improved nutrient management, paddy rice cultivation techniques, livestock manure management.

## QU-A-L-ITY Criteria (Article 4)

CRCF activities must satisfy four cumulative quality principles (**QU-A-L-ITY**):

| Principle | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **QU**antification | Net removal benefit measurable, transparent, and minus emissions from the activity |
| **A**dditional | Goes beyond standard practice and legal requirements |
| **L**ong-term storage | Durability appropriate to activity category |
| **SusaibilITY** | No harm to other EU environmental objectives (biodiversity, water, soil) |

## Certification Methodologies (Article 8)

- Commission adopts detailed methodologies via delegated acts for each activity type
- **Expert Group on Carbon Removals** (Article 7) advises the Commission
- Methodologies under development (2025-2026):
  - DACCS methodology: adopted 2025 (draft)
  - Biochar methodology: adopted 2025 (draft)
  - Peatland restoration: under consultation
  - Carbon farming soil organic carbon: pilot phase
  - Long-lived wood products: under consultation
- Operators (project developers) apply for certification via accredited certification bodies

## EU Registry (Article 12)

- Commission establishes **EU-wide electronic registry** of CRCF units
- Each certified removal unit has unique identifier, traceability, and status (issued, held, cancelled, retired)
- Registry expected operational by 2027
- Interoperability with EU ETS Union Registry

## Unit Types

CRCF creates **tradable certified removal units**:
- **CRCF units from permanent removals** (1 tonne CO2e each)
- **Carbon farming units** (subject to reversal risk buffer)
- **Carbon storage in products units** (subject to reversal risk buffer)
- **Soil emission reduction units**

## Interaction with EU ETS

- CRCF units are **distinct from** ETS allowances
- Currently **not fungible** with EU ETS (no direct use for compliance)
- Commission ETS Review (2028): may propose inclusion of permanent removals in ETS compliance (net-zero ETS debate)
- **Article 30** of Climate Law: certified removals counted toward EU 2040 target

## Relationship to National Targets

- Member States use CRCF for compliance with Effort Sharing Regulation (Reg (EU) 2023/857) and LULUCF Regulation (Reg (EU) 2023/839)
- LULUCF target: net removals of -310 Mt CO2e by 2030
- National Energy and Climate Plans (NECPs) must account for CRCF contributions

## Voluntary vs Regulatory Use

- Corporate net-zero claims under Green Claims Directive (in trilogue) must use CRCF-certified removals for any EU-based offset claim
- **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)**: currently does not credit CRCF removals; possible future integration
- Voluntary carbon markets (Verra, Gold Standard) can adopt CRCF methodologies

## Critiques and Risks

- **Greenwashing concerns**: insufficient permanence for biochar and carbon farming
- **Cost**: EUR 300-1000/tonne for DACCS today, declining to EUR 100-200 by 2040
- **Monitoring burden**: high MRV cost for smallholder farmers
- **Reversal risk**: wildfires, droughts, land-use change can release stored carbon
- **Land-use competition**: tension with food production, biodiversity

## Related EU Instruments

- **European Climate Law** (Reg (EU) 2021/1119): 2050 neutrality, 2040 -90% target
- **EU ETS** (Directive 2003/87/EC): emissions trading (no CRCF integration yet)
- **LULUCF Regulation** (Reg (EU) 2023/839): -310 Mt CO2e target by 2030
- **Effort Sharing Regulation** (Reg (EU) 2023/857): national non-ETS targets
- **Green Claims Directive** (in trilogue): CRCF certification required for EU offset claims
- **Industrial Carbon Management Strategy** (COM(2024) 62): CCUS and CRCF roadmap
- **Nature Restoration Law** (Reg (EU) 2024/1991): linked objectives for peatlands, wetlands, forests
- **CAP** (Reg (EU) 2021/2115): eco-schemes for carbon farming
