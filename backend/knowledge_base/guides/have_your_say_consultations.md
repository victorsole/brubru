# Have Your Say: the European Commission's Public Consultations and Feedback Portal

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is:** "Have Your Say" (officially renamed "Public Consultations and Feedback" but still branded and searched as Have Your Say) is the European Commission's single online portal where citizens, businesses, NGOs and public authorities can comment on EU policies and laws at every stage of the policy cycle, from the first idea through to review of a law already in force.
- **Official URL:** https://have-your-say.ec.europa.eu/index_en (consultations search: https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say/initiatives_en)
- **Legal basis:** Article 11 TEU, in particular Article 11(3) TEU, which obliges the Commission to "carry out broad consultations with parties concerned" to ensure the Union's actions are coherent and transparent. Operationalised through the Commission's Better Regulation Guidelines, Commission Staff Working Document [SWD(2021) 305 final](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021SC0305) of 3 November 2021, and the accompanying Better Regulation Toolbox (2023 edition), plus the 2016 Interinstitutional Agreement on Better Law-Making (OJ L 123, 13.4.2016).
- **Who runs it:** The Commission's Secretariat-General coordinates the framework; each lead Directorate-General runs the individual consultation for its own file. Impact assessments and evaluations that trigger a consultation are quality-checked by the independent Regulatory Scrutiny Board (`regulatory_scrutiny_board`).
- **Four feedback mechanisms, four different clocks:**
  - Call for evidence / inception impact assessment (or combined evaluation roadmap for reviews of existing law): typically open a minimum of 4 weeks, often run in parallel with a longer public consultation on major files.
  - Public consultation (accompanying an impact assessment, evaluation or fitness check): minimum 12 weeks.
  - Feedback on an adopted legislative proposal, before or during negotiations with the co-legislators: 8 weeks, and every comment received is forwarded to the European Parliament and the Council.
  - Feedback on a draft delegated act or implementing act: 4 weeks.
- **Reporting duty:** the Commission must publish a factual summary report of a closed public consultation within 8 weeks of its closure, and a synopsis report (annexed to the impact assessment or evaluation) explaining how stakeholder input was used.
- **Scale (2025 figures, published by the Commission):** over 9.1 million visits to the portal; 51 legislative proposals opened for feedback; 98 public consultations (291,841 contributions); 201 call-for-evidence documents (242,053 feedback comments); 224 draft delegated/implementing acts (13,684 feedback comments published); 31,874 position papers submitted.
- **Who can respond:** anyone, citizens, companies, trade associations, NGOs, academics, public authorities, in any of the 24 official EU languages, without needing to register on the EU Transparency Register (though organisations are strongly encouraged to). An EU Login account is required to submit feedback; submissions are published (with an option to submit anonymously to the public).

## What the portal actually does

"Have Your Say" is not one consultation type but a single search-and-submission front end for **four distinct instruments** that the Commission uses at different points in the policy cycle, described in the Better Regulation Guidelines. Understanding which instrument is live on a given file, and what its deadline is, is the single most useful piece of information for a policy professional tracking an EU dossier.

### 1. Call for evidence / inception impact assessment (earliest stage)

Before drafting a new law, or before reviewing an existing one, the Commission publishes a short document describing the problem, the policy options under consideration, and its planned consultation strategy. For **new initiatives** this is called a "call for evidence" (which doubles as the inception impact assessment). For **evaluations or fitness checks of existing legislation**, the equivalent document is the "combined evaluation roadmap / inception impact assessment", a format introduced in the November 2021 Better Regulation Guidelines reform that merged the old separate roadmap and inception impact assessment documents into one.

Both formats are open for feedback for a minimum of 4 weeks. On major files the call for evidence is frequently launched **at the same time** as a 12-week public consultation questionnaire, with the two closing on different dates (for example the Commission's 2026 post-2030 renewable energy framework review ran a 4-week call for evidence to 16 April 2026 alongside a 12-week public consultation to 12 June 2026).

This is the earliest point at which the Commission's thinking on a file becomes public, and the earliest point at which outside evidence can still shape the scope of the eventual proposal.

### 2. Public consultation (pre-proposal, evidence-gathering stage)

Once an initiative is confirmed to need an impact assessment, evaluation or fitness check, the Commission runs a structured **questionnaire-based public consultation**, open for a **minimum of 12 weeks**, available in principle in all 24 official EU languages. Where the call for evidence gathers general views on whether and how to act, the public consultation is designed to gather structured, comparable evidence, on impacts, costs, benefits and preferred options, that feeds directly into the impact assessment or evaluation report.

The Commission must publish a **factual summary report** within 8 weeks of the consultation closing, and a fuller **synopsis report** annexed to the impact assessment or evaluation staff working document, explaining which views were received and how they were, or were not, reflected in the final proposal.

### 3. Feedback on the adopted legislative proposal (post-adoption, pre-negotiation stage)

After the College of Commissioners formally adopts a legislative proposal and it is published, the Commission opens an **8-week feedback window** on the text itself, run before or in parallel with the start of negotiations in the European Parliament and the Council. This is distinct from the earlier public consultation: the proposal text is now fixed, and stakeholders are reacting to the actual legal drafting rather than to policy options.

Every comment submitted in this window is compiled and **forwarded to both co-legislators**, the European Parliament and the Council, under the 2016 Interinstitutional Agreement on Better Law-Making commitment that the three institutions keep each other informed of stakeholder feedback they receive. This 8-week window runs in parallel with the 8-week period national parliaments have to submit reasoned opinions on subsidiarity grounds under Protocol No. 2 to the Treaties.

### 4. Feedback on draft delegated and implementing acts (post-legislative, technical stage)

Once a regulation or directive has empowered the Commission to adopt delegated acts (Article 290 TFEU) or implementing acts (Article 291 TFEU), draft texts are published on Have Your Say for a **4-week feedback period** before formal adoption, alongside their parallel scrutiny in the relevant comitology committee (see `comitology_and_delegated_acts_registers`). This is often the last point at which a technical or sectoral stakeholder can flag a drafting problem before a measure becomes binding, and is frequently overlooked because the political story has moved on by the time delegated or implementing acts appear.

### 5. Feedback on existing law ("Have Your Say: Simplify!")

A separate strand of the portal lets anyone flag an existing EU law they consider burdensome, unclear or outdated. This feeds the Commission's simplification and burden-reduction work (successor to the REFIT programme) and, historically, the Fit for Future Platform's opinion-drafting process (`refit_fit_for_future_platform`). Its relevance is broader than it first appears: any policy professional who spots an implementation problem in force can register it here, independent of any live legislative file.

## How to monitor and respond, step by step

1. **Search the portal by policy area, keyword, document type ("call for evidence", "public consultation", "draft act") or closing date** at https://ec.europa.eu/info/law/better-regulation/have-your-say/initiatives_en. Filters let you isolate, for example, only open draft delegated acts, which are otherwise easy to miss.
2. **Register on the EU Transparency Register** if responding on behalf of an organisation (see `transparency_register_eu` for the registration mechanics); this is recommended but not legally required to submit feedback.
3. **Create an EU Login account** to submit a response; anonymous publication of your own submission (name withheld, content published) is available as an option.
4. **Read the underlying document, not just the questionnaire.** A call for evidence or inception impact assessment states the Commission's working assumptions about the problem and the policy options it is already leaning towards; a public consultation questionnaire is often narrower than the underlying issue, so a free-text position paper submitted alongside the questionnaire carries more weight than tick-box answers alone.
5. **Note the closing date and submit before Brussels midnight (CET/CEST) on that date**, and check the confirmation email plus your submission's appearance on the public page.
6. **Track what happens next**: the factual summary report (within 8 weeks of a public consultation closing) and the synopsis report (annexed to the impact assessment or evaluation, published alongside the eventual proposal) show exactly how the Commission used the input received.

## Why early engagement beats late engagement

The four feedback windows are not equally powerful. By the time a legislative proposal is adopted and open for its 8-week post-adoption feedback, the legal text, the policy options chosen, and the balance of the impact assessment are already fixed: feedback at that stage can flag drafting problems and inform the European Parliament and Council's negotiating position, but it cannot reopen the underlying policy choice. Feedback on a draft delegated or implementing act is narrower still: the primary legislation's parameters are locked, and only the technical detail is open.

The **call for evidence / inception impact assessment stage** is where the problem definition, the list of policy options under consideration, and the choice of whether the Commission acts at all are still genuinely open. A submission at this stage that supplies data, cites a national precedent, or flags an option the Commission's initial framing omitted can change what ends up in the eventual public consultation questionnaire and, from there, in the impact assessment itself. This is why experienced Brussels policy professionals prioritise scanning calls for evidence over any later stage: it is the only window where the shape of the eventual law is still being decided rather than merely refined.

Practically, this means: track the Commission Work Programme and DG-level roadmaps for initiatives relevant to your sector months before a call for evidence appears (see `better_regulation_enforcement_communication` for how the annual Better Regulation reporting cycle signals upcoming files), set a recurring portal search on your policy area, and treat "call for evidence closing in 4 weeks" as a higher-priority alert than "public consultation closing in 12 weeks", precisely because the shorter window closes faster and carries more leverage per submission received.
