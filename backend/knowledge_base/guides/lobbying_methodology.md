## The 6-Step Advocacy Process

### Step 1: Policy Mapping

**What it is:** Identifying all relevant EU initiatives that could impact your interests.

**How to do it:**
1. Search EUR-Lex for existing legislation in your sector
2. Check the Commission's legislative planning (CWP - Commission Work Programme)
3. Monitor public consultations on "Have Your Say" portal
4. Review European Parliament committee agendas
5. Track Council presidency priorities

**Key questions to answer:**
- What laws currently govern my sector?
- What's being proposed or revised?
- Which DGs and committees have jurisdiction?
- What's the timeline?

**Brubru advantage:** Use the EU Law Comply feature to scan your sector instantly.

### Step 2: Stakeholder Mapping

**What it is:** Identifying who decides, who influences, and who can help or hinder your goals.

**The XY Matrix:**
- **X-axis:** Attitude (Hostile → Neutral → Supportive)
- **Y-axis:** Influence (Low → High)

**Priority quadrants:**
1. **High influence + Hostile** = Convert or neutralise (highest priority)
2. **High influence + Supportive** = Mobilise as champions
3. **Low influence + Supportive** = Potential coalition partners
4. **Low influence + Hostile** = Monitor, don't engage

**Key actors to map:**
- Rapporteur and shadow rapporteurs
- Relevant commissioners and cabinet members
- Council working party chairs
- Key MEPs on committees
- Permanent Representation contacts
- NGOs and industry associations in your space

See: `stakeholder_mapping.md` for detailed framework.

### Step 3: Position Development

**What it is:** Crafting your message and policy ask in a way that resonates with EU institutions.

**The winning formula:**
```
PROBLEM (EU-framed) + EVIDENCE + SOLUTION + EU BENEFIT = Compelling Position
```

**Rules for effective positions:**
1. **Lead with EU interests, not yours.** Frame everything as serving single market, competitiveness, consumer protection, or strategic autonomy
2. **Be specific.** "Amend Article 5(2)(b)" beats "We have concerns about Article 5"
3. **Provide legal language.** Draft the amendment you want to see
4. **Cite impact data.** Jobs, SMEs affected, trade figures
5. **Anticipate counter-arguments.** Address them preemptively

**Position paper structure:**
1. Executive summary (1 paragraph)
2. Context (why this matters to EU)
3. Key concerns (max 3)
4. Specific recommendations (with draft text)
5. About your organisation (brief credibility statement)

**Length:** Maximum 2 pages. Nobody reads more.

---

### Step 4: Coalition Building

**What it is:** Amplifying your voice by aligning with others who share your position.

**Why it matters:**
- Single voices are easily dismissed
- Coalitions demonstrate broad support
- Coalition members provide political cover to MEPs and officials
- Cross-border coalitions are especially powerful in EU context

**Coalition strategies:**
1. **Industry associations** - Join or form sector alliances
2. **National federations** - Leverage member state industry bodies
3. **SME alliances** - SME impact carries political weight
4. **Cross-sector** - Unusual alliances get attention (e.g., tech + civil society)
5. **Academic backing** - Expert endorsements add credibility

**Coalition management:**
- Agree on shared positions (not identical positions)
- Decide on joint vs. complementary actions
- Establish speaking order for meetings
- Share intelligence and contacts

---

### Step 5: Strategic Engagement

**What it is:** Delivering your message to the right people at the right time through the right channels.

**Engagement hierarchy (effectiveness):**
1. **Direct meetings** - Highest impact, hardest to get
2. **Events & roundtables** - You control the framing
3. **Written submissions** - Formal record, basis for meetings
4. **Informal contacts** - Coffee, receptions, corridor chats
5. **Public campaigns** - Pressure tactic, use sparingly

**Timing is everything:**

| Phase | Who to Target | What to Do |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| Pre-proposal | Commission (DG + SG) | Input to impact assessment |
| Proposal published | EP rapporteur | Shape draft report |
| Committee stage | Shadow rapporteurs, coordinators | Push amendments |
| Plenary | Political groups | Vote recommendations |
| Trilogue | EP negotiating team + Council presidency | Red lines, compromise text |
| Implementation | Commission + Member States | Technical input, guidelines |

**Meeting best practices:**
- Request meetings early (2-3 weeks ahead)
- Prepare a 1-page leave-behind
- Bring 2-3 people maximum
- Make a specific ask
- Follow up within 48 hours with summary

---

### Step 6: Monitoring & Adaptation

**What it is:** Tracking progress and adjusting strategy based on how the file evolves.

**What to monitor:**
- Amendment submissions and committee votes
- Plenary voting lists
- Council working party meeting outcomes
- Trilogue calendar and negotiating boxes
- Press coverage and political statements
- Competitor/opponent activities

**Adaptation triggers:**
- Your position is losing support → Reassess messaging, find new allies
- Timeline accelerating → Intensify engagement
- New proposal elements emerge → Update position
- Key ally loses influence → Find new champion
- Political context shifts → Reframe argument

**After the vote:**
- Win or lose, debrief: What worked? What didn't?
- Thank supporters
- Plan for implementation phase
- Document contacts and lessons for next file

## Intervention Windows

The EU legislative process has specific moments when influence is possible. Miss them, and the train has left the station.

### Commission Phase (Highest Influence Potential)

**Impact Assessment consultation:** 12 weeks to shape problem definition
**Public consultation:** Formal input opportunity, gets referenced in proposal
**Expert groups:** If you can get a seat, direct access to drafting
**Informal dialogue:** DG officials are accessible before publication

> **Pro tip:** Once the Commission publishes a proposal, 80% of the text is locked. Get in early.

### Parliament Phase (Highest Visibility)

**Draft report:** Rapporteur's first text, window to influence structure
**Amendment deadline:** MEPs submit changes, this is your moment
**Committee vote:** Amendments adopted or rejected
**Plenary amendments:** Last chance for text changes

> **Pro tip:** Target shadow rapporteurs from multiple groups. They form the committee majority.

### Council Phase (Most Opaque)

**Working parties:** Technical experts negotiate text
**COREPER:** Ambassadors resolve political issues
**Council meeting:** Ministers adopt or refer back

> **Pro tip:** Work through your national Permanent Representation. They're your entry point to Council.

### Trilogue Phase (Final Stretch)

**Negotiating boxes:** Track what's still open vs. closed
**Political trilogues:** Final compromises
**Lawyer-linguist review:** Last technical window

> **Pro tip:** Once trilogue reaches "agreed text," it's effectively over.

## Timing Strategies

### The EU Calendar

**September - December:** Autumn legislative push
**January - March:** New initiatives, budget cycle
**April - June:** Pre-summer sprint, files cleared before recess
**July - August:** Recess (but Council working parties continue)

**Election cycles matter:**
- 18 months before EP elections: Window closing
- Post-election 6 months: New Parliament, everything restarts
- Commission mid-term: Priorities reassessed

### Speed vs. Depth Trade-offs

**Fast-track response:** When proposal surprises you
- Immediate stakeholder coalition outreach
- Rapid position paper (even imperfect)
- Early rapporteur contact

**Long-game influence:** When you see it coming
- Expert group participation
- Relationship building before file emerges
- Thought leadership (studies, events)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Engaging too late** - After committee vote, options are limited
2. **Overloading messages** - Three asks maximum, ideally one
3. **Ignoring the Council** - Half the legislature, often overlooked
4. **Position without solution** - Complaints without alternatives fail
5. **Underestimating procedure** - OLP vs. special procedures changes everything
6. **Burning bridges** - Today's opponent may be tomorrow's ally
7. **Focusing only on "your" MEPs** - Coalition building requires cross-group work
8. **Ignoring national level** - Member states implement and can influence Council
