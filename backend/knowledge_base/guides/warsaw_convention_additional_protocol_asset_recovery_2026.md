# EU signs the Additional Protocol to the Warsaw Convention on asset recovery and money laundering (14 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What:** on **14 October 2026** the EU signed the **Additional Protocol to the Council of Europe Convention on Laundering, Search, Seizure and Confiscation of the Proceeds from Crime and on the Financing of Terrorism**, known as the **Warsaw Convention**. The EU ambassador **Alexandre Stutzmann** signed for the Union at the Council of Europe in Strasbourg.
- **Content (Commission summary):** strengthened rules on **freezing and confiscation**, **asset management**, **police and judicial cooperation** and **prevention of money laundering**, to make cross-border asset recovery more effective across Council of Europe states.
- **Also signed that day:** **Georgia, Greece, Hungary, Luxembourg, Monaco, Montenegro, San Marino and the United Kingdom**.
- **History:** Council authorised the Commission to negotiate on **13 June 2024**; the Council of Europe Committee of Ministers **adopted the Protocol on 15 May 2026**.
- **Mixed agreement:** it covers EU and Member State competences, so the **EU and the Member States** must each sign.
- **Not in force:** signature is not ratification. **Next:** the Council decides on **conclusion** of the Protocol **and of the Warsaw Convention itself**, which the EU signed in **2009** but **has never ratified**; both need the **consent of the European Parliament**.
- **Why (Commission's figure):** **only 1% of criminal assets are recovered in the EU.** The EU's own rules: the 2024 Asset Recovery and Confiscation Directive (EU) 2024/1260, the 2018 Regulation on mutual recognition of freezing and confiscation orders (EU) 2018/1805, and the 2024 anti-money laundering package; the ProtectEU internal security strategy.
- **What was NOT read:** the Protocol text and its article numbers.

## How to answer
"What did the EU sign on asset recovery in October 2026?": on 14 October 2026 it signed the Additional Protocol to the Council of Europe's Warsaw Convention (freezing, confiscation, asset management, cooperation, AML). It is not in force for the EU: the Council must conclude it, with Parliament's consent, together with the Warsaw Convention itself, which the EU signed in 2009 but never ratified.
