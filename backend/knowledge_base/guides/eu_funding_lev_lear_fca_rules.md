# LEV / LEAR / FCA Rules: Legal Entity Validation + LEAR Appointment + Financial Capacity Check

## QUICK FACTS

- **Participant Identification Code (PIC)**: Unique identifier for all entities applying for EU funding; validated once for lifetime use across all EU programmes via the Funding and Tenders Portal Participant Register
- **Legal Entity Validation (LEV)**: One-time central validation of entity existence, legal form, address, and legal capacity to enter financial relations with the EU; performed by the Central Validation Service (REA)
- **LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative)**: Single mandatory point of contact for each participant; appointed by the legal representative; must be employed by the participant and able to commit the organisation legally; responsible for updating legal data and managing access rights in the Participant Register
- **LEAR appointment is mandatory**: Without a validated LEAR, the participant cannot update legal entity data in the Participant Register or sign contracts electronically in the Funding and Tenders Portal
- **Financial Capacity Check (FCA)**: Assessment of whether a beneficiary has adequate financial resources to carry out the action; required when EU contribution to a beneficiary exceeds EUR 500,000 (or as specified per call)
- **Required documents for LEV**: Signed legal entity identification form, valid VAT document (not older than 1 year), registration extract (1 year max) for private entities, identity documents for natural persons
- **LEAR appointment documents**: LEAR appointment letter (signed by legal representative and LEAR), valid identity documents (passport or ID card, both sides) of legal representative and LEAR, document(s) proving the legal representative's empowerment
- **FCA documents**: Audited or certified financial statements for the most recent closed accounting period, including balance sheet, profit/loss statement and notes showing staffing and equity participations; self-certification of accounts validity may be accepted for small actions
- **Validation timeline**: Legal entity validation typically occurs during preparation of the first successful proposal; LEAR appointment can run in parallel but must be finalised before contract signature
- **Two-year rule (SME status)**: SME status depends on ceilings for two consecutive accounting periods; headcount and turnover must stay below ceilings for two periods to obtain status, and above ceilings for two periods to lose it
- **Account Administrator (AccAd)**: Optional delegated role; LEAR can delegate administrative tasks to one or more colleagues via the LEAR/Authorised User tab screen to reduce single-point-of-failure risk
- **Ex-post verification**: EU services reserve the right to carry out checks, audits or investigations at any time; false declarations may result in funding termination, recovery of undue amounts, financial penalties, administrative sanctions, or exclusion from future EU funding
- **Maintenance of PIC and LEAR data**: Participant must immediately update the Participant Register if legal situation changes (name, address, legal form, status); LEAR must be replaced immediately if no longer able to exercise the role (e.g. job change, dismissal, death)

## Participant Identification Code (PIC)

### PIC Overview

Every entity applying for EU funding through the Funding and Tenders Portal requires a unique Participant Identification Code (PIC). The PIC is assigned upon successful legal entity validation and serves as the entity's permanent identifier across all EU funding programmes and calls.

### PIC Validity and Reuse

Once validated, the PIC remains valid for:

- All ongoing applications and contracts within the system
- All future EU funding actions (grants, procurements, prizes, contribution agreements)
- Both low-value and high-value contracts

Re-registration or re-validation is not required when applying to new calls or transitioning to other funding programmes. This eliminates repeated administrative burden for repeat applicants.

### Who Assigns the PIC

The Central Validation Service (housed within the European Research Executive Agency, REA) assigns and manages the Participant Identification Code upon successful legal entity validation. Entities do not choose their own PIC; it is generated automatically upon approval.

## Legal Entity Validation (LEV)

### Purpose and Scope

Legal entity validation is a mandatory requirement under the EU Financial Regulation (2018/1046, Articles 128 and 147). Its primary purpose is to:

1. Verify that the participant exists as a legal entity capable of entering into financial relations with the EU
2. Confirm the correctness of legal data (legal form, address, official name)
3. Record specific legal statuses used in EU funding programmes (SME, research organisation, NGO, international organisation, etc.)

The validation applies consistently across all call types (grants, procurements, prizes, contribution agreements) and is independent of the specific funding programme or call.

### Types of Legal Entities

#### Natural Persons

Natural persons (including self-employed persons and freelancers) are recognised as legal entities by default. However, the Central Validation Service will refuse validation of persons unable to legally enter into contractual relations with the EU (e.g. minors).

#### Branches

Branches do not have legal personality distinct from their mother-company and cannot be validated as independent entities. The mother-company (registered in its country of incorporation) is the legal entity for validation purposes.

#### Foreign Companies

Companies registered in a country under a special status (e.g. 'société de droit étranger') are not recognised as legal entities. The mother-company in the country of incorporation is the entity for validation.

#### Entities Within a Parent Organisation

Departments, centres, offices, or units without legal personality (despite administrative or budgetary autonomy) are not legal entities. They must be validated as part of their parent organisation.

#### Entities Without Legal Personality (Exception)

By exception, certain private entities without formal legal personality may be eligible for EU grants if they demonstrate:

1. Representatives with the capacity to undertake legal obligations on the entity's behalf, AND
2. Equivalent financial and operational capacity to a legal person, including:
   - Patrimony/assets/capital separated from members/owners
   - Rules ensuring creditors can rely on such assets and are reimbursed prior to distribution among owners/members

This exception applies only to grants, not to procurement contracts.

### Specific Legal Statuses

In addition to basic legal entity validation, the Central Validation Service records the following specific legal statuses that affect eligibility or funding rates in certain EU programmes:

- **Private entity / Public body**: Mutually exclusive statuses; private bodies are incorporated under private law; public bodies are incorporated as public bodies by national law and governed by public law
- **Non-profit / For-profit**: Non-profit entities must have a legal or statutory obligation not to distribute profits to shareholders or members; profits must be reinvested in the entity's activities
- **Small or Medium Sized Enterprise (SME)**: Entities with fewer than 250 employees and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million and/or balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43 million
- **Mid-capitalisation enterprise (Mid-cap)**: Entities not meeting SME criteria but with up to 3,000 employees
- **Non-Governmental Organisation (NGO)**: Voluntary, independent from government, non-profit organisations that are not political parties or trade unions; must be validated as non-profit first
- **Civil Society Organisation (CSO)**: Third-sector organisations distinct from government and business; typically self-declared and accepted for private non-profit entities
- **International Organisation (IO)**: Intergovernmental organisations established under international public law, including specialised entities set up by international organisations
- **Research Organisation**: Non-profit entities with research and/or technological development as their main objective; supporting or financing research by others does not qualify
- **Secondary or Higher Education Establishment**: Entity recognised by national education system and able to deliver state-recognised diplomas; may be public or private

Self-declared statuses (SME, mid-cap, NGO, CSO, research organisation, education establishment) remain the responsibility of the participant; the Central Validation Service may verify them against supporting documents at any time.

### False Declarations and Consequences

If false declarations or self-assessments are discovered (whether intentional or unintentional), the EU services may:

- Withdraw and correct the validated status or statuses
- Terminate or withdraw ongoing grants, procurements, prizes or contribution agreements and recover undue amounts paid
- Impose financial penalties or administrative sanctions, including exclusion from future EU funding
- Inform the European Anti-Fraud Office (OLAF)

## LEAR Appointment (Responsibilities, Documents, Signature)

### Role and Responsibilities of the LEAR

The Legal Entity Appointed Representative (LEAR) is the single point of contact between the participant and the EU Funding and Tenders Portal. Upon validation, the LEAR is responsible for:

1. **Managing legal entity data**: Updating the participant's legal and other data in the Participant Register; notifying the Central Validation Service of any changes
2. **Access control**: Giving access rights to the Portal electronic exchange system to authorised personnel
3. **Signature management**: Entering and updating the names of:
   - **Legal representatives and signatories (LSIGNs)**: Persons authorised to commit the organisation legally by signing contracts and authorising amendments
   - **Financial signatories (FSIGNs)**: Persons authorised to sign cost statements on behalf of the organisation
4. **Continuity and stability**: Ensuring the role is fulfilled reliably; immediately replacing themselves if they can no longer exercise the role

The LEAR can delegate administrative tasks to one or more Account Administrators (AccAds) if needed, reducing single-point-of-failure risk.

### Position and Eligibility of the LEAR

#### Employment Requirement

The LEAR must be someone employed within the entity, most commonly in the central administrative services. They must be capable of ensuring the stability and continuity needed to fulfil their role and honour the participant's contractual obligations with the EU.

#### Natural Persons

Participants who are natural persons automatically hold the LEAR role; this role cannot be attributed to another person. However, the natural person LEAR may appoint an Account Administrator to assist with routine tasks.

#### Multiple LEAR Appointments

Each participant may appoint only one LEAR. The participant assumes full responsibility for choosing the LEAR. If the LEAR is no longer able to exercise the role (job change, dismissal, death, illness), they must be immediately replaced.

### Legal Representative (Appointment Authority)

The LEAR must be officially appointed by the participant's legal representative. The legal representative is a person duly authorised to act on behalf of the entity and to commit it entirely vis-a-vis third parties.

#### Who Can Be a Legal Representative

- **For-profit companies**: Directors, board members, or officers empowered to represent the entity outside day-to-day business
- **Non-profit organisations**: Board members, directors or officers with full representation powers
- **Public entities and ministries**: Ministers (by default), Deputy Ministers, State/General Secretaries and their deputies, and Directors of specific sectors (legal, financial, European/international relationships) are considered legal representatives without further verification
- **International organisations**: Persons designated in the treaty creating the organisation

#### Who Cannot Be a Legal Representative

- Persons tasked only with daily management cannot be recognised as legal representatives, even if they have broad operational authority
- Persons empowered to represent the entity only within financial or other specific limits
- Persons empowered to represent only a division, department or unit (unless the legal documents explicitly grant full entity-wide representation)

#### deputising persons

If the legal representative is temporarily absent or unable to sign, other persons empowered to act as legal representatives (deputising persons) are accepted by default. The Central Validation Service does not verify the reason for the temporary absence or the legitimacy of the deputy; this remains the participant's responsibility.

### LEAR Appointment Timing

The LEAR appointment process can run in parallel with the legal entity validation but can only be finalised once the participant's PIC is validated.

LEAR appointment is not a condition for submitting a proposal or tender in the Funding and Tenders Portal, but validation must be completed before the grant, procurement or contribution agreement can be signed.

### Documents Required for LEAR Appointment

The participant must upload scanned versions of the following documents via the Participant Register:

#### 1. LEAR Appointment Letter

- Completed, dated and signed by both the legal representative and the appointed LEAR
- Specifies LEAR roles, duties and access rights
- Includes the declaration of consent to the Funding and Tenders Portal Terms and Conditions
- Template available from the Commission

#### 2. Identity Documents

Integral copies of valid official identity documents (issued by national authorities):

- **For the legal representative**: Passport or national ID card (both sides for ID cards); driving licences accepted if recognised under applicable national law; card/badge issued by the participant is NOT acceptable
- **For the LEAR**: Passport or national ID card (both sides); driving licences accepted if recognised under applicable national law

Identity documents must be:

- Valid (not expired) at the time of LEAR appointment
- Integral copies (all sides or pages)
- NOT redacted or marked with deletions
- Contain clear photo and signature

#### 3. Document(s) Proving Empowerment of the Legal Representative

The document(s) must clearly:

- Indicate the role/function of the person as legal representative
- Identify and appoint the person by name and personal data

Documents vary by country, legal form and status:

- **Public entities**: Law, decree, official act, official gazette
- **International organisations**: Copy of the treaty establishing the organisation
- **Private entities**: Decision of assembly, official documents from council of administration or directors board, company registry excerpts

The documents must be valid (not expired) at the time the LEAR is appointed. In exceptional cases, a declaration of honour may be accepted if no other document exists.

#### Special Case: Natural Persons

Natural persons do not need to prove their empowerment but must still submit the documents listed above, signed by themselves alone. A copy of their ID documents is required.

#### Special Case: Ministries

For ministries:

- Ministers are considered legal representatives by default (no legal documents proving empowerment required)
- Deputy Ministers, State Secretaries/General Secretaries and their deputies are also recognised as legal representatives by default
- Directors of legal, financial or European/international affairs sectors are considered legal representatives by default
- Other roles (Director, Head of Department, Head of Unit) are considered to have limited representation powers and must provide legal documents proving full entity-wide empowerment
- Ministers may exceptionally submit a badge or service card instead of an ID document; this exception does NOT apply to other representatives

### Signatures on LEAR Documents

The LEAR appointment documents must be signed by persons who are legal representatives of the participant. Signatures can be:

- Handwritten signatures, OR
- Qualified electronic signatures within the meaning of Regulation 910/2014 (eIDAS); the Central Validation Service will verify that the signature is based on a valid qualified certificate and was created by a secure creation device (accepted trust service providers listed on eIDAS Dashboard)

#### Signature Requirements

- The names of the legal representative and LEAR must match exactly those in their official identity documents
- LEAR documents signed with acronyms, nicknames or initials that do not match identity documents will NOT be accepted
- If the entity's internal requirements mandate joint signature of more than one person to legally engage the entity, the participant is fully responsible for ensuring that requirement is satisfied before submission (the Central Validation Service will not verify internal signature requirements)

## Financial Capacity Check (FCA) -- When, Threshold, Methodology

### When the FCA is Required

The Financial Capacity Check is required when:

- The EU contribution to a single beneficiary exceeds EUR 500,000 in most Horizon Europe calls, OR
- The call specifications set a different threshold for FCA (always check the call text for the precise limit)

The FCA is conducted before the grant agreement is signed to verify that the beneficiary has adequate financial resources to undertake the action.

### Who Performs the FCA

The Central Validation Service (REA) collects and assesses the financial documents needed for the FCA. The granting authority (the Commission, a Joint Undertaking, or an Executive Agency) makes the final decision on financial capacity approval.

### Financial Capacity Assessment Methodology

The assessment evaluates whether the beneficiary has:

1. **Adequate liquidity**: Cash, bank balances, short-term investments sufficient to cover potential cash flow shortfalls
2. **Stable financial position**: Assets exceeding liabilities, positive working capital
3. **Debt management capacity**: Existing debt levels manageable relative to resources
4. **Organisational resources**: Staff and expertise to execute the action

The assessment considers:

- The nature and duration of the action
- The beneficiary's track record with EU funding (if applicable)
- The action budget and expected cash flow
- Sectoral and market conditions

### Documents Required for FCA

For each beneficiary requiring an FCA, the following documents must be submitted:

#### 1. Audited or Certified Financial Statements

- Most recent closed accounting period (normally the last financial year)
- If the entity's accounting period does not coincide with the calendar year, provide the most recent closed period
- For newly established entities without closed accounts: a self-declaration including a business plan with bona fide estimates

Documents must include:

- Balance sheet statement
- Profit and loss account, clearly showing annual turnover from sales of goods and services
- Notes/annexes to financial statements indicating:
  - Staff headcount (expressed in annual working units)
  - Equity participations in other entities with percentage shareholding and voting rights
  - Details of sources and uses of funds

#### 2. VAT Document or VAT Exemption Proof

- Valid VAT registration document or proof of VAT exemption
- Not older than one year

#### 3. Audited or Certified Accounts Declaration

- Self-certification confirming the validity of the accounts submitted
- Acceptable for small actions below certain financial thresholds (typically EUR 430,000 EU contribution); above that threshold a Certificate on Financial Statements (CFS) or external audit opinion is required

#### 4. Proof of Legal Representative

- For private entities: Company registry extract proving the current legal representative
- For public entities: Official document confirming the legal representative's role

#### 5. For Entities with a Guarantor

If a guarantor is covering the action, documents must show:

- Details of the guarantor (legal entity validation, financial statements)
- Scope of the guarantee
- Guarantor's financial capacity to cover potential losses

#### 6. For Natural Persons and Sole Traders

- Personal identification document (copy of ID card or passport)
- Personal financial statement or tax assessment confirming income and assets
- If self-employed: tax return or business accounts for the most recent closed period

### Certificate on Financial Statements (CFS)

When the EU contribution to a beneficiary exceeds approximately EUR 430,000 in most calls, a **Certificate on Financial Statements** (CFS) or external audit opinion is required rather than self-certification. The CFS must be:

- Prepared by an independent external auditor (audit firm or state audit office)
- Dated and signed by the auditor
- Confirm the reliability and accuracy of the submitted financial statements
- Cover the most recent closed accounting period

### Duration of FCA Validity and Renewal

The FCA is valid for the duration of the grant agreement and any extensions. If the agreement is extended, a new FCA may be requested if:

- More than 18 months have passed since the original FCA
- Material changes in the beneficiary's financial situation have occurred
- The granting authority has concerns about ongoing financial capacity

## Account Administrator Role

### Definition and Purpose

An Account Administrator (AccAd) is a delegated representative to whom the LEAR delegates specific administrative tasks in the Funding and Tenders Portal and Participant Register. The Account Administrator role:

- Reduces single-point-of-failure risk if the LEAR is temporarily unavailable
- Allows day-to-day operational tasks to be handled by other staff members
- Does NOT transfer the LEAR's legal responsibility for the accuracy of data and updates

The LEAR remains fully accountable for all data in the Participant Register and all actions taken by Account Administrators.

### Appointment and Management

The LEAR appoints and manages Account Administrators via the 'LEAR/Authorised User' tab screen in the Participant Register. An Account Administrator may be assigned specific permissions (e.g. view-only, edit legal data, manage LSIGNs and FSIGNs).

The LEAR can appoint one or more Account Administrators; there is no limit on the number. However, the LEAR should ensure that internal segregation of duties and approval processes are maintained.

## Documents Required (Incorporation, VAT, Accounts)

### Incorporation / Legal Establishment Documents

#### For Private Entities

- **Registration extract** not older than one year from an official national register (company registry, business register, trade register)
- Documents older than one year may be accepted if they indicate a validity period (e.g. "valid for two years") expressly stated in the document, provided submission occurs within that validity window

#### For Public Bodies

- Copy of the act, law, decree or decision establishing the entity as a public body
- If no formal establishment act exists: any other official legal document proving public body status

#### For Natural Persons

- Integral copy (both sides, legible, in colour recommended) of a valid identity card or passport

#### For Entities Without Legal Personality (Exception)

- Copy of an official document attesting that representatives have the capacity to undertake legal obligations on the entity's behalf
- Copy of an official document proving financial and operational viability equivalent to a legal person, including:
  - Document showing patrimony/assets/capital separated from members/owners
  - Rules ensuring creditors can rely on such assets and are reimbursed before distribution to owners/members

### VAT Document

- Official VAT registration document issued by national tax authorities, OR
- Proof of VAT exemption (for organisations exempted from VAT by law)
- Not older than one year

If the entity is not registered for VAT, proof of exemption (e.g. official letter from tax authorities) is required.

### Financial Statements and Accounts

#### Standard Requirements

- Approved financial statements for the most recent closed accounting period
- Balance sheet statement
- Profit and loss account (showing annual turnover from sales of goods/services)
- Annexes/notes to financial statements including:
  - Staff headcount (annual working units)
  - Equity participations with shareholding percentages and voting rights
  - Other material information (debt, liabilities, provisions)

#### For Newly Established Entities

Entities that do not yet have closed accounts must submit:

- Self-declaration including a business plan with bona fide financial estimates for the period necessary to generate turnover

#### For Entities Without Turnover (Long Time-to-Market)

Entities with little or no turnover but demonstrating engagement in economic activity must submit:

- Approved financial statements showing investments and operational activity
- Self-declaration with business plan showing expected return and market entry timeline
- Evidence of investment made (capital expenditure, R&D spending)

#### For SME and Mid-Cap Status Verification (If Requested)

Additional documents:

- Graph showing complete ownership structure (all direct and indirect shareholders)
- Proof of direct and indirect shareholders and their shareholding percentages and voting rights
- Documents explaining ownership control and corporate governance (statutes, articles of association, shareholder agreements, memorandum of understanding)

#### Language

- Documents in any EU official language are acceptable
- Participants may be requested to provide English free (unofficial) translation
- Non-EU language documents must be accompanied by free English translation (certified translation may be requested if doubt exists)
- Participant assumes full responsibility for translation accuracy

## Validation Timeline

### Typical Sequence

1. **Proposal submission and evaluation**: Participant applies via the Funding and Tenders Portal without requiring prior validation
2. **Proposal selection**: Following evaluation, if the proposal is selected, the coordinator is invited to the Grant Agreement Preparation (GAP) phase
3. **LEV invitation**: Central Validation Service contacts the participant (via the Participant Register) requesting legal entity validation documents (if not already validated)
4. **LEAR appointment invitation**: In parallel or following LEV validation, the Central Validation Service invites LEAR appointment documents
5. **Document submission and assessment**: Participant submits documents via the Participant Register; the Service assesses completeness and validity
6. **Validation approval**: Upon approval, PIC is assigned and LEAR is validated in the Participant Register
7. **Contract signature**: Once validation is complete, the participant can sign the grant agreement electronically via the Portal

### Timeline Estimates

- **Legal Entity Validation**: 4-12 weeks from document submission (depending on document completeness and need for clarifications)
- **LEAR Appointment validation**: 2-4 weeks from document submission
- **Financial Capacity Check**: 4-8 weeks (if required)
- **Overall Grant Agreement Preparation (GAP)**: Target 8 months from call deadline to signature (as a Commission KPI)

### Clarification and Incomplete Submissions

The Central Validation Service may request clarifications or additional information if:

- Submitted documents do not match the participant's declarations
- Documents are outdated (e.g. VAT document or registration extract older than 1 year)
- Documents are illegible, incomplete, incorrect, contradictory or unclear
- Specific legal statuses (SME, non-profit, research organisation) require further verification

Participants are normally given a reasonable timeframe (typically 10-20 calendar days) to respond. If the participant does not respond in time, the Service may refuse validation or finalise it based on available information.

## Maintenance: Keeping the PIC + LEAR Data Current

### Participant Responsibility

Once a PIC is validated and a LEAR appointed, the participant assumes ongoing responsibility for keeping all data current in the Participant Register.

### Updating Legal Information

If the participant's legal situation changes, the participant must request an update in the Participant Register. Changes include:

- Legal form (e.g. conversion of a company type, merger, acquisition)
- Name of the organisation
- Address (registered office or headquarters)
- Legal status (e.g. change from for-profit to non-profit)
- SME status (if the participant moves above or below SME thresholds)

#### Update Process

- Requests to update validation must be submitted by the participant's LEAR
- Must be accompanied by supporting documents proving the change (e.g. company registry extract, deed of merger, new VAT certificate)
- The effective date of change is the date the act establishing the change enters into force (unless the legal act specifies another date)
- For SMEs and mid-caps, the effective date is the closure date of the accounting period showing the status change; for mergers/acquisitions leading to loss of SME status, the effective date is the ownership-change date

#### Impact on Ongoing Contracts

The practical consequences of changes depend on the specific funding programme rules. Changes to SME status, for example, may affect:

- Eligibility for SME-specific funding rates (higher percentages)
- Eligibility for future calls restricted to SMEs
- Statistical reporting and monitoring

Participants are responsible for updating this information promptly to ensure accuracy in statistics and eligibility checks.

### LEAR Replacement and Succession

The LEAR must be immediately replaced if they are no longer able to exercise the role. Reasons include:

- Job change or resignation from the organisation
- Dismissal
- Death
- Serious illness or disability
- Extended leave (more than 6 months)

#### LEAR Replacement Process

1. The participant's legal representative appoints a new LEAR
2. Submit the new LEAR appointment letter (same documents as initial appointment)
3. The Central Validation Service validates the new LEAR appointment (typically 2-4 weeks)

There should be no gap in LEAR coverage; the participant should plan for succession to avoid disruption. If no LEAR is appointed, the participant cannot update legal data or sign new contracts.

### Account Administrator Updates

If Account Administrators are appointed, the LEAR can:

- Add new Account Administrators
- Modify permissions of existing Account Administrators
- Remove Account Administrators

Changes are made in the 'LEAR/Authorised User' tab screen of the Participant Register and do not require Central Validation Service approval (the LEAR has autonomous control).

### LSIGNs and FSIGNs Management

The LEAR must keep the lists of:

- **LSIGNs (legal signatories)**: Persons authorised to sign contracts and amendments on behalf of the organisation
- **FSIGNs (financial signatories)**: Persons authorised to sign cost statements

updated in the Participant Register whenever signatories change. This is critical to prevent signature disputes or rejection of electronically signed documents.
