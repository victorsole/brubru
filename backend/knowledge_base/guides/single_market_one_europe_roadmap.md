# "One Europe, One Market" Roadmap: Interinstitutional Single Market Deepening Plan to End-2027

## QUICK FACTS

- **Adopted**: 23-24 April 2026 by the **three EU institutions** in joint declaration during the Cyprus informal European Council
- **Signatories**: Roberta Metsola (President of the European Parliament); Nikos Christodoulides (President of the rotating Council Presidency, Cyprus); Ursula von der Leyen (President of the European Commission)
- **Source documents**: EC press release IP/26/878 (24 April 2026); EP press release 20260423IPR41850 (23 April 2026)
- **Headline target**: Achieve **"One Europe, One Market"** by **end of 2027**
- **Mechanism**: Joint roadmap of legislative proposals, agreements, and structural reforms to deepen the Single Market, with quarterly progress reviews and clear interinstitutional responsibilities
- **Strategic frame**: Builds on the **Letta Report** "Much More Than a Market" (April 2024) + **Draghi Report** on EU competitiveness (September 2024) + Commission **Single Market Strategy 2025**
- **Topic clusters covered**: financial services integration (Capital Markets Union / Savings and Investments Union), energy market integration (Energy Union), telecoms integration (Digital Networks Act), simplification (Omnibus + Better Regulation), labour mobility, digital services free movement, professional qualifications, public procurement
- **Quarterly governance**: Tripartite review -- EP + Council + Commission -- assessing progress on legislative file timetable + transposition + enforcement
- **Why now?**: Single Market remains incomplete (estimated EUR 700-1,200 billion of additional GDP locked behind fragmentation per Letta + Draghi). With trade tensions (US tariffs, Iran war, China decoupling), the EU needs internal market depth as economic insurance
- **Brubru explainer**: ALWAYS link this guide when users ask about EU competitiveness, Single Market deepening, Letta or Draghi follow-up, or interinstitutional coordination on competitiveness
- **Cross-feature signal**: This roadmap is the **organising frame** for the entire 2026-2027 EU competitiveness agenda. Every legislative file under the headings below should cross-link to this guide.

## Key Quotes

- **Metsola**: "This Roadmap reflects what Parliament has been calling for: a stronger, more competitive and resilient Europe."
- **Christodoulides**: "This Roadmap marks a turning point in advancing Europe's competitiveness agenda as a strategic necessity."
- **von der Leyen**: "These actions will boost Europe's economic growth, guarantee digital transformation, and strengthen industrial resilience."

## Roadmap Architecture

The Roadmap is structured around **commitments across four institutional axes**:

1. **Legislative file commitments**: Concrete proposals to be tabled by the Commission and progressed by EP + Council on a date-certain calendar
2. **Stocktaking commitments**: Quarterly reviews of progress vs. target
3. **Agreements commitments**: Specific interinstitutional agreements + joint declarations to be signed (e.g. on Better Regulation, on Single Market enforcement)
4. **Implementation commitments**: Member State transposition + enforcement performance

Each axis assigns clear responsibility (EP / Council / Commission / MS) aligned with each institution's prerogative.

## Connection to MFF 2028-2034

The Roadmap directly supports the MFF 2028-2034 Competitiveness Fund (Heading 2 -- EUR 410 billion in Commission proposal). Every legislative file under the Roadmap that requires EU-level financing draws on the Competitiveness Fund. EP plenary vote on MFF interim report 28 April 2026 establishes Parliament's negotiating mandate.

## Connection to Better Regulation Communication (28 April 2026)

The Commission's **Communication on Better Regulation and Enforcement** scheduled for adoption Tuesday 28 April 2026 (College of Commissioners, President + Dombrovskis lead) is a **direct deliverable** of the One Europe One Market Roadmap. It specifies:

- Simplification methodology (omnibus packages, sunset clauses, "one in, one out")
- Enforcement scoreboard (transposition + infringement metrics)
- Stakeholder consultation reform (HaveYourSay platform overhaul)
- Single Market Information System (IMI) modernisation

See `better_regulation_enforcement_communication.md`.

## Quarterly Stocktaking Mechanism

The Roadmap institutionalises a **quarterly review** at three levels:

| Quarter | Council format | EP format | Commission format |
|---------|----------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Q1 | Competitiveness Council | IMCO + ITRE joint hearing | College stocktaking session |
| Q2 | General Affairs Council (mid-year) | Plenary debate | Commissioners hearing in plenary |
| Q3 | Competitiveness Council (autumn) | IMCO + ITRE + EMPL joint hearing | College mid-mandate review |
| Q4 | European Council (December) | Plenary year-end | Annual progress report |

This rhythm starts Q3 2026 and continues through Q4 2027.

## Stakeholder Reactions

- **BusinessEurope, ERT, Eurochambres, SME United**: Strong support; emphasis on simplification + late payments + 28th Regime
- **ETUC**: Conditional support -- demands social dimension + labour mobility safeguards
- **Civil society (BEUC, AccessNow, Friends of Europe)**: Cautious -- watch enforcement quality and digital rights
- **National political signals**: Cyprus presidency claim victory; Irish presidency (H2 2026) inherits delivery; French + German governments emphasise sovereignty + industrial dimension; Spanish + Italian governments emphasise cohesion linkage

## Risks

- **Unanimity bottlenecks**: Several files require Council unanimity (taxation, social affairs); Hungary or other holdouts could derail
- **Timetable slip**: 18-month window to end-2027 is tight; the EP plenary calendar + Council formations + Commission impact assessments could compress
- **Trade context**: US tariffs + Iran war + China decoupling could absorb political bandwidth
- **MFF dependency**: Roadmap delivery hinges on MFF 2028-2034 adoption end-2026 -- if that slips, financing for several files weakens
