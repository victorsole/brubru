# EU Annual Budgetary Procedure (Article 314 TFEU)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal basis**: Article 314 TFEU (annual budgetary procedure); Article 312 TFEU (MFF, multi-annual envelope that caps all annual budgets)
- **Annual calendar in brief**: Institutional estimates submitted by 1 July; Commission adopts Draft Budget (DB) by 1 September; Council adopts its position and notifies EP by 1 October; EP first reading (42-day window); if amendments adopted, Conciliation Committee convened within 10 days; 21-day conciliation; EP and Council each vote within 14 days of agreement; budget declared adopted by EP President
- **Budget 2026**: Procedure 2025/0210(BUD) -- General Budget of the European Union for the financial year 2026. OEIL page: `https://oeil.secure.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2025/0210%28BUD%29`
- **Budget 2027 (in progress)**: EP estimates of revenue and expenditure registered as 2025/2247(BUI) on 28 April 2026; Guidelines for 2027 Budget Section III procedure 2025/2246(BUI). Commission Draft Budget expected by 1 September 2026. BUDG committee page (2026 procedure): `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/budg/budgetary-procedures/2026-procedure`
- **Budget 2025 (preceding year)**: BUDG committee page: `https://www.europarl.europa.eu/committees/en/budg/budgetary-procedures/2025-procedure`
- **BUDG rapporteur structure**: Three roles per annual cycle -- (1) Section III rapporteur (Commission budget, the largest section, roughly 97% of total), (2) Other Sections rapporteur (Sections I-II and IV-X covering EP, Council, CJEU, ECA, EESC, CoR, Ombudsman, EDPS, EEAS), (3) Pilot Projects and Preparatory Actions (PP/PA) rapporteur tracking Parliament-initiated budget lines
- **Conciliation Committee**: 28 MEPs (reflecting BUDG political-group composition) + 28 Council representatives (Presidency representative + Member State permanent representatives or finance attachés); co-chaired by EP BUDG chair and rotating Presidency
- **Amending budgets (DABs)**: Commission submits Draft Amending Budgets during the financial year; each follows an accelerated version of Article 314 TFEU procedure; typically 6-12 DABs per year
- **Letters of Amendment (LOAs)**: Commission may supplement the Draft Budget before EP first reading (typically October-November) to update macro-economic forecasts or revenue estimates
- **Pilot Projects and Preparatory Actions**: Exclusive parliamentary instrument -- PPs max EUR 40 million each (EUR 40 million total/year); PAs max EUR 50 million each (EUR 100 million total/year); used to test new EU activities ahead of legislative proposals
- **Relationship to MFF**: Annual budgets must remain within the MFF commitment and payment ceilings set under Article 312 TFEU; the Interinstitutional Agreement on budgetary discipline governs flexibility instruments between the MFF ceiling and the annual budget
- **Own Resources Decision**: Annual revenues are governed by the Own Resources Decision (currently Decision 2020/2053); contributions from Member States calculated on GNI-based, VAT-based, and traditional own resources (customs duties); plastics levy added in 2021
- **Discharge**: Article 319 TFEU; ECA annual report published October/November following year; EP CONT committee leads; EP plenary spring vote (April/May of the year after the financial year). Closely linked to the annual budgetary cycle

## Article 314 TFEU Framework

Article 314 TFEU establishes the annual budgetary procedure as the EU's main fiscal governance mechanism. Unlike the Multiannual Financial Framework (which requires unanimity in Council under Article 312 TFEU), the annual budget follows a co-decision-like process in which the European Parliament and the Council of the EU are co-equal budgetary authorities.

The Treaty of Lisbon (in force 1 December 2009) consolidated and strengthened Parliament's budgetary powers. Before Lisbon, the annual budget was split into "compulsory expenditure" (mainly the CAP, over which Parliament had less control) and "non-compulsory expenditure". Article 314 TFEU abolished that distinction: Parliament now co-decides the full budget.

The procedure has a strict sequential calendar, and failure to agree triggers a provisional twelfths regime (Article 315 TFEU), under which each month the EU may spend up to one-twelfth of the previous year's appropriations. Provisional twelfths slow policy implementation and are politically costly for both institutions, creating a strong incentive to reach conciliation agreement.

The annual budget covers two types of appropriations:
- **Commitment appropriations (CA)**: legal pledges to fund future obligations (multi-year projects)
- **Payment appropriations (PA)**: actual cash transfers in the current financial year

The gap between CAs and PAs represents outstanding payment obligations (reste à liquider, or RAL), which accumulates when CAs are committed faster than PAs are disbursed.

## The Annual Calendar in Detail

The Article 314 TFEU calendar is set by treaty and cannot be unilaterally varied by either institution.

### Phase 1: Institutional Estimates (by 1 July)

Each EU institution prepares its own estimates of revenue and expenditure and submits them to the Commission by 1 July. The Commission consolidates these into a single draft. The EP's internal estimates are handled through a separate BUI-type procedure (see Budget 2027: 2025/2247(BUI)) voted in BUDG and then plenary.

### Phase 2: Commission Draft Budget (by 1 September)

The Commission adopts the Draft Budget by 1 September of the year preceding the financial year. The DB sets the Commission's proposed appropriations for all budget sections and serves as the starting point for interinstitutional negotiations.

The DB reflects:
- Agreed MFF ceilings
- Updated macro-economic forecasts
- Carry-overs from the previous year
- Commission legislative priorities
- Revenue forecasts under the Own Resources Decision

Letters of Amendment (LOAs) may follow in October-November to update forecasts before Parliament's first reading.

### Phase 3: Council Position (by 1 October)

The Council (ECOFIN, with preparatory work in the Budget Committee) adopts its position on the DB and notifies Parliament by 1 October. The Council position typically reduces commitment appropriations and adjusts payment appropriations relative to the Commission's DB, reflecting Member States' preference for fiscal restraint.

Council can reduce or increase specific budget lines; it cannot modify own resources under the annual procedure (own resources require a separate Own Resources Decision under Article 311 TFEU).

### Phase 4: EP First Reading (42-day window from notification)

Parliament has 42 days from receipt of the Council position to:
(a) do nothing -- in which case the Council position is deemed adopted and the budget passes
(b) approve the Council position -- budget adopted
(c) adopt amendments by majority of component MEPs

In practice, Parliament almost always adopts amendments. The lead is BUDG, with opinions from spending committees (AGRI, ENVI, ITRE, TRAN, REGI, etc.) on lines within their purview. Sectoral committees submit opinions via the associated committees procedure.

### Phase 5: Conciliation Committee (convened within 10 days)

If Parliament amends the Council position, the Conciliation Committee is convened within 10 days of the vote. The Committee has 21 days to agree on a joint text. It consists of:
- 28 MEPs (BUDG-led, reflecting political group proportions; typically chaired by BUDG committee chair)
- 28 Council representatives (Presidency representative plus delegation of finance attachés)
- Commission attends as broker; it may update the DB during conciliation via a LOA if new facts warrant

The 21-day period is rigid. Political negotiations run in parallel: trilateral meetings (trilogues) between the BUDG chair, the Presidency, and the Budget Commissioner form the core of conciliation dynamics.

If the Committee fails to agree within 21 days, the Commission must submit a new Draft Budget. This has occurred only once in post-Lisbon history (for Budget 2011, when a last-minute deal was struck after the formal conciliation period expired, requiring a technical additional week via Article 314(8) TFEU).

### Phase 6: Votes Following Conciliation

After conciliation agreement:
- Parliament has 14 days to confirm (simple majority) or reject (two-thirds majority of votes cast, representing majority of component MEPs)
- Council has 14 days to confirm or reject

If Parliament rejects the joint text, the Commission must submit a new DB (another full cycle begins). If Council rejects but Parliament approves, Parliament can confirm its own position definitively (majority of component MEPs + two-thirds of votes cast).

Once adopted, the EP President formally declares the budget adopted.

## BUDG Rapporteur Roles and Nomination Cycle

Each annual cycle, the BUDG committee nominates rapporteurs by political group rotation (D'Hondt-based allocation):

### Section III Rapporteur (Commission Budget)
Responsible for the largest and most politically significant section: the Commission's own programme spending across all headings. This rapporteur leads the conciliation delegation and is the chief interlocutor with the Council Presidency. Role typically falls to large groups (EPP, S&D, Renew) alternating by seniority.

### Other Sections Rapporteur
Covers the budgets of all other EU institutions: EP (Section I), European Council and Council (Section II), Court of Justice of the EU (Section IV), European Court of Auditors (Section V), Economic and Social Committee (Section VI), Committee of the Regions (Section VII), European Ombudsman (Section VIII), European Data Protection Supervisor (Section IX), and European External Action Service (Section X). This role is less politically prominent but requires close coordination with each institution's secretariat-general.

### Pilot Projects and Preparatory Actions (PP/PA) Rapporteur
Manages Parliament's exclusive right to initiate new budget lines via Pilot Projects (testing new activities, max EUR 40M each) and Preparatory Actions (precursor to legislative proposals, max EUR 50M each). The PP/PA rapporteur collects requests from all EP committees, assesses feasibility with the Commission (which must declare whether it can implement each PP/PA), and negotiates the final list during conciliation.

PP/PA instruments are a significant lever: they allow Parliament to fund activities before any legislative mandate exists and to signal political priorities to the Commission. Successful PAs often evolve into full EU programmes.

### Nomination Cycle
Rapporteurs are typically nominated in the first quarter of the year preceding the budget (for Budget 2027, nominations occur in early 2026 following the guidelines vote on procedure 2025/2246(BUI)). The D'Hondt allocation across political groups determines which group nominates each rapporteur; within-group candidate selection follows group rules. Shadow rapporteurs from each other group are nominated in parallel.

## Conciliation Committee: Composition and 21-Day Timeline

The Conciliation Committee is the central interinstitutional arena of the annual budgetary procedure.

**EP side (28 members):**
- BUDG members in proportion to political group size in Parliament
- Chaired by BUDG committee chair (currently in EP10: political group assignment varies by year)
- Supported by BUDG secretariat and EP Budget Service

**Council side (28 members):**
- Presidency representative (typically the Finance Minister of the rotating presidency)
- Supported by national permanent representatives' budget attachés and the Council Secretariat-General Budget Service

**Commission:**
- Budget Commissioner and DG BUDG Director-General attend as mediators; can propose additional modifications via an updated DB but cannot formally amend a DB already before conciliation without triggering procedural complications

**The 21-day timeline in practice:**

Days 1-7: Intensive trilateral meetings. Each side shares its opening position on the main spending divergences (typically: EP pushes higher payment appropriations, especially for cohesion and research; Council seeks lower totals and tighter commitments).

Days 8-14: Political-level negotiations. Budget Commissioner brokers. LOAs (Letters of Amendment) sometimes used to accommodate new revenue data. Working-party level agrees non-contentious lines.

Days 15-21: Final political package. Agreement typically crystallises in the last 48-72 hours under time pressure. The joint text must be formally signed by co-chairs within the 21-day deadline.

**Post-conciliation votes (14 days each):**
Both institutions must vote to confirm the joint text. In practice, BUDG recommends confirmation and plenary follows. Council confirmation is by qualified majority.

## Amending Budgets (DABs) and Letters of Amendment

### Draft Amending Budgets (DABs)

The Commission submits DABs during the financial year to amend the adopted budget in light of:
- Updated macro-economic or revenue forecasts
- Emergency needs (natural disasters, refugee crises)
- Carry-over requests
- New legislative acts requiring fresh appropriations
- Member State requests for targeted interventions

A typical financial year sees 6-12 DABs. Each follows an abbreviated version of Article 314 TFEU: Council position, EP vote (usually without full conciliation if the amounts are uncontested). The Commission may propose "fast-track" or "urgent" DABs for humanitarian or crisis needs.

Key examples:
- **EUSF (European Union Solidarity Fund) mobilisation**: DABs are the standard vehicle for mobilising the Solidarity Fund following major natural disasters (floods, earthquakes). The Commission typically proposes a DAB within weeks of a disaster assessment.
- **Reserve for aid to Türkiye / Ukraine**: Additional emergency appropriations frequently require DABs during the year.
- **Positive revenue surprise**: When customs duty receipts or fines exceed forecast, a DAB adjusts payment appropriations upward (reducing Member State GNI contributions proportionally).

### Letters of Amendment (LOAs)

LOAs are technical modifications the Commission may submit to its own Draft Budget before Parliament completes its first reading (typically in October-November). LOAs may update:
- Agricultural market price forecasts (the largest LOA category historically)
- Revenue estimates following updated GNI data
- Emergency spending requirements that arose after the DB was adopted

LOAs are folded into the Council position or Parliament's amendments and then negotiated during conciliation.

## Brubru Tracking Angles

The annual budgetary procedure touches every EU policy area. Key tracking angles for public-affairs professionals:

**By sector:**
- **Research and innovation** (Horizon Europe appropriations): ITRE committee opinion on BUDG; changes to R&D payment appropriations signal real commitment levels vs MFF ceiling pledges
- **Cohesion and regional policy** (ERDF, ESF+, Cohesion Fund): REGI and EMPL committee opinions; payment appropriations vs commitments gap (RAL) is a standing controversy between EP and Council
- **Agriculture** (CAP direct payments, rural development): AGRI committee opinion; LOAs most common in agriculture (price volatility); CAP's share of total budget is largest single item
- **Migration and borders** (AMIF, BMVI, Frontex budget): LIBE committee opinion; politically sensitive given migration policy deadlock
- **Climate and environment** (LIFE, Just Transition Fund): ENVI committee opinion; climate-spending tracking under 35% MFF target (Budget 2027 will face first mandatory MFF climate-tag review)
- **Defence** (EDF, SAFE, military mobility): ITRE and AFET opinions; unprecedented budget growth since 2022

**By instrument:**
- **Pilot Projects**: Track which committees successfully introduce PPs; PPs in digital, health, and defence have highest conversion rates to full programmes
- **Reserve lines**: Budget reserves (Globalisation Adjustment Fund, Solidarity Fund reserve, Emergency Aid Reserve) signal where Council and EP expect crisis pressure
- **Margin vs MFF ceiling**: The annual budget is typically 2-5% below the MFF commitment ceiling; the unused margin is a political bargaining chip

**By timeline:**
- **September**: Commission DB published -- first comprehensive annual numbers; compare against Commission Work Programme priorities
- **October-November**: Council position + EP committee opinions -- divergence map becomes clear; LOAs filed
- **November-December**: Conciliation -- peak lobbying window; specific budget lines still movable during the 21 days

## Relationship to MFF and Own Resources

### MFF as Multi-Annual Envelope (Article 312 TFEU)

The MFF sets binding annual ceilings on commitment and payment appropriations for each heading. Annual budgets may not exceed these ceilings. The current MFF (2021-2027, Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2020/2093) expires at the end of 2027; the next MFF (2028-2034, proposal COM(2025) 570 final) is under negotiation.

The relationship is hierarchical: the MFF governs (unanimity in Council + EP consent), and annual budgets implement (co-decision-equivalent via Article 314 TFEU). If the MFF negotiation fails to conclude before year-end, annual budgets for 2028+ operate under provisional MFF "twelfths" of the last agreed MFF year, creating severe implementation disruption.

**Flexibility instruments** between the MFF ceiling and the annual budget include:
- Global Margin for Commitments: unused MFF margins carried forward from previous years
- Flexibility Instrument: up to EUR 915 million/year for unexpected needs (not assignable to a specific heading)
- Emergency Aid Reserve: EUR 1.2 billion/year for non-EU-country crises
- EU Solidarity Fund: up to EUR 1 billion/year for natural disasters

### Own Resources Decision (Article 311 TFEU)

Revenue side of the EU budget is governed separately from expenditure. The Own Resources Decision (currently 2020/2053) sets the contribution rules for Member States. New own resources proposed under MFF 2028-2034 (ETS share, CBAM revenue, e-waste levy, corporate resource, tobacco excise) require a new Own Resources Decision, which must be ratified by all Member States' national parliaments, adding a constitutional-level constraint to budget reform.

Annual budgets do not modify own resources. If revenue exceeds expenditure in a given year, the surplus is returned to Member States as a negative contribution in the following year's budget.

### Discharge (Article 319 TFEU)

Following each financial year, Parliament exercises discharge: the EP CONT committee reviews the European Court of Auditors' annual report and specific observations, then recommends (or refuses) discharge to the full plenary. Discharge is political oversight, not a court proceeding, but refusal has significant reputational consequences. See sibling guide `eu_discharge_procedure_cont` (planned) for full coverage.
