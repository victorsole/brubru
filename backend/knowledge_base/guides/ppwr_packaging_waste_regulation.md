## Overview

The PPWR is the EU's first Regulation to cover the entire life-cycle of packaging, from design through to collection, sorting, recycling and re-use. It replaces Directive 94/62/EC, which had governed EU packaging policy for three decades, with a single directly applicable Regulation designed to end the fragmentation caused by 27 different national transpositions. Packaging represents roughly 40% of the plastics and 50% of the paper used in the Union, and around 36% of municipal solid waste, so the Regulation treats packaging design, substances of concern, recyclability, recycled content, re-use and waste management as one connected policy problem rather than separate national rulebooks.

The Regulation sits inside the European Green Deal and the Circular Economy Action Plan (CEAP), which committed to making all packaging reusable or recyclable in an economically viable way by 2030. It complements the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781), which does not address packaging as a specific product category, and interacts closely with the Waste Framework Directive (2008/98/EC), the Single-Use Plastics Directive (EU) 2019/904 (which prevails as lex specialis for the single-use plastic items it covers) and the Market Surveillance Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, which the PPWR now formally brings packaging under.

Structurally, the PPWR combines binding EU-wide design and substance rules (recyclability grades, recycled-content targets, PFAS and heavy-metal limits) with binding waste-management targets placed on Member States (recycling rates, waste prevention rates, deposit-and-return-system collection rates) and binding operational obligations placed directly on economic operators (empty-space limits, reuse targets, refill offers, extended producer responsibility). It is one of the most detailed and article-dense pieces of EU environmental legislation adopted to date.

## Chapter I – General provisions (Articles 1-4)

Article 1 sets the subject matter: requirements for the entire life-cycle of packaging on environmental sustainability and labelling, plus extended producer responsibility, waste prevention, and collection and treatment (including recycling) of packaging waste. Article 2 gives the Regulation the widest possible scope: it applies to all packaging regardless of the material used, whatever sector generates it (industry, retail, offices, services, households), and to all packaging waste. Directive 2008/68/EC on the transport of dangerous goods prevails in the event of a conflict.

Article 3 contains 71 numbered definitions, the backbone of the whole Regulation. Key ones: packaging is defined broadly enough to include service packaging (items filled at the point of sale), disposable dispensing items, and tea/coffee bags and single-serve system units (both are explicitly brought inside the definition of packaging because of their disposal behaviour, even though they are consumed with the product). Sales packaging, grouped packaging and transport packaging replace the old primary/secondary/tertiary terminology. The producer is defined around who first makes packaging or packaged products available on the territory of a given Member State – a company importing a packaged product from another Member State or a third country and supplying it locally becomes the producer for extended-producer-responsibility purposes. Composite packaging is defined as two or more materials that cannot be separated manually, unless one material is an insignificant part (no more than 5% of total mass).

Article 4 establishes free movement: Member States cannot prohibit, restrict or impede the placing on the market of packaging that complies with the sustainability, labelling and information requirements of Articles 5 to 12, and may not maintain additional national sustainability or information requirements that conflict with the Regulation.

## Chapter II – Sustainability requirements (Articles 5-11)

**Article 5 – Substances in packaging.** The sum of the concentrations of lead, cadmium, mercury and hexavalent chromium present in packaging or packaging components must not exceed 100 mg/kg, continuing the limit already established under Directive 94/62/EC (with the existing derogations in Commission Decisions 2001/171/EC for glass and 2009/292/EC for plastic crates and pallets kept in force until replaced by delegated acts). From 12 August 2026, food-contact packaging may not be placed on the market if it contains per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) at or above: 25 ppb for any single targeted PFAS; 250 ppb for the sum of targeted PFAS; or 50 ppm total fluorine (with a documentation obligation on the supply chain to prove the source of any fluorine content). By 31 December 2026 the Commission, assisted by ECHA, must report on substances of concern in packaging generally, and by 12 August 2030 it must evaluate whether the PFAS restriction needs amending or repealing to avoid overlap with REACH, the Food Contact Materials Regulation or the POPs Regulation.

**Article 6 – Recyclable packaging.** All packaging placed on the market must be recyclable. Recyclability is expressed through recyclability performance grades A, B or C (Table 3 of Annex II), combining a "design for recycling" assessment and a "recycled at scale" assessment. The design-for-recycling condition applies from 1 January 2030 (or 24 months after the relevant delegated acts enter into force, whichever is later); the recycled-at-scale condition applies from 1 January 2035. From 1 January 2030, packaging that does not reach at least Grade C cannot be placed on the market at all. **From 1 January 2038, Grade C packaging is banned outright – only Grade A or B packaging may still be placed on the market.** The Commission must adopt the delegated acts establishing design-for-recycling criteria and grades by 1 January 2028. Innovative packaging (new materials delivering demonstrable environmental benefit) can be placed on the market for up to five years without meeting the criteria, subject to notification to the competent authority. Exemptions apply to immediate/outer medicinal packaging, contact-sensitive medical-device packaging, dangerous-goods transport packaging and certain lightweight-material sales packaging (wood, cork, textile, rubber, ceramic, porcelain, wax).

**Article 7 – Minimum recycled content in plastic packaging.** By 1 January 2030 (or three years after the relevant implementing act, whichever is later), the plastic part of packaging must contain a minimum percentage of post-consumer recycled content, calculated as an average per manufacturing plant and year:
- 30% for contact-sensitive PET packaging (except single-use beverage bottles)
- 10% for contact-sensitive non-PET plastic packaging (except single-use beverage bottles)
- 30% for single-use plastic beverage bottles
- 35% for all other plastic packaging

By 1 January 2040, those targets rise to:
- 50% for contact-sensitive PET packaging
- 25% for contact-sensitive non-PET plastic packaging
- 65% for single-use plastic beverage bottles
- 65% for all other plastic packaging

Exemptions apply to immediate/outer medicinal and veterinary packaging, contact-sensitive medical-device and in-vitro-diagnostic-device packaging, compostable plastic packaging, dangerous-goods transport packaging, infant/young-children food packaging, and plastic parts under 5% of a packaging unit's total weight. The Commission must adopt the calculation-and-verification methodology and recycling-technology sustainability criteria by 31 December 2026, and by 12 February 2032 must report on whether the 2030 targets worked and whether the 2040 targets are feasible.

**Article 8 – Biobased feedstock.** By 12 February 2028 the Commission must review the technological state of biobased plastic packaging and, where appropriate, propose sustainability requirements, targets, or the possibility of using biobased feedstock to meet the Article 7 recycled-content targets for contact-sensitive food packaging where suitable recycling technology is not yet available.

**Article 9 – Compostable packaging.** By 12 February 2028, permeable tea/coffee/beverage bags and non-permeable single-serve system units, plus sticky labels affixed to fruit and vegetables, must be compostable in industrially controlled conditions (and home-compostable where a Member State requires it). Member States may additionally require very lightweight and lightweight plastic carrier bags, and certain other packaging that was already subject to a national compostability requirement, to be compostable, provided suitable bio-waste collection and treatment infrastructure exists. All other packaging, including packaging made of biodegradable plastic polymers, must instead be designed for material recycling by 12 February 2028.

**Article 10 – Packaging minimisation.** By 1 January 2030, manufacturers and importers must ensure packaging weight and volume are reduced to the minimum necessary for functionality. Packaging with features that only increase perceived volume (double walls, false bottoms, unnecessary layers) cannot be placed on the market, with narrow exceptions for packaging designs or trademarks protected before 11 February 2025, and for products carrying a protected geographical indication (wine, spirit drinks, craft and industrial products) or covered by an EU quality scheme.

**Article 11 – Reusable packaging.** Packaging placed on the market from 11 February 2025 is only "reusable" if it meets nine cumulative conditions covering multiple rotations, safety and hygiene, ability to be emptied/reloaded without damage, reconditioning capability (Annex VI Part B), and – notably – it must also independently satisfy the Article 6 recyclability requirements. By 12 February 2027 the Commission must adopt a delegated act establishing minimum rotation numbers for the most common reusable packaging formats.

## Chapter III – Labelling, marking and information requirements (Articles 12-14)

**Article 12 – Labelling of packaging.** From 12 August 2028 (or 24 months from the relevant implementing acts, whichever is later), packaging must carry a harmonised pictogram-based label showing material composition to help consumers sort waste correctly; compostable packaging must be labelled to make clear it is not suitable for home composting and must not be discarded in nature. Packaging containing substances of concern must be marked through a standardised, open digital-marking technology. Reusable packaging placed on the market from 12 February 2029 must carry a label plus a QR code or equivalent digital data carrier giving information on the re-use system, collection points, and trip/rotation tracking. Recycled-content and biobased-content labelling remain voluntary but must follow the harmonised format if used. The Commission must adopt the implementing acts establishing the harmonised label formats and digital-marking methodology by 12 August 2026.

**Article 13 – Labelling of waste receptacles.** By 12 August 2028 (or 30 months from the relevant implementing acts), Member States must ensure harmonised labels enabling separate collection are affixed to waste receptacles, matching the packaging labels established under Article 12.

**Article 14 – Environmental claims.** Voluntary environmental claims about packaging properties covered by this Regulation (recyclability, recycled content, reusability) may only be made where the packaging exceeds the applicable minimum legal requirement, and must specify whether the claim covers the whole packaging unit, part of it, or all packaging placed on the market by that operator.

## Chapter IV – General obligations (Articles 15-23)

Chapter IV lays out parallel due-diligence obligations for each link in the supply chain: manufacturers (Article 15) must run the Article 38 conformity assessment, draw up the technical documentation and EU declaration of conformity, and keep records for 5 years (single-use packaging) or 10 years (reusable packaging); suppliers (Article 16) must give manufacturers everything needed to demonstrate conformity; authorised representatives (Article 17) can be appointed by written mandate; importers (Article 18) must verify the manufacturer's conformity assessment before placing packaging on the market and must not do so if they suspect non-compliance; distributors (Article 19) must verify producer registration and labelling before making packaging available; fulfilment service providers (Article 20) must not let warehousing or dispatch conditions jeopardise compliance. Article 21 makes an importer or distributor that private-labels or modifies packaging assume full manufacturer obligations. Article 22 requires economic operators to identify their supply-chain counterparties on request (5 or 10 years, depending on packaging type). Article 23 obliges packaging waste management operators to report annually to competent authorities and to producers/PROs.

## Chapter V – Obligations of economic operators to reduce packaging and packaging waste (Articles 24-33)

**Article 24 – Empty space.** By 1 January 2030 (or three years from the relevant implementing act), the maximum empty-space ratio for grouped, transport and e-commerce packaging is **50%**. By 12 February 2028, sales packaging must also have its empty space minimised to what packaging functionality requires. E-commerce operators using sales packaging as e-commerce packaging, and reusable packaging within a re-use system, are exempt from the 50% ceiling.

**Article 25 – Restrictions on certain packaging formats.** From 1 January 2030, economic operators may not place on the market packaging in the formats and uses listed in Annex V. Recitals confirm this list is built around single-use plastic packaging for food and beverages consumed on-premises in the HORECA sector (banned outright), plus specific formats tied to fresh fruit and vegetables and to re-use-system access requirements, with a narrow micro-enterprise derogation where accessing re-use infrastructure is not technically feasible. The Commission must publish explanatory guidelines, including a non-exhaustive list of exempted fruit and vegetables, by 12 February 2027.

**Articles 26-27 – Reuse obligations.** Economic operators placing reusable packaging on a Member State's market for the first time must ensure a re-use system is in place there (Annex VI), and must participate in one or more re-use systems, reconditioning packaging (Annex VI Part B) before it is reused.

**Article 28 – Refill.** Operators offering refill must inform end users of eligible container types, hygiene standards and consumer liability; from 1 January 2030, final distributors with a sales area over 400 m² must endeavour to dedicate 10% of that area to refill stations for food and non-food products.

**Article 29 – Reuse targets.** From 1 January 2030: at least **40%** of transport packaging and sales packaging used for transport (pallets, crates, boxes, intermediate bulk containers, drums, etc.) must be reusable within a re-use system, rising to a 70% endeavour-target by 2040; packaging moved between an operator's own sites, or to a linked/partner enterprise, or to another economic operator within the same Member State, must be 100% reusable; at least **10%** of non-cardboard grouped packaging (boxes used to create stock-keeping units) must be reusable, rising to a 25% endeavour-target by 2040; and final distributors must make at least **10%** of alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages available in reusable sales packaging, rising to a 40% endeavour-target by 2040. Wine, aromatised wine products, spirit drinks and milk/milk products are exempt from the beverage target. Micro-enterprises placing under 1,000 kg of packaging on the market annually are exempt; Member States may grant a renewable 5-year exemption to economic operators where the Member State is well ahead on recycling and waste-prevention targets; final distributors below 100 m² of sales area, on islands under 2,000 inhabitants, or in municipalities under 54 persons/km² may also be exempt. Pooling of final distributors is capped at 40% market share and 5 members per pool (unless operating under the same brand).

**Article 30 – Calculation methodology.** The Commission must adopt the re-use target calculation methodology by 30 June 2027; the obligation to demonstrate achievement applies from 1 January 2030 or 18 months after that implementing act.

**Article 31 – Reporting.** Economic operators must report re-use data annually from calendar year 2030; the Commission must establish a European observatory on re-use by 12 February 2027.

**Articles 32-33 – Take-away sector.** By 12 February 2027, HORECA final distributors selling hot/cold beverages or ready-prepared food in take-away packaging must let consumers bring their own container, at no extra cost. By 12 February 2028, they must also offer a reusable-packaging option within a re-use system, again at no extra cost; micro-enterprises are exempt; from 2030, final distributors must endeavour to offer 10% of take-away products in reusable format.

## Chapter VI – Plastic carrier bags (Article 34)

Member States must achieve a sustained reduction in lightweight plastic carrier bag consumption, defined as **no more than 40 lightweight plastic carrier bags per capita per year by 31 December 2025, and every year thereafter**. Member States may exclude from this target very lightweight bags needed for hygiene or to prevent food wastage for loose products. By 12 February 2032, the Commission must report on whether other packaging materials are substituting for plastic bags with worse environmental impact and propose reduction measures if so.

## Chapter VII – Conformity of packaging (Articles 35-39)

Test, measurement and calculation methods must be reliable, accurate and reproducible (Article 35). Harmonised standards and Commission common specifications each give rise to a presumption of conformity (Articles 36-37). Conformity assessment (Article 38) uses the internal-production-control module set out in Annex VII – conformity assessment is the manufacturer's sole responsibility, mirroring the "New Legislative Framework" modules from Decision 768/2008/EC. The manufacturer draws up a single EU declaration of conformity (Article 39, model in Annex VIII), which can bundle declarations required under other Union acts. Notably, **CE marking does not apply to PPWR compliance** – CE marking on packaging, where it appears, only reflects compliance of the packaged product with other Union product law, to avoid consumer confusion.

## Chapter VIII – Management of packaging and of packaging waste (Articles 40-57)

**Article 40 – Competent authority.** Each Member State must designate one or more competent authorities and notify the Commission by 12 July 2025.

**Article 43 – Waste prevention targets.** Each Member State must reduce packaging waste generated per capita, against a 2018 baseline, by at least **5% by 2030, 10% by 2035, and 15% by 2040**.

**Articles 44-47 – Producer registration and extended producer responsibility (EPR).** Each Member State must establish a national producer register within 18 months of the first relevant implementing act (due by 12 February 2026); producers must register in every Member State where they first make packaging available, with a simplified reporting track for producers placing under 10 tonnes/year. Registration must be granted within 12 weeks. Under Article 45, producers bear extended producer responsibility per Articles 8/8a of Directive 2008/98/EC, with PPWR additionally requiring the EPR fee to cover the cost of labelling waste receptacles and of compositional waste surveys. Producers established in another Member State or a third country selling via distance contracts must appoint an authorised representative for EPR. Producers may discharge EPR collectively through a producer responsibility organisation (PRO, Article 46), which must be authorised (Article 47) following a verification process capped at 18 weeks, and must provide a financial guarantee to cover waste-management costs in the event of non-compliance or insolvency.

**Articles 48-49 – Collection.** Member States must ensure systems for the return and separate collection of all packaging waste; packaging meeting the design-for-recycling criteria must be collected for recycling, and incineration or landfill of such packaging is prohibited (subject to a narrow exception). Mandatory collection objectives must be set by 1 January 2029.

**Article 50 – Deposit and return systems (DRS).** By **1 January 2029**, Member States must ensure at least **90% by weight annual separate collection** of single-use plastic beverage bottles and single-use metal beverage containers up to 3 litres, achieved through deposit-and-return systems with a deposit charged at the point of sale. Wine and aromatised-wine products, spirit drinks, and milk/milk products are excluded from the mandatory scope; containers under 0.1 litres may also be excluded. A Member State may be **exempted from establishing a DRS if it already achieves an 80% separate collection rate by 2026** and, by 1 January 2028, notifies the Commission with a credible implementation plan showing how it will still reach 90%. If separate collection then falls and stays below 90% for three consecutive years, the exemption lapses and a DRS must be established within roughly two years. New or restructured DRS must meet the Annex X minimum requirements by 1 January 2029; pre-existing DRS that already hit the 90% target by that date are exempt from Annex X (with a 2035 backstop if the target is missed).

**Article 52 – Recycling targets.** Member States must achieve, across their whole territory: by **31 December 2025**, a minimum of **65%** by weight of all packaging waste recycled, with material-specific floors of 50% plastic, 25% wood, 70% ferrous metals, 50% aluminium, 70% glass and 75% paper/cardboard; by **31 December 2030**, a minimum of **70%** overall, with material-specific floors of 55% plastic, 30% wood, 80% ferrous metals, 60% aluminium, 75% glass and 85% paper/cardboard. A Member State may postpone the 2030 material-specific deadlines by up to five years under strict conditions (maximum 15 percentage-point derogation, and floors that the recycling rate must never drop below regardless of the postponement).

**Articles 53-54 – Calculation rules.** Recycling is measured, as a rule, at the point packaging waste enters the recycling operation, with narrower derogations for sorting-output measurement and average loss rates; Member States may credit up to 5 percentage points of adjusted target achievement to reusable sales packaging placed on the market and actually reused.

**Article 56 – Reporting.** Member States must report recycling, plastic-carrier-bag consumption and DRS separate-collection data to the Commission annually; the relevant implementing acts on formats and methodology are due by 12 February 2027, and DRS collection-rate reporting begins with calendar year 2028.

**Article 57 – Packaging databases.** Member States must establish harmonised, publicly accessible, machine-readable packaging databases within 12 months of the Article 56 implementing acts being adopted.

## Chapter IX – Safeguard procedures (Articles 58-62)

Market surveillance authorities that suspect packaging presents a risk to the environment or human health must evaluate it and require corrective action (Article 58); unresolved objections escalate to a Union safeguard procedure with an implementing act determining whether the national measure was justified (Article 59). Article 60 covers compliant packaging that nonetheless presents a risk. Article 61 sets up an automated data interconnection between the EU's product market-surveillance information system (ICSMS) and customs risk-management systems for packaging entering the Union at the border. Article 62 lists formal non-compliance triggers (missing or incorrect EU declaration of conformity, non-functional QR codes, incomplete technical documentation, breaches of the excessive-packaging, restricted-format, reuse, refill or recycled-content rules) and the resulting withdrawal, recall or penalty consequences.

## Chapter X – Green public procurement (Article 63)

By **12 February 2030**, the Commission must adopt implementing acts setting minimum mandatory green public procurement requirements for public contracts under Directives 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU where packaging or packaged products represent **more than 30% of the estimated contract value**. The requirements apply to procurement procedures launched 12 months or more after the implementing act enters into force, and may take the form of technical specifications, selection criteria or contract performance conditions. Contracting authorities may derogate on public-security, public-health or unresolvable-technical-difficulty grounds.

## Chapter XI – Delegated powers and committee procedure (Articles 64-65)

The power to adopt delegated acts (covering, among others, the Article 5 heavy-metal/PFAS thresholds, Article 6 design-for-recycling criteria, Article 7 recycled-content adjustments, Article 11 minimum rotations, and several Article 29 reuse-target exemptions) is conferred on the Commission for **10 years from 11 February 2025**, tacitly renewable unless the European Parliament or Council objects. Implementing acts follow the examination procedure via the committee established under Article 39 of Directive 2008/98/EC.

## Chapter XII – Amendments (Articles 66-67)

**Article 66** amends Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 (market surveillance) by adding both Directive (EU) 2019/904 and this Regulation to its Annex I scope, formally bringing packaging under the EU's horizontal market-surveillance and customs-control framework.

**Article 67** amends Directive (EU) 2019/904 (the Single-Use Plastics Directive): it clarifies that the SUP Directive is lex specialis over the PPWR except where Article 25(1) and (6) PPWR (restrictions on single-use plastic packaging formats in Annex V point 3) prevail; it deletes the SUP Directive's own recycled-content rules for plastic beverage bottles from 2030 (since PPWR Article 7 now governs this exclusively); and it expands the SUP Directive's restricted-products list (Part B of its Annex) to add shrink wrap used at airports or train stations to protect luggage, polystyrene or other plastic protective chips, and **multi-pack plastic rings used as grouped packaging** – all now banned as single-use plastics. This last addition, under Article 67(5), applies from **12 February 2029** rather than the Regulation's general application date.

## Chapter XIII – Final provisions (Articles 68-71)

**Article 68 – Penalties.** Member States must lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties by **12 February 2027**; penalties for breaches of Articles 24 to 29 (empty space, restricted formats, reuse and refill obligations) must specifically include administrative fines.

**Article 69 – Evaluation.** The Commission must evaluate the Regulation's contribution to the internal market and to environmental sustainability by **12 August 2034**, with a dedicated section on the impact on the agri-food system and on food waste.

**Article 70 – Repeal and transitional provisions.** Directive 94/62/EC is repealed from **12 August 2026**, but several of its provisions stay alive on transitional timetables: Article 8(2) (until 30 months after the Article 12(6) implementing act); Article 9(1)-(2) essential requirements (until 31 December 2029); Article 5(2)-(3), Article 6(1)(d)-(e) and Article 6a (until 31 December 2028); and Article 12(3a)-(4) reporting obligations (until 31 December 2028, except data transmission to the Commission, which runs until 31 December 2029). Commission Decision 97/129/EC (the old packaging-material identification system) is repealed from 12 August 2028. Commission Decisions 2001/171/EC and 2009/292/EC (heavy-metal derogations) remain in force until replaced by PPWR delegated acts.

**Article 71 – Entry into force and application.** The Regulation entered into force on **11 February 2025**, 20 days after its 22 January 2025 publication in the Official Journal, and applies from **12 August 2026**, except for Article 67(5) (the new SUP restrictions), which applies from 12 February 2029.

## Related Brubru guides

- Single-Use Plastics Directive (EU) 2019/904 – lex specialis for single-use plastic items; amended by PPWR Article 67 to add shrink wrap, protective chips and multi-pack plastic rings to its restricted-products list
- Waste Framework Directive (2008/98/EC) – the general waste-hierarchy and extended-producer-responsibility framework the PPWR builds on
- Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) – complementary product-level ecodesign framework; does not cover packaging as its own product category
- REACH (Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006) – primary chemical-safety framework for substances in packaging; PPWR's PFAS and heavy-metal rules operate alongside it
- Market Surveillance Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 – amended by PPWR Article 66 to bring packaging within its scope
- Food Contact Materials Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 – governs contact-sensitive packaging exemptions throughout the PPWR
