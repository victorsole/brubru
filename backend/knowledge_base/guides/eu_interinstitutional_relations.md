# EU Interinstitutional Relations (EP-Council-Commission)

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: The legal and political frameworks governing relations between the **European Parliament, Council of the EU, and European Commission** -- including the EP-EC Framework Agreement, the Interinstitutional Agreement on Better Law-Making, and the budgetary IIA
- Lead EP committee: **AFCO** (Constitutional Affairs) -- chair currently Salvatore De Meo (EPP, Italy) as of November 2024
- Legal basis: Article 295 TFEU (interinstitutional cooperation), Article 17(1) TEU (Commission as guardian of the Treaties), Article 230 TFEU (EP-Commission relations), Article 311 TFEU (Own Resources)

## The four key interinstitutional agreements

### 1. IIA on Better Law-Making (BLMR, 2016)
- Text: Interinstitutional Agreement of 13 April 2016 on better law-making (OJ L 123, 12.5.2016, p. 1, CELEX 32016Q0512(01))
- Parties: EP + Council + Commission
- Content: annual + multi-annual programming, joint political priorities, impact assessments, trilogue practice, transparency, delegated + implementing acts, subsidiarity
- Linked to **Better Regulation Guidelines** (Commission internal) and the upcoming **28 April 2026 Better Regulation and Enforcement Communication** (see `better_regulation_enforcement_communication`)

### 2. IIA on Budgetary Discipline, Cooperation and Sound Financial Management (2020)
- Text: Interinstitutional Agreement of 16 December 2020 (OJ L 433I, 22.12.2020, p. 28)
- Parties: EP + Council + Commission
- Content: MFF 2021-2027 implementation, annual budget procedure, NGEU arrangements, Own Resources introduction schedule, joint declarations on flexibility
- Current status: **UNDER NEGOTIATION for 2028-2034 MFF** -- AFCO draft report 2025/2159(ACI) on the interinstitutional agreement between EP, Council and Commission on budgetary discipline, on cooperation in budgetary matters and on sound financial management is the EP's preparation for the next IIA
- This IIA pairs with the MFF Regulation (COM(2025) 570) and the Own Resources Decision

### 3. EP-Commission Framework Agreement
- Text: Framework Agreement on relations between the European Parliament and the European Commission (OJ L 304, 20.11.2010 -- last revised)
- Parties: EP + Commission only (not Council)
- Content: legislative programming, State of the Union address, Commissioners-designate hearings, access to Commission information, Commissioner attendance at EP meetings, delegated + implementing acts accountability
- **Under revision** -- AFCO draft report 2025/2243(ACI) tabled 24 April 2026 prepares the Framework Agreement update for the von der Leyen II Commission
- Rule of thumb: this is the "operating manual" for day-to-day EP-Commission work -- confirmation hearings are governed here, as is the "green light" for Commissioner portfolio reshuffles

### 4. EP Rules of Procedure -- Rule 135 and related
- Rule 135 (Appointments to Union agencies and bodies) -- AFCO draft report **2025/2262(REG)** tabled 24 April 2026 is a procedural amendment to tighten EP scrutiny of candidates for EU agency leadership (EFSA, EMA, ECHA, Europol, EUAA, etc.)
- Context: ongoing debates about political accountability of EU agencies, especially after the Pfizergate transparency case (von der Leyen SMS) and agency-level controversies

## Article 19 TEU focus (draft report 2025/2263(INI))

**Article 19 TEU** establishes that the **CJEU and national courts together** ensure respect for EU law. The draft report explores:

- Preliminary reference procedure (Article 267 TFEU) -- how national courts refer EU law questions to the CJEU
- CJEU jurisprudence on **judicial independence** (Poland PiS cases C-619/18, C-204/21, Hungary C-718/21 etc.) -- Article 19(1) TEU as the constitutional basis for the EU's rule-of-law jurisprudence
- Direct effect + supremacy -- core doctrines protected under Article 19 TEU
- Interaction with Article 258 TFEU (Commission infringements) and Article 260 TFEU (pecuniary penalties)
- Recent developments: CJEU judgments on Romanian judicial reforms, Spanish amnesty law reference (pending), French judicial independence cases

## Why this matters

The interinstitutional layer is where **power is actually negotiated** between the institutions. Reforms of the Framework Agreement, IIA BLMR, or Rule 135 materially change:

- How much scrutiny Commissioners face (hearings, access to documents, SMS disclosure)
- How much influence the EP has over EU agency appointments
- How fast trilogues converge
- How much transparency applies to legislative drafting (trilogues remain opaque despite CJEU case T-540/15 *Emilio De Capitani*)
- How MEPs can vote (proxy voting for pregnancy -- first time this has been seriously addressed)
