# Combined Transport Directive (Dir 92/106/EEC) and the proposed recast

## QUICK FACTS

- **Base instrument**: Council Directive 92/106/EEC of 7 December 1992 on the establishment of common rules for certain types of combined transport of goods between Member States
- **CELEX**: 31992L0106
- **EUR-Lex**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31992L0106
- **OJ reference**: OJ L 368, 17.12.1992, p. 38
- **Lead DG**: DG MOVE (Mobility and Transport)
- **Commissioner**: Apostolos Tzitzikostas (Sustainable Transport and Tourism)
- **Last EP committee touch (live file)**: TRAN, see Brubru carriage "Proposal for a directive on common rules for combined transport of goods"
- **Status**: Base Directive 92/106/EEC is in force. The 2017 proposed recast (COM(2017) 648 final) was withdrawn 2020 after Council deadlock. A fresh proposal is anticipated under the 2024-2029 Commission mandate as part of the broader Greening Freight Package conversation. As of May 2026 a new Commission text is announced but not yet tabled (TRAN carriage status: ANNOUNCED).

## What "combined transport" means in EU law

Article 1 of Directive 92/106/EEC defines combined transport as the carriage of goods between Member States where the lorry, trailer, semi-trailer (with or without tractor unit), swap-body or container uses **the road on the initial or final leg of the journey** and the other leg is performed **by rail, inland waterway or maritime services** of more than 100 km as the crow flies. The road leg is capped at the distance to the nearest suitable rail terminal (or 150 km, whichever is shorter for certain corridors).

This narrow legal definition matters because it is the gate to a set of regulatory advantages.

## Incentives in the current Directive

| Article | Incentive | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Article 2** | Free cabotage for the road leg | A foreign operator can carry the road leg without a separate cabotage authorisation |
| **Article 4** | Liberalisation from haulage quotas | The combined transport operation is exempt from national quota systems |
| **Article 6** | Mandatory fiscal relief | Member States must reimburse or reduce the vehicle tax (Eurovignette base) proportional to the rail/inland-waterway leg |

These instruments date from 1992. Their effectiveness has eroded as the road haulage market has liberalised and the rail freight share has stagnated around 17-18% of land freight tonne-kilometres (well below the EU Green Deal target of 30% by 2030).

## Why the 2017 recast failed

COM(2017) 648 final proposed (i) widening the definition to include short-distance multimodal operations, (ii) tightening data-sharing obligations, (iii) introducing a fiscal aid scheme for new terminals, and (iv) Member State reporting on bottlenecks. Council could not agree a General Approach; the file was formally withdrawn in 2020. Key blockers: Member States with strong national hauliers (FR, NL) feared an expansion of the cabotage exemption; Member States with strong rail terminal infrastructure (DE, IT) wanted stronger investment obligations.

## What a 2026-2027 recast is likely to cover

Based on the **Greening Freight Package** trajectory (COM(2023) 441-444) and the **CountEmissionsEU Regulation** (32024R3091, see `emissions_accounting_transport_services.md`):

1. **Updated definition** to capture short-distance multimodal operations including urban consolidation centres
2. **Alignment with TEN-T Regulation 2024/1679** terminal categorisation (Core / Extended Core / Comprehensive)
3. **Standardised digital documentation** via eFTI Regulation 2020/1056 implementation
4. **State aid envelope for terminals** modelled on the Italian SA.33412 / SA.34369 and Czech C12/06 precedents
5. **Modal-shift bonus** linked to CountEmissionsEU reporting verified data
6. **Convergence with the Eurovignette Directive 2022/362** so that internalised CO2 + air-pollution road tolls feed back into the combined transport equation

## How combined transport sits next to the FERRMED +FIRRST vision

FERRMED's **Fast, Flexible Integrated Rail-Road System of Transport (+FIRRST)** is essentially a 21st-century operational architecture for what Directive 92/106/EEC covers in 20th-century legal form. +FIRRST proposes:

- Pass-through intermodal terminals (no shunting required)
- 740 m freight trains as the standard unit
- Real-time slot booking via a Mobility-as-a-Service layer
- A pilot corridor Rotterdam / Antwerp / Duisburg to Barcelona

A modernised Combined Transport Directive that recognises pass-through operations and supports terminal investment would be the regulatory enabler for +FIRRST. This is the natural Brubru entry point into FERRMED's policy conversation.
