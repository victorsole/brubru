# EU-Switzerland PNR Agreement: concluded by Council Decision (EU) 2026/2360, published 26 October 2026, not yet in force

## QUICK FACTS
- **IS IT IN FORCE? NO, NOT as of 27 October 2026. The Agreement is CONCLUDED, NOT IN FORCE.** Two different dates must never be merged: (1) the Council **Decision** (EU) 2026/2360 took effect on its adoption, 1 October 2026 (Article 2 of the Decision); (2) the **Agreement** itself enters into force only on the first day of the month after Switzerland notifies the Union of its competent authorities (Article 19(2) of the Agreement), and the Council's General Secretariat will publish that date in the Official Journal. No such notice existed when this was written. The correct answer is: "concluded by the Council on 1 October 2026, published 26 October 2026, entry into force not yet published". Never say "in force" or "marking its entry into force".
- **What was published (Official Journal L, Monday 26 October 2026):** **Council Decision (EU) 2026/2360 of 1 October 2026** (CELEX 32026D2360, ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/dec/2026/2360/oj) concluding the **Agreement between the European Union and the Swiss Confederation on the transfer of Passenger Name Record (PNR) data for the prevention, detection, investigation and prosecution of terrorist offences and serious crime**, and the agreement text itself, OJ L 2026/2361 (CELEX 22026A02361, ELI http://data.europa.eu/eli/agree_internation/2026/2361/oj), with a Joint Declaration.
- **Steps:** signing authorised by Council Decision (EU) 2026/643 of 23 February 2026 (OJ L 17.3.2026); **signed 5 March 2026**; European Data Protection Supervisor opinion 19 December 2025; **European Parliament consent 16 September 2026** (procedure 2025/0343(NLE)); Council adoption 1 October 2026, Luxembourg, Irish presidency (J. O'Callaghan). Legal basis: Articles 16(2) and 87(2)(a) TFEU with Article 218(6)(a). **Ireland takes part** (notified 9 February 2026); **Denmark does not** (Protocol 22).
- **One-way transfer, EU to Switzerland.** Objective (Article 1): enable air carriers to transfer PNR data from the Union to Switzerland and set the conditions under which the **Swiss Passenger Information Unit (Swiss PIU)** may process them; and deepen police and judicial cooperation. **Scope (Article 1(3)):** carriers operating passenger flights between the Union and Switzerland, and carriers incorporated or storing data in the Union that fly to or from Switzerland. Swiss legal basis: the Federal Act of 21 March 2025 on the Processing of Air Passenger Data (Air Passenger Data Act, RS 129).
- **How data move:** "push" method only, by the carrier into the Swiss PIU's database (Article 3); the Parties may later agree to use the EU API-PNR router of Regulation (EU) 2025/13 (Article 4). **19 PNR data elements** in the Annex, from the PNR record locator to API data and all historical changes. Sensitive data (special categories) must be deleted immediately (Article 8); every positive match from automated assessment is reviewed by a human (Article 7).
- **Storage (Article 11): at most 5 years**, and only while an objective link to the purposes remains; after an initial period set by Swiss law, only for passengers with objective evidence of a risk of terrorism or serious crime; **depersonalised (identifying fields masked) at the latest six months after receipt (Article 12)**; irrevocable deletion or anonymisation at the end, with a derogation for ongoing proceedings.
- **Sharing:** disclosure within Switzerland case by case (Article 13); to other countries only under conditions (Article 14); **exchange with Europol, Eurojust and Member State PIUs** (Article 15); independent supervisory authority (Article 16); logging (Article 10). Suspension by notification (Article 20); **termination three months after notice** (Article 21).
- **Not read:** Articles 5, 6, 9, 16 to 18, 22 and 24 (titles only); Joint Declaration in part. Article numbers as in the OJ text.

## Why it matters
Switzerland is in Schengen but outside the EU PNR Directive. The agreement gives Swiss authorities the EU-origin PNR feed under EU-style safeguards (purpose limitation, five-year cap, six-month masking, human review of hits, independent oversight, Europol and Eurojust exchange). Airlines flying EU-Switzerland routes will have to push the 19 elements to the Swiss PIU once the agreement is in force and the Swiss PIU has notified its technical requirements (Article 3).
