# Sudan Humanitarian Crisis: EU Response

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Sudan civil war (since April 2023) and EU humanitarian/diplomatic response
- Scale: World's largest humanitarian emergency -- displacement figures exceed 11 million, including over 3 million refugees in neighbouring countries (Chad, Egypt, South Sudan, Libya, Central African Republic, Ethiopia)
- Parties: Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF, led by Abdel Fattah al-Burhan) vs Rapid Support Forces (RSF, led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo / "Hemedti"). Fighting broke out 15 April 2023.
- Famine declared: IPC Famine Review Committee confirmed famine in parts of Darfur (August 2024); famine conditions now confirmed or projected in multiple regions
- EU humanitarian aid: The Commission has mobilised EUR 400+ million for Sudan and the region since 2023 (figures compiled by ECHO/DG INTPA)
- Sexual and gender-based violence: Human rights monitors report systematic use of SGBV as weapon of war, particularly by RSF and allied militia in Darfur
- International humanitarian law violations: Both parties credibly accused of war crimes for three years; ICC reopened investigations relating to Darfur
- EU sanctions: Council Decision (CFSP) 2023/2135 -- restrictive measures framework for individuals/entities undermining Sudan's stability and political transition
- EEAS / Kallas portfolio: Commissioner for Equality, Preparedness and Crisis Management (humanitarian dimension); HR/VP Kaja Kallas (foreign policy and sanctions)
- EPRS Briefing (14 April 2026): "Sudan's humanitarian crisis: Needs and responses" -- overview of needs, EU humanitarian assistance, IHL violations. Ref: EXAS_BRI(2026)783612

## Context

Since 15 April 2023, Sudan has been engulfed in civil war between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF). What began as a power struggle in Khartoum has destabilised the entire country, with fighting spreading across Darfur, Kordofan, Gezira, and eastern Sudan. The humanitarian consequences dwarf any other active crisis: more people have been displaced from Sudan than from any other country, famine is confirmed in multiple locations, and humanitarian access is systematically obstructed by both parties.

## EU Humanitarian Assistance

DG ECHO and the broader EU humanitarian envelope have funded:
- Life-saving food assistance via WFP and implementing partners
- Emergency health interventions (cholera, malaria, malnutrition treatment)
- Protection activities, particularly for women and girls at risk of SGBV
- Support for refugees in Chad, South Sudan, Egypt, Ethiopia, Central African Republic

## Restrictive Measures (Sanctions)

The EU sanctions framework for Sudan targets individuals and entities:
- Undermining the country's stability and political transition
- Committing serious human rights violations or abuses
- Obstructing humanitarian assistance

Sanctions include asset freezes and travel bans on listed individuals.

## Political Process

The EU supports mediation efforts led by regional organisations (African Union, IGAD) and aligned with UN Security Council resolutions. Calls for immediate cessation of hostilities, humanitarian ceasefires, and a return to a civilian-led transition remain the EU's core political demands.
