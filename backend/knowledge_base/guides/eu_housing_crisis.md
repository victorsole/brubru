# EU Housing Crisis: EP Special Committee Report

## QUICK FACTS
Report: A10-0025/2026
Procedure: 2025/2070(INI) (own-initiative report)
Committee: HOUS (Special Committee on the Housing Crisis)
Rapporteur: Borja Gimenez Larraz (EPP, Spain)
Plenary vote: 10 March 2026 -- ADOPTED (367 for, 166 against, 84 abstentions)
Type: Non-legislative resolution (first report from the new HOUS committee)
Key message: Housing affordability is a crisis requiring EU-level coordination on supply, finance, skills, and tenant protection
HOUS Committee chair: Irene Tinagli (S&D, Italy)

## Crisis in Numbers

- Rents increased 28.8% from 2010 to Q2 2025
- House prices rose 60.5% in the same period
- 10.6% of EU urban population experiences housing cost overburden (spending more than 40% of disposable income)
- Approximately 1.3 million people homeless on any given night in the EU
- Young adults leave parental homes at average age 26.3
- Only 7% of Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) funds allocated to social housing

## Plenary Vote Highlights (10 March 2026)

The resolution was adopted with a strong majority (367-166-84) during the Strasbourg plenary (9-12 March 2026). Key additions beyond the committee report:

- Tax incentives for low/middle-income first-time buyers, removal of registration fee barriers
- Reallocation of unused Recovery and Resilience Plan funds toward social/affordable housing
- Mandatory quality standards for new dwellings (insulation, energy efficiency, air quality)
- Minimum "Made in EU" origin requirements for EU co-financed construction projects
- Balanced regulation of short-term rentals (platform accountability)

## Key Recommendations

### Supply and Simplification
- Propose a Housing Simplification Package reducing administrative burdens
- Establish 60-day target for housing permit issuance
- Mobilise publicly-owned land and conduct brownfield assessments
- Support prefabrication methods that reduce construction time by 20-50%

### Finance and Investment
- Double cohesion policy funding for affordable housing
- Reform VAT application to construction and renovation
- Strengthen support from national promotional banks and EIB Group
- Better use of InvestEU and structural funds

### Labour and Skills
- Expand vocational education and apprenticeships in construction
- Improve working conditions and wages to attract skilled workforce
- Facilitate cross-border worker mobility recognition

### Protection and Rights
- Strengthen tenant protections and property owner safeguards
- Combat illegal property occupation through coordinated EU action
- Ensure accessibility standards for persons with disabilities

### Governance
- Empower local and regional authorities with direct EU fund access
- Establish an EU housing summit
- Implement multilevel governance approach respecting subsidiarity

## Political Context

The HOUS committee is new in the 10th legislature (established 2025). This is its first major report. Housing is primarily a Member State competence (Article 153(1)(j) TFEU excludes housing from EU legislative harmonisation), but the EU has a facilitative role through legislation on energy efficiency (EPBD), funding (cohesion, RRF), and coordination (European Semester country-specific recommendations).
