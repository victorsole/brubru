## Overview

The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) requires operators and traders placing certain commodities and derived products on the EU market -- or exporting them from the EU -- to ensure those goods have not contributed to deforestation or forest degradation anywhere in the world after 31 December 2020. It replaces the EU Timber Regulation (Regulation (EU) 995/2010, CELEX 32010R0995), which is repealed from the date of EUDR application.

The EUDR introduces mandatory due diligence, a geolocation traceability obligation, and a country benchmarking system that classifies source countries by deforestation risk. It is one of the most significant pieces of EU environmental trade legislation, with wide extraterritorial reach affecting exporters in Brazil, Indonesia, Ivory Coast, Ghana, and other major commodity-producing countries.

After intense pressure from trading partners and industry, the Commission proposed a one-year delay in October 2024. The EP and Council adopted the delay regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/3234) in December 2024, pushing the large-operator deadline from 30 December 2024 to 30 December 2025 and the SME deadline to 30 June 2026.

## Scope: Seven Commodities and Derived Products

The EUDR covers seven commodities and a defined list of derived products:

| Commodity | Key Derived Products |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Cattle | Meat, leather, live animals, hides |
| Cocoa | Chocolate, cocoa butter, cocoa powder |
| Coffee | Roasted coffee, coffee extracts, instant coffee |
| Oil palm | Palm oil, palm kernel oil, margarine, cosmetics (where palm-derived) |
| Rubber | Tyres, gloves, certain rubber articles (HS codes listed in Annex I) |
| Soya | Soy flour, soy oil, soybean meal, tofu, animal feed |
| Wood | Timber, paper, pulp, printed books, furniture, charcoal |

Annex I to the Regulation contains the full list of Combined Nomenclature (CN) codes. The Commission may expand the commodity list via delegated acts by 30 December 2025 based on a review of scientific evidence.

## Due Diligence Requirements

Operators (those who first place products on the EU market or export them) must complete three steps before placing any product on the market:

1. **Information collection**: Gather the country of production, geolocation coordinates (polygons or points) of all plots of land where the commodity was produced, quantity, supplier details, and any relevant verification certifications.

2. **Risk assessment**: Assess the risk that the product is non-compliant (deforestation-linked or produced illegally under source country law). Factors include the country classification, presence of forests, certification schemes, and third-party audits.

3. **Risk mitigation**: If the risk assessment does not yield a "negligible" risk finding, the operator must take additional measures (audits, spot checks, further documentation) before placing the product on the market.

Traders (those who make products available after the first market placement) have lighter obligations: they must keep records and, if they are not SMEs, they must also carry out due diligence equivalent to operators.

Operators must submit a **due diligence statement** via an EU information system (to be established by the Commission) before each market placement. Competent authorities in Member States carry out checks.

## Country Benchmarking System

The EUDR establishes a three-tier risk classification of countries (or parts of countries):

| Tier | Classification | Consequence |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Low risk | Countries with negligible deforestation risk, robust governance | Simplified due diligence (only checks and statement required; full risk assessment not mandatory) |
| Standard risk | Default for all countries not yet classified | Full due diligence required |
| High risk | Countries with elevated deforestation, weak governance | Enhanced due diligence; competent authorities conduct at least 15% checks on operators |

The Commission adopts the country classification via delegated acts. The benchmarking system was a key contested element: producing countries lobbied intensively against being labelled high-risk. As of March 2026, the Commission has not yet published the first delegated act establishing country classifications, pending the delayed application date.

## Enforcement

- **Competent authorities**: Each Member State designates one or more competent authorities. These carry out documentary checks, risk-based physical and laboratory checks, and market checks.
- **Minimum check rates**: Standard risk countries: at least 3% of operators checked per year; high-risk countries: at least 15%; low-risk countries: at least 1%.
- **Penalties**: Member States must set penalties that are "effective, proportionate and dissuasive." The Regulation specifies minimum standards: fines of at least 4% of the operator's annual EU turnover, confiscation of the non-compliant product or its proceeds, and temporary exclusion from public procurement.
- **Customs interface**: Products subject to the EUDR must be declared at EU customs. The EU information system for due diligence statements is linked to the EU Single Window Environment for Customs (EU SWE-C).

## Controversy and Implementation Delays

The EUDR has been one of the most contested pieces of EU environmental legislation since its adoption:

- **Third-country pushback**: Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, the United States, Argentina, and Australia formally protested the regulation, arguing it constituted an illegal trade barrier under WTO rules, imposed an undue compliance burden on smallholders, and lacked adequate consultation. Joint letters were sent to the Commission in 2023 and 2024.

- **Smallholder concerns**: Civil society organisations raised concerns that geolocation requirements would exclude smallholder farmers in cocoa and coffee supply chains who lack the technical capacity to provide polygon-level land coordinates.

- **Industry complexity**: Operators in the wood and paper sector argued the CN code list in Annex I created legal uncertainty and that the IT system for due diligence statements was not ready.

- **Delay controversy**: The Commission's October 2024 proposal to delay by one year divided the EP. The Greens/EFA group opposed it strongly; EPP and ECR supported delay. The delay regulation passed with a qualified majority but remains politically sensitive.

- **Omnibus simplification package**: The Commission's Omnibus I package (February 2026, COM(2026)87) proposes further amendments to simplify the EUDR's due diligence requirements, particularly for SMEs, and to revise the country benchmarking methodology. This may result in a second amendment regulation in 2026, potentially softening the compliance burden but also weakening environmental ambition. The Omnibus package is subject to ordinary legislative procedure (co-decision).
