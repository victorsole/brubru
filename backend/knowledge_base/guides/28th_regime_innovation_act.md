## The 28th Regime

### Concept
The "28th regime" creates an optional EU-wide corporate legal framework that companies can choose instead of their national company law. It does not replace national systems but adds a 27+1 option:

- Companies can incorporate under a single EU framework valid across all Member States
- Eliminates the need to navigate 27 different national corporate law regimes
- Aims to reduce compliance costs for cross-border operations
- Builds on precedents like the Societas Europaea (SE), but with broader scope and simpler procedures

### Rationale
- The Draghi Report (September 2024) identified regulatory fragmentation as a key barrier to EU competitiveness
- Companies operating across the single market face 27 different sets of corporate law rules
- Start-ups and scale-ups disproportionately affected by cross-border compliance costs
- The 28th regime aims to make it as easy to do business across the EU as within a single Member State

### Legal and Political Context
- Treaty basis: Likely Article 114 TFEU (internal market harmonisation) or Article 352 TFEU (flexibility clause)
- The European Parliament has repeatedly called for deeper single market integration
- Member States may resist perceived loss of regulatory autonomy in corporate law
- Existing EU company law instruments (SE Regulation, Cross-Border Mergers Directive) provide precedents

## European Innovation Act

### Scope
The European Innovation Act is a legislative package aimed at:
- Removing regulatory barriers to innovation across the EU
- Streamlining approval processes for innovative products and services
- Creating innovation-friendly regulatory sandboxes
- Supporting scale-up of European companies
- Addressing the "valley of death" between research and commercialisation

### Key Elements (Expected)
- Regulatory sandboxes framework across sectors
- Innovation principle in EU legislation (systematic innovation impact assessment)
- Start-up and scale-up support measures
- Cross-border testing and experimentation corridors
- Harmonised rules for deep tech commercialisation
