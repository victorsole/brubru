## Key Provisions

- **EU-ESO (Articles 78-79)**: EU employee stock option plan. Warrants issued to board members and employees (not >25% shareholders). Minimum 24-month vesting. Non-transferable, no consideration. Tax only when shares are disposed of (not at grant, vesting, or exercise). Taxable income = fair market value at disposal minus acquisition price. MS free to set rate but must be no less favourable than national stock option schemes.
- **SAFE and KISS (Article 68)**: Convertible instruments (Simple Agreements for Future Equity, Keep It Simple Securities) and warrants expressly enabled. General meeting or authorised board decides issuance. No pre-emptive rights on shares from convertible instruments. Non-par value shares (Article 61) facilitate down-round pricing.
- **Fast-track liquidation (Articles 83-87)**: Solvent EU Inc. companies can close in ~3 months. Conditions: ceased activity, no assets/liabilities (or creditor consent), no pending proceedings. Directors file declaration + removal application simultaneously (all online). Creditors have 30 days to oppose. Tax authority has 30 days for clearance (silence = deemed clearance). Directors personally liable for false declarations.
- **Simplified insolvency (Articles 88-102)**: Only for EU Inc. innovative startups. Court decides within 6 months. No lawyer required. Digital-only. Electronic auction via e-Justice Portal.
- **Prohibited requirements (Article 103)**: MS cannot deny public support based on HQ location, impose authorisation based on registered office, require local representative/physical presence, or deny use of payment accounts from another MS.
- **Shares and transfers (Articles 53-60)**: Dematerialised in digital register (may use DLT). No bearer shares. Transfer fully online via eIDAS signature, no notary required. Multiple share classes allowed.
- **Governance (Articles 42-52)**: Board of directors (1+ natural persons, at least 1 EU-resident). Meetings fully online or hybrid. Written resolutions.
- **Financing (Articles 61-77)**: No minimum capital, no legal reserves. Non-par value shares default. Distributions only if balance sheet test + solvency test (12 months).
- **Complementary measures**: European Business Wallet, EuVECA review, cross-border telework for startups, Skills Portability Initiative, specialised courts

## What is EU Inc.?

EU Inc. is a new, optional EU-wide corporate legal form -- a "28th regime" that exists alongside the 27 national company law systems. Any founder (natural or legal person) can choose to incorporate under this single, harmonised set of EU-wide rules instead of national company law. The company is registered in a Member State of choice but recognised across all 27 Member States without additional formalities.

Unlike the existing Societas Europaea (SE), which requires EUR 120,000 minimum capital and can only be created from existing cross-border companies, EU Inc. has no minimum capital requirement and can be formed from scratch by any person.

## Why EU Inc.?

- 27 different national corporate law systems create fragmentation and cross-border compliance costs
- Europe creates more startups than the US but most fail to scale within the continent
- The Draghi Competitiveness Report (September 2024) and Letta Single Market Report (April 2025) identified regulatory fragmentation as a key barrier
- March 2025 European Council called on Commission to "propose an optional 28th company law regime allowing innovative companies to scale up"
- Part of the Competitiveness Compass (January 2025), Savings and Investments Union, Single Market Strategy, and Start-up and Scale-up Strategy

## Complete Article-by-Article Overview

### Chapter I -- General Principles (Articles 1-12)

**Article 1 -- Subject matter**: Creates the EU Inc. legal form, an EU central interface for registration, measures to reduce administrative burden (once-only principle), removes obstacles to financing, harmonises certain insolvency aspects, and prohibits discriminatory national measures.

**Article 2 -- Definitions**: 30 key definitions including EU central interface, BRIS, business register, shareholder, articles of association, branch, fully online procedure, employee participation, formation, filing, object of the company, single-member company, liquidation, digital EU power of attorney, EU Company Certificate, legalisation, similar formality, registration of a branch, related party, distribution, digital register of shares, share transfer, digital share certificate, multilateral trading facility, regulated market, convertible instruments, warrants, pre-emptive right, dissolution, solvent liquidation.

**Article 3 -- Legal form and general principles**: EU Inc. is a limited liability company. Key characteristics: shareholders not liable for company obligations; legal personality upon registration; may be formed by natural or legal persons; formed ex nihilo (Arts 16-19) or through conversions/mergers/divisions (Art 21); allocated EUID upon registration; unlimited duration unless articles say otherwise.

**Article 4 -- Rules applicable to EU Inc.**: Governed by this Regulation + articles of association. Matters not covered fall to national law of the Member State of registered office. Member States designate which national legal form applies to EU Inc. companies.

**Article 5 -- Legal personality**: Registered in business register of chosen Member State. Acquires legal personality on date of registration. Pre-registration acts create joint and several liability for the persons who performed them.

**Article 6 -- Name**: Must be followed by "EU Inc." mention; must fulfil name function; must not mislead; must not contravene public policy; must differ from existing company names in BRIS. Branches include company name + branch indication.

**Article 7 -- Articles of association**: Mandatory. Must be digital, machine-readable, structured data. Must be drawn up in an official language + international business language. Standard and non-standard versions. Non-standard: MS official language prevails in discrepancy.

**Article 8 -- EU templates for standard articles of association**: Optional standardised templates via EU central interface or business register. Commission establishes multilingual templates via implementing acts.

**Article 9 -- Registered office**: Registered office and central administration OR principal place of business must be in the Union.

**Article 10 -- Principle of digital-only procedures**: ALL procedures under this Regulation must be carried out exclusively fully online. No paper alternatives. Communications between company, shareholders, and share purchasers also fully online (unless articles provide otherwise). Exception: physical presence may be required case-by-case for identity fraud prevention.

**Article 11 -- Payments**: Must be possible via widely available cross-border online payment services.

**Article 12 -- Employee participation**: For ex nihilo formation: subject to employee participation rules of the MS of registered office. For cross-border operations: rules from Directive 2017/1132 (Articles 86l, 133, 160l) apply. National employment law is NOT affected by this Regulation.

### Chapter II -- EU Central Interface, Formation and Filing (Articles 13-24)

**Article 13 -- Company application form**: Harmonised, fully digital, machine-readable. Directors identified via eIDAS electronic identification. Includes: company formation data, TIN/VAT issuance data, beneficial ownership register data. Accompanied by articles of association (may use EU templates). Commission provides automatic trade mark verification (BRIS interconnection with EUIPO).

**Article 14 -- Preventive control**: Articles of association subject to preventive administrative, judicial, or notarial control at formation and for amendments. Verifies: formal requirements, mandatory minimum content (Annex), name/object compliance, legal capacity, capital contributions.

**Article 15 -- EU central interface**: Established by Commission as part of BRIS access point (Article 22, Directive 2017/1132). Transmits applications to MS business registers. Registration decision remains with national authorities. Enables real-time tracking. Provides IP rights information (trade marks, designs).

**Article 16 -- Fast-track formation through EU central interface**: Using EU templates: registration completed within 48 hours from submission, at maximum cost of EUR 100. Non-euro MS: equivalent amount per OJ exchange rate (+/- 5% for rounding).

**Article 17 -- Formation without EU templates via central interface**: Using custom articles of association: registration completed within 5 working days.

**Article 18 -- Fully online formation with the business register**: Direct national route (not via EU central interface). Same application form + articles. Same deadlines as Arts 16/17.

**Article 19 -- Formation of EU Inc. subsidiaries**: Companies in Annex II/IIB of Directive 2017/1132 or existing EU Inc. can form subsidiaries via central interface or online. No duplicate document submission (retrieved automatically from BRIS via EUID).

**Article 20 -- Once-only submission for authorities**: Business register immediately exchanges company data digitally with tax authorities (TIN), VAT authorities, beneficial ownership register, and social security authorities. EU Inc. not required to submit information separately to these authorities.

**Article 21 -- Creation through domestic and cross-border conversions, mergers or divisions**: EU Inc. may be created by: domestic conversion, domestic merger, domestic division, cross-border conversion/division/merger. Two-year/two-accounts waiting period for domestic operations. Cross-border operations per Directive 2017/1132. EU Inc. may also change back into a national company (same waiting period).

**Article 22 -- Disqualified directors**: Must declare any disqualification circumstances. Currently disqualified persons in any MS cannot become directors. MS must exchange disqualification information (per Directive 2017/1132, Article 13i).

**Article 23 -- Signature of digital filing**: Via eIDAS trust services (Regulation 910/2014) or European Business Wallet signing function. Covers all forms, resolutions, personal declarations.

**Article 24 -- Electronic filing agent**: EU Inc. may authorise a person to sign and submit on its behalf via digital EU power of attorney.

### Chapter III -- Accessibility and Cross-Border Use of EU Inc. Information (Articles 25-35)

**Article 25 -- Documents in business register**: Comprehensive list of publicly available information: name, legal form, registered office, electronic presence, object (NACE code), articles of association (all language versions), directors and board members, accounting documents, single-member identity, winding-up/liquidation information.

**Article 26 -- Documents through BRIS**: All Art 25 info + employee numbers + group information. Key documents free of charge. Other document costs cannot exceed register maintenance costs.

**Article 27 -- Filing and up-to-date information**: File via EU central interface or business register. Amendments to articles within 5 days. Consolidated version auto-created for EU template amendments.

**Article 28 -- Once-only principle during lifecycle**: Public authorities must consult BRIS instead of requesting information from the company, except for suspected abuse/fraud.

**Article 29 -- Company identity disclosure**: All business communications must contain: company name, EUID, registered office address, digital correspondence address, digital presence (website).

**Article 30 -- EU Company Certificate**: Multilingual, issued by business register. Accepted in all MS as sufficient evidence of incorporation. Compatible with European Digital Identity Wallets and Business Wallets.

**Article 31 -- Digital EU power of attorney**: Multilingual template in all EU languages. Compatible with European Digital Identity and Business Wallets.

**Article 32 -- Exemption from legalisation**: Digital copies certified by business register and authenticated via eIDAS/Business Wallets are exempt from legalisation and apostille in other MS.

**Article 33 -- Exemption of translation**: No translation required when information available in articles of association, EU Company Certificate, or BRIS. AI translation agents (approved by MS) accepted for certified translations.

**Article 34 -- Central digital register**: Commission develops EU central interface towards central digital register (implementing acts 18 months after application date).

**Article 35 -- Provisions related to BRIS**: All documents searchable in all EU languages via BRIS. Commission establishes implementing acts within 9 months for: templates, application forms, data exchange lists, technical specifications for EU Company Certificate/power of attorney compatibility with Business Wallets.

### Chapter IV -- Cross-Border Branches (Articles 36-41)

**Article 36 -- General principles**: EU Inc. may open branches in other MS. Each branch registered with local business register.

**Article 37 -- Branch registration**: Via EU central interface (same deadlines/cost ceiling as Art 16) or directly with national business register.

**Article 38 -- Branch application form**: Digital, machine-readable. Electronically signed by director. Business register auto-retrieves EU Inc. info from BRIS via EUID.

**Article 39 -- Once-only submission for branch**: Same once-only data exchange with tax, VAT, social security, beneficial ownership authorities.

**Article 40 -- Branch information**: Publicly available: branch name, address, activities, authorised representatives, EU Inc. details (register, name, legal form, office, registration number, EUID, directors, winding-up, insolvency, closure).

**Article 41 -- Cross-border mobility**: Cross-border conversions, divisions, mergers per Directive 2017/1132 (Chapters I, II, IV).

### Chapter V -- Organisation (Articles 42-52)

**Article 42 -- Company bodies**: Board of directors (one or more natural persons); at least one director resident in the EU. Board exercises all powers not reserved to general meeting. General meeting appoints/dismisses directors and approves annual accounts.

**Article 43 -- Representation**: Directors represent the company. Multiple directors = co-directors (joint representation by default). Articles may allow individual representation. Company bound by directors' acts even beyond company object.

**Article 44 -- Directors' duties**: Act in accordance with articles, in good faith, with reasonable care/skill/diligence. Liable for breach causing loss. Safe harbour: not liable for good faith business decisions with reasonable prudence. Further liability governed by national law.

**Article 45 -- Conflicts of interest**: Director must inform board/general meeting of conflicts. Must refrain from conflicted decisions unless authorised by general meeting or articles.

**Article 46 -- Related party transactions**: Articles may require approval for related party transactions. Breach = director/shareholder liability. Single-member company: contracts with sole member in writing (except current operations).

**Article 47 -- Meetings by electronic means**: General meetings and board meetings may be held fully online or hybrid. MS must not restrict online meetings. Shareholders must be able to attend, vote, and be identified electronically.

**Article 48 -- Written resolutions**: Decisions may be adopted without meeting via written resolutions (electronic means accepted). Same majority as general meeting. Single-member: decisions in writing.

**Article 49 -- Quorum and majorities**: Quorum = simple majority of shares present/represented. Decisions by simple majority (unless articles say otherwise). Amendments to articles = two-thirds majority. Financial commitment increases require shareholder consent.

**Article 50 -- Amendment of articles**: By general meeting (two-thirds majority). Board may make non-substantive editorial changes.

**Article 51 -- Protection of class rights**: Class rights changes require approval of that class (two-thirds within class).

**Article 52 -- Withdrawal of a shareholder**: Court may order buyout if company affairs conducted oppressively. Price = fair value at date of application. EU Inc. + other shareholders jointly liable for payment.

### Chapter VI -- Digital Register, Shares and Share Transfers (Articles 53-60)

**Article 53 -- Registered shares**: Shares dematerialised in digital register. Registration = constitutive effect and evidence of ownership. May use distributed ledger technology. No bearer shares.

**Article 54 -- Digital register of shares**: Mandatory, up-to-date. Contains: shareholder identity/address, share identifier, nominal value (if any), subscription date, redeemability, consideration amounts, transfer dates, sequence numbers, share classes, co-holders, encumbrances/pledges/restrictions. Accessible to shareholders and interested parties (per GDPR). Digital share certificates issued on request and after each transfer.

**Article 55 -- Equality and classes of shares**: Default: equal rights and obligations. Articles may create multiple classes with different rights (preferences, multiple voting rights, veto rights, governance rights, transfer restrictions).

**Article 56 -- Exercise of shareholder rights**: Rights effective upon digital register entry. Co-holders appoint common representative.

**Article 57 -- Voting rights**: One share = one vote (default). Articles may provide for no voting rights or restricted voting. Non-voting shares excluded from quorum/majority calculations.

**Article 58 -- Transfer of shares**: Freely transferable unless articles restrict. Transferee and transferor jointly liable for outstanding consideration.

**Article 59 -- Digital transfer procedure**: Fully online. No notarial deed or additional formality required by MS. Valid via qualified electronic seal/signature (eIDAS) or Business Wallet. Transfer effective upon registration in digital register. Company reviews documentation within 3 working days.

**Article 60 -- Access to public markets**: MS must not prohibit EU Inc. from multilateral trading facilities (SME growth markets). MS may also allow admission to regulated markets (national legislation required).

### Chapter VII -- Financing (Articles 61-77)

**Article 61 -- Non-par value shares**: Default: no nominal value, no fraction of capital. Articles may opt for par value shares. Cannot mix par and non-par.

**Article 62 -- Amount of capital**: No minimum capital requirement. No legal reserves requirement. Capital in euro (or national currency of non-euro MS).

**Article 63 -- Maintenance of equity**: Assets maintaining capital not distributable (unless capital reduced per Art 77). Non-capital consideration freely distributable.

**Article 64 -- Consideration for shares**: Any transfer of economic value (cash or in-kind). Shares may be issued against capital contribution OR non-capital consideration, or both. Capital contributions paid upon issue. Non-capital consideration: within 5 years.

**Article 65 -- In-kind considerations**: May be contributed to capital (except work/services). Must have determinable value. Independent expert report required (may be waived by general meeting for non-capital contributions).

**Article 66 -- Issuance of first shares**: Declared in articles of association. Registered in digital register at creation.

**Article 67 -- Issuance of new shares**: General meeting decides. Articles may authorise board to issue up to specified maximum. Subscriptions fully online. No notarial deed required. Valid via qualified electronic seal/signature.

**Article 68 -- Instruments entitling to new shares**: Convertible instruments (SAFEs, KISS notes) and warrants. General meeting decides (may authorise board). Board issues shares to satisfy claims from instruments. Articles 64, 65, 67(3)-(8) apply.

**Article 69 -- Pre-emptive rights**: Existing shareholders have pre-emptive rights on new shares (14-day exercise period). General meeting/board may modify or exclude. No pre-emptive rights on shares from convertible instruments.

**Article 70 -- Capital increase**: By issuing new shares (capital contributions) or converting reserves.

**Article 71 -- Capital increase from reserves**: General meeting decides. Reserves earmarked for specific purposes not convertible.

**Article 72 -- Distributions**: General meeting decides (may authorise board). Only if board certifies both: (a) balance sheet test: assets > liabilities + capital; (b) solvency test: able to pay debts for 12 months. Directors personally liable for improper distributions. Shareholders must return unlawful distributions.

**Article 73 -- Subscription of own shares**: Company cannot subscribe its own shares.

**Article 74 -- Acquisition of own shares**: General meeting decides. Board may be authorised. Only from distributable funds (Art 72). Own shares as security subject to same limits.

**Article 75 -- Treatment of own shares**: May hold in treasury, transfer, or cancel. No rights attached to treasury shares. Cancellation by general meeting (or authorised board).

**Article 76 -- Redeemable shares**: Company may issue redeemable shares. Minimum 24-month vesting. Redeemed = cancelled. Redemption price from distributable funds only.

**Article 77 -- Capital reduction**: General meeting decides. Balance sheet + solvency test required (signed by all directors + independent expert report). Directors liable for improper reductions.

### Chapter VIII -- EU Employee Stock Option Plan (Articles 78-79)

**Article 78 -- EU-ESO**: Company may establish an EU employee stock option plan (EU-ESO) issuing warrants to eligible persons. Eligible: board members and employees of company + subsidiaries. NOT eligible: persons holding >25% of voting rights. Minimum 24-month vesting period. Warrants non-transferable, issued for no consideration. Exercise consideration paid in cash. Board authorised to issue warrants and satisfy claims (new shares or treasury shares). No pre-emptive rights on EU-ESO shares.

**Article 79 -- Taxation of EU-ESO warrants**: Harmonised deferred taxation across all MS. No taxable event at: grant, vesting, or exercise. Tax arises only when shares obtained by exercising the warrant are disposed of. Taxable income = fair market value at disposal minus acquisition price. MS free to determine tax characterisation and rate. Must be no less favourable than national stock option tax treatment.

### Chapter IX -- Closure of Solvent EU Inc. Companies (Articles 80-87)

**Article 80 -- Dissolution**: File dissolution information fully online.

**Article 81 -- Nullity**: Only by court decision on exhaustive grounds: invalid articles (no name/object/first shares), unlawful activities, lack of legal capacity of all founders. Does not affect existing commitments.

**Article 82 -- Once-only submission during liquidation**: All filing fully online. Business register informs national authorities digitally.

**Article 83 -- Fast-track liquidation**: Available if: ceased economic activity, no assets (or distributed), no liabilities (or creditor consent), no pending proceedings. Directors personally liable for false declarations. Books kept 6 years.

**Article 84 -- Fast-track procedure**: File dissolution notification + removal application simultaneously. Accompanied by: directors' declaration (qualified e-signatures), financial statement, creditor consent evidence, appointed person's declaration (keep books 6 years).

**Article 85 -- Opposition of creditors**: May oppose within 30 days. Business register may refuse fast-track if claims well-founded. Creditors retain rights against directors (jointly and severally liable).

**Article 86 -- Deadlines**: Tax authority has 30 days for tax clearance (extendable by 30 days). Silence = deemed clearance.

**Article 87 -- Removal from register**: After deadlines expire with no objections and tax clearance: business register removes EU Inc. Loss of legal personality. Status publicly available.

### Chapter X -- Insolvency Proceedings (Articles 88-102)

Applies to EU Inc. companies that are **innovative startups** (as defined in the Commission Recommendation on definitions of innovative enterprises, startups, and scaleups).

**Article 88 -- Scope**: Only EU Inc. innovative startups.

**Article 89 -- Rules**: Simplified winding-up proceedings available. Insolvent = unable to pay debts as they mature (MS define specific conditions).

**Article 90 -- Insolvency practitioner**: Appointed per Regulation 2015/848. Exception: derogation possible if startup has up-to-date balance sheet and filed annual statement.

**Article 91 -- Digital communication**: All communications by digital means.

**Article 92 -- Request for opening**: By debtor or creditor. Standard form, no lawyer required.

**Article 93 -- Decision**: Court/authority decides without delay.

**Article 94 -- Stay of enforcement**: Automatic stay of individual enforcement actions.

**Article 95 -- Claims**: Insolvency practitioner (or debtor) prepares list. 30-day objection period. Undisputed claims deemed admitted.

**Article 96 -- Procedure**: Asset realisation and distribution. Court may close without realisation if no/insufficient assets.

**Article 97 -- Electronic auction systems**: Each MS must establish electronic auction platforms (by 24 months after entry into force). Connected to European e-Justice Portal.

**Article 98 -- Interconnection**: Commission establishes interconnection system (36 months after entry into force). Central access via e-Justice Portal.

**Articles 99-101**: Costs borne by MS (interconnection by EU budget). Commission as data controller. Assets sold via electronic auction.

**Article 102 -- Closure**: Court decides within 6 months (extendable once by 6 months, then converts to ordinary winding-up).

### Chapter XI -- Prohibited Requirements (Article 103)

**Article 103 -- List of prohibited requirements**: MS must treat EU Inc. no less favourably than national companies. MS must NOT (for EU Inc. with registered office in another MS): deny public support based on headquarters location; impose authorisation/requirements based on registered office location; require local representative or physical presence; deny use of payment accounts from other MS.

### Chapter XII -- Final Provisions (Articles 104-109)

**Article 104 -- Data protection**: Subject to GDPR (2016/679) and Regulation 2018/1725.

**Article 105 -- Accounting**: Subject to accounting law of MS of registered office.

**Article 106 -- Penalties**: MS must provide effective, proportionate, dissuasive penalties for: failure to file/update documents, failure to maintain digital share register, false declarations.

**Article 107 -- Committees**: BRIS committee (Regulation 182/2011) assists Commission. Insolvency committee (Directive 2019/1023) for Chapter X.

**Article 108 -- Reporting and review**: Commission evaluation 5 years after application. Every 5 years: review EUR 100 formation cost ceiling (HICP adjustment).

**Article 109 -- Entry into force and application**: 20 days after OJ publication. Applies 12 months after entry into force.

### Annex -- Minimum content of articles of association

- Legal form
- Company name
- Address of registered office (including MS)

- Amount of capital (including if EUR 0)
- Total number of shares issued

## Key Innovations Compared to National Company Law

| Feature | EU Inc. | Typical national law |
|---------|---------|---------------------|
| Minimum capital | EUR 0 | EUR 1 to EUR 120,000 |
| Registration time | 48 hours (fast-track) | Days to weeks |
| Registration cost | Max EUR 100 | Varies widely |
| Process | 100% digital | Often paper/in-person |
| Share transfers | Digital, no notary | Often notary required |
| Shares | Dematerialised, digital register | Paper certificates common |
| Par value | Optional (non-par default) | Usually mandatory |
| SAFE/KISS instruments | Expressly enabled | Legal uncertainty |
| Employee stock options | Harmonised EU-ESO + deferred tax | 27 different regimes |
| Cross-border branches | One-click via BRIS | Separate national procedures |
| Insolvency (startups) | Simplified, 6 months, digital | Lengthy, expensive |
| Fast-track closure | ~3 months for solvent companies | Often 12+ months |
| Once-only principle | Automatic data exchange with authorities | Multiple submissions |

## Complementary Measures (Communication COM(2026) 320)

3. **Cross-border telework**: Exploring 100% remote work for startup/scaleup employees
4. **Skills Portability Initiative**: Facilitating labour mobility
5. **Specialised courts**: Encouraging MS to establish dedicated courts for EU Inc. disputes
6. **Recommendation on definitions**: Harmonised definitions of innovative enterprise, innovative startup, innovative scaleup

## Definitions (from Recommendation)

- **Innovative enterprise**: R&D costs >= 10% operating costs or >= 5% sales (3-year average); OR developing a major innovation with market/tech risk
- **Innovative startup**: Innovative enterprise + <100 employees + <EUR 10M turnover/balance + <10 years operating
- **Innovative scaleup**: Innovative enterprise + >EUR 10M turnover/balance + 20% employee/revenue growth in last 2 years + <750 employees or not publicly listed
