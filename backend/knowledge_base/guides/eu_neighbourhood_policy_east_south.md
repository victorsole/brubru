# European Neighbourhood Policy: Eastern Partnership and Southern Neighbourhood

## QUICK FACTS

- **Policy name:** European Neighbourhood Policy (ENP)
- **Launched:** 2003 (Communication COM/2003/104), formalised 2004; major review 2015; institutional overhaul 2024-2025 with split of former DG NEAR into DG ENEST (Eastern) and DG MENA (Southern)
- **Legal basis:** Article 8 TEU ("The Union shall develop a special relationship with neighbouring countries, aiming to establish an area of prosperity and good neighbourliness, founded on the values of the Union and characterised by close and peaceful relations based on cooperation")
- **Geographic scope:** 16 partner countries total -- 6 Eastern Partnership (EaP) + 10 Southern Neighbourhood
- **Eastern Partnership (6):** Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus (suspended since August 2020), Georgia (partly suspended, democratic backsliding crisis 2024-2026), Moldova (EU candidate since June 2022), Ukraine (EU candidate since June 2022)
- **Southern Neighbourhood (10):** Algeria, Egypt, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Libya, Morocco, Palestine, Syria (re-engaging post-Assad, 2024-2026), Tunisia
- **Primary funding instrument:** NDICI-Global Europe (Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument), Regulation (EU) 2021/947, EUR 79.5 billion total (2021-2027) -- approximately EUR 80 billion including the External Action Guarantee top-up
- **NDICI amendment (2026):** Regulation (EU) 2026/995 (29 April 2026) increases External Action Guarantee efficiency; CELEX 32026R0995; OJ L 2026/995
- **Responsible Commissioners:** Marta Kos (Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood, DG ENEST); Dubravka Suica (Mediterranean, DG MENA)
- **Key DGs:** DG ENEST (Eastern) + DG MENA (Southern + Gulf); EEAS coordinates diplomatic and CFSP dimensions across both; DG INTPA (development-finance methodology and sub-Saharan overlap)
- **Association Agreements + DCFTAs:** In force with UA/MD/GE (Eastern); Association Agreements in force across most of the Southern Neighbourhood; Mediterranean DCFTAs (ALECA) paused for Morocco and Tunisia
- **Flagship 2026 initiative:** Pact for the Mediterranean -- presented October 2025, first Action Plan April 2026, second Action Plan expected autumn 2026 (DG MENA lead)
- **Eastern overlap with enlargement:** UA, MD, GE are simultaneously EaP partners and EU candidate countries; DG ENEST now manages both tracks jointly
- **Georgia note (June 2026):** EU accession process for Georgia frozen by Commission and EP following "Georgian Dream" government's foreign agents law (adopted May 2024) and post-election democratic backsliding; EU Delegation still present; some NDICI programming continued
- **Belarus note:** All EaP programme funds to Belarus suspended since August 2020 (Lukashenko fraudulent election + Belarusian role in war against Ukraine from 2022); bilateral NDICI programming redirected to civil society and exiled community; EU Delegation in Minsk formally functions at reduced capacity
- **EP lead committee:** AFET (Foreign Affairs); DEVE (Development) for development-linked ENP spending; INTA for Association Agreement trade pillars
- **Cross-links:** `dg_enest_overview`, `dg_mena_overview`, `dg_intpa_overview`, `eeas_institution_hrvp_kallas` (planned), `global_gateway_strategy`, `latin_america_eu_relations`, `eu_syria_cooperation_agreement_2026` (planned), `lebanon_humanitarian_crisis` (planned), `iran_strait_hormuz_eu_response` (planned), `eu_china_relations` (planned)

The ENP is the EU's primary policy framework for managing relations with the 16 countries that share its land or maritime borders but are not pursuing EU membership -- with the important contemporary exception that three Eastern partners (Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia) have candidate status, creating an unprecedented overlap between the neighbourhood and enlargement tracks.

## 1. ENP Origin, 2015 Review, and Post-2024 Reorganisation

### 1.1 Origin (2003-2004)

The ENP was proposed by the Commission in May 2003 (COM(2003) 104) in anticipation of the 2004 enlargement wave that would bring the EU's borders to Belarus, Ukraine, and Moldova in the east, and closer to the Southern Mediterranean. The core logic was that the EU needed a structured partnership framework short of membership for countries in its immediate vicinity.

The 2004 Barcelona Process (Euro-Mediterranean Partnership, launched 1995) was already running for the south. The ENP folded the Barcelona framework into a single overarching policy while extending its principles eastward. Action Plans were agreed bilaterally, setting country-specific reform benchmarks.

### 1.2 The 2015 Review

Following the 2014 annexation of Crimea, the collapse of the EaP summit ambitions, and instability across the Southern Neighbourhood (Libya, Syria), the Commission and EEAS conducted a comprehensive review of ENP (COM(2015) 500, November 2015). Key findings:

- One-size-fits-all approach had failed: countries wanted differentiated engagement
- The "more for more" incentive structure (more EU support for more reform) had not delivered consistent results
- Security and stabilisation needed to sit alongside democratisation and trade
- The review introduced "differentiation" as an explicit principle: each partner country sets its own level of ambition

### 1.3 The 2024-2025 Institutional Overhaul

When the Von der Leyen II Commission took office in December 2024 and became operational in early 2025, the former DG NEAR (Neighbourhood and Enlargement Negotiations) was dissolved and split into two distinct Directorates-General:

- **DG ENEST** (Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood): Commissioner Marta Kos (Slovenia). Manages accession negotiations with Western Balkans, Türkiye, Ukraine, Moldova and Georgia, alongside EaP cooperation with Armenia, Azerbaijan, and (frozen) Belarus. See `dg_enest_overview`.
- **DG MENA** (Middle East, North Africa and Gulf): Commissioner Dubravka Suica (Croatia). Manages the southern ENP, the Pact for the Mediterranean, Gulf partnerships, and Syria/Lebanon/Libya crisis response. See `dg_mena_overview`.

The split reflects geopolitical logic: Ukraine and Moldova's candidacies, plus Georgia's (frozen) candidacy, have transformed the eastern dimension into a pre-accession track requiring the same instruments as Western Balkans. The southern dimension, by contrast, involves no membership prospect and is more closely linked to migration management, energy security (gas from Algeria; renewables from Morocco and Egypt), and post-conflict stabilisation.

## 2. Article 8 TEU: The Constitutional Anchor

Article 8 TEU (introduced by the Lisbon Treaty, in force December 2009) reads:

> "1. The Union shall develop a special relationship with neighbouring countries, aiming to establish an area of prosperity and good neighbourliness, founded on the values of the Union and characterised by close and peaceful relations based on cooperation.
> 2. For the purposes of paragraph 1, the Union may conclude specific agreements with the countries concerned. These agreements may contain reciprocal rights and obligations as well as the possibility of undertaking activities jointly. Their implementation shall be the subject of periodic consultation."

Article 8 TEU is the first explicit treaty basis for EU neighbourhood policy. It operates alongside Articles 216-218 TFEU (international agreements) and Articles 3(5) and 21 TEU (external action principles). Association Agreements concluded under Article 217 TFEU give legal effect to the differentiated partnerships. The DCFTAs sit within those Association Agreements as their trade chapters.

## 3. Eastern Partnership: Country-by-Country Status

The Eastern Partnership was launched on 7 May 2009 in Prague, bringing together the EU with Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Moldova, and Ukraine in a dedicated multilateral framework. The EaP summit cycle (every two years) is the primary multilateral format; bilateral Association Council / Cooperation Council meetings are the country-level mechanism.

### EaP Multilateral Track

The post-2022 EaP summit cycle was suspended (Russia's war against Ukraine made the established format unworkable with Belarus still nominally included). The Commission and EEAS have shifted to a bilateral format supplemented by themed ministerial meetings (digital, energy, transport, border management).

## 4. Southern Neighbourhood: Country-by-Country Status

The Southern Neighbourhood dimension has its roots in the 1995 Barcelona Process (Euro-Mediterranean Partnership), which was relaunched in 2008 as the Union for the Mediterranean (UfM), a separate intergovernmental body co-chaired by the EU and a Southern Mediterranean country. The ENP sits alongside the UfM; they are complementary frameworks with different membership (UfM includes both EU Member States and southern partners as co-equals).

| Country | AA Status | DCFTA/Trade Notes | Current Engagement Priority |
|---------|-----------|------------------|------------------------------|
| **Algeria** | Association Agreement in force 1 September 2005 | No DCFTA; hydrocarbons dominate trade (gas critical for EU energy security post-2022) | Energy security; migration; no ALECA process |
| **Egypt** | Association Agreement in force 1 June 2004 | ALECA informal discussions only; priority partnership (2021) signed | Strategic Partnership (March 2024): EUR 7.4B package; migration management; post-IMF programme economic stabilisation |
| **Israel** | Euro-Mediterranean Association Agreement in force 1 June 2000 | Trade pillar in force; some EU-Israel science/research cooperation | Post-October 2023: EP called for suspension of association (non-binding resolutions); Council did not act; trade flows continue |
| **Jordan** | Association Agreement in force 1 May 2002 | EU-Jordan partnership priorities 2021-2027; enhanced EPA-style trade benefits; DCFTA (Jordan Compact) in force since 2016 | Macro-financial assistance; Syrian refugee hosting (over 650,000); stabilisation anchor |
| **Lebanon** | Association Agreement in force 1 April 2006 | No DCFTA; trade preferences under ENP | Lebanon in systemic crisis: currency collapse, Hezbollah disarmament post-ceasefire, reconstruction phase 2025-2026; EUR 1B+ EU humanitarian package. Cross-link: `lebanon_humanitarian_crisis` (planned) |
| **Libya** | No Association Agreement (Framework Agreement negotiations stalled since 2008) | No trade agreement; GSP+ not applicable | Governance vacuum; EU supports UNSMIL process; migration cooperation (controversial); cross-link: `sudan_humanitarian_crisis` (planned) for regional displacement context |
| **Morocco** | Association Agreement in force 1 March 2000 | ALECA (DCFTA) negotiations suspended (Western Sahara ECJ case + political blockage) | EU-Morocco Strategic Partnership 2024; renewable energy (Xlinks, hydrogen); fisheries agreement renegotiation |
| **Palestine** | Euro-Mediterranean Interim Association Agreement on trade (in force 1 July 1997) | Trade preferences; no full AA | EU largest donor; humanitarian response post-October 2023; UNRWA funding controversy |
| **Syria** | Cooperation Agreement (1977); no AA | EU suspended cooperation 2011 | Post-Assad transition (from December 2024): phased re-engagement; EU lifted some sanctions March 2025; see `eu_syria_cooperation_agreement_2026` (planned); DG MENA leads |
| **Tunisia** | Association Agreement in force 1 March 1998 | ALECA negotiations: launched 2016, suspended 2019, informally relaunched 2024 | EU-Tunisia MoU (July 2023): EUR 105M economic + EUR 105M migration; political backsliding under Saied; Commission monitoring democratic governance conditions |

### Regional Partners and the UfM

The Union for the Mediterranean (UfM, Barcelona secretariat) includes all 27 EU Member States plus 16 Southern Mediterranean and Middle East partners (the ENP 10 + Jordan, Israel, the Palestinian Authority, plus Turkey, Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Monaco). The UfM focuses on project-based cooperation rather than policy conditionality. Key UfM project areas: water, environment, regional business development, and women's empowerment.

## 5. Association Agreements and DCFTAs

Association Agreements (AAs) concluded under Article 217 TFEU are the most comprehensive bilateral framework the ENP offers short of accession. They include three pillars: political dialogue, sector-specific cooperation, and a trade/economic pillar. The Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Areas (DCFTAs) are the trade pillar of Eastern AAs, designed to align partner economies with the EU internal market acquis.

### Eastern AA/DCFTA Summary

| Country | AA Signed | DCFTA Provisional Application | Full Entry into Force |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Ukraine | June 2014 | 1 January 2016 | 1 September 2017 |
| Moldova | June 2014 | 1 September 2014 | 1 July 2016 |
| Georgia | June 2014 | 1 September 2014 | 1 July 2016 |

DCFTAs are asymmetric in implementation: the EU opened its market to partner exports immediately, while partners have transition periods (up to 10 years for sensitive sectors). Ukraine's DCFTA has been operating under emergency war-mode trade liberalisation since June 2022: under Regulation (EU) 2022/1300 (renewed/extended 2024), Ukrainian exports to the EU are exempt from remaining tariffs and quotas for a transitional period, subject to agricultural safeguard clauses on sunflower oil, poultry, maize, and sugar reactivated in 2023-2024 following complaints from Polish and Romanian farmers.

### Southern Mediterranean Association Agreements

Southern partners have older Euro-Mediterranean Association Agreements (most concluded 1995-2006 under the Barcelona framework). These provide significant trade preferences for industrial goods and partial agricultural liberalisation, but do not include DCFTA-style regulatory harmonisation. The ALECA (Agreement on a Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area) process was intended to upgrade the trade pillar with Morocco and Tunisia to near-DCFTA standards. Both ALECA negotiations are currently stalled:

- **Morocco ALECA**: ECJ rulings (2016, 2021) on Western Sahara's exclusion from EU trade agreements created legal uncertainty; negotiations paused.
- **Tunisia ALECA**: Launched 2016, suspended after six rounds in 2019 amid Tunisian civil society opposition; informal restart consultations 2024-2025 but no formal resumption.

## 6. NDICI-Global Europe: Funding Architecture

NDICI-Global Europe (Regulation (EU) 2021/947, in force 14 June 2021) is the primary EU external financing instrument for 2021-2027, consolidating 12 previous instruments (ENI, DCI, IcSP, EDF, etc.) into a single framework.

| Component | Allocation (EUR billion) | Scope |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------|
| Geographic programmes | 60.4 | Bilateral + regional programming by region |
| Thematic programmes | 6.4 | Democracy/human rights; civil society; stability; global challenges |
| Rapid response pillar | 3.0 | Crisis response, flexible financing |
| External Action Guarantee (EAG) | 40.0 (guarantee ceiling) | EFSD+ blended finance + guarantees for private investment (Global Gateway) |
| **Total envelope** | **~79.5** | 2021-2027, covering development, neighbourhood, and global cooperation |

Within the Geographic programmes, the Neighbourhood (East + South) receives the largest regional allocation. The NDICI Neighbourhood Regulation window (former ENI instrument) is approximately EUR 19.3 billion for the neighbourhood region (East + South combined) over 2021-2027.

Programming follows multiannual indicative programmes (MIPs) agreed between the Commission and partner countries, typically covering 4-5 years. MIPs set sector priorities and indicative allocations; annual action programmes specify individual projects.

The 2026 amendment (Regulation (EU) 2026/995, CELEX 32026R0995) strengthens the External Action Guarantee governance to accelerate Global Gateway project pipelines. This affects both neighbourhood regions: Morocco's hydrogen investments and Ukraine's reconstruction are both EAG-eligible.

## 7. Reform and Growth Facility: The Accession-Adjacent Upgrade

Sitting alongside NDICI for the Eastern partners pursuing accession, two dedicated facilities have been established:

**Reform and Growth Facility for the Western Balkans** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1449, in force 25 May 2024): EUR 6 billion (EUR 2B grants + EUR 4B concessional loans) for Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia. Conditioned on reform milestones. See `dg_enest_overview`.

**Reform and Growth Facility for Moldova** (Regulation (EU) 2025/535): EUR 1.9 billion (2025-2027). Moldova's first dedicated instrument outside NDICI, reflecting its accelerated accession track. Conditioned on rule-of-law milestones and anti-corruption benchmarks.

Ukraine operates under the separate **Ukraine Facility** (Regulation (EU) 2024/792): EUR 50 billion (2024-2027) for macro-financial assistance, budgetary support, and reconstruction. This is a sui generis instrument, not part of NDICI or the standard neighbourhood framework.

Georgia's Reform and Growth Facility track was also under discussion but was placed on hold following the freezing of accession negotiations in March 2025.

## 8. Pact for the Mediterranean (2026)

The Pact for the Mediterranean is the flagship initiative of DG MENA under Commissioner Suica. It was:

- Presented by the Commission: 16 October 2025
- Launched at the Barcelona Anniversary Ministerial: 28 November 2025 (30th anniversary of the 1995 Barcelona Declaration)
- First Action Plan published: 17 April 2026
- Second Action Plan: expected autumn 2026

The Pact replaces the ad-hoc bilateral "partnership priorities" and "strategic partnership" frameworks that had proliferated under ENP without a unifying political document. It covers five thematic pillars:

1. **Prosperity and trade**: renewed ALECA ambitions (Morocco, Tunisia); digitisation of customs; services liberalisation for Jordan and Egypt
2. **Energy and climate**: the EU-Africa Green Energy Initiative; North Africa-Europe electricity interconnection (via MEDGRID); hydrogen (Morocco's 2030 green hydrogen ambitions; Egypt's renewables scale-up)
3. **People, mobility and migration**: legal pathways (Talent Partnerships); anti-smuggling cooperation; EU-Tunisia MoU 2023 model replicated; return and readmission
4. **Security and resilience**: counter-terrorism; maritime security; Sahel spillover management; hybrid threats
5. **Human development**: health, education, research (Horizon International partnerships), youth employability

The Pact does NOT create a new legal instrument or funding stream -- it is a political framework that organises existing NDICI programming and bilateral agreements. Cross-link: `dg_mena_overview` for the DG-level architecture.

## 9. Syria: Re-Engagement and the 2026 Cooperation Agreement

The fall of the Assad regime in December 2024 opened a new chapter in EU-Syria relations. The EU had suspended the 1977 Cooperation Agreement in 2011 and maintained a comprehensive sanctions regime. Post-December 2024:

- EU lifted a first tranche of sanctions on Syrian state entities (March 2025) while maintaining targeted sanctions on Assad-era figures
- Humanitarian access improved; EU resumed operational engagement with Syrian authorities
- DG MENA is leading negotiations on a new framework agreement to replace the 1977 Cooperation Agreement
- EEAS re-opened an EU Delegation in Damascus (operational since early 2025)

A new EU-Syria Cooperation Agreement is under negotiation as at June 2026. This is tracked separately in `eu_syria_cooperation_agreement_2026` (planned). Cross-link also with `lebanon_humanitarian_crisis` (planned) given the Lebanon-Syria interconnection for reconstruction.

## 10. Brubru Tracking Angles by Country/Theme

**Ukraine:** Largest EU external spending commitment in history (EUR 50B facility + NDICI macro-financial). Track via `ukraine_support_loan_recovery_2026` (planned). AFET + BUDG joint scrutiny. Budget control MEPs: Monika Hohlmeier (EPP), Victor Negrescu (S&D).

**Moldova:** Fastest-moving candidate track; Reform and Growth Facility benchmarks published quarterly by Commission. Monitor via DG ENEST progress reports (annual enlargement packages, typically October each year).

**Georgia:** Freeze is the story. Monitor AFET resolutions; human rights committee (DROI subcommittee) reports; Georgian civil society petitions to EP.

**Morocco/Tunisia:** ALECA stall and Pact for the Mediterranean energy pillar are the two live threads. North Africa-Europe hydrogen interconnection (MEDGRID, EuroAfrica Interconnector) is a major infrastructure file.

**Egypt:** EUR 7.4B strategic partnership (March 2024) is the largest bilateral package ever for Egypt. Tracks through ECFIN (macro-financial), DG ENER (hydrogen/solar), and DG HOME (migration).

**Lebanon:** Post-ceasefire reconstruction phase (2025-2026). EU is the largest donor. ECHO leads humanitarian; DG MENA leads political-economic reconstruction. See `lebanon_humanitarian_crisis` (planned).

**Syria:** Re-engagement is the live file. Council Working Party on Mashreq/Maghreb (COMEM) is the main intergovernmental track. EP AFET Committee receives DG MENA briefings quarterly.

**Israel/Palestine:** Post-October 2023 tensions have placed the EU-Israel Association Agreement under formal EP review pressure (non-binding). The association remains in force; the Commission has not proposed suspension. Monitor AFET resolutions + DEVE development-aid flows to Palestine.

**NDICI tracking:** Midterm review of NDICI-Global Europe was due 2024 and is the primary instrument-level file. Track via BUDG + DEVE committees.
