# METSAF — Middle East Crisis Temporary State Aid Framework (29 April 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Title**: Middle East crisis Temporary State aid Framework (METSAF)
- **Adopted**: Wednesday 29 April 2026 (Commission press conference, Strasbourg)
- **Press release**: IP/26/894 (29 April 2026)
- **Commission landing page**: https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/state-aid/legislation/metsaf_en
- **Lead Commissioner**: **Teresa Ribera** (EVP for a Clean, Just and Competitive Transition) — DG COMP responsibility
- **Lead DG**: DG COMP (Competition); coordination with DG ENER, DG MOVE, DG AGRI, DG MARE
- **Type**: Temporary State aid Framework — Commission communication setting compatibility criteria for Member State aid notifications under Article 107(3)(b) TFEU ("serious disturbance in the economy of a Member State") and Article 107(3)(c) TFEU
- **Duration**: in force until **31 December 2026** (sunset built in; Commission may extend if Middle East crisis persists)
- **Geopolitical trigger**: Iran war + closure of the Strait of Hormuz from March 2026; energy and fertiliser price spike. Per President von der Leyen at EP Strasbourg 29 April: "the war costs us EUR 500 million a day"
- **Political framing**: Commission's State Aid arm of the **AccelerateEU** crisis response (see `accelerateeu_fossil_energy_crisis.md`). Designed as the short-term cushion paired with the medium-term electrification push

## Eligible sectors
Aid may be granted to companies active in:
1. **Agriculture** — primary producers (price increases of fuel + fertiliser)
2. **Fisheries and aquaculture** — fuel-cost compensation
3. **Land transport** — road haulage, rail freight, inland waterways
4. **Maritime transport** — intra-EU short sea shipping (note: international long-haul maritime is excluded)
5. **Energy-intensive industries** — electricity-cost compensation under a temporary widening of the Clean Industrial Deal State Aid Framework (CISAF)

## Aid intensities and forms

### Track 1 — Sectoral fuel/fertiliser cost compensation
- **Up to 70 % of the extra costs** caused by the price increase of fuel and (for agriculture only) fertiliser, verified at beneficiary level
- Reference period for "extra cost" calculation: prices observed **before 28 February 2026** (pre-conflict baseline)
- Cumulation rules: combinable with EU ETS State Aid Guidelines aid up to half the METSAF aid amount

### Track 2 — Simplified flat-rate option
- **Up to EUR 50,000 per beneficiary** based on a general estimate of fuel consumption in the sector, using relevant proxies
- Designed to reduce administrative burden for small farmers, hauliers, and fishers — minimal documentation required

### Track 3 — CISAF temporary adjustment for energy-intensive industries
- **Aid intensity for electricity price subsidies raised from 50 % to 70 %** of eligible electricity consumption cost
- "**No further increase in decarbonisation efforts will be required**" of recipients (deliberate softening to speed deployment)
- Cumulation: stackable with EU ETS State Aid Guidelines aid up to half of the METSAF amount

## Notification + approval procedure
- All Member State schemes must be **notified to the Commission** under the standard State aid procedure
- Commission commits to a **rapid approval procedure** (decisions in days, not months)
- Schemes must be **targeted, temporary, and proportionate** (Ribera at press conference, 29 April 2026)
- Each notification will be assessed **case-by-case** to confirm consistency with the framework

## Distinction from sister State aid instruments
| Framework | Trigger | Status (April 2026) | Sectors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary Crisis and Transition Framework (TCTF) | COVID + Russia/Ukraine war | Expired 30 June 2024 (some elements rolled into CISAF) | Energy, manufacturing, REPowerEU |
| **CISAF** (Clean Industrial Deal State Aid Framework) | Decarbonisation + competitiveness | In force | Clean tech investment, energy-intensive industry |
| **METSAF** (this guide) | Iran war + Strait of Hormuz | **Adopted 29 April 2026, in force until 31 Dec 2026** | Agriculture, fisheries, transport, energy-intensive industry |
| EU ETS State Aid Guidelines | ETS-induced indirect electricity cost | Permanent | Energy-intensive sectors with significant ETS cost pass-through |

## Stakeholder reactions (29 April 2026 first wave)

- **Member States advocating activation**: France, Germany, Italy, Spain — ready to notify schemes within days
- **Member States cautious on State aid loosening**: Netherlands, Nordics — concerned about single-market level playing field; supportive of sunset
- **Industry**: agriculture (Copa-Cogeca) + fisheries (Europêche) welcome the EUR 50,000 flat-rate; haulage (IRU) presses for higher cap; energy-intensive industry (Eurofer, CEFIC) push for the 70 % electricity intensity cap to become permanent
- **Trade unions**: ETUC presses for job-condition strings on aid grants
- **NGOs**: CAN Europe + WWF EU concerned that "no further decarbonisation effort required" line risks weakening climate conditionality of state subsidies
