## Legal Nature and Status

The Eurogroup is an informal body. It is not a formal EU institution and not a Council configuration under Article 16 TFEU. Its legal standing derives from Protocol No 14 to the Treaty on European Union and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, attached by the Treaty of Amsterdam (1997) and carrying the same legal force as the Treaties themselves. Protocol 14 states that the Ministers of the Member States whose currency is the euro may meet informally to discuss questions related to their specific responsibilities in respect of the single currency.

Because the Eurogroup takes no legally binding decisions, it does not follow the full procedural safeguards of the Council. There is no voting on record and no co-decision with the European Parliament. Conclusions represent political commitments. Formal legal acts arising from Eurogroup consensus -- such as country-specific recommendations under the European Semester, or euro adoption decisions -- are subsequently adopted by ECOFIN or the Council acting in the relevant legal formation.

The Treaty of Lisbon (2009) reinforced the Eurogroup by introducing the elected presidency (replacing the previous six-month rotation) and by anchoring the body in primary law via Protocol 14. The Euro Summit at Heads of State or Government level is provided for in Article 12 of the Treaty on Stability, Coordination and Governance (TSCG, the Fiscal Compact, signed 2 March 2012, in force 1 January 2013).

## Composition and Attendees

The 21 eurozone Member States, and therefore the 21 members of the Eurogroup as of 2026, are: Austria (1999), Belgium (1999), Finland (1999), France (1999), Germany (1999), Ireland (1999), Italy (1999), Luxembourg (1999), Netherlands (1999), Portugal (1999), Spain (1999), Greece (2001), Slovenia (2007), Cyprus (2008), Malta (2008), Slovakia (2009), Estonia (2011), Latvia (2014), Lithuania (2015), Croatia (2023), Bulgaria (2026).

**Bulgaria's accession (1 January 2026):** The European Commission and ECB published off-cycle convergence reports on 4 June 2025, confirming Bulgaria met all five convergence criteria. The Eurogroup endorsed accession unanimously on 19 June 2025. Council Decision (EU) 2025/1407 of 8 July 2025 formally approved the adoption of the euro by Bulgaria, with the conversion rate irrevocably fixed at 1 EUR = 1.95583 BGN (the ERM II central rate since Bulgaria joined that mechanism on 10 July 2020). Bulgaria first participated as a full Eurogroup member at the meeting on 19 January 2026.

Six EU Member States remain outside the eurozone: Czech Republic, Denmark, Hungary, Poland, Romania, Sweden. Denmark holds a formal Treaty opt-out (Maastricht Protocol). The remaining five are legally obliged to adopt the euro once convergence criteria are met but control their own timeline by deferring ERM II entry. As of the 2026 Convergence Report, no non-opt-out state currently meets all criteria or participates in ERM II.

Standard attendees at each Eurogroup meeting, in addition to the 21 finance ministers, are: the Commissioner for Economy (Valdis Dombrovskis, Commissioner for Economy and Productivity, von der Leyen II Commission 2024-2029); the President of the European Central Bank (Christine Lagarde); and, for items concerning the European Stability Mechanism, the ESM Managing Director.

## The President

Under Protocol No 14, Eurogroup members elect their President by a majority for a renewable 2.5-year term. The President chairs meetings, sets the agenda, develops the work programme, reports to ECOFIN (including to non-euro ministers), represents the Eurogroup at international fora such as the G7 Finance Ministers, and conducts Economic Dialogues with the European Parliament. The Eurogroup President also serves as Chair of the Board of Governors of the European Stability Mechanism.

**Current President (as of June 2026):** Kyriakos Pierrakakis (Minister of Economy and Finance, Greece), elected 11 December 2025, took office 12 December 2025, for a 2.5-year mandate until approximately June 2028. His first Eurogroup meeting was 19 January 2026, at which Bulgaria was formally welcomed as the 21st euro area member.

**Immediate predecessor:** Paschal Donohoe (Ireland), who served from 13 July 2020 across three electoral mandates (the third commencing 13 July 2025), resigned in November 2025 to take the position of Managing Director and Chief Knowledge Officer at the World Bank. Makis Keravnos (Cyprus, Finance Minister) chaired the Eurogroup on an acting basis for the period between Donohoe's departure and Pierrakakis taking office.

**Earlier Presidents:** Mario Centeno (Portugal, December 2017 to July 2020); Jeroen Dijsselbloem (Netherlands, January 2013 to January 2018). Prior to 2013, the Eurogroup presidency rotated every six months.

## Eurogroup Working Group (EWG)

The EWG is the permanent preparatory body of the Eurogroup. It is composed of representatives of euro area Member States in the Economic and Financial Committee (EFC), together with representatives of the European Commission and the European Central Bank. The EFC itself is established under Article 134 TFEU as a preparatory body for the Council and the Commission on economic and financial matters.

The EWG meets regularly, in advance of each Eurogroup meeting, to prepare discussions, carry out analytical work, and follow up on commitments made at Eurogroup level. The Chair is elected by EWG members. Current Chair: Tuomas Saarenheimo (Finland), first elected 6 February 2020 and confirmed for a fourth two-year term on 16 February 2026 (term running to approximately February 2028). The EWG Secretariat is provided by the General Secretariat of the Council.

## Inclusive Format

When euro area enlargement or euro adoption processes are on the agenda, the Eurogroup convenes in "inclusive format", bringing together euro area finance ministers alongside the finance ministers of Member States that are preparing for euro adoption, that are in ERM II, or that have expressed a formal interest in the process. The format was introduced to maintain continuity and dialogue with candidate Member States through the convergence process.

Bulgaria participated in inclusive format Eurogroup meetings in the years prior to its formal entry on 1 January 2026. As of 2026, no EU Member State currently participates in ERM II outside the eurozone (Denmark participates in ERM II but holds an opt-out from eurozone membership). Inclusive format meetings remain available for future pre-accession discussions with states such as Romania, Poland, or Hungary should they move towards ERM II participation.

## Euro Summit at Heads of State or Government Level

The Euro Summit convenes the Heads of State or Government of all eurozone Member States, together with the President of the European Commission and the President of the European Central Bank. The Eurogroup President also attends. Euro Summits are grounded in Article 12 of the Treaty on Stability, Coordination and Governance (TSCG, 2012) and are typically held on the margins of European Council meetings, usually twice per year.

The Euro Summit addresses the broad strategic orientations of the euro area, including: major structural reform priorities, the international role of the euro, long-term governance questions (including Banking Union completion and Capital Markets Union/Savings and Investment Union development), and any crisis or exceptional circumstances requiring a political signal at the highest level.

The first Euro Summit of 2026 (19-20 March 2026) focused on euro area economic developments, the international role of the euro, and the Savings and Investment Union. European Council President Antonio Costa, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, ECB President Christine Lagarde, and Eurogroup President Kyriakos Pierrakakis all participated. The March 2026 Euro Summit endorsed the Savings and Investment Union as a strategic priority for the euro area in 2026.

## Relationship with ECOFIN

The Eurogroup operates in a structured relationship with ECOFIN (Economic and Financial Affairs Council), which brings together all 27 EU finance ministers:

- The Eurogroup meets monthly the day before ECOFIN, shaping the political consensus that ECOFIN translates into formal decisions
- The Eurogroup President reports to ECOFIN after each meeting, including to non-euro ministers
- Formal ECOFIN decisions on euro area matters (Stability Programme recommendations, Country-Specific Recommendations under the European Semester, euro adoption Council Decisions) are legally adopted by ECOFIN, but differentiated voting rules apply to certain euro area acts under Articles 136 and 138 TFEU
- ECOFIN remains the formal legislative Council for all EU economic and financial legislation, including Banking Union laws, Capital Markets Union/Savings and Investment Union measures, and economic governance reform

## Main Workstreams

**Banking Union:** The Eurogroup is the central political forum for Banking Union progress. The two operational pillars (Single Supervisory Mechanism under the ECB, and Single Resolution Mechanism) function. The third pillar, a European Deposit Insurance Scheme (EDIS), remains politically stalled. The CMDI reform package (Crisis Management and Deposit Insurance, comprising SRM, BRRD, and DGSD amendments) was signed into law on 30 March 2026 and published in the OJ on 20 April 2026. See `banking_union_reform`.

**Savings and Investment Union:** Following the Draghi Report on EU competitiveness (September 2024) and the Commission Competitiveness Compass (2025), the Savings and Investment Union replaced Capital Markets Union as the overarching label for euro area capital market integration. The Eurogroup under Pierrakakis has made it a flagship workstream. See `savings_and_investment_union`.

**ESM oversight:** The Eurogroup President chairs the ESM Board of Governors. The ESM is the permanent euro area crisis lending facility (established by the ESM Treaty, entered into force 2012, amended 2021). The 2021 amendment was designed to give the ESM a backstop role for the Single Resolution Fund, though the Italian ratification delay prolonged implementation. The legacy European Financial Stability Facility (EFSF) is in wind-down mode. See the planned Brubru guide `esm_efsf_crisis_lending_framework`.

**Euro adoption process:** The Eurogroup provides a unanimous political endorsement (by recommendation) as part of the formal procedure for a Member State to adopt the euro. The full sequence is: (1) Commission Convergence Report assessment; (2) ECB Convergence Report; (3) European Parliament consultation; (4) ECOFIN Decision; (5) Eurogroup endorsement by euro area ministers (political step under the inclusive format). The Council Decision is the binding legal act. Convergence Reports are published every two years by the Commission and ECB, or off-cycle on request as Bulgaria demonstrated in 2025.

**European Semester (euro area dimension):** The Eurogroup validates the euro area economic analysis, including the Euro Area Recommendation and the Annual Sustainable Growth Survey. See `european_semester_communication`.

**Economic governance reform:** The revised Stability and Growth Pact framework (Regulation (EU) 2024/1263 and Directive (EU) 2024/1264, in force June 2024) replaced annual Stability Programmes with medium-term fiscal structural plans. The Eurogroup reviews euro area aggregate fiscal stance and monitors draft budgetary plans each autumn. Escape clauses were activated in 2025-2028 for 12 euro area members to allow higher defence spending. See `eu_budget_emu_law`.

**Digital euro:** The proposed Digital Euro Regulation (COM(2023) 369, proposal July 2023) is currently under co-decision. The Eurogroup has been consulted as part of the legislative process and Pierrakakis identified the digital euro as a 2026 priority for the Eurogroup.

**Monetary policy coordination (ECB interface):** The Eurogroup does not direct ECB monetary policy (the ECB is independent under Article 130 TFEU), but the ECB President reports to the Eurogroup at each meeting on the monetary policy outlook, and the Eurogroup discusses the interaction between fiscal policies and the monetary stance. See `ecb_monetary_policy`.

## Eurogroup Transparency and Accountability

The Eurogroup publishes:
- A letter from the President to ministers ahead of each meeting, setting out the agenda
- Written summaries of conclusions after each meeting
- Press conferences by the President following meetings
- An annual work programme

The Eurogroup President appears before the Economic and Monetary Affairs (ECON) Committee of the European Parliament in regular Economic Dialogues under Article 3 of Protocol No 14. The European Parliament ECON committee may also issue resolutions on euro area economic policy, which the Eurogroup responds to informally.

The ESM Board of Governors (chaired by the Eurogroup President) publishes its own annual report and financial statements, with oversight by the ESM Board of Auditors. ESM activities were also reviewed following the 2021 Treaty amendment discussions.
