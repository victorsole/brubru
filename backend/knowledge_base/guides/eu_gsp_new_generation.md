# New Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP) -- Post-2027 Framework

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU Generalised Scheme of Preferences -- unilateral tariff preferences for developing countries. New regulation post-2027 (current regime expires 31 December 2027)
- Current regime: Regulation (EU) No 978/2012 (applying generalised tariff preferences), CELEX 32012R0978, extended to 2027 by Regulation (EU) 2023/2663
- Replacement proposal: Commission review and new proposal expected 2026, to enter into force 1 January 2028
- Legal basis: Article 207 TFEU (Common Commercial Policy)
- Responsible Commissioner: Maros Sefcovic (Trade and Economic Security)
- Responsible DG: DG TRADE (Unit F.1 Trade-related instruments for sustainable development)
- EP lead committee: INTA (International Trade); DEVE opinion
- Council configuration: FAC (Trade)
- Three arrangements: (1) Standard GSP (reduced MFN tariffs on ~66% of EU tariff lines for ~77 eligible countries); (2) GSP+ (full tariff removal on same tariff lines for vulnerable countries committing to 27 core international conventions on human rights, labour, environment, good governance); (3) Everything But Arms (EBA -- full duty/quota-free access for all products except arms, for Least Developed Countries)
- EPRS At a Glance (20 April 2026): "A new generalised scheme of preferences" -- summary of Commission review, trade volumes, conditionality debate. Ref: EPRS_UNK_f54ab66b6d1a
- Trade volume (2024 baseline): EUR ~65 billion of EU imports under GSP preferences. EBA alone ~EUR 25 billion
- EBA beneficiaries (47 LDCs as of 2024): Afghanistan, Bangladesh (until graduation in Nov 2026), Cambodia, Nepal, Myanmar (partial suspension 2020-), Yemen, etc.
- GSP+ beneficiaries (2024): 8 countries -- Bolivia, Cabo Verde, Kyrgyz Republic, Mongolia, Pakistan, Philippines, Sri Lanka, Uzbekistan
- Conditionality triggers: suspension possible for serious and systematic violations of the 27 core conventions (examples: Belarus suspended 2007, Sri Lanka suspended 2010-2017, Myanmar partial suspension 2020-)
- **LATEST (28 April 2026)**: **Plenary vote Item 40** "Generalised Tariff Preferences Scheme" -- rapporteur **Bernd Lange (S&D, Germany, INTA chair)** at 12:00 voting block (OJQ-10-2026-04-28_EN). Vote sets EP position on the post-2027 GSP framework feeding Commission's expected 2026 proposal.

## Why a New Regulation

The current GSP Regulation expires on 31 December 2027. The 2022 Commission proposal for the new GSP (COM(2021) 579) stalled in interinstitutional negotiations over:
- Whether to expand the list of 27 core conventions (including Paris Agreement on Climate)
- Whether to add new graduation rules for rapidly developing countries (India, Indonesia, Vietnam graduated during current cycle)
- How to integrate CBAM and forced-labour exclusions
- Bangladesh graduation (UN LDC category exit November 2026 -- triggers 3-year transition period)

The Commission is expected to retable a revised proposal in 2026 incorporating EPRS-flagged issues.

## Key Legislative Points Expected

- **Graduation rules**: tightened thresholds for "upper-middle-income" countries
- **New conventions**: likely addition of Paris Agreement, new labour conventions (ILO 189 Domestic Workers, ILO 190 Violence and Harassment)
- **Forced labour**: cross-reference to Forced Labour Regulation (EU) 2024/3015
- **Environmental conditionality**: stronger link to Regulation on Deforestation-free products (EUDR)
- **CBAM alignment**: clarifying how GSP preferences apply to products under CBAM scope
- **Automatic safeguards**: tightening of threshold triggers for safeguard investigations
- **Smoother EBA-to-GSP transition**: managing Bangladesh graduation and other LDC exits
