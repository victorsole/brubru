## Why revise the 2012 Directive

The 2012 Victims' Rights Directive (VRD) was the EU's first horizontal instrument on victim protection. Its 2020 evaluation flagged persistent implementation gaps across Member States:
- **Information**: many victims do not receive the information mandated by Article 4 in language or form they understand
- **Access to support services**: uneven coverage across Member States; specialised support for victims of sexual violence, terrorism, hate crime, and trafficking is patchy
- **Vulnerable victims**: protection regimes for children, victims of gender-based violence, and victims of hate crime require strengthening
- **Compensation**: cross-border compensation procedures (interaction with the 2004 Compensation Directive 2004/80/EC) remain slow and opaque
- **Digital transformation**: 2012 text did not contemplate online crime victims, cyber-stalking, online sexual exploitation as core categories
- **Coordination with horizontal acts**: VRD interacts with the Anti-Trafficking Directive (2011/36/EU), the Combating Sexual Abuse and Sexual Exploitation Directive (2011/93/EU), the Combating Terrorism Directive (2017/541/EU), and the **Anti-Violence Against Women Directive** (Directive (EU) 2024/1385, "Istanbul Convention act").

## Key elements of the revision (COM(2023) 424)

1. **Right to information**: strengthened obligations on first-contact authorities; "user-friendly" duty (plain language, accessible formats); dedicated information rights for victims with disabilities
2. **Universal helpline**: EU-wide single helpline number (`116 006`) for victims, with specialist sub-helplines (gender-based violence, terrorism, hate crime) — Member States required to fund + staff
3. **Specialist support services**: minimum standards for trauma-informed support; obligation to ensure availability across all Member States; pathway for cross-border referral
4. **Protection of vulnerable victims**: enhanced individual assessment under Article 22; targeted protection measures for child victims, victims of gender-based violence, victims of hate crime, victims with disabilities
5. **Right to compensation**: Member State authority responsible for cross-border compensation must use an EU coordination point; clearer timelines
6. **Restorative justice safeguards**: minimum standards for safe + voluntary access; safeguards against re-victimisation
7. **Digital + remote rights**: video-conference hearings as standard option; remote access to case files; digital first-information packages
