# Sub-national delegations to the EU: Catalonia, German Länder, Belgian regions and beyond

## QUICK FACTS

- **Status:** Sub-national representations hold NO seat in Coreper, NO Council voting right; they are political, cultural, and economic liaison offices operating alongside national Permanent Representations
- **Legal gateway -- Article 16(2) TEU:** "The Council shall consist of a representative of each Member State at ministerial level, who may commit the government of that Member State and cast the vote." Federal and quasi-federal Member States (Germany, Belgium, Austria) use this provision to send regional ministers to Council meetings when the subject falls within their competence
- **Catalonia:** The Delegació del Govern de Catalunya davant la Unió Europea is the Generalitat de Catalunya's permanent office in Brussel·les; it monitors legislation, advocates Catalan interests, and liaises with EU institutions and the European Parliament
- **German Länder:** All 16 Länder maintain Brussels offices (Landesvertretungen); Bayern, Baden-Württemberg, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Berlin, Hamburg, and others run their own staffed representations
- **Belgian regions:** Flanders, Wallonia, the Brussels-Capital Region, the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles (French Community), and the German-speaking Community all maintain Brussels representations; under Belgium's unique federal coordination system, a regional or community minister can cast Belgium's vote in Council when the matter falls within their exclusive competences
- **UK home nations (post-Brexit):** Scotland House Brussels and the Wales Brussels Office continue to monitor EU developments; since 31 January 2020 they no longer hold EU sub-national membership status but remain active policy-monitoring presences
- **Faroe Islands and Greenland:** Both have Brussels representations; neither is an EU Member State territory (Greenland left in 1985, the Faroe Islands never joined); their offices focus on fisheries, trade, and OCT-related relations
- **Committee of the Regions (CoR):** The main formal interface for sub-national voices in EU law; advisory body with 329 members, mandatory consultation on cohesion, transport, environment and more (see `committee_of_the_regions`)
- **EP intergroups and the European Alliance (EA) group in the CoR:** Sub-national regionalist parties cluster in the EA group in the CoR and in several EP intergroups, advancing minority-language rights, subsidiarity protections, and cohesion policy priorities
- **Over 300 regional offices** operate in Brussels; they form a parallel advocacy circuit that shapes EU policy well before proposals reach co-decision

## Overview

Sub-national territories -- regions, autonomous communities, Länder, devolved nations -- pursue EU-level interests directly from Brussel·les without displacing their national governments. These offices complement, rather than replace, the national Permanent Representations (PermReps) that dominate the formal Council machinery. Their work ranges from early-warning monitoring on cohesion funding and agricultural regulation to direct lobbying of Commission directorate-generals on proposals that affect their constitutional competences.

The web of sub-national EU offices is extensive. Estimates put the number of regional representations in Brussels at over 300. Alongside the national PermReps, these offices form a parallel circuit of advocacy, intelligence-gathering, and coalition-building that sophisticated EU affairs professionals use to shape legislation well before a proposal enters co-decision.

## Legal Status: Article 16(2) TEU and Sub-national Participation

The formal gateway for sub-national participation in the Council of the EU is **Article 16(2) TEU**:

> "The Council shall consist of a representative of each Member State at ministerial level, who may commit the government of that Member State and cast the vote."

The operative phrase is "at ministerial level." In Germany, a Bundesrat minister representing a Land can attend and vote in the Council when the agenda concerns Land-level competences (culture, education, broadcasting). In Belgium, a Regional or Community minister can represent Belgium when the matter falls within their exclusive competences. Austria, with its nine Bundesländer, uses an analogous mechanism.

What sub-national offices in Brussels are NOT:
- Seated at Coreper (the ambassadorial body that prepares Council decisions)
- Entitled to an independent Council vote separate from their national government
- Accredited as diplomatic missions under the Vienna Convention

What they ARE:
- Political liaison offices with informal access to Commission officials, EP assistants, and Council Working Party delegates
- Early-warning antennae for legislation affecting regional competences
- Advocates for cohesion funds, structural investment, and region-specific regulatory interests
- Coordination hubs between regional governments and their national and European Parliament delegations
- Members of cross-regional coalitions (such as the "Four Motors for Europe" network: Baden-Württemberg, Catalonia, Lombardy, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes)

## Catalonia: Delegació del Govern de Catalunya davant la Unió Europea

Catalonia maintains a permanent delegation in Brussel·les: the **Delegació del Govern de Catalunya davant la Unió Europea**, operating under the Generalitat de Catalunya's Departament d'Acció Exterior i Unió Europea.

**Website:** https://exteriors.gencat.cat/en/ambits-dactuacio/afers_exteriors/delegacions_govern/ue/delegacio/

The delegation's principal activities include:
- Monitoring EU legislative proposals that affect Catalan competences: language policy, culture, trade, agriculture, tourism, digital economy
- Building coalitions with other stateless-nation and regional offices in Brussels (Flanders, Bavaria, Scotland, Brittany, South Tyrol, Basque Country, Galicia)
- Liaison with CoR members nominated by the Generalitat
- Engaging with Catalan and Spanish MEPs on strategically significant legislative files
- Reporting back to the Generalitat on the EU policy pipeline and upcoming initiatives

The delegation sits alongside a broader Catalan ecosystem in Brussels: Patronat de Turisme, Catalan-interest business associations, and European-level organisations in which Catalonia holds membership.

**Catalonia in the CoR:** The Generalitat nominates members to the CoR's full and alternate member lists. Catalan CoR members typically sit within the **European Alliance (EA)** political group, which brings together regionalist and autonomist parties from across the EU.

**Brubru relevance:** Brubru's Catalan core audience includes professionals working on EU affairs from a Catalan institutional or civil-society perspective. The delegation is the primary Brussels contact point for Catalan organisations engaged in EU advocacy. Brubru Chat can assist with monitoring legislative files of direct Catalan institutional interest and identifying relevant Commission consultation windows.

## Belgian Regional Offices

Belgium's federal structure makes it constitutionally unique in the EU. Under Belgian law, Regions and Communities exercise EU competences directly in their areas, and the **Belgian intra-federal coordination system** (governed by cooperation agreements) determines which minister -- federal, Regional, or Community -- represents Belgium in each Council configuration.

| Entity | Nature | Brussels role |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Vlaanderen (Flanders) | Region and Community combined | Flanders House Brussels (Vlaams Huis); active on agriculture, innovation, culture, foreign trade |
| Région wallonne (Wallonia) | Region | Délégation Wallonie-Bruxelles; agriculture, environment, economic development |
| Région de Bruxelles-Capitale | Region | Brussels International (Bruxelles International); urban policy, economic affairs |
| Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles (French Community) | Community | Shares Wallonia delegation infrastructure; education, culture, youth |
| Deutschsprachige Gemeinschaft | Community | Smallest entity; coordinates via the Wallonia delegation |

Belgium's Council coordination protocol: before each Council meeting, the five entities meet under a rotating inter-entity presidency to agree a single Belgian position. When agreement cannot be reached, Belgium abstains. Under this mechanism, Flemish and Walloon ministers have formally voted as "Belgium" in Education, Culture, Agriculture and Environment Councils.

## UK Home-Nation Offices Post-Brexit

**Scotland House Brussels** was established in 1999 as the Scottish Executive's (later Scottish Government's) Brussels office. Since Brexit took effect on 31 January 2020, Scotland House no longer represents an EU sub-national entity, but continues to operate as a policy-monitoring and political liaison presence. It engages with MEPs, Commission officials, and EU think-tanks on issues of Scottish interest: fisheries, energy policy, financial services, and trade.

**Website:** https://www.gov.scot/about/contact-information/scotland-house-brussels/

**Wales Brussels Office:** The Welsh Government maintained a Brussels presence; post-Brexit its capacity was substantially reduced. Verify current operational status against gov.wales communications before citing specific staff or functions.

**Northern Ireland:** The Northern Ireland Executive maintained a Brussels liaison office. Post-Brexit, Northern Ireland's relationship with the EU is structurally distinct due to the Windsor Framework governing its position within the EU single market for goods. This creates unique EU-monitoring needs for Belfast-based advocacy professionals.

## Faroe Islands and Greenland

**Faroe Islands:** A self-governing territory of Denmark, the Faroe Islands never joined the EU. Faroese fisheries access to EU waters is governed by bilateral fisheries agreements. The Faroese government (Løgting) maintains a Brussels representation focusing on fisheries negotiations, EEA-adjacent bilateral relations, and Nordic cooperation issues.

**Greenland:** Following a referendum in 1982, Greenland left the European Communities in 1985 (via the Greenland Treaty). Since then, Greenland holds the status of Overseas Country and Territory (OCT) associated with the EU under Annex II TFEU and the OCT Decision. Naalakkersuisut (Greenland's Government) maintains a representation in Brussels focused on fisheries partnership agreements, climate and Arctic policy dialogue, and critical raw materials cooperation. Greenland's strategic mineral deposits have made this office increasingly relevant to EU supply-chain diversification discussions since 2023.

## Coordination with National PermReps

Sub-national offices do not bypass the national Permanent Representation. The relationship is complementary:

| Function | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| **Intelligence-sharing** | Regional offices alert the national PermRep to legislation touching regional competences; the PermRep feeds back Council positions and internal political dynamics |
| **Informal lobbying** | Regional offices cultivate direct contacts at Commission DG level; they advocate to DG AGRI, DG REGIO, DG GROW officials without necessarily routing through the PermRep |
| **Political contacts** | Regional offices maintain direct access to their national MEPs and EP committee coordinators; they can request EP hearings or written questions independently |
| **Conflict resolution** | When regional interests diverge from national policy, the PermRep holds formal seniority, but sustained regional pressure through the CoR, EP intergroups and Commission contacts can shift national positions in Council Working Parties |

The Catalan delegation is formally part of the Spanish foreign affairs system for coordination purposes, but retains its own political and programmatic agenda. This dual logic -- operating within the legal frame of the Spanish state while advancing specifically Catalan interests -- defines the institutional character of the Brussel·les delegation.

## Committee of the Regions and EP Intergroup Interface

**Committee of the Regions (CoR):** The principal formal mechanism for sub-national input into EU legislation. It issues mandatory opinions on cohesion, transport, environment, health, education, and employment proposals, and may refer subsidiarity violations to the CJEU. Sub-national Brussels offices actively lobby CoR rapporteurs and track CoR opinions as a barometer of regional political sentiment within the EU institutions. See `committee_of_the_regions`.

**CoR political groups relevant to sub-national regionalism:**
- **European Alliance (EA):** Groups regionalist and autonomist parties including representatives from Catalonia, Flanders, the Basque Country, Galicia, Scotland, Brittany, South Tyrol, and similar stateless-nation or strong-region constituencies. The EA is the CoR's most explicitly sub-national political formation.
- **EPP, Renew, PES:** Also contain strong sub-national members from German Länder, Belgian regions, and large Spanish autonomous communities.

**EP intergroups relevant to sub-national interests:**
- **Intergroup on Regions and Local Authorities, Subsidiarity and Multilevel Governance:** Active cross-party grouping of MEPs from regionalist parties or strong regional constituencies.
- Catalan, Basque, Galician, and Flemish MEPs have participated in informal coordination networks on cultural recognition, minority-language rights in EU contexts, and subsidiarity enforcement.
- The **Conference of European Regional Legislative Assemblies (CALRE)** -- a formal network outside the EU institutions -- links regional parliaments with legislative powers (Catalonia's Parliament is a member) and feeds positions into CoR and EP processes.

## Sub-national EU Policy Entrepreneurship

Where sub-national representations punch above their weight:

**Cohesion policy (MFF cycle):** During each Multiannual Financial Framework negotiation, regional offices from less-developed regions or those facing reclassification mount intensive campaigns. Direct pressure on Commissioner cabinets and Council Presidency counterparts can shift individual country allocations by hundreds of millions of euros.

**State aid pre-notification:** Regional authorities (Flanders on semiconductor investment, Bayern on hydrogen hubs, Catalonia on biomedical clusters) engage DG COMP directly on pre-notification discussions for large regional state-aid schemes, bypassing the national-government intermediary at the technical level.

**Language and cultural rights:** Catalan and other minority-language communities use the EP petition process, CoR opinions, and Commission dialogue to advance recognition in EU contexts, including requests for Catalan to be added as an official EU language (a matter requiring unanimity in Council and therefore dependent on Spanish government support).

**Regulatory lobbying depth:** Large Länder offices (Bayern, NRW, Baden-Württemberg) maintain specialist advisers on automotive regulation, pharmaceuticals, chemical safety, and digital policy. Their technical depth rivals that of many national PermReps.

**Transnational coalition-building:** The "Four Motors for Europe" (Baden-Württemberg, Catalonia, Lombardy, Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes) illustrates how sub-national regions form trans-European coalitions outside the national-government track. Founded in 1988, the network coordinates positions on innovation, industrial policy, and climate in parallel with but independently of the four respective national governments.
