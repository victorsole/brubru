# EEA: European Environment Agency

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** European Environment Agency (EEA) -- the EU's environmental knowledge and data agency
- **Founding act:** Council Regulation (EEC) No 1210/90 of 7 May 1990 established the EEA; it became operational in 1994. The founding text was later **codified** (consolidated without substantive change) as **Regulation (EC) No 401/2009** of 23 April 2009 on the EEA and the European Environment Information and Observation Network; CELEX 32009R0401
- **Seat:** Copenhagen, Denmark
- **Executive Director:** Leena Ylä-Mononen (Finland), in post since 1 June 2023 for a five-year term (through 2028)
- **Membership:** **32 member countries** (the EU27 plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland and Türkiye) plus a group of **cooperating countries** in the Western Balkans (Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia). The EEA's own flagship 2025 assessment counts **38 member and cooperating countries** in total; exact cooperating-country counts vary slightly between EEA publications as accession and partnership status evolves, so treat 38 as the current headline figure rather than a fixed constant.
- **Eionet:** the **European Environment Information and Observation Network**, a partnership of the EEA and national reference centres/focal points in every member and cooperating country. Eionet is the EEA's main channel for collecting, quality-checking and pooling environmental data; it has operated since the EEA became operational in 1994.
- **Reportnet 3:** the EEA's current e-reporting infrastructure (reportnet.europa.eu) through which countries, national institutions and other bodies submit the data flows required under EU environmental and climate law to Eionet and the EEA.
- **Flagship assessment:** **"Europe's environment 2025"**, published 29 September 2025 -- the EEA's seventh integrated State of the Environment Report (SOER) since 1995 (following SOER 2010, 2015 and 2020). It combines a narrative assessment, 35 thematic briefings and country profiles covering 38 countries.
- **Climate-ADAPT:** the European climate adaptation platform (climate-adapt.eea.europa.eu), run jointly by the EEA and the European Commission, hosting adaptation knowledge, tools and national adaptation strategies/plans.
- **European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change (ESABCC):** the EU's independent climate science advisory body, established by **Article 10a**, inserted into Regulation (EC) No 401/2009 by the **European Climate Law** (Regulation (EU) 2021/1119). It has 15 independent members appointed for renewable four-year terms; its **Secretariat is hosted by the EEA** in Copenhagen. The Board's second term began 24 March 2026.
- **What the EEA does NOT do:** it has no enforcement, inspection or sanctioning powers over Member States or companies. It is a knowledge and data body, not a regulator -- compare it with **ECHA** (chemicals authorisation) or **EMA** (medicines authorisation), which do have binding regulatory functions.
- **Underpins:** the **European Climate Law**, the **Zero Pollution Action Plan**, the **8th Environment Action Programme** monitoring framework, and the reporting/indicator architecture behind EU air quality, greenhouse-gas, water and biodiversity legislation (including the **Nature Restoration Law**).
- **Mandate status:** the EEA's founding regulation was last substantively amended in 2021 (the European Climate Law's Article 10a, creating the ESABCC secretariat function). No confirmed, formally tabled Commission proposal to recast Regulation (EC) No 401/2009 itself could be verified as of this guide's publication -- if a recast proposal is tabled, check the EEA's own "who we are" pages and EUR-Lex CELEX 32009R0401 procedure history for the live status before citing one.

The EEA is the EU's standing knowledge and data agency for the environment and climate. It does not set policy or enforce it; it builds and coordinates the evidence base -- through Eionet, Reportnet 3 and its own assessments -- that the Commission, Parliament, Council and Member States use to design, monitor and evaluate environmental and climate law.

## Legal Basis and Mandate

- **Regulation (EC) No 401/2009** is the current consolidated legal basis. It codifies (restates without substantive change) the original founding act, **Council Regulation (EEC) No 1210/90**, together with its intervening amendments (including Regulation (EC) No 933/1999, which opened EEA membership to non-EU European countries).
- Legal basis in the Treaties: environmental policy competence (Article 192 TFEU and predecessor Treaty articles).
- The regulation defines the EEA's tasks (Article 2): providing objective, reliable and comparable information at European level to enable the Union and Member States to take environmental protection measures, assess results and inform the public; and coordinating Eionet.
- **Article 10a**, inserted by the European Climate Law (Regulation (EU) 2021/1119), gives the EEA a specific statutory role: hosting the Secretariat of the European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change.
- The EEA's mandate is periodically widened by sectoral legislation that assigns it specific data, indicator or reporting tasks (for example under air quality, water and biodiversity law) without necessarily amending Regulation 401/2009 itself -- Brubru users tracking a specific EEA task should check the sectoral act (e.g. the Nature Restoration Law's monitoring provisions) rather than assume it is captured in the founding regulation.

## What the EEA Actually Does

### 1. Coordinating Eionet
Eionet is a network, not a single database: each member and cooperating country designates a **National Focal Point** and a set of **National Reference Centres** covering specific environmental themes (air, water, climate, biodiversity, land use, waste and more). The EEA coordinates this network, sets common methodologies and quality standards, and pools the resulting data into EU-wide assessments.

### 2. Running Reportnet 3
Reportnet 3 is the technical infrastructure through which countries fulfil their **legal reporting obligations** under EU environmental and climate directives and regulations (for example under air quality, industrial emissions, greenhouse-gas monitoring and water legislation). It replaces earlier generations of the EEA's reporting tools and is designed to reduce duplicate reporting by countries across overlapping legal obligations (the EU's "streamlining environmental reporting" agenda).

### 3. Flagship and thematic assessments
- **State of the Environment Report (SOER):** the EEA's headline integrated assessment, published roughly every five years. The current edition, **"Europe's environment 2025"**, was published 29 September 2025 and combines a cross-cutting narrative, 35 thematic briefings and country-level profiles.
- **Indicators:** a continuously updated set of environmental and climate indicators (air quality, greenhouse-gas emissions, water status, biodiversity, land take, circular economy, and more), each tracking progress against EU policy targets.
- **Datahub:** the EEA's public data-download service, giving direct access to the underlying datasets behind its indicators and assessments.
- **Country fact sheets and maps/dashboards:** country-specific and thematic visualisations built on the same underlying data.

### 4. Climate adaptation knowledge hub
**Climate-ADAPT** is the EU's platform for climate change adaptation knowledge, jointly maintained by the EEA and the Commission. It hosts national and sectoral adaptation strategies, case studies, tools (including risk and vulnerability assessment methods) and the EEA's own adaptation-progress reporting, such as its recurring assessment of Member States' adaptation planning and action.

### 5. Hosting the European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change (ESABCC)
The ESABCC is legally independent of the EEA (its members act in a personal, independent capacity and are not EEA staff), but the EEA hosts its Secretariat in Copenhagen under Article 10a of Regulation 401/2009. The Board provides independent scientific advice on the EU's climate targets, climate law coherence and progress toward the 2050 climate-neutrality objective, and on the EU's alignment with the Paris Agreement.

### 6. Supporting sectoral EU environmental and climate law
The EEA's data and indicators feed directly into the monitoring and review cycles of, among others: the **European Climate Law** (climate-neutrality trajectory monitoring), the **Zero Pollution Action Plan** (the 2030 zero-pollution targets for air, water and soil), the **8th Environment Action Programme** (the EU's environmental strategic framework to 2030), and reporting obligations under EU air quality, water and biodiversity legislation, including the monitoring architecture the **Nature Restoration Law** will rely on as Member States submit their national restoration plans.

## Governance and Structure

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Management Board | One representative per member/cooperating country, two Commission representatives, two scientific personalities appointed by the European Parliament, and observers; sets strategic direction and adopts the work programme and budget |
| Executive Director | Day-to-day management and legal representation of the agency; currently Leena Ylä-Mononen (Finland) |
| Scientific Committee | Advises the Management Board and Executive Director on scientific matters |
| National Focal Points | One per member/cooperating country; the country-level entry point into Eionet |
| National Reference Centres | Thematic experts nominated by each country, feeding specific data domains into Eionet |

## The EEA in the Wider EU Environmental and Climate Architecture

| Body | Role | Relationship to the EEA |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| EEA | Environmental and climate knowledge, data, indicators, assessments | -- |
| Eionet | Network of national reference centres/focal points | Coordinated by the EEA; the EEA's primary data-collection channel |
| ESABCC | Independent scientific advice on EU climate policy | Secretariat hosted by the EEA (Article 10a, Regulation 401/2009); members are independent of the EEA |
| European Commission (DG ENV, DG CLIMA) | Sets environmental and climate policy, proposes legislation | Principal user of EEA data/assessments; co-runs Climate-ADAPT with the EEA |
| ECHA | Chemicals registration, evaluation and authorisation (REACH) | Sister decentralised agency; has binding regulatory powers the EEA does not have |
| Member States | Report data through Eionet/Reportnet 3; implement adaptation and mitigation policy | Supply the underlying data the EEA aggregates and assesses |

## Why This Matters for Advocacy and Compliance

- **Evidence base, not enforcement:** the EEA does not investigate, inspect or sanction. Companies and sectors should treat EEA indicators, SOER assessments and Climate-ADAPT case studies as authoritative, citable EU evidence for policy submissions and compliance narratives, not as a source of legal obligations in themselves.
- **Reporting architecture:** if an organisation's compliance function submits environmental data under an EU directive or regulation, that data very likely flows through **Reportnet 3** into Eionet and the EEA -- understanding this pipeline clarifies which national authority actually receives, validates and forwards a given report.
- **Climate adaptation planning:** stakeholders engaging on climate-adaptation policy (infrastructure, insurance, agriculture, water-intensive sectors) should track **Climate-ADAPT** and the EEA's recurring national-adaptation-progress assessments directly, since these shape the evidence the Commission uses when reviewing Member States' adaptation strategies.
- **Climate science credibility:** for submissions referencing the EU's climate-neutrality trajectory or Paris Agreement alignment, the **ESABCC**'s independent scientific opinions (hosted, not authored, by the EEA) carry particular weight as a primary EU source.
- **Zero Pollution and Nature Restoration monitoring:** organisations tracking the **Zero Pollution Action Plan** or the **Nature Restoration Law**'s national restoration plans should expect the EEA's indicator and reporting infrastructure to become the practical measurement layer for both files as implementation matures.
