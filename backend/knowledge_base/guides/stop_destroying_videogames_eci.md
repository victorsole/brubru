## ECI mechanics

The European Citizens' Initiative is one of the two formal participatory-democracy instruments of the EU (alongside the right of petition to the EP). Established by Article 11(4) TEU and operationalised by Regulation (EU) 2019/788, the ECI allows 1 million EU citizens from at least 7 Member States to ask the Commission to propose legislation in an area of its competence.

**ECI procedural stages**:
1. **Registration** by the Commission — formal admissibility check
2. **Signature collection** — 12 months to gather 1 million signatures; thresholds per Member State (sliding scale)
3. **Submission** — validated signatures submitted; Commission must publish a Communication within 6 months explaining what action (legislative or non-legislative) it intends to take, or why it does not intend to act

The Commission's response is **not legally binding to propose legislation** — but a public Communication is mandatory, and the political cost of inaction on a successful ECI is significant.

## What the ECI asks for

"Stop Destroying Videogames" asks the Commission to:
- Require publishers selling or licensing video-games in the EU to leave games **in a playable state** when official support ends
- Include online-dependent / live-service games — players who paid for the game should retain a functional version (offline mode, local server option, community-server support) even after the publisher discontinues servers
- Strengthen consumer rights against unfair contract terms that allow publishers to render purchased games unplayable
- Anchor the requirement in EU consumer / contract law — likely through the Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU) or the Sale of Goods Directive (2019/771)

## Political + legal context

The ECI reaches the Commission in a context of:
- Heightened attention to **planned obsolescence** in the **Right to Repair Directive** (Directive (EU) 2024/1799) and the **EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation** (Reg (EU) 2024/1781)
- Consumer-protection scrutiny of **subscription / live-service business models** under DG JUST + national consumer authorities (recent CPC enforcement actions on loot boxes, microtransactions)
- The **Digital Fairness Act** Commission Communication of 3 October 2024 + forthcoming proposal — examining manipulative interface design, digital addiction, dark patterns
- **Player Coalition initiatives** in France, Germany, the Netherlands pushing national parliamentary inquiries on video-game support lifecycle
