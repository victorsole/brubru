# Stakeholder Mapping for EU Public Affairs

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Stakeholder identification, analysis, and engagement prioritisation for EU advocacy
- Scope: European Parliament, European Commission, Council of the EU, informal influencers
- Core tool: Influence x Attitude Matrix (XY Matrix) with 4 priority quadrants
- Priority 1: High Influence + Hostile -- convert or neutralise
- Priority 2: High Influence + Supportive -- mobilise as champions
- Priority 3: Low Influence + Supportive -- build coalitions
- Priority 4: Low Influence + Hostile -- monitor only, do not engage
- EP priority order: Rapporteur > Shadow rapporteurs > Coordinators > Committee members
- Commission priority order: Cabinet > DG/Director > Head of Unit
- Council entry point: Your national Permanent Representation
- Mapping steps: 6 (Identify file, Map formal actors, Map informal influencers, Assess attitude, Assess influence, Prioritise)
- Key rule: Focus on 15-20 stakeholders that matter; reassess at each legislative phase

A practical framework for identifying, analysing, and engaging the people who shape EU policy outcomes.

## Why Stakeholder Mapping Matters

In EU lobbying, you don't need to convince everyone. You need to convince the right people at the right time. Stakeholder mapping tells you:
- Where to invest your limited time and resources
- Who can champion your position
- Who might block you and how to respond
- Which relationships to build before you need them

## The Influence × Attitude Matrix

This is the core tool that professional consultancies use to prioritise stakeholders.

```

                 Hostile ←——————→ Supportive
                    │               │
    HIGH    ┌───────┼───────────────┼───────┐
            │       │               │       │
            │  NEUTRALISE    │   MOBILISE   │

            ├───────┼───────────────┼───────┤
            │       │               │       │
    LOW     │  MONITOR      │   BUILD       │
            │  (Low priority)│   COALITION  │

            └───────┼───────────────┼───────┘
```

### Quadrant Strategies

#### 1. High Influence + Hostile (TOP LEFT)
**Highest priority. These stakeholders can kill your position.**

**Who they are:**
- Rapporteur who disagrees with you
- Commissioner with opposing view
- Large competitor lobbying against you
- Powerful NGO campaigning on your issue

**Strategy options:**
1. **Convert:** Find common ground, address their concerns
2. **Neutralise:** Make opposing you costly (political, reputational)
3. **Contain:** Limit their influence through counter-coalitions
4. **Wait out:** Sometimes people move on to other files

**Engagement approach:**
- Seek early dialogue before positions harden
- Understand their motivations and constraints
- Look for trades ("we can support X if you accommodate Y")
- Never burn the bridge - they may hold different files later

---

#### 2. High Influence + Supportive (TOP RIGHT)
**Your champions. Mobilise them actively.**

**Who they are:**
- Shadow rapporteur aligned with you
- Commissioner cabinet member who "gets it"
- Friendly PermRep official
- Allied industry association

**Strategy options:**
1. **Arm them:** Provide talking points, data, draft text
2. **Amplify:** Help them make the case publicly
3. **Coordinate:** Align timing and messaging
4. **Protect:** Don't overload them or make them look like your puppet

**Engagement approach:**
- Regular contact (but not excessive)
- Brief them before key votes/meetings
- Share intelligence about opposition
- Thank them publicly and privately
- Make them look good

---

#### 3. Low Influence + Supportive (BOTTOM RIGHT)
**Coalition partners. Numbers matter in Brussels.**

**Who they are:**
- Small MEPs who agree with you
- Junior officials in relevant DGs
- SMEs and start-ups in your sector
- Regional stakeholders
- Academic experts

**Strategy options:**
1. **Aggregate:** Combine voices into coalitions
2. **Amplify:** Help them reach higher-influence actors
3. **Cultivate:** Today's assistant is tomorrow's director

**Engagement approach:**
- Include in joint letters and statements
- Share platforms and speaking opportunities
- Provide resources they lack
- Build relationships for the long term

---

#### 4. Low Influence + Hostile (BOTTOM LEFT)
**Monitor but don't engage. Waste of resources.**

**Who they are:**
- Activists with limited political access
- Fringe political actors
- Hostile journalists without major reach
- Competitors too small to matter

**Strategy options:**
1. **Ignore:** Don't engage, don't legitimise
2. **Monitor:** Keep awareness in case influence grows
3. **Don't antagonise:** No reason to create enemies

**Caution:** Low influence can change. NGOs gain momentum. Journalists break stories. Reassess periodically.

## Mapping EU Decision-Makers

### European Parliament

| Role | Influence Level | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Rapporteur | Very High | Authors the report, controls structure |
| Shadow rapporteurs | High | Represent political groups, negotiate |
| Committee chair | High | Controls agenda and timing |
| Coordinators | High | Decide group positions |
| Political group advisors | Medium-High | Brief MEPs, shape positions |
| Committee members | Medium | Vote on amendments |
| APAs | Medium | Gatekeepers, research support |
| Other MEPs | Low-Medium | Plenary votes only |

**Priority order:** Rapporteur → Shadows → Coordinators → Committee members

---

### European Commission

| Role | Influence Level | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Commissioner | Very High | Political direction |
| Cabinet members | Very High | Commissioner's inner circle |
| Director-General | High | DG leadership |
| Director | High | Unit oversight |
| Head of Unit | Medium-High | Day-to-day drafting |
| Policy officer | Medium | Writes the text |
| Secretariat-General | High | Inter-service coordination |

**Priority order:** Cabinet → DG/Director → Head of Unit

**Timing matters:** Once interservice consultation begins, positions are locked.

---

### Council of the EU

| Role | Influence Level | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Presidency | Very High | Sets agenda, brokers compromises |
| Working party chair | High | Manages technical negotiations |
| COREPER ambassadors | Very High | Political-level decisions |
| PermRep attachés | Medium-High | Your national contact |
| National ministry officials | Medium | Feed into PermRep positions |
| Council Secretariat | Medium | Institutional memory, procedure |

**Priority order:** Your PermRep → Presidency → Key member state PermReps

**Reality check:** Council is opaque. PermReps are your entry point.

## Building Your Stakeholder Map

### Step 1: Identify the File

- What's the legislative proposal or policy initiative?
- What procedure (OLP, special, delegated act)?
- What's the timeline?

### Step 2: Map the Formal Actors

For each institution:
- Who has formal responsibility?
- Who will vote or decide?
- What's the chain of command?

### Step 3: Map the Informal Influencers

- Which NGOs are active on this file?
- Which industry associations?
- Which think tanks or academics?
- Which media are covering it?
- Which member states have strong positions?

### Step 4: Assess Attitude

For each stakeholder, determine:
- **Supportive:** Aligned with your position
- **Neutral:** No position yet or persuadable
- **Hostile:** Opposed to your position
- **Unknown:** Requires research

### Step 5: Assess Influence

Consider:
- Formal role in the procedure
- Political weight and seniority
- Expertise and credibility
- Network and alliances
- Track record on similar files

### Step 6: Prioritise

Place each stakeholder on the matrix. Focus on:
1. Converting or neutralising high-influence hostiles
2. Mobilising high-influence supporters
3. Building coalitions with low-influence supporters
4. Monitoring (but not engaging) low-influence hostiles

## Dynamic Stakeholder Management

### Positions Change

- Initial positions often soften during negotiations
- Political circumstances shift (elections, scandals)
- New information changes calculations
- Coalition dynamics evolve

**Action:** Reassess your map at each phase (Commission → EP → Council → Trilogue)

### Influence Changes

- People get promoted, move on, retire
- Committee assignments change
- Political groups reshuffle
- Elections reset everything

**Action:** Maintain relationships beyond single files

### New Actors Emerge

- Unexpected stakeholders enter the debate
- Media attention elevates obscure players
- Crises create new coalitions

**Action:** Stay alert, update map continuously

## Common Mapping Mistakes

1. **Mapping too many stakeholders** - Focus on the 15-20 that matter
2. **Static mapping** - Positions and influence change; update regularly
3. **Overweighting formal roles** - Informal influence often matters more
4. **Ignoring the Council** - Half the legislature, often undermapped
5. **Mapping only supporters** - Know your opponents better than yourself
6. **Confusing visibility with influence** - Loud doesn't mean powerful
7. **Missing second-order actors** - Who influences your influencers?
