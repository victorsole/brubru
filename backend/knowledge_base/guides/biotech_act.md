## Why This Regulation

The EU biotechnology industry has grown more than twice as fast as the overall EU economy. In 2022, it accounted for EUR 38.1 billion of Union GDP and contributed 913,160 jobs (75% in health biotech). Each job in industrial biotech generates 3.4 additional jobs in the broader economy.

Despite a strong scientific base (21% of world's top 10% most-cited publications in biology, biomedical research, and clinical medicine), the EU faces a critical competitiveness gap:
- EU captures only 7% of global health biotech VC investment (vs USA 63%, China 14%)
- US biopharma start-ups receive 9x more late-stage funding than EU equivalents
- EUR 219 billion VC invested in US health biotech vs EUR 25 billion in EU (2015-2025)
- 66 out of 67 EU biotech companies that went public in the last 6 years listed on non-EU stock exchanges
- EU share of commercially sponsored clinical trials in the EEA dropped from 22% (2013) to 12% (2023); China rose from 5% to 18%
- EU clinical trial authorisation takes on average 113 days for multinational trials; competing regions issue decisions in 60 days

## The 7 Pillars

### Pillar 1: Strategic Projects (Chapter II)
Establishes a framework for recognising and supporting two categories of biotechnology strategic projects:
- **Health biotechnology strategic projects**: recognised at Member State level. Criteria: contribute to competitiveness, resilience, or security of the Union's biotech sector. Permit-granting capped at 10 months.
- **High-impact health biotechnology strategic projects**: recognised at EU level through a two-tier process (Member State assessment + Commission decision). Permit-granting capped at 8 months, with possible 3-month extension.

Both categories receive: accelerated permitting, priority administrative support, "highest national significance" designation under national law, public interest override for nature/environmental protection (Habitats, Birds, Water Framework Directives).

Strategic mapping of the Union's biotech ecosystem to be completed within 6 months of entry into force.

### Pillar 2: Access to Funding (Chapter III)
- EU Health Biotechnology Investment Pilot in partnership with the European Investment Bank Group
- Combines equity instruments and venture-style debt tailored to biotech risk profiles
- Projects contributing to a Capital Booster Pilot may be recognised as high-impact strategic projects
- Late-stage capital focus: Series B, C, and growth-stage funding gap
- State aid rules apply

### Pillar 3: Biosimilars Competitiveness (Chapter V)
- EMA guidelines to facilitate authorisation of biosimilar medicinal products
- Strategic projects focused on biosimilar R&D, manufacturing, and marketing authorisation
- International cooperation for biosimilar clusters
- Biological medicines represent 41% of EU pharmaceutical spending (EUR 95 billion out of EUR 228 billion in 2024)

### Pillar 4: AI and Data as Enablers (Chapter VI)
- Aligns with Apply AI Strategy, AI Continent Action Plan, Data Union Strategy
- EMA guidance on AI use across the medicinal product lifecycle
- Trusted AI testing environments and data-quality accelerators
- AI-enabled biotechnology projects can qualify as high-impact strategic projects
- Digital-by-default principle for biotech development

### Pillar 5: Novel Health Biotech Products (Chapter VII)
- Regulatory tools for products that fall outside existing health legal frameworks
- Union-wide, cross-framework regulatory status repository
- Foresight Panel for Emerging Health Innovation: horizon-scanning, anticipatory governance
- Regulatory sandboxes for health biotech products at early development stage
- Interactions between medicinal products and medical devices

### Pillar 6: Biosecurity and Biodefence (Chapter VIII)
- Framework for preventing misuse of biotechnology products of concern
- **24-hour reporting requirement** for suspicious transactions to national contact points
- Red flags: unclear customer identity, orders inconsistent with normal business, unusual labelling/shipping/confidentiality requests, deliveries to addresses lacking legitimate biotech justification
- Online marketplaces included in reporting obligations
- EU biodefence capability projects eligible for high-impact strategic project status
- Linked to Dual Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821 and Preparedness Union Strategy

### Pillar 7: Legislative Simplification (Chapter IX)
Amends 6 existing EU regulations to reduce time-to-market:

**Clinical Trials Regulation (EU) 536/2014:**
- Multinational trial authorisation: 106 days reduced to 75 days (incl. validation and ethical review)
- Initial clinical trial authorisation (no info request): 75 days reduced to 47 days
- Substantial modification assessment: 96 days reduced to 47 days (parallel options)
- Substantial modification (no info request): 64 days reduced to 33 days
- Additional 50 days for ATMP assessment: eliminated
- New category: "minimal-intervention clinical trials"
- Mandatory EU harmonised templates
- Single core dossier for investigational products
- Clinical trial sandboxes for innovative approaches
- AI and digitalisation uptake in clinical trials fostered
- Accelerated multinational trials for public health emergencies

**ATMP Regulation (EC) 1394/2007:**
- Exemption from GMO environmental risk assessment for clearly delineated categories of advanced investigational therapy medicinal products presenting no or negligible risk
- CHMP verification via revised Regulation 726/2004
- Commission delegated acts to amend ATMP definitions to keep pace with scientific advancement

**General Food Law (EC) 178/2002:**
- EFSA pre-submission advice broadened to include scientific matters (study design, testing strategies)
- Renewal-related advice merged with pre-submission into single unified procedure
- Study notification procedural delay: 6 months reduced to 3 months
- EFSA staff to chair panels and serve as vice-chairs of Scientific Committee (without voting rights)
- Regulatory sandboxes for innovative food technologies

**Veterinary Medicinal Products (EU) 2019/6:**
- Environmental assessment of GMO-containing veterinary products solely under Environmental Risk Assessment, removing duplicate GMO assessment
- Extra year of SPC for veterinary biotech products for zoonotic diseases
- Regulatory sandboxes for animal health innovation

**STEP Regulation (EU) 2024/795:**
- Health biotechnology strategic projects and high-impact projects recognised under Biotech Act deemed to contribute to STEP objectives

**SoHO Regulation (EU) 2024/1938:**
- Regulatory sandbox for substances of human origin used as starting materials for innovative medicinal products

## Governance

**European Health Biotechnology Steering Group (Article 20):**
- Member State representatives + Commission
- Advises on strategic project recognition, biosecurity, implementation
- Ensures consistent application across the Union

**National single points of contact:**
- Each Member State designates one or more authorities
- Interface between project promoters and permitting authorities
- Same as the point of contact under the Permitting Regulation (speeding-up environmental assessments)

**EU Health Biotechnology Support Network:**
- National and regional antennas to support biotech SMEs, start-ups, and innovators
- Navigating regulatory pathways, funding opportunities, scaling up, networking
- Builds on existing national and European networks

**Foresight Panel for Emerging Health Innovation:**
- Horizon-scanning and cross-framework dialogue
- Anticipatory governance for novel health biotech products

## Regulatory Sandboxes

The Act introduces regulatory sandboxes at multiple levels:
- **Health biotech products** (Chapter VII): Union-level sandbox for products at early development stage that fall outside existing health legal frameworks
- **Clinical trials** (Chapter IX): sandbox for testing innovative trial approaches
- **Food and feed** (Chapter IX): sandbox for innovative food technologies under harmonised conditions
- **Veterinary medicine** (Chapter IX): sandbox for animal health innovation
- **SoHO** (Chapter IX): sandbox for innovative use of substances of human origin

## Consultation Data

**Call for evidence** (14 May - 11 June 2025): 222 valid contributions from business associations (63), companies (50), NGOs (44), academic institutions (20), public authorities (14), EU citizens (14).

**Public consultation** (4 August - 10 November 2025): 359 contributions. 91 companies, 61 business associations, 47 NGOs, 44 academic institutions, 54 EU citizens, 21 public authorities. Industry sector: 38 large enterprises, 53 SMEs (24 micro, 17 small, 12 medium). 16 respondents identified as private investors.

Key consultation findings:
- 90.3% agreed on positive economic impact; 89.7% on positive social impact; 80.2% on positive environmental impact
- Main regulatory barriers: assessment/authorisation to market (63-76% agreement), pre-commercial testing/clinical trials, scaling up production
- EU regulatory environment perceived as less predictable (40.8% agreed) and more complex (64.6% agreed) than competitors
- 5 cluster barriers: insufficient financial support, insufficient public support, inability to reach critical mass, insufficient start-up incubators, insufficient collaboration

## Political Context

- Announced by Commission President von der Leyen in July 2024 Political Guidelines
- Commission Communication "Building the future with nature: Boosting Biotechnology and Biomanufacturing in the EU" (COM(2024) 137 final, March 2024)
- EP resolution "Future of the EU biotechnology and biomanufacturing sector" (ITRE own-initiative 2025/2008(INI), rapporteur Hildegard Bentele, adopted 10 July 2025): recommended a Chief Biotechnology Officer, a biotech omnibus, a common clinical-trials framework and next-generation regulatory sandboxes. The Commission's Act delivers most of this competitiveness agenda but in health-only form, creates a Steering Group and Support Network rather than a single Chief Biotechnology Officer, and leaves the bioethics safeguards of the parallel SANT report (2025/2087(INI), rapporteur Margarita de la Pisa Carrión) largely outside its operative articles.

## Implementation Timeline

- Entry into force: 20 days after publication in OJ

- Evaluation: 5 years after entry into application, then every 5 years
