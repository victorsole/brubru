# The President of the European Parliament and the Vice-Presidents

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: the President is the European Parliament's elected presiding officer and chief external representative, the closest equivalent to a national parliament's Speaker. The 14 Vice-Presidents deputise for the President in a fixed order of precedence and sit alongside the President on the Bureau.
- **Legal basis**: European Parliament's Rules of Procedure, term 10 (2024-2029): Rule 15 (Nominations and general provisions), Rule 16 (Election of President, opening address), Rule 17 (Election of Vice-Presidents), Rule 19 (Term of office of Officers), Rule 20 (Vacancies), Rule 21 (Early termination of an office), Rule 22 (Duties of the President), Rule 23 (Duties of the Vice-Presidents). Full consolidated text: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/rules/rules20240716/Rules20240716_EN.pdf. Treaty basis for two of the President's signature powers: Article 297(1) TFEU (signature of acts adopted under the ordinary legislative procedure) and Article 314(9) TFEU (declaring the Union's annual budget finally adopted).
- **Current President (verified July 2026)**: Roberta Metsola (European People's Party, Malta), re-elected on 16 July 2024 with 562 votes in the first ballot. This is her second term: she first became President in January 2022, completing the remainder of the late David Sassoli's term, then won a full 2.5-year term of her own in July 2024. Her current term runs to mid-January 2027, when the Parliament elects a President for the second half of the 10th parliamentary term (2024-2029). No handover has taken place as of July 2026; treat any claim of an earlier handover as false.
- **Election**: MEPs elect the President by secret ballot at the first sitting of each half of the parliamentary term (the constituent sitting in year 1, and again at the mid-term point in year 3). A political group or at least 36 MEPs can nominate a candidate (Rule 15). An absolute majority of votes cast is required in each of the first three ballots; if nobody reaches that threshold, a fourth, decisive ballot is held between only the two candidates with the most votes in the third ballot, decided by simple/relative majority (Rule 16).
- **Term of office**: 2.5 years, renewable without limit (Rule 19). A vacancy is filled only for the remainder of the predecessor's term (Rule 20).
- **The 14 Vice-Presidents**: elected on the same sitting as the President, immediately afterwards, by the same absolute-majority mechanism applied to a single multi-seat ballot (Rule 17). They take precedence in the order in which they were elected, ties broken by age. If the presidency falls vacant, the Vice-President first in the order of precedence acts as President until a new one is elected (Rule 20(2)).
- **Official pages**: President's site: https://the-president.europarl.europa.eu/en ; Presidency overview: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/about-parliament/en/organisation-and-rules/organisation/the-president ; Bureau members list: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/meps/en/organizations/bureau.

## 1. What the President actually does

Rule 22(1) gives the President the duty to "direct all the activities of Parliament and its bodies" and "all powers that are necessary to preside over the proceedings of Parliament and to ensure that they are properly conducted." In practice this covers several distinct functions:

- **Chairing plenary**: opening, suspending and closing sittings; ruling on the admissibility of amendments, other texts put to the vote, and parliamentary questions; enforcing the Rules of Procedure; maintaining order; calling and closing debates; putting matters to the vote and announcing results (Rule 22(2)). The President may only speak in a debate to sum up or call speakers to order, unless personally taking part as an MEP, in which case a Vice-President takes the chair.
- **Chairing Parliament's governing bodies**: the President chairs both the Bureau (administrative/budgetary decisions) and the Conference of Presidents (agenda-setting, legislative planning, external relations of Parliament as an institution). See `ep_bureau_conference_of_presidents` for how those two bodies work.
- **External representation**: the President represents Parliament in relations with the other EU institutions (Commission, Council, European Council) and with non-member countries and international organisations, including addressing European Council summits at the start of each meeting and receiving visiting heads of state and government.
- **Signing legislation**: under Article 297(1) TFEU, every legislative act adopted under the ordinary legislative procedure (the co-decision procedure between Parliament and Council, used for the large majority of EU law) must be signed jointly by the President of the European Parliament and the President-in-Office of the Council before it can be published in the Official Journal and enter into force. This is a formal, non-discretionary act that closes the legislative procedure.
- **Signing the EU budget**: under Article 314(9) TFEU, once the annual budgetary procedure has run its course, the President of Parliament declares the budget finally adopted, and that declaration (not a Council act) is what makes the budget legally binding for the year. See `mff_2028_2034` for the broader multiannual framework this annual budget sits inside.
- **Legal representation**: the President represents Parliament in legal proceedings, including cases before the Court of Justice of the European Union where Parliament is a party (for example, actions to annul a Council or Commission act, or Parliament defending its own acts).

## 2. Electing the President: up to four ballots

The election happens at the opening sitting of each half of the parliamentary term: the constituent sitting after a European election (mid-2024 for term 10), and again roughly 2.5 years later. The outgoing President or, if unavailable, the oldest Member present, chairs proceedings until a new President is elected (Rule 14).

- **Nomination**: a political group, or at least 36 MEPs acting together, may put forward a candidate (Rule 15). Candidates need not be nominated by their own group only.
- **Voting**: secret ballot. In each of the first three rounds, a candidate needs an absolute majority of the valid votes cast (not of all MEPs, and blank/invalid votes are excluded from the base). If no one clears that bar after three rounds, a fourth and final ballot is held between only the top two vote-getters from round three, decided by whoever gets more votes (Rule 16). A tie between the final two is broken in favour of the older candidate.
- **July 2024 result**: Roberta Metsola was re-elected in the first ballot with 562 votes, comfortably clearing the absolute-majority threshold, reflecting the backing of the EPP, S&D and Renew Europe groups that also underpinned Ursula von der Leyen's re-election as Commission President that same week.

## 3. The 14 Vice-Presidents (elected 16 July 2024, order of precedence)

Immediately after the President is elected, Parliament elects the Vice-Presidents on a single ballot with up to 14 seats to fill (Rule 17). Candidates who clear an absolute majority in the first round are declared elected, ranked by votes received; any seats left unfilled go to a second ballot under the same rule, and a third ballot (if still needed) is decided by relative majority. On 16 July 2024, 11 of the 14 seats were filled in the first ballot and the remaining 3 in a second ballot. The resulting order of precedence, as published by Parliament:

Individual seats can change earlier if a Vice-President resigns, is replaced nationally, or leaves the chamber; always check the current Bureau members list (linked in QUICK FACTS) for the live roster rather than assuming this list is unchanged.

**What Vice-Presidents do (Rule 23)**:
- Chair plenary sittings in rotation whenever the President is absent, unable to preside, or wishes to speak in a debate as an ordinary Member. The stand-in is always determined by the order of precedence above.
- Sit on the Bureau (see `ep_bureau_conference_of_presidents`) and carry out duties assigned to them under the Bureau and Conference of Presidents rules.
- Receive individual portfolios from the President (for example, relations with national parliaments, transparency, or digitalisation), and can be delegated ceremonial or representational duties, such as standing in for the President at specific events.
- If the presidency itself falls vacant mid-term, the Vice-President first in the order of precedence acts as President until Parliament elects a replacement (Rule 20(2)).

## 4. Why this matters to a policy professional

- **Timing leverage**: because the President chairs the Conference of Presidents (which sets the plenary agenda) and the Bureau (which controls Parliament's administration and resources), the office has real influence over which files get plenary time and when, even though the President does not personally decide committee assignments or rapporteurships.
- **Signature bottleneck**: no ordinary-legislative-procedure act becomes law, and no annual EU budget takes legal effect, without the President's signature or declaration. In practice this is a formality once Parliament and Council have agreed, but it fixes the exact moment an act is legally complete, which matters for entry-into-force calculations.
- **Political weight of the office**: the President's public statements at European Council summits, on international visits, and on rule-of-law or institutional controversies carry Parliament's institutional voice, distinct from any single political group's position. Political groups also treat the presidency, and the Vice-Presidencies, as posts to be distributed under inter-group agreements (the EPP-S&D-Renew "coalition" arrangement that also delivered the 2024 Commission College), so the composition of the Bureau is a live signal of the majority coalition inside the 10th Parliament.
- **Vice-Presidents as access points**: each Vice-President's portfolio and country/group background can make them a more relevant institutional contact than the President for a specific file or region.
