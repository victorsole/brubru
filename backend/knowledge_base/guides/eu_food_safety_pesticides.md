## Overview

The EU's pesticide regulatory framework is one of the strictest in the world, based on a dual system: (1) approval of active substances at EU level, and (2) authorisation of plant protection products (PPPs) at Member State level. Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs) ensure that food on the EU market -- whether domestically produced or imported -- does not contain unsafe pesticide residues.

The framework embodies the precautionary principle: active substances must be proven safe before approval, with the burden of proof on the applicant (typically the agrochemical company). EFSA conducts independent peer-reviewed risk assessments that form the scientific basis for Commission decisions.

## Active Substance Approval (Regulation 1107/2009)

### Process
1. **Application**: Company submits dossier to a Rapporteur Member State (RMS)
2. **Draft Assessment Report**: RMS evaluates and produces a DAR
3. **EFSA Peer Review**: EFSA conducts an independent peer review with public consultation
4. **EFSA Conclusion**: Published scientific opinion on risk to humans, animals, and the environment
5. **Commission Decision**: Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF) votes; Commission adopts implementing regulation
6. **Approval period**: Maximum 15 years (first approval); 15 years (renewal). Cut-off criteria apply for carcinogenic, mutagenic, reprotoxic (CMR) substances and endocrine disruptors

### Key Principles
- Approval is for the active substance at EU level; the final product (formulation) is authorised by Member States
- Zonal system: EU divided into 3 zones (North, Centre, South) for mutual recognition of product authorisations
- Emergency authorisations (Article 53): Member States can grant 120-day emergency use for unapproved substances in exceptional circumstances -- frequently controversial
- Substitution candidates: substances that meet certain hazard criteria are flagged for replacement when safer alternatives exist

### Recent Developments
- Glyphosate: renewed for 10 years in November 2023 (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/2660), sparking significant political debate
- REFIT evaluation: ongoing assessment of pesticide legislation effectiveness
- Sustainable Use Regulation (SUR): Commission withdrew the proposed regulation (COM(2022) 305) in February 2024 after Council and EP disagreements; future of pesticide reduction targets uncertain
- Pan-Europe advocacy: NGOs pushing for unlimited approval reform; Commission accused of abandoning science-based approach

## Maximum Residue Levels (Regulation 396/2005)

### What are MRLs?
An MRL is the highest level of a pesticide residue legally tolerated in or on food or feed when pesticides are applied according to Good Agricultural Practice (GAP). MRLs are not safety thresholds -- they are set well below levels that could pose a risk to health.

### How MRLs are set
1. Applicant or Member State submits application
2. Evaluating Member State prepares a reasoned opinion
3. EFSA conducts consumer risk assessment (acute and chronic dietary exposure)
4. Commission sets the MRL via implementing regulation
5. Default MRL: 0.01 mg/kg where no specific MRL is established (precautionary)

### Scope
- All foodstuffs for human or animal consumption listed in Annex I to Regulation 396/2005
- Applies to domestic production and imports equally
- Exceptions: substances in Annex VII may temporarily exceed MRLs if products are not for immediate consumption and proper controls exist

### Import Tolerances
Non-EU countries can apply for import tolerances where a pesticide is used in the exporting country but not approved in the EU. EFSA assesses whether the residues pose a risk to EU consumers. This is a frequent trade friction point, particularly with the US, Brazil, and other major agricultural exporters.

## EFSA's Role

EFSA performs four main functions in pesticide oversight:

1. **MRL applications**: Consumer risk assessment for active substance/crop combinations; proposes MRLs
2. **MRL reviews**: Comprehensive review of existing MRLs for approved substances across all crops (Article 12 reviews)
3. **Ad hoc risk assessment**: Specialist evaluations supporting EU legislative decisions on specific substances
4. **Annual monitoring reports**: Analyses pesticide residue data from all Member States, Norway, and Iceland

Key finding from recent monitoring: EFSA concludes there is a low overall risk to consumer health from estimated exposure to pesticide residues in tested foods, though specific substance/crop combinations occasionally exceed MRLs.

Recent EFSA publications:
- Cumulative risk assessment: pesticide effects on kidneys (February 2026)
- Lambda-cyhalothrin MRL review (March 2026)

## Monitoring and Enforcement

- **EU coordinated programme**: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/585 establishes multiannual control programmes
- **Member State programmes**: National monitoring with sampling and analysis of agreed product ranges
- **RASFF**: Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed flags MRL exceedances for immediate action
- **100,000+ samples** analysed annually across 600+ pesticides tested
- Non-EU countries must provide certification demonstrating compliance with EU MRL requirements

## Relevance for Advocacy

Pesticide regulation is one of the most politically charged EU policy areas, sitting at the intersection of public health, agriculture, trade, and environment. Key advocacy entry points:
- SCoPAFF committee votes on active substance approvals and MRL amendments
- EFSA public consultations during peer review of active substances
- EP ENVI and AGRI committee hearings on pesticide policy
- Article 53 emergency authorisations at national level
- Import tolerance applications (trade dimension)
- Farm to Fork Strategy successor measures
