## Overview

The **European Grids Package** is a legislative package proposed by the European Commission as part of the broader Clean Energy Industrial Plan. It addresses the critical bottleneck of electricity grid infrastructure, which the Commission has identified as the single largest obstacle to achieving the EU's 2030 and 2050 climate and energy targets.

Europe's electricity grids were designed for a centralised, fossil-fuel-based energy system. The transition to renewables, electrification of transport and heating, and the integration of distributed energy resources (solar panels, batteries, heat pumps) require a fundamentally different grid architecture. The Commission estimates that **EUR 584 billion** in grid investment is needed by 2030 alone -- roughly double the current investment trajectory.

The package proposes revisions to the TEN-E Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2022/869) and accompanying measures to accelerate grid permitting, strengthen cross-border interconnection, and mobilise investment in both transmission and distribution networks.

## Key Components

### 1. TEN-E Regulation Revision

The revision of the trans-European energy networks regulation targets several structural weaknesses in the current framework:

- **Updated project categories:** Expanded scope to include offshore hybrid interconnectors, hydrogen-ready pipelines, CO2 transport infrastructure, and smart grid technologies at distribution level
- **Faster project selection:** Streamlined process for Projects of Common Interest (PCIs) and Projects of Mutual Interest (PMIs), with binding timelines for regional group assessments
- **Cross-border cost allocation (CBCA):** Reformed methodology to better reflect the system-wide benefits of interconnection projects, addressing the "free rider" problem where transit countries bear costs without proportionate benefits
- **Distribution grid inclusion:** For the first time, the TEN-E framework explicitly addresses distribution-level grid investments, recognising that 70% of renewable connections happen at distribution level

### 2. Grid Investment Framework

The package establishes a comprehensive investment framework:

- **Grid Investment Plans:** Each Member State must adopt a 10-year Grid Investment Plan, aligned with National Energy and Climate Plans (NECPs) and updated biennially
- **Anticipatory investment:** Regulatory frameworks must allow Transmission System Operators (TSOs) and Distribution System Operators (DSOs) to invest ahead of confirmed demand, removing the "chicken and egg" problem of grid capacity
- **Blended finance mechanisms:** Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) Energy grants combined with European Investment Bank (EIB) loans and private capital, with first-loss guarantees for higher-risk cross-border projects
- **Target:** Close the EUR 584 billion investment gap through a combination of regulated asset base returns, EU-level grants, and innovative financing instruments

### 3. Permitting Acceleration

Grid permitting is the most acute bottleneck -- transmission projects currently take 10-15 years from planning to operation in many Member States:

- **Maximum permitting timelines:** 3 years for transmission projects of common interest, 2 years for distribution grid reinforcements
- **One-stop shop:** Each Member State must designate a single competent authority for grid permitting, consolidating what can currently involve 10+ authorities
- **Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA):** Grid investment plans undergo SEA at plan level, reducing the need for individual project-level Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs)
- **Overriding public interest:** Grid projects on the PCI/PMI list benefit from a presumption of overriding public interest, streamlining assessments under the Habitats and Birds Directives
- **Digital permitting:** Mandatory digitalisation of permit applications, with interoperability requirements for national permitting systems

### 4. Offshore Grid Development

Offshore wind deployment requires dedicated offshore grid infrastructure:

- **North Sea offshore grid:** Building on the Esbjerg and Ostend Declarations, the package provides a legal framework for meshed offshore grids connecting multiple countries and wind farms
- **Offshore bidding zones:** Regulatory framework for offshore bidding zones to facilitate the efficient dispatch of offshore wind generation
- **Hybrid interconnectors:** Legal and regulatory clarity for projects that combine interconnection and renewable energy connection functions
- **Target:** Support the EU's offshore renewable energy target of 111 GW by 2030 (up from approximately 19 GW installed in 2025)

### 5. Cross-Border Cost Sharing

One of the persistent obstacles to cross-border grid projects is the asymmetric distribution of costs and benefits:

- **Reformed CBCA methodology:** Benefits assessed on a system-wide basis including security of supply, market integration, and emissions reduction
- **Binding arbitration:** ACER (Agency for the Cooperation of Energy Regulators) gains stronger arbitration powers where Member States cannot agree on cost allocation
- **Compensation for transit countries:** Explicit mechanisms for compensating countries that host transit infrastructure without being primary beneficiaries
- **Socialisation of offshore grid costs:** Framework for sharing the costs of meshed offshore grids among all countries that benefit from the resulting market integration

## Energy Council Debate: 16 March 2026

The TTE (Energy) Council on 16 March 2026 holds a policy debate on the Grids Package. Key positions:

### Supportive Member States
- **Germany, France, Netherlands, Denmark:** Strong support for accelerated permitting and offshore grid development. Germany in particular pushes for faster north-south transmission corridors (SuedLink, SuedOstLink) to be facilitated by EU-level permitting rules
- **Nordic countries (Sweden, Finland):** Support cross-border investment framework, particularly for the Nordic-Baltic grid integration

### Cautious Member States
- **Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece:** Support interconnection in principle but resist binding investment obligations that could increase network tariffs for consumers. Iberian Peninsula interconnection with France remains politically sensitive
- **Central and Eastern Europe (Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary):** Concerns about cost allocation methodology and the speed of transition away from gas infrastructure

### Key Tensions
- **National grid operator resistance:** Incumbent TSOs and DSOs in several Member States lobby against anticipatory investment rules, arguing they increase regulated asset base risk
- **Consumer impact:** Member States with lower household incomes resist tariff increases to fund grid investments, even where EU co-financing is available
- **Permitting sovereignty:** Several Member States view the proposed maximum permitting timelines as an intrusion into national planning law competences
- **Gas infrastructure:** Disagreement on whether the TEN-E revision should still include gas infrastructure categories or phase them out entirely

## Political Landscape

### European Parliament
- **ITRE committee** leads, with opinions expected from ENVI (environment) and TRAN (transport)
- **EPP position:** Generally supportive of grid investment but cautious on permitting rules that override national procedures
- **S&D position:** Pushes for stronger consumer protection provisions and affordable energy transition
- **Renew position:** Strongly supportive of market-based investment incentives and cross-border integration
- **Greens/EFA position:** Supports faster permitting for renewables grid connections but insists on environmental safeguards

### Stakeholders
- **ENTSO-E** (European Network of Transmission System Operators for Electricity): Broadly supportive but seeks flexibility on investment timelines
- **E.DSO** (European Distribution System Operators): Welcomes distribution grid inclusion in TEN-E but concerned about regulatory burden on smaller DSOs
- **WindEurope, SolarPower Europe:** Strongly supportive, argue grid bottlenecks are the primary constraint on renewable deployment
- **BEUC** (European Consumer Organisation): Insists on cost transparency and limits on tariff increases passed to consumers
- **National Regulatory Authorities (via CEER):** Mixed views on ACER's enhanced arbitration powers

## Investment Gap Analysis

The Commission's EUR 584 billion estimate breaks down approximately as follows:

- **Transmission grids (onshore):** EUR 200-250 billion for reinforcement and expansion
- **Distribution grids:** EUR 250-300 billion for smart grid upgrades, digitalisation, and capacity expansion
- **Offshore grids:** EUR 50-80 billion for offshore wind connections and meshed grids
- **Cross-border interconnectors:** EUR 30-50 billion for new interconnection capacity

Current annual grid investment across the EU is approximately EUR 50-60 billion per year. To close the gap by 2030, annual investment would need to roughly double, requiring both regulatory reform and new financing instruments.

## Key Statistics

- EU electricity interconnection level: approximately 12% on average (below the 15% 2030 target)
- Member States below 10% interconnection: Spain, Portugal, Ireland, Cyprus, Malta
- Average transmission project permitting time: 10-15 years (varies by Member State)
- Share of renewable connections at distribution level: approximately 70%
- Offshore wind installed capacity (EU): approximately 19 GW (2025), target 111 GW by 2030
- Number of PCI electricity projects (5th PCI list): 67 transmission and storage projects
- CEF Energy budget (2021-2027): EUR 5.84 billion

## Regulatory Framework

The Grids Package sits within a broader regulatory architecture for EU energy infrastructure:

- **TEN-E Regulation (2022/869):** Framework for Projects of Common Interest and cross-border infrastructure planning
- **Electricity Regulation (2019/943):** Internal electricity market rules, including bidding zone configuration and cross-zonal capacity allocation
- **Electricity Directive (2019/944):** Common rules for the internal market for electricity, including consumer rights and DSO unbundling
- **Electricity Market Design reform (2024/1747):** Revised market rules including Contracts for Difference, capacity mechanisms, and demand response
- **Renewable Energy Directive (2023/2413):** RED III acceleration provisions for renewable energy permitting, which depend on grid capacity
- **Net-Zero Industry Act (2024/1735):** Strategic project permitting framework that complements grid infrastructure acceleration
