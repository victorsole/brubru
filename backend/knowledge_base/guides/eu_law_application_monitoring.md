# EU Law Application Monitoring + Article 258 TFEU Infringement Procedures

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: Commission Annual Report on **Monitoring the Application of Union Law** -- the Commission's yearly accountability report on how Member States transpose and enforce EU legislation, plus the infringement procedure pipeline under Article 258 TFEU
- 2023-2025 report (the one under plenary debate 28 April 2026): Commission Communication COM(2025) series, published July 2025; covers calendar years 2023 + 2024 + early 2025
- EP plenary debate: Item 111 on OJQ-10-2026-04-28_EN agenda, Tuesday 28 April 2026, 15:00-22:00 debate block, Strasbourg
- **EP rapporteur: Dainius Žalimas** (Renew, Lithuania), JURI lead committee, procedure 2025/2XXX(INI) -- final OEIL reference to be captured from JURI document register
- Legal base: **Article 258 TFEU** (Commission action for failure to fulfil Treaty obligations), **Article 259 TFEU** (Member State v Member State), **Article 260 TFEU** (pecuniary penalties for non-compliance with CJEU judgments)
- Commissioner responsible: **Michael McGrath** (Justice, Democracy and the Rule of Law) since December 2024 -- merged responsibilities with Dombrovskis's Implementation and Simplification portfolio since 28 April 2026 Better Regulation Communication (see `better_regulation_enforcement_communication`)
- Infringement procedure stages:
  1. **EU Pilot** (informal pre-infringement dialogue, not formally mandatory since 2017)
  2. **Letter of formal notice** (Article 258, 2 months to respond)
  3. **Reasoned opinion** (Article 258, 2 months to comply)
  4. **Referral to the CJEU** (Article 258)
  5. **Second referral under Article 260** (pecuniary penalties if MS does not comply with CJEU judgment)
- Key 2023-2024 statistics to expect in the Report (historical pattern, exact figures from Commission):
  - Approximately 650-750 new infringement cases opened per year
  - Approximately 200-300 cases closed per year
  - Open cases at year-end typically around 1,500-1,700
  - Top offenders historically: Greece, Italy, Spain, Germany, Portugal (by absolute numbers); late transposition dominates
  - Directive transposition rate target: Member States must transpose directives by deadline; Single Market Scoreboard publishes half-yearly transposition deficit

## Why this matters -- the "implementation gap"

The Draghi Report (September 2024) and Letta Report (April 2024) both highlighted that the EU's regulatory ambition is undermined by **uneven Member State implementation**. Weaknesses in transposition and enforcement:

1. **Distort the Single Market** -- same directive, different rules in 27 MS
2. **Erode citizens' confidence** -- fundamental rights and consumer protection vary
3. **Slow the green and digital transitions** -- CSRD, CBAM, AI Act, DMA, DSA all need uniform enforcement
4. **Undermine the EU's external credibility** -- hard to demand rule of law from candidate countries if internal enforcement is weak

The 2023-2025 report is the first to explicitly link these themes, consistent with the Dombrovskis + McGrath merged "simplification + enforcement" portfolio.

## Key infringement cases 2023-2025 (illustrative, from public Commission press releases)

- **Hungary**: multiple cases on judicial independence, anti-LGBTIQ law, asylum systems, media law -- major backlog pre-Magyar election
- **Poland**: pre-2023 judicial reforms cases; most closed after PiS departure
- **Italy**: late transposition of multiple environmental + consumer directives; media-concentration concerns
- **Germany**: delayed transposition of CSRD, CBAM implementing acts, environmental directives
- **France**: Article 49.3 and emergency powers; consumer protection cases
- **Bulgaria, Romania, Greece**: structural transposition delays
- **Member States on CJEU judgment non-compliance**: Article 260 Second Referral cases on waste management, air quality (PM10, NO2)
