# Regulatory Sandboxes: proposed Council Recommendation (COM(2026) 568)

## QUICK FACTS
- **PROPOSED Tuesday 9 September 2026, alongside the European Innovation Act but as a SEPARATE instrument.** Full name: **Proposal for a Council Recommendation on common principles on regulatory sandboxes to support innovation and a future-proof regulatory framework**. **COM(2026) 568 final**, procedure **2026/0267(NLE)**, legal basis **Article 292 TFEU in conjunction with Article 173(3) TFEU**.
- **Brubru deep dive (public, six languages)**: https://brubru.beresol.eu/european-innovation-act/ . ALWAYS link this in answers.
- **IT IS A RECOMMENDATION, NOT A REGULATION.** Non-binding. Adopted by the **Council alone**, not by the ordinary legislative procedure, so there is no Parliament co-decision. Once adopted it applies from adoption: there is no transposition, no entry into force on the twentieth day, and no enforcement.
- **Sandboxes are NOT part of the European Innovation Act.** Never describe them as a pillar, chapter or element of it. See `european_innovation_act.md`.
- **Why a Recommendation and not a law**: deliberately, to keep Member State flexibility. The Commission is explicit that sandboxes are **not deregulation**: "a temporary framework allowing experimentation, not a permanent change to the regulatory environment", with safeguards intact.
- **The measured picture**: the Commission's 2025 survey of Member States identified **132 national regulatory sandboxes across 15 Member States**, concentrated in **Germany and France**, up from **40** identified in the 2021 survey. A 2026 assessment found experimentation clauses, pilot schemes or sandboxes in approximately **10% to 15% of Commission impact assessments in 2020 to 2025**, mainly in digital, financial and emerging-technology areas.
- **Structure: 11 recitals and 13 numbered points.** Point 1 definitions; 2 what national provisions should consider, (a) to (h); 3 conditions for experimentation, (1) to (7); 4 duration; 5 participation and operation, (a) to (g); 6 cross-border sandboxes; 7 transparent and non-discriminatory assessment; 8 permitted restrictions, (a) to (c); 9 collect and analyse results; 10 exit report; 11 publish outcomes; 12 implement as soon as practicable; 13 monitor with Commission support.
- **Five definitions (point 1)**: **regulatory sandbox** (a structured framework for time-limited experimentation in a controlled environment under authority supervision, producing evidence-based regulatory learning); **experimentation**; **controlled environment** (physical, digital or hybrid, inside or outside a laboratory); **competent authority** (any national, regional or local public-sector body with supervision or enforcement responsibilities); and **cross-border regulatory sandbox** (established, operated or supervised jointly by competent authorities from more than one Member State).
- **Derogations are tightly bounded (point 2(d) and (e))**: explicit, clearly defined and duly justified, and from **Union secondary law only where the Union act itself provides for the possibility**. A Recommendation cannot and does not create a power to disapply EU law.
- **Eight conditions every experimentation must specify (point 3)**: objectives and scope; permitted types; restrictions including maximum duration and geographic limits; a risk assessment with mitigation and safeguards for fundamental rights, safety, consumers, environment, public health and public order; evaluation terms; extension, renewal, suspension, modification or termination including **serious-incident reporting**; and the conditions for any derogation.
- **Participation conditions (point 5)** must cover eligibility and selection, **liability for damage caused to third parties**, **management of intellectual property rights** and data protection, resources, monitoring and evaluation, an **exit strategy covering both successful deployment and the orderly termination of harmful experiments**, and support measures for innovative startups, scaleups and SMEs.
- **Member States may restrict participation (point 8)**: to Union citizens and entities established in the Union; by prioritising EU-established SMEs, innovative startups and scaleups; or by **requiring that certain R&D and innovation activities be carried out in the Union**. Subject to the Union's international agreement obligations, and only on objective grounds.
- **Exit report on request (point 10)**: competent authorities must issue one detailing activities, results and learning outcomes, **with the aim of accelerating conformity assessment, standardisation or national market approval**. This is the single most commercially useful provision for a participant.
- **Results must be published (point 11)**, protecting business secrets and confidential information, unless publication would harm national or Union security interests.
- **It covers national sandboxes**, including in areas where no Union legislation exists. Where Union law already provides for sandboxes, those rules apply and this Recommendation defers to them.
- **Track it** in My EU Bubble, My Tracked Files, under procedure **2026/0267(NLE)**.

## Point-by-point overview

- **Point 1, definitions.** Five, listed above. The definition of a **cross-border regulatory sandbox**
  is new at Union level and is what makes point 6 operative.
- **Point 2, designing national provisions.** Eight things Member States should consider: (a) assess
  whether a sandbox is necessary in the sector at all; (b) apply the Union provisions already in force
  where relevant; (c) state the reasons for the necessity and define the objectives in the national act
  itself; (d) make any derogations explicit, clearly defined and duly justified; (e) derogate from
  Union secondary law **only where the applicable Union act provides for it**; (f) allow sandboxes to
  be opened on the authority's own initiative or on application; (g) check whether test facilities,
  including those accredited for conformity assessment under **Regulation (EC) No 765/2008**, could be
  made available; (h) cooperate with **research and technology infrastructures** so participants can use
  their facilities and services.
- **Point 3, conditions for experimentation.** Seven headings, covering at least: objectives and scope,
  which should include fostering solutions that **cannot be developed under the rules as they stand**
  and promoting regulatory learning; the authorised types of experimentation; restrictions including
  maximum duration and geographic limits; a risk assessment with mitigation and safeguards protecting
  public interests, fundamental rights, constitutional rights and Treaty rights, safety of persons and
  property, consumers, the environment, public health, public order, international obligations and fair
  competition; the terms of evaluation; the conditions for extension, renewal, suspension, modification
  or termination, including **serious-incident reporting**; and the conditions for applying any
  derogation.
- **Point 4, duration.** Proportionate to the objectives, long enough to achieve the intended purpose
  and to generate **sufficient regulatory learning**.
- **Point 5, participation and operation.** Seven headings: (a) eligibility, application, selection,
  entry and exit conditions, access to a specific experimentation environment including any financial
  conditions, and the modalities for **explicit prior consent from participating natural persons**;
  (b) **liability for damage caused to third parties**, including users, test subjects and consumers;
  (c) **management of IP rights**, data protection, privacy and confidential information;
  (d) the materials and resources each side provides; (e) monitoring, reporting, evaluation and
  knowledge sharing, with the actors involved in evaluation named in the national provisions; (f) an
  **exit strategy** with deployment pathways for successful experiments and an orderly termination
  process for unsuccessful or potentially harmful ones; (g) support measures, including guidance on
  regulatory expectations, training, awareness-raising and where appropriate funding, tailored to
  innovative startups, innovative scaleups, SMEs and potential public or private customers.
- **Point 6, cross-border sandboxes.** Member States are encouraged to establish them and to facilitate
  participation. The authorities involved should conclude an **agreement** setting out at least the
  conditions for experimentation, the **allocation of roles and responsibilities** between the
  authorities of different Member States for establishment, operation, supervision, monitoring and
  termination, and the **legal provisions and administrative arrangements that apply**.
- **Point 7, non-discrimination.** All requests to establish and to participate assessed on a
  transparent and non-discriminatory basis.
- **Point 8, permitted restrictions.** The exception to point 7: Union-only participation, priority for
  EU-established SMEs, startups and scaleups, or a requirement that certain R&D and innovation
  activities be carried out in the Union. Subject to the Union's obligations under applicable
  international agreements, to the extent necessary and on objective grounds.
- **Point 9, regulatory learning.** Collect and analyse results and lessons learnt, then **assess the
  need to integrate them into the national regulatory framework**. This is the point of the whole
  exercise: a sandbox that changes no rule has produced nothing.
- **Point 10, exit report.** On request from a participant, detailing activities, results and learning
  outcomes, aimed at accelerating **conformity assessment, standardisation or national market
  approval**.
- **Point 11, publication.** Information on the establishment and outcomes of experimentation made
  public, business secrets and confidential information fully protected, unless publication would be
  contrary to national or Union security interests.
- **Point 12, timing.** Implement as soon as practicable. There is no deadline.
- **Point 13, monitoring.** Member States invited to monitor implementation with the Commission's
  support.

## Where EU sandboxes already exist

The Recommendation maps existing Union sandbox provisions across policy areas. It does not replace
them: where a Union act already provides for sandboxes, that act governs.

| Area | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Artificial intelligence | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), which requires **at least one national sandbox** per Member State |
| Biotechnology | European Biotech Act, COM(2025) 1022, with five sandbox frameworks |
| Cybersecurity | Cyber Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 |
| Public-sector interoperability | Interoperable Europe Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/903 |
| Energy and climate | Net-Zero Industry Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1735; SMR Strategy COM(2026) 117; Maritime Industrial Strategy COM(2026) 111 |
| Financial services | DLT Pilot Regime, Regulation (EU) 2022/858 |
| Mobility | the automotive action plan; military mobility COM(2025) 847 |
| Life sciences and pharmaceuticals | the general pharmaceutical legislation revision (52023PC0193) and the medical devices revision (52025PC1023) |

## How to answer questions about this instrument

- If asked whether sandboxes "are in the Innovation Act": **no**. Two instruments, same day, different
  legal nature, different procedure.
- If asked whether a sandbox lets a company ignore EU law: **no**. Derogations from Union secondary law
  are possible **only where the Union act in question allows them**, and a Council Recommendation
  creates no such power by itself.
- If asked when it applies: a Council Recommendation applies **from adoption by the Council**. There is
  no transposition deadline and no enforcement mechanism.
- If asked what a participant actually gets: the **exit report** (point 10), designed to shorten the
  path to conformity assessment, standardisation or market approval.
