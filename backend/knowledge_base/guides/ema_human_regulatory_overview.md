# EMA Human-Medicines Regulatory Framework: Research to Post-Authorisation

## QUICK FACTS
- What this is: the **canonical map of the human-medicine lifecycle** in the EU, from early research through to withdrawal, as EMA itself structures it (EMA's own "human regulatory overview" groups the same three stages: Research and Development, Marketing Authorisation, Post-Authorisation)
- **European Medicines Agency (EMA)**: EU decentralised agency, headquartered in **Amsterdam** (Domenico Scarlattilaan 6, relocated from London after Brexit); serves roughly 450 million EU/EEA residents; Executive Director **Emer Cooke**
- EMA does not authorise medicines alone: it sits at the centre of the **European medicines regulatory network**: EMA + the national competent authorities of the Member States (coordinated via the **Heads of Medicines Agencies, HMA**) + the European Commission, which takes the final legally binding decision
- **Stage 1, Research and Development**: scientific advice and protocol assistance from EMA; orphan designation (COMP, Reg (EC) 141/2000); Paediatric Investigation Plans (PDCO, Reg (EC) 1901/2006); ATMP classification (CAT, Reg (EC) 1394/2007); clinical trials run under Reg (EU) 536/2014 via the **CTIS** portal
- **Stage 2, Marketing Authorisation**: two routes to a licence: the **centralised procedure** (Reg (EC) 726/2004, one EMA application, one Commission decision valid EU/EEA-wide, CHMP opinion within 210 days) or the **national / mutual-recognition / decentralised** routes under Directive 2001/83/EC (single-Member-State authorisations, extended by recognition)
- **Expedited and special-case pathways sitting on top of the standard routes**: **accelerated assessment** (CHMP timeline cut from 210 to 150 days for medicines of major public health interest); **conditional marketing authorisation** (Reg (EU) 2022/1616, recast of Reg (EC) 507/2006: provisional approval for unmet medical needs on less-than-complete data, valid 1 year, renewed annually, subject to specific obligations); **authorisation under exceptional circumstances** (Article 14(8) Reg 726/2004: for products where comprehensive data can never realistically be supplied, e.g. very rare diseases, reassessed annually, indefinite); **PRIME (PRIority MEdicines)**: EMA's voluntary early-support scheme (launched 2016) for medicines with potential to address a significant unmet medical need, giving a dedicated rapporteur and enhanced scientific-advice interaction, often feeding into accelerated assessment later
- **Stage 3, Post-Authorisation**: variations (Commission Reg (EC) 1234/2008, as amended by Reg (EU) 712/2012) to change an approved dossier; **renewals** (5-year cycle, then generally unlimited validity after the first renewal); **referrals** (Union-wide procedures under Articles 31, 20, 107i and related provisions of Dir 2001/83/EC and Reg 726/2004, triggered by safety concerns, disagreement between Member States, or Commission/Member State request); **withdrawals and suspensions**; **shortages monitoring** via the European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP) and EMA's reinforced crisis mandate under Reg (EU) 2022/123
- **Pharmacovigilance**: continuous safety monitoring runs across the whole lifecycle, not just post-authorisation: **EudraVigilance** database, **PSURs**, **Risk Management Plans**, the **inverted black triangle (▼)** for medicines under additional monitoring, led by the **PRAC**. Legal base: Reg (EU) 1235/2010 + Directive 2010/84/EU. See `pharmacovigilance_directive_2010_84.md`
- **Seven EMA scientific committees**: CHMP (human medicines), PRAC (pharmacovigilance), COMP (orphan designation), PDCO (paediatric), CAT (advanced therapies), CVMP (veterinary), HMPC (herbal). See `ema_scientific_committees.md`
- **Interface with Health Technology Assessment**: once a medicine is authorised, EU-level Joint Clinical Assessments under the HTA Regulation (Reg (EU) 2021/2282) run in parallel with national pricing/reimbursement decisions, which remain a Member State competence; see `eu_pharmaceutical_framework.md`
- **Reform in progress**: the entire framework (Dir 2001/83/EC + Reg 726/2004 + the orphan and paediatric Regulations) is being replaced by the 2023 pharmaceutical legislation package, provisional agreement reached 11 December 2025; see `eu_pharmaceutical_legislation_reform.md`
- Responsible DG: **DG SANTE** (Health and Food Safety), Commissioner Olivér Várhelyi. EP lead committee: **SANT** (Public Health). Legal base: Article 114 TFEU (internal market) + Article 168 TFEU (public health)

## Why this guide exists

Brubru holds detailed guides on the individual pillars of EU medicines law: the centralised procedure, the Community Code, clinical trials, orphan medicines, paediatric medicines, advanced therapies, pharmacovigilance and the ongoing reform: but nothing that walks a reader through the **lifecycle as EMA itself presents it**. This guide is that entry point: it follows EMA's own "human regulatory overview" structure (Research and Development -> Marketing Authorisation -> Post-Authorisation) and signposts to the detailed Brubru guide for each stage. Use it when a question is about the *overall pathway* a medicine takes through EU regulation, or about a step (accelerated assessment, conditional MA, exceptional circumstances, PRIME, referrals, shortages) that does not have its own dedicated guide.

## The European medicines regulatory network

EMA is not a stand-alone regulator. It coordinates a network of:
- The **national competent authorities** of the EU/EEA Member States, which run their own authorisation procedures for products outside the centralised scope and remain responsible for enforcement, inspections and pharmacovigilance on the ground
- The **Heads of Medicines Agencies (HMA)**, the network body bringing the national regulators together
- The **European Commission**, which takes the legally binding EU-wide marketing-authorisation decision following a positive CHMP opinion (EMA gives scientific opinions; the Commission decides)
- EMA's own **scientific committees**, drawing on thousands of experts nominated by the national authorities

## Stage 1: Research and Development

Before a marketing-authorisation application is ever filed, a developer can engage EMA through:
- **Scientific advice and protocol assistance**: non-binding guidance on the design of quality, non-clinical and clinical studies, reducing the risk of late-stage data gaps. Protocol assistance is scientific advice specifically for orphan-designated products, and comes with fee reductions
- **Orphan designation** (Reg (EC) 141/2000): for medicines targeting conditions affecting no more than 5 in 10,000 people in the EU, granted by COMP. See `orphan_medicinal_products_regulation.md`
- **Paediatric Investigation Plans (PIPs)** (Reg (EC) 1901/2006): the agreed paediatric study programme reviewed by PDCO; required for (almost) every new medicine before an adult marketing-authorisation application can be validated. See `paediatric_medicines_regulation.md`
- **ATMP classification** (Reg (EC) 1394/2007): CAT confirms whether a product is a gene therapy, somatic cell therapy or tissue-engineered product, which brings the mandatory centralised route into play. See `advanced_therapy_medicinal_products_regulation.md`
- **Clinical trials** (Reg (EU) 536/2014): authorised and coordinated EU-wide through the CTIS portal, with a reporting Member State leading joint scientific assessment. See `clinical_trials_regulation.md`
- **PRIME**: EMA's early-support scheme for medicines targeting a significant unmet medical need, assigning a dedicated rapporteur from CHMP or PRAC and more frequent scientific-advice touchpoints, intended to build the evidence base needed for a later accelerated assessment

## Stage 2: Marketing Authorisation

### The two standard routes
- **Centralised procedure** (Reg (EC) 726/2004): one application to EMA, one CHMP opinion, one European Commission decision valid across the EU/EEA. Mandatory for biotech products, ATMPs, orphan medicines and new active substances for cancer, HIV/AIDS, diabetes, neurodegenerative, auto-immune and viral diseases; optional for genuine therapeutic innovation. Standard CHMP opinion timeline: 210 days. See `ema_centralised_procedure_regulation.md`
- **National / mutual-recognition / decentralised procedures** (Dir 2001/83/EC): a single Member State authorisation (national procedure), one Member State's authorisation extended to others (mutual recognition), or a simultaneous multi-country application led by a Reference Member State (decentralised procedure). The default route for generics, well-established substances and traditional herbal medicines. See `community_code_directive_2001_83.md`

### Expedited and special-case pathways
These are not separate legal regimes but faster or more flexible ways of using the two routes above, all run through the centralised procedure:

| Pathway | What it does | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Accelerated assessment | Cuts the CHMP timeline from 210 to 150 days | Medicine of major public health interest, especially therapeutic innovation |
| Conditional marketing authorisation (Reg (EU) 2022/1616) | Provisional authorisation on less-than-comprehensive data, valid 1 year, renewed annually with specific obligations | Unmet medical need, seriousness of the condition, benefit of early availability outweighs the risk of less complete data |
| Authorisation under exceptional circumstances (Art 14(8) Reg 726/2004) | Authorisation where comprehensive efficacy/safety data can never realistically be generated | Very rare diseases, ethical constraints on data collection, current scientific knowledge insufficient |
| PRIME | Enhanced early dialogue and a dedicated rapporteur ahead of the application | Significant unmet medical need, feeds into eligibility for accelerated assessment |

## Stage 3: Post-Authorisation

Once authorised, a medicine's dossier keeps changing and being monitored:
- **Variations** (Commission Reg (EC) 1234/2008, amended by Reg (EU) 712/2012): any change to the approved marketing authorisation, classified by risk (minor Type IA/IB, major Type II, extensions)
- **Renewals**: the marketing authorisation is valid for 5 years, renewed once on a risk-benefit re-evaluation, then generally unlimited (subject to the Commission being able to require a further renewal on safety grounds)
- **Referrals**: EU-wide procedures (under provisions of Dir 2001/83/EC and Reg 726/2004, including the urgent Union procedure of Articles 107i-107k for fast safety action) triggered by a safety signal, a disagreement between Member States, or a request from the Commission or a Member State; can lead to label changes, restrictions, suspensions or withdrawals across the whole EU
- **Withdrawals and suspensions**: on safety grounds, on the marketing-authorisation holder's own initiative, or where the sunset clause bites (a centralised authorisation lapses if the product is not actually placed on the market within 3 years, or is off the market for 3 consecutive years)
- **Shortages monitoring**: the European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP) and EMA's reinforced crisis-management mandate (Reg (EU) 2022/123), including the Medicines Shortages Steering Group (MSSG) and the Emergency Task Force, track and respond to EU-wide supply disruption for centrally authorised and other critical medicines

### Pharmacovigilance runs throughout
Safety monitoring is not a discrete "final stage": it runs from first authorisation onward. Marketing-authorisation holders operate an audited pharmacovigilance system with a Qualified Person Responsible for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV), report suspected adverse reactions to **EudraVigilance** (15 days for serious reactions, 90 days for non-serious), and submit **Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs)** and **Risk Management Plans (RMPs)**. Medicines under additional monitoring (new active substances, biologicals/biosimilars) carry the **inverted black triangle (▼)**. The **PRAC** leads EU-level signal detection and safety assessment. Full detail: `pharmacovigilance_directive_2010_84.md`.

## The seven EMA scientific committees

| Committee | Acronym | Core role |
|---|---|---|
| Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use | CHMP | Scientific opinions on human marketing-authorisation applications |
| Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee | PRAC | Safety signal assessment, PSURs, risk-minimisation recommendations |
| Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products | COMP | Orphan designation |
| Paediatric Committee | PDCO | Paediatric Investigation Plans, waivers, deferrals |
| Committee for Advanced Therapies | CAT | Scientific assessment of ATMPs, classification opinions |
| Committee for Medicinal Products for Veterinary Use | CVMP | Veterinary medicines (outside the human-medicines scope of this guide) |
| Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products | HMPC | Herbal medicine monographs |

Meeting calendars and the working parties that support these committees are covered in `ema_scientific_committees.md`.

## Interface with Health Technology Assessment

A positive marketing-authorisation decision does not by itself determine price or reimbursement, which remain a Member State competence. Since the HTA Regulation (Reg (EU) 2021/2282) became applicable on 12 January 2025, in-scope medicines (oncology medicines and ATMPs first, orphans from 2028, all centrally authorised medicines from 2030) also go through an EU-level **Joint Clinical Assessment (JCA)**, run in parallel with the marketing-authorisation process where possible via joint EMA-HTA scientific consultations. Full detail in `eu_pharmaceutical_framework.md`.

## Where the framework is heading

The whole architecture described here: the Community Code, the centralised-procedure Regulation, and the orphan and paediatric Regulations: is under revision through the 2023 pharmaceutical legislation reform package. A provisional political agreement was reached on 11 December 2025; formal Council adoption and the European Parliament's second reading are pending. Key changes on the table include a restructured data-and-market-protection formula, tiered orphan exclusivity, an antimicrobial-resistance transferable exclusivity voucher, and streamlined EMA procedures. Full detail: `eu_pharmaceutical_legislation_reform.md`.
