# State of the Union 2026: the address

## QUICK FACTS
- **What**: the annual State of the Union address of the President of the European Commission to the European Parliament, given by **Ursula von der Leyen** on **Wednesday 16 September 2026** in **Strasbourg**, with Canada's Prime Minister **Mark Carney** in the chamber as her guest. Published text: Commission reference SPEECH/26/1868.
- **Framing**: "the state of our Union is the strongest it has ever been. The state of our Union can also feel as precarious as it has ever been." She named "the tipping points of our time: **climate change and AI**".
- **A speech, not law**: nothing announced binds anyone. Each item still needs a Commission proposal, and most then need Parliament and Council.
- **By Brubru's count: 45 commitments, 44 classified**: **26 new** (no legal text, no date), **9 scheduled** (a date, no text), **6 already exist** (law in force or body operating), **3 proposed** (text tabled, not adopted). One (a sanctions regime against smugglers she called already proposed) could not be verified and is left out. The classification is editorial judgement. Security and borders is the most announcement-heavy chapter: 8 of its 11 counted commitments are new.
- **Trade, "signed" deals: 2 of 5.** She said the EU "signed" free trade agreements this year with India, Mercosur, Mexico, Australia and Indonesia. Signed in 2026: **EU-Mercosur** (17 January 2026 in Paraguay; interim trade agreement provisionally applied since 1 May 2026) and **EU-Mexico** (22 May 2026). Concluded but NOT signed: **India** (Commission proposed signature to the Council on 11 September 2026), **Australia** (negotiations concluded 24 March 2026), **Indonesia** (negotiations concluded 23 September 2025; proposals to the Council 29 June 2026).
- **Her numbers, checked**: confirmed: about €1 billion a day trade deficit with China (€359.8 billion in 2025, Eurostat); a further 35% drop in irregular crossings (Frontex, January to August); almost 1 litre in 4 lost from water pipes (22.9%, EurEau). Above the record: "an additional €90 billion" for imported fossil fuels since the Hormuz crisis, where the Commission's own brochure the same day says over €80 billion; nearly €1 billion for Gaza, where the Commission's figure is €883.6 million.
- **Canada as "the first associate member of the EU"**: the EU Treaties contain no associate-membership status. The nearest existing tool is an association agreement (Article 217 TFEU).
- **"Europe's own Article 4" or "an Emergency Security Protocol"** for incidents below armed aggression: it would sit alongside what the Treaties already hold, a mutual-assistance clause for armed aggression (Article 42(7) TEU), a solidarity clause for terrorist attacks and disasters (Article 222 TFEU) and a general duty to consult on foreign and security policy (Article 32 TEU). No explicit emergency protocol of this kind exists.
- **New European Emergency Response Framework** for mass movements that are "engineered and weaponised": it would sit alongside the Crisis Regulation of the Pact on Migration and Asylum, in application since **12 June 2026**, which already provides crisis protocols and tools against the instrumentalisation of migrants.
- **Dates to watch** (Commission-agenda dates are tentative, from its agenda of 14 September 2026; the Commission may change its planning):
  - **17 September 2026**: EU Kids Act, adoption by written procedure closing 10:00.
  - **6 October**: European product package.
  - **20 October**: climate resilience framework, enlargement package, new Arctic strategy, 2027 Commission work programme.
  - **28 October**: border and migration package (Frontex, digital returns).
  - **November**: industrial AI initiatives in five sectors (her date).
  - **Autumn**: Digital Fairness Act proposal (her date).
  - **Before the end of the year**: Quality Jobs Act (her date).

## About this guide

Quotes are from the published text, which the Commission marks "check against delivery". The speech as delivered in the chamber can differ in places from the text, so the published text is the version to quote. The commitments ledger was checked on 16 September 2026 against the law as it stood that day.

## Children online: her outline of the EU Kids Act

"Tomorrow, the Commission is proposing the EU KIDS ACT." What follows is her description in the address of a proposal whose text was not yet published on 16 September 2026; the legal text may differ, so attribute it to her:
- no social media under the age of 13;
- no personal account under the age of 15;
- from 13 until a child turns 15, only mini accounts set up and supervised by parents, with limited features and time restriction;
- between 15 and 18, safe design an obligation for the platforms;
- the burden of proof reversed: "Platforms will have to prove that they are safe."
She then called for a wider framework against addictive design for everyone, the Digital Fairness Act, "in autumn". See `eu_minors_social_media_age_limits` and `digital_fairness_act`.

## The Canada chapter

- **Guest**: "I have invited Prime Minister Mark Carney of Canada to be here today." She said that "thanks to CETA, our trade in goods has grown by 75 % in less than a decade" (her figure, not checked by Brubru).
- **From CETA to an "Alliance for the Future"**, "to create a common prosperity and economic security space": intelligent manufacturing; a tech alliance; integrated defence industrial bases; the Arctic as "a flagship joint project"; energy, critical minerals and batteries; AI, quantum, cyber and economic security.
- **Associate membership**: "I would like to work with you on opening the door for Canada to be the first associate member of the EU." The EU Treaties contain no associate-membership status; the nearest existing tool is an association agreement (Article 217 TFEU). Treat it as a political proposal with no legal form yet.
- **Law today**: CETA has been provisionally applied since 2017 and is still not fully in force, because investment protection awaits national ratifications.
- **Canada elsewhere in the speech**: a partner for AI model evaluation, verification and early warning (with the United Kingdom and others), and one of the partners named for a European Security Council (with Norway, Ukraine and the United Kingdom).
- **Arctic**: a new EU Arctic Strategy is on the Commission's tentative agenda for 20 October 2026. See `eu_arctic_policy`.
- See `eu_canada_relations` for the wider relationship.

## What the address is NOT

- **Not law.** A State of the Union address creates no legal obligation. An announcement becomes law only after a Commission proposal and, for legislation, adoption by Parliament and Council.
- **Not a fixed calendar.** Dates she gave ("in November", "in autumn", "before the end of the year") are her own. Dates on the Commission's tentative agenda are marked to be confirmed, and the Commission may change its planning. Say "planned for", never "will be adopted on".
- **Not a record of what is signed or in force.** Where the address describes something as done, check the record: see the trade row above.
- **Not the debate.** Replies in the plenary debate that followed are a separate record and are not covered here.
