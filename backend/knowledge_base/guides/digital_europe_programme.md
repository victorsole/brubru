# Digital Europe Programme (DIGITAL) 2021-2027

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2021/694 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2021 establishing the Digital Europe Programme (CELEX 32021R0694)
- Budget: EUR 7.588 billion in the original 2021 financial envelope, topped up to roughly EUR 8.2 billion for 2021-2027 through MFF adjustments and the STEP top-up for critical technologies
- Five specific objectives (Article 3(2), "distinct but interdependent"):
  1. **High-Performance Computing** -- delivered mainly through the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking (EuroHPC JU)
  2. **Artificial Intelligence** -- including AI Factories and common European data spaces
  3. **Cybersecurity and Trust** -- delivered mainly through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre (ECCC)
  4. **Advanced Digital Skills** -- specialist training, traineeships, short courses in HPC, AI, cybersecurity, data
  5. **Deployment, Best Use of Digital Capacity and Interoperability** -- rollout of digital public services and technologies across the economy and society
- European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIHs): at least one per Member State, one-stop shops giving SMEs, mid-caps and public-sector bodies access to testing, financing advice, training and networking for digital transformation
- Implementing bodies: European Health and Digital Executive Agency (HADEA) manages advanced digital skills, AI/data/cloud deployment and EDIH coordination; EuroHPC JU manages supercomputing and AI Factories; ECCC manages the cybersecurity strand; the European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT) is involved in specific innovation actions
- Topped up by STEP (Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform, Regulation (EU) 2024/795) to reinforce critical and emerging technologies including AI, semiconductors and biotech
- Complements: Connecting Europe Facility Digital strand (CEF Digital, connectivity infrastructure) and Horizon Europe Cluster 4 "Digital, Industry and Space" (research and innovation); DEP itself is a deployment programme, not primarily a research one
- AI Factories and the newer AI Gigafactories initiative under the AI Continent Action Plan are financed partly through DEP and partly through EuroHPC JU own resources plus Member State and private co-investment
- Key official sources: `digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/activities/digital-programme` (DG CNECT programme page); `hadea.ec.europa.eu/programmes/digital-europe-programme_en` (implementing agency)

## What DEP Is

The Digital Europe Programme (DIGITAL, or DEP) is the EU's dedicated funding instrument for building and deploying digital capacity, as distinct from funding digital research. Where Horizon Europe funds the science and early-stage technology development behind digital transformation, DEP funds the infrastructure, capability and skills needed to put mature digital technologies into use across public administrations, businesses (especially SMEs) and citizens. It runs for the same 2021-2027 period as the current Multiannual Financial Framework and is managed at policy level by the Commission's Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology (DG CNECT), with day-to-day implementation delegated to executive agencies and joint undertakings.

DEP was legally established by Regulation (EU) 2021/694 of 29 April 2021, adopted alongside the other digital and connectivity instruments of the 2021-2027 MFF package. Its financial envelope was originally set at EUR 7.588 billion in current prices; subsequent budgetary top-ups, notably through STEP, have brought cumulative resources available to the programme's objectives closer to EUR 8.2 billion across the period. Brubru users should treat EUR 7.588 billion as the regulation's baseline figure and EUR ~8.2 billion as the programme's effective envelope once top-ups are included.

## The Five Specific Objectives

Article 3(2) of Regulation (EU) 2021/694 sets out five specific objectives, described in the regulation as "distinct but interdependent" and forming the core of a coherent package rather than five isolated funding streams:

1. **High-Performance Computing** -- building, deploying, and operating an integrated, demand-driven, user-and-application-oriented, world-class supercomputing and data infrastructure ecosystem in the EU.
2. **Artificial Intelligence** -- building up and strengthening core AI capacities, including data and algorithm libraries, making these available to all businesses and public administrations, and reinforcing testing and experimentation facilities.
3. **Cybersecurity and Trust** -- supporting the procurement of advanced cybersecurity equipment, tools and data infrastructures, and building up EU-wide cybersecurity capacity.
4. **Advanced Digital Skills** -- supporting the design and delivery of long- and short-term training and courses for students, IT professionals and the workforce more broadly, in HPC, AI, cybersecurity, distributed ledgers and other advanced digital technology fields.
5. **Deployment, Best Use of Digital Capacity and Interoperability** -- ensuring the wide use across the economy and society of digital technologies, in particular HPC, AI and cybersecurity solutions, including through EDIHs, and supporting interoperability among public administrations.

## EuroHPC and AI Factories

The High-Performance Computing objective is delivered mainly through the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, a legally and financially separate public-private partnership (Member States, the EU and private members) that pools resources to acquire, deploy and operate world-class supercomputers across the EU. DEP funding is one of the EuroHPC JU's core financing sources, alongside direct Member State contributions and Horizon Europe research funding.

Since the 2023-2024 push to accelerate EU AI capacity, EuroHPC's supercomputing infrastructure has become the backbone of "AI Factories": dedicated compute facilities, built around EuroHPC supercomputers, that give startups, SMEs, researchers and public administrations access to the processing power needed to train and fine-tune large AI models. A newer "AI Gigafactories" initiative, launched under the Commission's AI Continent Action Plan, aims at an even larger scale of dedicated AI compute infrastructure. Financing for both AI Factories and AI Gigafactories draws on a combination of DEP, EuroHPC JU own resources, Member State co-financing and private investment; DEP is not the sole funder but is a structurally important contributor, particularly for the AI specific objective and for skills and deployment support around these facilities.

## ECCC and Cybersecurity

The Cybersecurity and Trust objective is delivered mainly through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre (ECCC), based in Bucharest, working with a network of National Coordination Centres in each Member State and a broader Cybersecurity Competence Community of industry, research and public-sector actors. The ECCC channels DEP (and Horizon Europe) funding into cybersecurity capacity-building: procurement of advanced tools and infrastructure, support for SMEs' cybersecurity readiness, and coordinated investment aligned with the EU's Cybersecurity Strategy and the Cybersecurity Act. See the dedicated `cybersecurity_act.md` guide for the certification framework and ENISA's role; the ECCC's remit under DEP is complementary, focused on funding and capacity rather than certification.

## European Digital Innovation Hubs (EDIHs)

EDIHs are the DEP's principal deployment mechanism for SMEs, mid-caps and public-sector bodies. Each Member State hosts at least one EDIH (larger Member States host several), co-financed by DEP and national/regional budgets, typically anchored in existing research institutes, universities or technology clusters. EDIHs act as one-stop shops offering:
- Test-before-invest access to AI, HPC and cybersecurity technologies
- Advice on financing digital transformation projects (including signposting to other EU and national instruments)
- Skills training and networking with peers and technology providers
- Support tailored to the digital maturity of the requesting organisation, from first digitalisation steps to advanced deployment

EDIHs are coordinated centrally by HADEA, with an EU-wide network directory allowing businesses to find their nearest hub regardless of sector.

## Advanced Digital Skills

DEP's fourth objective funds long-term traineeships, short intensive courses and skills academies in HPC, AI, cybersecurity, data and other advanced digital fields, aimed at closing the EU's digital skills gap across both the private and public sectors. Calls are managed by HADEA and typically co-fund higher-education institutions, training providers and industry consortia; recent grant waves (mid-2026) have included multi-million-euro Advanced Digital Skills call agreements. This strand complements Erasmus+ and the European Skills Agenda rather than duplicating them, focusing specifically on the advanced technology skill sets that HPC, AI Factories, EDIHs and cybersecurity deployment require.

## Implementing Bodies

- **HADEA (European Health and Digital Executive Agency):** manages the bulk of DEP's day-to-day grant administration, in particular advanced digital skills, AI/data/cloud deployment actions, and coordination of the EDIH network.
- **EuroHPC Joint Undertaking:** manages the High-Performance Computing objective and the AI Factories/AI Gigafactories infrastructure built on EuroHPC systems.
- **ECCC (European Cybersecurity Competence Centre):** manages the Cybersecurity and Trust objective, working through National Coordination Centres.
- **EIT (European Institute of Innovation and Technology):** involved in specific innovation-focused actions linked to DEP objectives.
- **DG CNECT:** sets policy direction and work programmes at Commission level, overseeing the executive agencies and joint undertakings above.

## STEP Top-Up

The Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (STEP), established by Regulation (EU) 2024/795, added supplementary funding to several existing EU instruments, including DEP, to reinforce investment in critical and emerging technologies: digital and deep tech (including AI and semiconductors), clean and resource-efficient technologies, and biotechnologies. STEP funding under DEP is additional to the regulation's original EUR 7.588 billion envelope and is one of the main reasons the programme's effective resources are now cited closer to EUR 8.2 billion. See the planned sibling guide `step_strategic_technologies_for_europe.md` for the platform's full scope across all instruments it tops up.

## Relationship to CEF Digital and Horizon Cluster 4

DEP sits alongside two related but distinct EU digital funding instruments, and Brubru users should not conflate them:

| Instrument | Focus | Typical beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|
| Digital Europe Programme (DEP) | Deployment of mature digital capacity: HPC, AI, cybersecurity, skills, interoperability | SMEs, public administrations, EDIHs, research infrastructure operators |
| Connecting Europe Facility Digital (CEF Digital) | Physical and network connectivity infrastructure (broadband, 5G corridors, submarine cables, digital connectivity backbones) | Telecom operators, infrastructure builders, Member States |
| Horizon Europe Cluster 4 "Digital, Industry and Space" | Research and innovation, technology readiness levels up to demonstration/pilot | Universities, research organisations, R&D-active companies |

DEP is explicitly the deployment layer: it takes technologies that Horizon Europe research has matured and funds their rollout at scale, while CEF Digital funds the underlying connectivity infrastructure those technologies run on. A single policy area, such as AI, can therefore appear in Brubru guidance under all three instruments depending on whether the query concerns research funding, compute/deployment funding, or connectivity infrastructure.

## Brubru Engagement Angles

- **Funding search queries:** users asking about EU funding for AI compute, cybersecurity capacity-building, digital skills training or SME digital transformation should be pointed to DEP calls via HADEA, alongside the relevant EDIH for their Member State.
- **AI Factories / AI Gigafactories tracking:** treat these as a cross-cutting DEP/EuroHPC/AI Continent Action Plan story; do not attribute their full financing to DEP alone.
- **Cybersecurity funding vs regulation:** distinguish DEP/ECCC funding questions (money, capacity-building) from Cybersecurity Act and NIS2 compliance questions (legal obligations); see `cybersecurity_act.md` and `nis2_directive.md`.
- **Programme-vs-instrument confusion:** users sometimes ask about "Digital Europe" expecting CEF Digital's connectivity grants or Horizon Cluster 4's research calls; clarify DEP's specific deployment scope before answering.
- **Executive agency routing:** direct HADEA-specific procedural questions (calls, deadlines, beneficiary support) to HADEA's own programme page and National Contact Points network, not to DG CNECT policy pages.
