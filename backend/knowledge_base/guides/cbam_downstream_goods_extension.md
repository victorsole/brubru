## What the Commission Proposes (COM(2025) 989)

The CBAM transitional phase ran 2023-2025. The definitive period began 1 January 2026 with financial obligations on importers of carbon-intensive goods (cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen). The 2023/956 Regulation foresaw that the Commission would propose a scope extension. This proposal:

1. **Extends scope as of 1 January 2028** to selected steel- and aluminium-intensive downstream products (180 products)
2. **Anti-circumvention package**:
   - Enhanced reporting requirements
   - Improved traceability of goods
   - Integration of pre-consumer scrap in emissions calculations
   - Strengthened powers to address misdeclaration and abusive practices
3. **Article 27a** (new): "Serious and unforeseen circumstances" clause -- empowers Commission to adjust scope via delegated acts in extraordinary situations

## Chahim Draft Report (PE786.835, 10 April 2026)

The S&D rapporteur supports the Commission's overall direction but proposes substantive changes:

### Supports
- **Methodology** (trade intensity + cost-push indicators, emissions floor, technical feasibility) -- praised for predictability and investor certainty
- **Anti-circumvention package** (pre-consumer scrap accounting, traceability, anti-misdeclaration powers)
- Scope extension to 180 products as proposed (does not propose adding products at this stage, but signals openness in next review to **split CN codes** within the existing 180)

### Opposes / Modifies
- **Deletion of Article 27a** in its entirety. Reasoning: (a) risk that scope exclusions become target of sector-specific lobbying; (b) institutional concern -- product scope is a core element and should not be decided by delegated acts
- **Opposes inclusion of international carbon credits** for CBAM compliance: premature, counterproductive, price volatility, variable environmental integrity (citing European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change)
- Open to recognising **carbon pricing in Least Developed Countries (LDCs)** as a complementary CBAM compliance pathway -- subject to thorough risk and data assessment

- Recitals amended/added: 1, 8, 9, 9a (new), 9b (new), 10a (new), 11a (new), 13, 14, 20, 20a (new), 20b (new), 21, 36, 36a (new), 36b (new), 37, 45a (new), 47a (new), 50 (20 of 34 amendments touch recitals -- significant political framing rewrite)

The heavy concentration on recitals and Article 1 (which is the operative amending article) signals the rapporteur is reframing the political narrative of the file rather than rewriting the substance of the new product list.

## Political Context

CBAM is a flagship Fit for 55 instrument and a touchstone of EU climate-trade integration. The downstream extension is politically sensitive because:
- **Industry concerns**: downstream manufacturers (e.g., automotive, machinery, construction) fear cost pass-through from upstream CBAM
- **Trade partner concerns**: extending CBAM to downstream products multiplies WTO-compatibility complexity (Article III GATT, MFN questions)
- **Climate hawks**: deletion of Article 27a (Chahim position) hardens the regulation against political dilution
- **LDC pathway**: signals openness to differentiated treatment, addressing equity concerns under UNFCCC Article 4(7)
