DG INTPA is the European Commission service responsible for development cooperation and international partnerships in the Global South. It manages the largest EU external financing instrument (NDICI-Global Europe at EUR 79.5 billion), operates the EFSD+ blended-finance and guarantee architecture, and coordinates Team Europe Initiatives that combine EU budget resources, Member State bilateral programmes, and European development finance institutions (DFIs). It is the operational engine behind the Global Gateway strategy.

## From DEVCO to INTPA

The renaming from DG Development and International Cooperation (DG DEVCO) to DG International Partnerships (DG INTPA) in 2021 signalled a deliberate strategic repositioning. Where DEVCO framed external action as development assistance, INTPA frames it as mutually beneficial partnership -- reflecting the EU's shift from a donor-recipient model towards reciprocal engagement with middle-income and emerging-market countries. The change aligned with the launch of NDICI-Global Europe (which merged seven predecessor instruments) and with the unveiling of Global Gateway in December 2021. Structurally, DG INTPA works in close coordination with the European External Action Service (EEAS) and with the DG for European Neighbourhood and Enlargement (DG ENEST) and the DG for Middle East and Northern Africa Affairs (DG MENA), which cover the Neighbourhood and Gulf regions respectively.

## Mandate and Geographic Scope

DG INTPA's geographical remit covers:

- **Sub-Saharan Africa** -- the highest-value region; anchored by the AU-EU partnership and the EUR 150 billion Africa component of Global Gateway
- **Asia and Pacific** -- ASEAN connectivity, digital and green corridors, bilateral country programmes
- **Latin America and Caribbean** -- EU-LAC Global Gateway Investment Agenda (July 2023), association agreements, regional integration support
- **Overseas Countries and Territories (OCTs)** -- special status under EU Treaty; separate OCTA association

DG INTPA does NOT cover: EU candidate countries (DG ENEST IPA instrument), Eastern Partnership countries (DG ENEST ENI), Southern Neighbourhood countries (DG MENA), or humanitarian response (DG ECHO). Coordination protocols with all those services are governed by inter-service agreements and shared programming frameworks.

## Global Gateway as the Strategic Framework

Global Gateway (launched 1 December 2021) is DG INTPA's overarching investment strategy, offering partner countries a values-based alternative to the China Belt and Road Initiative (BRI). The EUR 300 billion mobilisation target (2021-2027) is not a budget line -- it is a blend of NDICI grants, EFSD+ guarantees, EIB/EBRD financing, Member State bilateral commitments, and private capital crowded in through blended finance.

Five investment pillars: Digital (fibre optic cables, data centres, 5G), Climate and Energy (renewables, green hydrogen, power grids), Transport (railways, ports, roads with sustainability standards), Health (vaccine manufacturing, pharmaceutical supply chains), Education and Research (higher education, Erasmus+ international strand).

Flagship corridor projects include the Lobito Corridor (Angola-Zambia-DRC, strategic minerals transport), the Trans-Caspian corridor (Central Asia to Europe via the South Caucasus and Black Sea), and the Africa-Europe Investment Package.

**Latest development (23 June 2026):** The Commission launched the Connectivity Agenda Platform to strengthen links between Europe and Central Asia through the Black Sea region and the South Caucasus. Commissioner Sikela co-hosted a high-level ministerial meeting with Commissioners Kos (Enlargement) and Tzitzikostas (Transport). Agreements with international financial institutions are expected to mobilise up to EUR 2 billion. Source: EC press release IP/26/1430.

## NDICI-Global Europe: The Three-Pillar Structure

NDICI-Global Europe (Regulation (EU) 2021/947, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/995) is the EU's primary external financing instrument. It merged seven predecessor instruments (EDF, DCI, ENI, PI, IcSP, EIDHR, and Instrument for OCTs) into a single unified structure.

**Budget breakdown (2021-2027):**

| Pillar | Amount |
|--------|--------|
| Geographic programmes | EUR 60.38 billion |
| Thematic programmes | EUR 6.36 billion |
| Rapid-response actions | EUR 3.18 billion |
| Cushion (reserve) | EUR 9.53 billion |
| **Total** | **EUR 79.5 billion** |

**Geographic pillar** allocations cover the Neighbourhood (shared with DG ENEST under NDICI), Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia-Pacific, and the Americas and Caribbean.

**Thematic pillar** covers: human rights and democracy; civil society organisations; stability and peace; global challenges (climate, migration, education, health, digital).

**Rapid-response pillar** enables fast disbursement for crisis situations, emerging challenges, and high-level foreign policy interests.

**Cushion** is a flexible reserve activated to respond to unforeseen crises or newly emerging priorities. It can be mobilised by Commission decision.

**2026 amendment:** Regulation (EU) 2026/995 (OJ L, 4 May 2026, CELEX 32026R0995) amended NDICI to increase efficiency of the External Action Guarantee: faster decision-making on guarantee allocations, simplified reporting obligations, clearer accountability split between Commission, EEAS, and implementing partners. This is the most substantive NDICI change since the 2021 launch.

## EFSD+ and the External Action Guarantee

The European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (EFSD+) is the blended-finance and guarantee architecture embedded within NDICI. Its purpose is to crowd in private investment by offering risk coverage to financial intermediaries (EIB, EBRD, national DFIs, commercial banks).

The External Action Guarantee (EAG), backed by the EU budget, provides EUR 53.4 billion in risk coverage -- the principal financial machinery behind the EUR 300 billion Global Gateway mobilisation target. The EAG covers equity investments, loans, guarantees, and blended-finance operations in partner countries.

Implementing partners include the European Investment Bank (EIB), European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD), Agence Française de Développement (AFD), KfW, and other European DFIs operating under EFSD+ guarantee agreements signed with the Commission.

For professionals seeking Global Gateway financing: engaging implementing partners early -- before project proposals reach DG INTPA desk officers -- is the standard pipeline pathway. EFSD+ guarantees require a "blendable" project with identifiable private co-investment potential.

## EU Trust Funds

EU Trust Funds are emergency and thematic instruments established outside the standard budget to enable faster pooling of resources from multiple donors. DG INTPA manages or co-manages four:

| Trust Fund | Status | Focus |
|------------|--------|-------|
| Bekou (Central African Republic) | Active | Stabilisation, reconstruction, governance |
| EUTF for Africa | Winding down | Migration management, development in origin/transit countries |
| Madad (Syria) | Active | Syrian refugee crisis response (Turkey, Jordan, Lebanon, Iraq, Egypt) |
| Colombia | Active | Peace process support, rural development |

Trust Fund governance involves a Board with EU Member States and other donors as contributors. They operate under specific contribution agreements and are subject to DG INTPA financial regulation. As these instruments mature and wind down, successor programming is typically absorbed into NDICI geographic allocations.

## Budget Support Modality

Budget support is a modality -- not an instrument -- in which the EU disburses funds directly to a partner government's national treasury, rather than to implementing organisations or projects. Conditions include:

- Macro-economic stability (IMF programme or equivalent)
- A credible national development strategy
- Satisfactory public financial management (PFM) systems
- Transparent and reliable national budget

Budget support accounts for a significant share of DG INTPA disbursements in lower-income countries, particularly in West Africa and the Pacific. It is politically attractive for partner governments (direct budget alignment) but contested in EU Member States where it is perceived as lacking conditionality. DG INTPA's budget support section publishes annual reports on disbursements and policy dialogue outcomes.

## Team Europe Initiatives and capacity4dev

**Team Europe Initiatives (TEIs)** are the flagship coordination mechanism that bundles EU institutional resources, Member State bilateral instruments, and European DFI finance behind a single development priority in a partner country or region. Examples include TEIs on renewable energy in sub-Saharan Africa, digital connectivity in Asia, and vaccine manufacturing in Africa.

The **Team Europe Explorer** is a public-facing mapping tool showing TEI investment flows by country and sector.

**capacity4dev** (https://capacity4dev.europa.eu/) is DG INTPA's knowledge-sharing platform for international development professionals. With 352 thematic and geographical groups, 37,000+ library resources, and 7,800+ members, it functions as the primary community of practice for EU external action practitioners. Relevant groups include those on budget support, procurement, gender, private-sector development, and country-specific programming.

**INTPA Academy** is the DG's learning and training offer for partner country officials, EU delegation staff, and implementing partners. Courses cover topics including results-based management, PRAG procedures, and EU development policy.

## PRAG: Procurement Rules for External Action

The Practical Guide to EU contract procedures for external actions (PRAG) is the binding reference document for all grants, service contracts, supply contracts, and works contracts financed by the EU budget in external action. Any organisation bidding for EU-funded contracts outside the EU must comply with PRAG rules.

Key PRAG principles:
- **Nationality and origin rules**: suppliers must be eligible (most DG INTPA contracts open to all countries; some Trust Funds have nationality restrictions)
- **Procurement thresholds**: open international tender vs. simplified procedures based on contract value
- **DEVCO Companion**: supplementary guidance document clarifying PRAG application in specific contexts
- **Visibility requirements**: EU branding and communication obligations on all contract outputs

PRAG is updated periodically by DG INTPA and is publicly available on the International Partnerships website. It is the primary operational reference for NGOs, consultancies, and companies bidding on EU development contracts. Non-compliance with PRAG eligibility or procurement rules is the most common reason for contract rejection or audit findings.

## Coordination with Other Services

DG INTPA does not operate in isolation. Key coordination relationships:

| Service | Coordination scope |
|---------|--------------------|
| EEAS | Political framework for all EU external action; shared programming in geographic regions; EUSR coordination |
| DG ENEST | Eastern Neighbourhood (ENI), Enlargement (IPA), Southern Neighbourhood -- geographical neighbours excluded from INTPA scope |
| DG MENA | Middle East, North Africa, Gulf -- separated from INTPA in the 2021 reorganisation |
| DG ECHO | Humanitarian response -- DG INTPA handles development/resilience; DG ECHO handles emergency relief; nexus programming spans both |
| DG TRADE | Trade agreements complement development partnerships; DG INTPA co-leads Economic Partnership Agreements (EPAs) with Africa, Caribbean, Pacific |
| EIB / EBRD | Implementing partners for EFSD+ guarantees and blended finance |

## Brubru Engagement Angles

**For NGOs and civil society organisations:**
- PRAG grants are the primary entry point; monitor INTPA tender notices on the Funding and Tenders portal (F&T) and country-level EU Delegation calls for proposals
- capacity4dev membership is free and useful for tracking thematic group updates and call-for-proposal announcements
- DG INTPA's Civil Society and Local Authorities (CSLA) thematic programme under NDICI allocates dedicated funds for CSO capacity-building and advocacy

**For development finance institutions (DFIs) and infrastructure developers:**
- EFSD+ guarantee programmes: approach EIB/EBRD/AFD/KfW as the primary channel for blended-finance structuring; direct INTPA engagement is for policy dialogue, not individual project finance
- Team Europe Initiatives offer a coordination umbrella for large-scale infrastructure projects; check the Team Europe Explorer for active TEIs by country
- Global Gateway pipeline is publicly mapped at the International Partnerships website; strategic alignment with a TEI significantly increases funding probability

**For consultancies and service providers:**
- Service contracts under PRAG (technical assistance, evaluations, policy support): monitor the Funding and Tenders portal and EU Delegation websites
- Framework contracts (SIEA -- Support to International, Environmental and Agricultural Programmes) are the main vehicle for technical assistance assignments; membership in an SIEA consortium is the most reliable pipeline for recurring work
- FWC SIEA lots cover sectors: governance, economic development, environment/climate, rural development, food security, health, education, migration, transport, energy

**For researchers and think tanks:**
- INTPA Academy and capacity4dev are primary dissemination channels
- Thematic evaluations and reviews procured by DG INTPA's Evaluation and Results unit (EVAL) are publicly tendered and published on the F&T portal
- Joint EEAS-INTPA analytical products (country strategies, multiannual indicative programmes) are available on the International Partnerships website by country
