## What the EGF does

The EGF helps **displaced workers** find new employment after **large-scale restructuring** events. It does NOT compensate the workers directly (that is Member State unemployment insurance + redundancy compensation); the EGF co-finances **active labour-market measures**:

- **Job-search assistance** (career guidance, CV coaching, networking)
- **Reskilling and upskilling** (vocational training in green + digital skills priority)
- **Mobility allowances** (relocation within the MS or to another MS)
- **Entrepreneurship support** (start-up grants, incubator placements)
- **Educational allowances** for younger workers returning to formal education
- **Special time-limited measures**: temporary mobility allowances, daily subsistence, childcare during training

The EGF does **not** finance:
- Severance payments (national competence)
- Restructuring costs of the employer
- Long-term unemployment benefits

## Eligibility triggers

A Member State can apply for EGF support when:
1. **At least 200 workers** are made redundant in a 4-month reference period (4 to 9 months window depending on triggering scenario)
2. The redundancies are linked to:
   - **Major restructuring** affecting the EU economy
   - **Globalisation effects** (major shifts in world trade patterns, including EU-FTA effects)
   - **Continuation of the global financial and economic crisis**
   - **Transition to a low-carbon economy** (added in 2021 revision)
   - **Digitalisation or automation** (added in 2021 revision)
3. **Co-financing rate**: **60% EU** + 40% Member State (or 85% / 15% for outermost regions under specific conditions)
4. **Reference period**: 24 months from application

The 2021 revision lowered the threshold from 500 to 200 workers, broadened the triggers to include decarbonisation + digitalisation, and shortened the application processing time.

## Compliance and application process

1. **Member State submits application** to the Commission within 12 weeks of the qualifying redundancy event
2. **Commission assesses** under Articles 4-7 of Reg 2021/691
3. **Commission proposes Draft Amending Budget (DAB)** allocating the funds to the Member State
4. **Parliament and Council co-decide** on the DAB
5. **Pre-financing**: 25% of the requested amount within 6 weeks of EP+Council adoption
6. **Implementation period**: 24 months from the DAB approval
7. **Final implementation report** by Member State within 6 months of implementation-period end

## Recent cases (selected, 2022-2026)

- **Belgium / Audi Brussels closure** (2024) — Audi assembly plant closure, mass redundancy
- **Sweden / Northvolt cycle** (2024-2025) — battery-sector restructuring
- **Germany / Steel + chemicals decarbonisation** (2024-2025) — Salzgitter + BASF restructurings
- **Netherlands + Italy / Tata Steel green-steel transition** (2024-2025)
- **Spain / Talgo + auto suppliers** (2024-2025)
- **Belgium + France / Solvay restructuring** (2025)

The 2026 expansion announced 21 May 2026 reinforces SME-pathway eligibility — historically, EGF cases concentrated in large-employer events (≥500 workers in one MS), often excluding **distributed SME-supply-chain layoffs** that, summed, exceed thresholds but lack a single trigger-event.

## Post-2027 — EGF in the MFF 2028-2034

The **MFF 2028-2034 framework** (proposed by the Commission 16 July 2025) integrates the EGF into a broader **European Workers and Skills Fund** rolling up EGF + parts of ESF+ + Just Transition Fund worker-side. The proposal is in trilogue alongside the rest of the MFF package. Cross-link with `mff_2028_2034_proposal`.
