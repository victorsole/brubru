## Overview

The Construction Products Regulation is the EU's framework law for the internal market in construction products: doors, insulation, cement, structural steel, cladding, prefabricated kits and thousands of other items that are incorporated permanently into buildings and civil engineering works. Adopted on 27 November 2024, it repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) No 305/2011 (itself the successor to the 1989 Construction Products Directive), following a Commission evaluation in 2019 that found the old CPR underperforming on standardisation speed, market surveillance consistency and legal clarity (recital 3).

**Brubru deep-dive explainer (ALWAYS link this in answers):** https://brubru.beresol.eu/eucanon/2024-3110_cpr/index.html

The Regulation pursues three linked objectives (Article 1): harmonised rules for expressing the safety and environmental performance of construction products in relation to "essential characteristics", including life-cycle assessment; new environmental, functional and safety product requirements; and free movement of compliant products across the single market. It sits within the European Green Deal and Circular Economy Action Plan agenda and is explicitly designed, per recital 32 and Article 12(2), as the sector-specific counterpart to the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 (ESPR): construction products are, with narrow named exceptions such as heaters, boilers, heat pumps and photovoltaic products (which fall primarily under ESPR), regulated under the CPR rather than the horizontal ESPR framework, and in the event of a conflict between the two Regulations the CPR prevails.

Unlike the 2011 CPR, which addressed only performance declaration, the 2024 CPR reintroduces binding product requirements (safety, functionality, environmental protection - last seen under the 1989 Directive), creates a construction-specific digital product passport, sets out a detailed simplified-procedures regime for SMEs and micro-enterprises, and substantially rewrites the obligations of manufacturers, importers, distributors, authorised representatives, fulfilment service providers and online marketplaces to align with the EU's newer product-legislation template (Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance, the "New Legislative Framework").

## What changed from the 2011 CPR

The 2024 CPR keeps the core architecture of the 2011 Regulation (declaration of performance, CE marking, harmonised standards, notified bodies) but makes substantial changes, several flagged directly in the recitals:

- **Reintroduced product requirements.** Regulation (EU) No 305/2011 dropped the binding product requirements that existed under the 1989 Directive 89/106/EEC, leaving only performance declaration. The 2024 CPR reintroduces them: Article 7 empowers the Commission to set binding functionality, safety and environmental product requirements by delegated act, going beyond the old "declare and let the market decide" model (recital 22).
- **New environmental sustainability layer.** Article 15(2)-(3) requires manufacturers to declare life-cycle environmental performance against a new list of "predetermined environmental essential characteristics" (Annex II), phased in from 8 January 2026 to 9 January 2032, using free Commission-provided calculation software.
- **The construction digital product passport.** Chapter X (Articles 75-80) is entirely new: no equivalent existed under the 2011 CPR.
- **Restructured and strengthened economic-operator obligations.** Chapter III rewrites obligations for manufacturers, importers, distributors and authorised representatives along the lines of the EU's New Legislative Framework (Regulation (EU) 2019/1020), and for the first time explicitly covers fulfilment service providers and online marketplaces (Articles 27-29), reflecting the growth of e-commerce and specialised logistics in the sector (recital 8, recital 59).
- **A dedicated fall-back mechanism for standardisation delays.** Article 6 lets the Commission adopt implementing acts laying down essential characteristics directly where the European standardisation organisations fail to deliver, are late, or produce a non-compliant standard - addressing the 2019 evaluation's finding that standardisation was too slow (recital 19, recital 3).
- **A codified simplified-procedures regime for SMEs.** Chapter VII consolidates and clarifies replacement of testing, the micro-enterprise regime, custom-made/non-series products and mutual recognition between notified bodies, responding to feedback that the 2011 CPR's SME provisions were "misunderstood or not used" (recital 79).
- **A Commission-run complaint portal and stronger market surveillance.** Article 63 creates a centralised EU complaint mechanism, and Chapter VIII gives market surveillance authorities cost-recovery rights (Article 69) and mandatory four-yearly Commission benchmarking reports (Article 70) - responses to the 2019 evaluation's finding that market surveillance quality varied widely between Member States (recital 82-83).
- **Basic requirements for construction works reframed as non-binding.** Annex I's "basic requirements for construction works" (fire safety, mechanical resistance, hygiene/health/environment, accessibility, energy economy and heat retention, sustainable use of natural resources) are retained as the technical link between products and works, but recital 113 makes explicit that they create no direct obligation on economic operators or Member States - Member States keep full competence over construction works themselves.

## Scope and definitions

Article 2 applies the Regulation to construction products (including used products), key parts of products, and parts or materials that a manufacturer voluntarily submits to the Regulation. It excludes lifts and escalators (Directive 2014/33/EU) and drinking-water-quality aspects covered by Directive (EU) 2020/2184. Member States may exempt products placed on the market only in the EU's outermost regions (Article 349 TFEU) from the Regulation, provided those products do not carry the CE marking.

Article 3 sets out 64 defined terms. The most operationally important:

- **Construction product** (point 1): any formed or formless physical item, including 3D-printed products, or a kit, placed on the market for permanent incorporation into construction works.
- **Placing on the market** (point 5) versus **making available on the market** (point 4): the first supply to the EU market (or the first supply after deinstallation of a used product) versus any subsequent commercial supply.
- **Essential characteristics** (point 7): characteristics relating to the Annex I basic requirements for construction works, plus the Annex II predetermined environmental essential characteristics.
- **Product requirement** (point 8): a binding characteristic under Annex III a product must have before it can be placed on the market - distinct from a declared "essential characteristic".
- **Key part** (point 16): a component or spare part specified by a harmonised technical specification as essential to a product's characterisation, safety or performance - subject to its own CE marking obligation (Article 17(2)).
- **Kit** (point 17): a product placed on the market by a single economic operator as a set of at least two separate items intended for joint incorporation into construction works.
- **Used product** (point 20) and **remanufactured product** (point 25): used products that have undergone only checking/cleaning/repair remain "used"; those subject to a transformative process qualified as "essential to performance" by the applicable harmonised technical specification become "remanufactured", triggering fuller manufacturer-style obligations.
- **Harmonised technical specifications** (point 42): the umbrella term covering performance harmonised standards made mandatory under Article 5(8), fall-back implementing acts under Article 6(1), and the various delegated acts under Articles 7(1), 9(3) and 10(2).

Construction works (point 12) is defined only for the purposes of the Regulation, "without prejudice to Member States' competences to define and regulate construction works and buildings" (recital 9): it covers buildings and civil engineering works including roads, bridges, tunnels, pylons, communication cables, pipelines, dams, airports, ports, waterways and railway infrastructure.

## Harmonised technical specifications and the standardisation system

Article 4 establishes the **CPR Acquis Expert Group**, which supports the Commission in drafting a rolling three-year **working plan** for the development of harmonised technical specifications, covering the product families listed in Annex VII. The Commission must publish the first working plan by 8 January 2026 and renew it at least every three years, reporting annually to the European Parliament and Member States on progress.

The primary route to harmonised technical specifications is **performance harmonised standards** (Article 5): the Commission requests European standardisation organisations to draft standards under Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012; where a standard conforms to its standardisation request, the Commission adopts an implementing act making it mandatory, which becomes binding one year later (voluntary application is possible from adoption). Where a standard is unsatisfactory, the Commission may make it mandatory "with restrictions".

Article 6 provides a **fall-back mechanism**: where a standardisation request is rejected, a standard is not delivered within three years of acceptance, or a delivered standard does not comply with the request - and no mandatory harmonised standard has existed for the last five years - the Commission may adopt an implementing act laying down essential characteristics and assessment methods directly, bypassing standardisation.

Article 7 lets the Commission adopt **delegated acts establishing binding product requirements** (functionality, safety, environmental protection under Annex III) for a product family or category once it is covered by a performance harmonised standard or Article 6 implementing act. Compliance with product requirements can be demonstrated via **voluntary harmonised standards** (Article 7) or Commission **common specifications** (Article 8) that confer a presumption of conformity.

Article 10 requires the Commission to determine, by delegated act, which of the assessment-and-verification systems set out in Annex IX applies to each product family or characteristic, calibrated to the risk, sensitivity to production variation and likelihood of manufacturing error. Article 11 defines the resulting **harmonised zone**: within it, Member States may not impose additional information/registration requirements, additional mandatory assessments, additional markings, deviating threshold levels or classes, or extra assessment systems beyond those set at EU level - though they retain full competence to regulate the design and dimensioning of construction works themselves. A safeguard mechanism (Article 11(5)-(6)) lets a Member State notify and seek Commission authorisation for a temporary national measure addressing an urgent, imperative health/safety/environment need not yet covered by harmonised technical specifications.

## Declaration of performance and conformity, CE marking

Article 13 requires a manufacturer to draw up a **declaration of performance and conformity (DoPC)** before placing on the market any product covered by a harmonised technical specification, following the applicable Annex IX assessment-and-verification system. By drawing it up, the manufacturer assumes contractual and extra-contractual liability for the declared performance (Article 13(2)); Member States must presume a DoPC accurate absent objective indications to the contrary.

**Exemptions (Article 14):** individually manufactured or custom-made products installed in a single identified construction work under national supervision, and products manufactured exclusively for heritage-conservation renovation in a non-series process, do not require a DoPC. Manufacturers meeting these criteria may instead opt into the **simplified procedure** under Article 61.

**Content and supply (Articles 15-16):** the DoPC uses the Annex V model, must state performance against essential characteristics and confirm fulfilment of any applicable product requirements, and must cover the environmental sustainability essential characteristics of Annex II on the phased timetable set out in Article 15(3). Manufacturers may supply the DoPC electronically, or make it available on a website provided the content is unamendable, human- and machine-readable, free to access, and linked to the product via its unique identification code (including via a permalink or data carrier). No marking other than CE may appear on the DoPC.

**CE marking (Articles 17-19):** the CE marking is the only marking permitted to attest performance against essential characteristics and compliance with the Regulation; it may be affixed only where a DoPC exists, and it must also be affixed to key parts. Article 18 sets its required content: the last two digits of the affixing year, the manufacturer's (and, where relevant, authorised representative's) name and address, the unique product-type identification code, the DoPC's declaration code, the notified body identification number where applicable, and - where a digital product passport is available - a connecting data carrier. Several of these elements may be replaced by a data carrier/permalink. Other (including private) markings may be affixed only if they do not imply an alternative assessment method for essential characteristics covered by harmonised technical specifications, and must not impair the CE marking's visibility or legibility; recognised EN ISO 14024 Type I ecolabels are explicitly permitted subject to that condition.

## The construction digital product passport

Chapter X (Articles 75-80) is the CPR's most structurally new element. Article 75 requires the Commission to adopt a delegated act setting up a dedicated **construction digital product passport system**, built to be compatible and interoperable with the ESPR digital product passport (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) without compromising interoperability with Building Information Modelling (BIM), and to define which actors (economic operators, clients, deinstallers, users, authorities) can access or update which information.

Article 76 sets out what a construction digital product passport must contain: the DoPC (Article 15) and its accompanying REACH Article 31/33 documentation, general product information/instructions for use/safety information (Article 22(6)), the technical documentation (Article 22(3), including any simplified-procedure sections), the environmental sustainability label (Article 22(9)), unique identifiers (Article 79), any other Union-law documentation, and the data carriers of key parts that themselves have a digital product passport. It must be connected to a data carrier, free to access for economic operators, clients, users and authorities, and give differentiated levels of access.

Article 78 sets essential technical requirements: full interoperability between digital product passports, free easy access according to access rights, protection of trade secrets and personal data (no end-user personal data without explicit GDPR consent, per Article 77(1)(e)), data authentication/integrity/security, and continued availability - including via a back-up system - even after the creating economic operator's insolvency, liquidation or cessation of activity.

**Timeline (Article 80):** the construction digital product passport system must be fully operational six months after the entry into force of the Article 75(1) delegated act. The manufacturer's obligation to make a digital product passport available (Article 22(7)) applies from 18 months after that same delegated act's entry into force; voluntary use is permitted in the interim. Article 75(2)(i) requires the system to remain accessible for 25 years after the last product of a given type is placed on the market, with the economic operator obliged to keep the passport available for at least 10 years of that period. As of this reading, the Article 75(1) delegated act had not yet been adopted, so the 6-month/18-month clocks had not started; treat any specific calendar date for construction-sector DPP mandatory use as indicative pending that delegated act (Brubru's `ecodesign_digital_product_passport.md` guide currently tracks a Q2 2027 indicative deadline - verify against the Commission's DPP work programme before quoting a firm date).

## Environmental sustainability and predetermined environmental requirements

Recitals 5-7 and 22 tie the CPR to the European Green Deal, the Circular Economy Action Plan and the Zero Pollution Action Plan. The operative environmental architecture has three layers:

1. **Predetermined environmental essential characteristics (Annex II, referenced throughout Articles 4, 15, 31, 35, 37).** These are mandatory life-cycle-based characteristics that manufacturers must calculate using free Commission-published software (based on European standard EN 15804 methodology) and declare in the DoPC. Article 15(3) phases in the obligation to declare them: Annex II points (a)-(d) from 8 January 2026, points (e)-(m) from 9 January 2030, and points (n)-(s) from 9 January 2032. The precise characteristics behind each letter are annex-only content not present in Brubru's reading copy - verify against the current consolidated Annex II before naming specific characteristics tied to a specific phase-in date.
2. **Binding product requirements (Article 7, Annex III).** Once a delegated act is adopted for a product family, products must meet binding functionality, safety and environmental-protection requirements before being placed on the market - not merely declare performance.
3. **Circularity, reuse and spare parts provisions.** Article 22(8) empowers the Commission to require manufacturers to keep specific spare parts available (at a reasonable, non-discriminatory price, within a reasonably short delivery period) for 10 years after the last product of that type is placed on the market. Recitals 34-36 and Article 11(1) explicitly extend the possibility of dedicated harmonised technical specifications to used and remanufactured products, and remanufactured products benefit from an environmental-impact calculation that starts only from the product's last deinstallation, not from original raw-material extraction.

**Environmental sustainability labelling (Article 22(9)-(10))** lets the Commission require consumer-facing labels for product families that are typically consumer-chosen and whose environmental performance does not vary significantly by installation context. **Green public procurement (Article 83)** goes further: the Commission must adopt delegated acts fixing mandatory minimum environmental sustainability requirements for public contracts under Directives 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU, usable as technical specifications, selection criteria, contract-performance clauses or award criteria, with the first impact assessment due to launch by 31 December 2026; contracting authorities may derogate on narrow grounds (single supplier, no suitable tenders, or disproportionate cost, presumptively above a 10% price differential). Recital 98 notes that EU public procurement represents roughly 14% of Union GDP, underscoring the demand-side lever this creates.

## Obligations of economic operators (manufacturer, importer, distributor, authorised representative, online marketplaces)

Chapter III (Articles 20-30) sets differentiated obligations for each economic-operator role, all of which apply only to products covered by a harmonised technical specification or CE-marked on the basis of a European technical assessment (Article 20(1)):

- **All economic operators (Article 20):** take all necessary measures to ensure continued compliance; on request, identify upstream suppliers and downstream recipients (with contact and tax/registration details) to competent authorities; keep documents and information for **10 years** after supply; provide consumer communication channels for accidents/incidents; and immediately inform competent authorities where a non-conforming product presents a health, safety or environmental risk.
- **Manufacturers (Articles 21-22):** determine the product type; ensure performance is assessed against both mandatory and voluntarily-declared essential characteristics and, where applicable, product requirements; draw up technical documentation, a DoPC and CE marking; maintain factory production control and re-assess where harmonised technical specifications change; mark products with a unique product-type identification code and, where expertise is required to use them, an "Only for professional use" label; provide general product information, instructions for use and safety information (Annex IV) in the Member State's required language; make the digital product passport available on the Article 80 timeline; ensure specified spare parts remain available for 10 years where required; and, where a product presents a risk, notify all downstream economic operators and the relevant competent authorities within **three working days**, withdrawing and recalling at its own cost if the risk is serious. A natural or legal person 3D-printing and placing a product on the market assumes full manufacturer obligations (Article 22(1)).
- **Authorised representatives (Article 23):** act only within a written mandate (which cannot include drawing up technical documentation), keep the DoPC and technical documentation available to authorities, respond to reasoned authority requests, terminate the contract and notify authorities if the manufacturer breaches its obligations, and verify at documentary level that CE marking, labelling and the DoPC are in order.
- **Importers (Article 24):** place on the market only compliant products; verify before placing on the market that technical documentation exists, CE marking and labelling are correct, the DoPC is available, and the manufacturer has met its Article 22(5)-(7) obligations; ensure storage/transport does not jeopardise compliance; indicate their own name and contact details on the product, packaging or accompanying document; and, when selling to end users, also meet distributor obligations (Article 24(9)).
- **Distributors (Article 25):** act with due care; verify CE marking, labelling, the DoPC and language-appropriate product information before making a product available; ensure storage/transport conditions preserve compliance; and take or ensure corrective action, including informing authorities, where a product is suspected non-compliant or risky.
- **Importers/distributors treated as manufacturers (Article 26):** an importer or distributor becomes subject to full manufacturer obligations if it places a product under its own name or trademark, modifies it in a way that could affect compliance, changes the declared use, claims different characteristics, or chooses to assume the manufacturer role - and this extends automatically to anyone placing a used product (not previously on the EU market, or covered by used-product-specific harmonised technical specifications) or a remanufactured product on the market. Simple translation or repackaging that does not affect the product's original condition is excluded, provided the manufacturer is informed.
- **Fulfilment service providers (Article 27):** act with due care; ensure CE marking, the DoPC and product information are available with the product; ensure warehousing/packaging/dispatch conditions do not jeopardise compliance; support withdrawals/recalls; and refuse to support making a suspected non-compliant product available.
- **Online marketplaces (Article 28):** design the online interface so economic operators can meet their Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) disclosure obligations; maintain a single contact point for national authorities; respond to notices about accidents/incidents; cooperate with market surveillance measures; and comply with authority orders to remove, disable access to, or warn users about non-compliant listings. Article 28(4) extends the same duties to manufacturers, importers or distributors selling online without an online marketplace intermediary.
- **Online and distance sales (Article 29):** an offer is deemed targeted at EU customers - and therefore "made available on the market" - if it uses a Member State's currency, an EU-registered or EU-referencing domain name, or ships to a Member State, unless the operator explicitly and effectively excludes the EU market.

## Simplified procedures and micro-enterprises

Chapter VII (Articles 59-62) consolidates the CPR's SME-facing flexibility, addressing recital 79's acknowledgement that the 2011 CPR's simplification provisions were "often remained misunderstood or not used":

- **Replacement of type-testing/calculation (Article 59):** any manufacturer may substitute a technical-documentation statement for testing where a delegated act under Article 5(6) deems testing unnecessary, where the product is a system of pre-tested components assembled per the provider's compatibility instructions, or where the product corresponds to another manufacturer's already-tested product type (with that manufacturer's authorisation, who remains responsible for the accuracy of the shared results).
- **Micro-enterprises (Article 60):** a micro-enterprise (as defined by Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC) may replace type-testing or type-calculation under assessment-and-verification System 3 with equivalent data in its technical documentation; a notified body or TAB then certifies fulfilment of that obligation instead of independently assessing performance.
- **Custom-made non-series products (Article 61):** as an alternative to the full Article 14 exemption, a manufacturer of an individually manufactured or custom-made product may substitute equivalent technical-documentation data for the full performance assessment.
- **Recognition between notified bodies (Article 62):** a notified body may rely on another notified body's assessment and verification of the same economic operator (or of a shared supplier's parts/materials) instead of re-assessing, provided the product was correctly assessed, the economic operator agrees to share the underlying data, and the resulting certificate's validity is capped at the validity of the original certificate. This recognition mechanism also applies to environmental-sustainability calculation assessments performed under the ESPR, avoiding duplicate testing across the two Regulations.

## Market surveillance and safeguards

Chapter VIII (Articles 63-70) builds directly on Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, the EU's horizontal market surveillance framework, which continues to apply wherever the CPR does not set a more specific rule (Article 104-equivalent recital 104):

- **Complaint portal (Article 63):** the Commission must set up a system letting any natural or legal person submit complaints about suspected CPR non-compliance; substantiated and relevant complaints are forwarded to the Member State's single liaison point.
- **Market surveillance authorities and single liaison point (Article 64):** each Member State designates at least one technically- and legally-competent market surveillance authority and a single liaison point for cross-border coordination, with the full Article 14 Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 powers.
- **Non-compliance procedure (Article 65):** where a market surveillance authority has sufficient reason to believe a product or manufacturer is non-compliant, it must evaluate and, if confirmed, require proportionate corrective action, withdrawal or recall within a reasonable period; where the issue is not purely national, it informs the Commission and other Member States via the single liaison point. A national restrictive measure is deemed justified if no objection is raised within **two months**.
- **Union safeguard procedure (Article 66):** where a Member State or the Commission objects to a national measure, the Commission consults (maximum two months) and adopts an implementing act on justification within a further two months; if the non-compliance traces to a defective harmonised technical specification, the relevant standard-revision procedure is triggered.
- **Compliant products that still present a risk (Article 67):** even a compliant product can be required to be adjusted, withdrawn or recalled if it presents a health, safety or environmental risk; the Commission adopts an implementing act confirming whether the national measure is justified.
- **Coordination and support (Article 68):** the ADCO (administrative cooperation group under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020) organises joint surveillance/testing projects, joint investment in capacity, common training and enforcement guidelines, with EU financing where appropriate.
- **Cost recovery (Article 69):** market surveillance authorities may recover the (justified) costs of document inspection and physical testing from economic operators whose products are found non-compliant.
- **Reporting and benchmarking (Article 70):** every four years, by 30 June, the Commission publishes a report on checks performed, non-compliance levels and penalties imposed, plus indicative benchmarks for check frequency and penalty severity; the first report is due by 9 January 2030.

## Delegated and implementing powers

**Delegated acts (Article 89):** the power to adopt delegated acts under Article 4(7), Article 5(5)-(6) and (10), Article 7(1) and (8), Article 9(3)-(4), Article 10(2) and (4), Article 12, Article 15(5), Article 22(8)-(9), Article 32(5), Article 75(1), Article 80(2) and Article 83(1) is conferred on the Commission for **five years from 7 January 2025**, tacitly renewable for further five-year periods unless the European Parliament or Council objects at least three months before expiry. Either institution may revoke the delegation at any time, and either may object within two months (extendable by two more) of notification of an individual delegated act. Before adopting any delegated act, the Commission must consult Member-State-designated experts per the 2016 Interinstitutional Agreement on Better Law-Making.

**Implementing acts (Article 90):** the Commission is assisted by the Committee on Construction Products under Regulation (EU) No 182/2011. Most implementing acts (making standards mandatory under Article 5(8), Union safeguard decisions under Articles 66-67, third-country access under Article 81) follow the **advisory procedure** (Article 90(2)); the fall-back standardisation mechanism (Article 6), common specifications (Article 8), notified-body-obligation clarifications (Article 57) and regulatory-status determinations (Article 84) follow the **examination procedure** (Article 90(3)); urgent health/safety/environment cases can trigger immediately applicable acts under Article 90(4).

## The transitional regime and repeal of Regulation (EU) No 305/2011

Article 94 repeals Regulation (EU) No 305/2011 with effect from **8 January 2026**, except for a defined subset - Article 2, Articles 4 to 9, Articles 11 to 18, Articles 27 and 28, Articles 36 to 40, Articles 47 to 49, Articles 52 and 53, Article 55, Articles 60 to 64, and Annexes III and V - which stay in force until **8 January 2040**. Article 95 sets out the detailed bridge:

- **Product contact points, TABs and notified bodies** designated under the old CPR are deemed designated under the new CPR, but TABs and notified bodies must be re-assessed and re-designated by their national authorities under the new rules by **8 January 2030** at the latest, following their normal periodic re-assessment cycle.
- **Harmonised standards** referenced under the old CPR's Article 17(5) list and still valid on 8 January 2026 remain usable under the old CPR until formally withdrawn or repealed by the Commission, and are treated as performance harmonised standards for the purposes of the new CPR's Article 5(7), Article 6(1) and Article 31(2).
- **European assessment documents (EADs)** referenced under the old CPR's Article 22 list by 8 January 2026 remain valid until **9 January 2031** (unless they expire earlier for other reasons); products can no longer be placed on the market on the basis of European technical assessments issued under those EADs after **9 January 2036**.
- **Certificates, test reports and ETAs** issued under the old CPR may be used as the technical basis for demonstrating compliance with the new CPR where the product type corresponds and the underlying requirements/methods remain valid, subject to the Article 62 mutual-recognition conditions applied mutatis mutandis.
- **Phase-in by product family:** the Chapter I-III obligations of economic operators apply to a given product family or category only from **one year after** the date the relevant implementing act (Article 5(8) or Article 6(1)) is adopted, unless a later date is specified - though economic operators may opt to apply the new rules earlier by voluntarily completing the full DoPC procedure. Within one year of that application date, the Commission must withdraw the corresponding old-CPR harmonised standard/EAD references from the Official Journal.

## Glossary

- **Construction product** - any formed or formless physical item, including 3D-printed products, or a kit, placed on the market for permanent incorporation into construction works (Article 3(1)).
- **Kit** - a product placed on the market by a single economic operator as a set of at least two separate items intended for joint incorporation into construction works (Article 3(17)).
- **Key part** - a component or spare part specified by a harmonised technical specification as essential to a product's characterisation, safety or performance, subject to its own CE marking obligation (Article 3(16), Article 17(2)).
- **Harmonised technical specification** - the umbrella term for performance harmonised standards made mandatory under Article 5(8), fall-back implementing acts under Article 6(1), and related delegated acts under Articles 7(1), 9(3) and 10(2) (Article 3(42)).
- **Essential characteristics** - characteristics of a product relating to the Annex I basic requirements for construction works, plus the Annex II predetermined environmental essential characteristics (Article 3(7)).
- **Product requirement** - a binding characteristic, set out in Annex III, that a product must have before it can be placed on the market, distinct from a declared essential characteristic (Article 3(8), Article 7).
- **Declaration of performance and conformity (DoPC)** - the manufacturer's mandatory statement of a product's performance against its essential characteristics and its compliance with applicable product requirements, drawn up before placing the product on the market (Article 13, Article 15, Annex V).
- **Assessment and verification system** - one of the Annex IX numbered systems (for example System 1+, System 3) that fixes which combination of manufacturer self-assessment and notified-body third-party tasks applies to a given essential characteristic or product requirement (Article 10).
- **European assessment document (EAD) / European technical assessment (ETA)** - the parallel route to CE marking for products not (yet) covered by harmonised standards: an EAD is the reference document developed by a technical assessment body, and an ETA is the individual assessment issued against it for a specific manufacturer's product (Article 3(18)-(19), Chapter IV).
- **Technical assessment body (TAB)** - a body designated by a Member State to issue European technical assessments on the basis of European assessment documents (Article 3(57), Chapter V).
- **Notified body** - a conformity assessment body authorised and notified to carry out third-party assessment, verification and validation tasks under the Regulation (Article 3(55), Chapter VI).
- **Harmonised zone** - the area of construction-product regulation occupied by harmonised technical specifications, within which Member States cannot impose additional national requirements, markings or assessments (Article 11).
- **Construction digital product passport** - the product-level digital record, accessible via a data carrier, that bundles the DoPC, product information, technical documentation and labelling for a construction product, hosted in the construction digital product passport system set up under Article 75 (Articles 75-76).
