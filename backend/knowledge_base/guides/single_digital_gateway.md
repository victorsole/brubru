# Single Digital Gateway (SDG) and Your Europe

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 2 October 2018, establishing a single digital gateway to provide access to information, to procedures and to assistance and problem-solving services (CELEX 32018R1724).
- The Single Digital Gateway (SDG) is the legal umbrella; **Your Europe** (`youreurope.europa.eu`, live since 2006) is the citizen- and business-facing portal that implements it.
- Obliges Member States to give full online access to: (1) information on EU and national rights, rules and obligations; (2) 21 key cross-border administrative procedures; (3) assistance and problem-solving services.
- 21 procedures deadline: 12 December 2023. They cover life events such as birth, residence, studying, working, moving, buying/registering a vehicle, starting and running a business, and retiring (Annex II of the Regulation).
- Once-Only Technical System (OOTS): live since 12 December 2023. Lets a public authority in one Member State request official evidence (certificates, diplomas, company extracts) directly from the issuing authority in another Member State, so users never resubmit documents already held by an administration. Around 80,000 competent authorities across the EU are in scope for onboarding as evidence providers, evidence requesters or intermediary platforms.
- "Once-only principle": the underlying policy concept that public administrations should not ask citizens or businesses for information they already have.
- Assistance services routed through Your Europe: SOLVIT (informal cross-border dispute resolution with public authorities), Your Europe Advice, European Consumer Centres, the Enterprise Europe Network, and an "assistance service finder" tool.
- Governance: an SDG Coordination Group, chaired by the European Commission and made up of national coordinators, steers implementation; an SDG dashboard tracks each Member State's compliance procedure by procedure.
- Estimated benefit: the Commission projects businesses save more than EUR 11 billion per year once the gateway and once-only exchange are fully operational.
- Policy owner: DG GROW (Directorate-General for Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs), which manages both the SDG policy page and Your Europe. Technical/interoperability backbone (OOTS, eIDAS integration) is built with DG DIGIT (Informatics).
- Non-compliant Member States face standard Treaty infringement procedures for failing to transpose or implement the Regulation's obligations.

## What the Single Digital Gateway Is

The Single Digital Gateway Regulation is an internal market instrument, not a data protection or cybersecurity one: its purpose is to make it as easy to complete a cross-border administrative procedure online as a purely domestic one. It sits alongside, and reinforces, the wider Single Market strategy (see `single_market_programme.md` and `single_market_one_europe_roadmap.md`) by tackling one very concrete category of cross-border friction: paperwork.

The Regulation rests on three pillars:

1. **Information**: every Member State must publish clear, up-to-date, plain-language information on rights, obligations and procedures on its national websites, indexed and made discoverable through Your Europe.
2. **Procedures**: the 21 procedures listed in Annex II must be fully available online, including for users based in another Member State, with no requirement to travel or submit paper documents in person.
3. **Assistance and problem-solving**: users who hit a wall (a national authority refusing to recognise an EU right, for example) can escalate to SOLVIT or Your Europe Advice rather than litigate.

## Your Europe Portal

Your Europe (`youreurope.europa.eu`) predates the SDG Regulation (it has operated since 2006) but the 2018 Regulation turned it into the Regulation's legal front door. It is organised into two main tracks:

- **Citizens**: travel, residence, work, healthcare, education, family, pensions, vehicles, consumer rights.
- **Businesses**: starting and running a company across borders, tax, employing staff, product requirements, finance and funding (including a searchable database of over 1,000 EU-linked financial institutions offering loans, microfinance and venture capital).

National websites that meet the gateway's quality criteria display the Your Europe logo, signalling to users that the page is part of the EU-wide network. The portal is available in the 24 official EU languages. It links out to national single points of contact rather than duplicating national administrations' own e-government systems.

## The 21 Procedures (Annex II)

Annex II of Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 lists 21 procedures, grouped around common life events, that Member States had to make available fully online, including for non-resident EU users, by 12 December 2023 (where the underlying national procedure exists at all). The clusters are:

- **Birth and civil status**: requesting proof of registration of birth.
- **Residence**: registering a change of residence/address.
- **Education**: applying for study grants or loans from a public institution; requesting recognition of academic diplomas, certificates or other proof of studies; enrolling in higher education.
- **Employment**: registering for social security as an employed or self-employed person; requesting a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC); notifying a change of employer or employment status for social security purposes.
- **Vehicles**: registering a vehicle originating from, or already registered in, another Member State; obtaining vehicle registration documents.
- **Business**: starting, running and closing a business (company registration, notification of business activities, VAT registration); submitting a company's initial application for a permit to provide cross-border services; declaring corporate income tax.
- **Retirement and pensions**: claiming and requesting information on pension and pre-retirement benefits from public schemes.

The Commission publishes a detailed explanatory "Scope of Annex II" guidance document that defines each procedure precisely, since national administrative structures differ. The authoritative source for the exact legal wording of all 21 items is Annex II of the Regulation itself, cross-checked against the SDG dashboard's procedure-by-procedure compliance tracker.

## Once-Only Technical System (OOTS)

The OOTS is the technical infrastructure that operationalises the once-only principle. Since 12 December 2023, it allows:

- A **requesting authority** (handling one of the Annex II procedures) to ask, with the user's explicit consent, for evidence held by another Member State.
- An **evidence provider** (the authority that issued the original document, e.g. a civil registry or a university) to transmit that evidence electronically, without the user acting as an intermediary carrying paper certificates.
- **Intermediary platforms** in some Member States to relay requests where the requesting and providing authorities are not directly connected.

OOTS is built on the existing eIDAS framework for cross-border identification and authentication, and on European Interoperability Framework standards. Rollout is uneven: Member States plug in evidence types and authorities incrementally, so the SDG dashboard is the only reliable way to check whether a specific procedure in a specific country is currently exchanging evidence automatically, or still requires a user to upload a certified copy.

## Assistance and Problem-Solving Services

Your Europe aggregates several pre-existing assistance mechanisms rather than replacing them:

- **SOLVIT**: free, informal dispute resolution when a public authority in another Member State misapplies EU internal market law (target: resolution within 12 weeks).
- **Your Europe Advice**: free personalised legal information on EU cross-border rights, answered by a network of lawyers.
- **European Consumer Centres Network**: cross-border consumer complaints and disputes.
- **Enterprise Europe Network**: business-specific support for cross-border trade, funding and partnerships.
- An **assistance service finder** helps a user identify which of the above (or which national equivalent) matches their problem.

## Governance and Monitoring

- **SDG Coordination Group**: chaired by the Commission, brings together national coordinators appointed by each Member State to steer implementation, agree technical specifications and share best practice.
- **SDG dashboard**: the Commission's public compliance tracker, showing, procedure by procedure and country by country, whether a service is available online, cross-border accessible, and connected to OOTS.
- **Infringement risk**: because the Regulation sets binding, dated obligations (December 2023 for the 21 procedures and OOTS), Member States that have not implemented specific procedures remain exposed to standard Article 258 TFEU infringement action from the Commission, in addition to the reputational cost of a red/amber rating on the dashboard.

## Why It Matters for Advocacy and Compliance

For SMEs and cross-border service providers, the SDG determines whether registering a subsidiary, hiring staff, or getting a qualification recognised in another Member State can be done end-to-end online or still requires a physical visit or notarised paperwork. For public-affairs professionals tracking the Single Market agenda, SDG compliance gaps are a recurring theme in the Commission's Single Market Barriers work (see `terrible_ten_single_market_barriers.md`) and in DG GROW's own databases (see `dg_grow_databases.md`). SDG/OOTS also intersects with product and consumer information obligations under EU product safety and consumer law (see `eu_product_safety_consumer.md`), and its underlying open-data and interoperability logic overlaps with the `data_europa_eu_open_data_portal.md` ecosystem.
