## Legal architecture

- **Underlying agreement**: Cooperation Agreement between the European Economic Community and the Syrian Arab Republic, signed 1977 (Title I trade preferences + Title II economic cooperation + financial protocol). Never fully replaced by an Association Agreement (the proposed 2008 EU-Syria Association Agreement was never finalised due to the 2011 uprising).
- **Suspension instrument (repealed)**: **Decision 2011/523/EU** of 2 September 2011, partially suspending application of the Cooperation Agreement in response to the violent repression of the civilian population by the Assad regime. The suspension covered trade preferences + European Investment Bank operations + technical cooperation.
- **Re-activation instrument (NEW)**: **Council Decision (EU) 2026/1087** of 11 May 2026 — repeals Decision 2011/523/EU in full. The Cooperation Agreement is therefore back in force in its 1977-vintage form (Title I trade preferences + Title II economic cooperation pathways).
- **Sanctions interaction**: The repeal of 2011/523 does NOT lift the targeted EU sanctions on individuals + entities linked to the Assad regime (those sit under separate CFSP decisions, e.g. Decision 2013/255/CFSP). The repeal is an **economic-relations instrument**, not a sanctions instrument.

## Political context

The repeal of Decision 2011/523/EU is the EU's formal recognition of the **post-Assad transition** (Assad regime fell December 2024; transitional authorities under interim government since January 2025). The EU is positioning ahead of:

1. **Reconstruction financing window**: World Bank estimates €250-300bn over 10 years; EU positions as convening platform (EIB + EBRD + Member State bilaterals + Gulf states + UN).
2. **Refugee return policy alignment**: 6 million Syrians displaced internally + ~5 million as refugees (mostly Türkiye, Lebanon, Jordan, Iraq, Germany, EU-27). Voluntary-return frameworks under negotiation.
3. **EU enlargement narrative parallel**: Syria reconstruction sits alongside Ukraine reconstruction as the two largest EU-coordinated reconstruction agendas of the 2024-2029 mandate.

## What to watch (next 12 months)

1. **Trade-preferences re-activation timetable** — Title I of the 1977 Cooperation Agreement is back in force, but practical implementation requires DG TAXUD + DG TRADE alignment on rules-of-origin + customs cooperation.
2. **EIB operations resumption** — pre-2011 EIB engagement with Syria was under the Euro-Mediterranean Partnership Facility; re-engagement requires Board decision.
3. **Refugee return framework** — formal EU-Syria readmission agreement is the next legal step; politically sensitive given remaining sanctions on transitional-authority members.
4. **Reconstruction conference** — likely Q3-Q4 2026 in Brussels or a Member State capital; modelled on the Brussels-IV-on-Syria humanitarian pledging conference format.
5. **CFSP-track sanctions review** — separate from the Cooperation Agreement repeal; targeted sanctions on Assad-era individuals expected to be reviewed annually.

## Brubru user questions addressed

- "Did the EU just unfreeze its relations with Syria?" → Yes, the Council repealed Decision 2011/523/EU on 11 May 2026, ending a 15-year partial suspension of the 1977 EU-Syria Cooperation Agreement. Targeted CFSP sanctions on Assad-era individuals remain in force.
- "What does this change for EU companies wanting to trade with Syria?" → Title I trade preferences of the 1977 Agreement are back in force, but implementing rules-of-origin + customs cooperation are still being aligned. EU companies should consult DG TRADE before assuming preferential access.
- "Is the EU funding Syria's reconstruction now?" → The 11 May decision is a legal-relations instrument, not a financing decision. Reconstruction financing is being convened via Commission + EIB + Member States + Gulf states — no committed EU funding figure as of 12 May 2026.
- "What about the sanctions?" → CFSP sanctions on individuals + entities linked to the Assad regime remain in force under Decision 2013/255/CFSP. Those are reviewed annually and sit on a separate legal track from the Cooperation Agreement.
