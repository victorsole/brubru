## Overview

The EU's framework for combating child sexual abuse material (CSAM) online consists of three layers:

1. **Criminal law** -- Directive 2011/93/EU establishes minimum rules on offences (CSAM production, distribution, possession, grooming) and penalties across Member States.

2. **Voluntary detection (temporary)** -- Regulation (EU) 2021/1232 temporarily derogates from the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC) to allow providers of number-independent interpersonal communications services (NI-ICS) to voluntarily scan for CSAM. Originally expired 3 August 2024, extended to 3 April 2026 by Regulation (EU) 2024/1307. Now expired; EP has adopted a position extending to 3 August 2027 with conditions (awaiting Council).

3. **Permanent regulation (proposed)** -- COM(2022) 209 proposes mandatory detection, reporting, and removal obligations for hosting services and interpersonal communications providers. Currently in trilogue negotiations between Parliament and Council.

## The Problem: Scale and Trends

- In 2024, NCMEC's CyberTipline received over 20.5 million reports of suspected online CSAM
- AI-generated CSAM reports surged from 4,700 (2023) to 67,000 (2024), a 1,325% increase
- 70% of all known CSAM in 2024 was traced to a European country (IWF data)
- Self-generated content is rising among younger age groups; most victims who produced self-generated content were aged 3-13 (INHOPE)
- 65% increase in CSAM among children aged 7-10 between 2022 and 2023
- In 2022, 68% of CyberTipline reports came from chats, messaging, or email services within the EU; 22% from social media or online gaming platforms
- Sextortion (financial) is a growing threat: fake accounts approach minors, coerce explicit content, then demand payment (FBI global warning, early 2023)
- Operation Cumberland (2025): 19 countries, Europol support, first major cases involving AI-generated CSAM

## Temporary Derogation: Current Status (Critical)

### Regulation (EU) 2021/1232 (as extended by 2024/1307)

### Key EP Amendments to the Extension (11 March 2026)

The Parliament adopted significant conditions on the extension:

1. **No solicitation detection**: Removed grooming detection from the derogation scope; limited to "known online child sexual abuse material" only
2. **Content data only**: Removed traffic data from processing scope; providers may only process content data
3. **Targeted detection**: Detection must be "targeted, specified and limited to individual users" identified through "reasonable grounds of suspicion" by a "competent judicial authority" (Amendment 5, Greens, adopted 235-203-174)
4. **No E2E encryption scanning**: Measures must not apply to end-to-end encrypted communications
5. **Shorter extension**: Changed end date from 3 April 2028 to 3 August 2027
6. **User/trusted flagger reports only**: Detection limited to cases where the provider has received a concrete report from users, trusted flaggers, or organisations

## Permanent Regulation: COM(2022) 209

### Commission Proposal Structure

The proposed regulation would:

1. **Mandatory risk assessments**: All providers must assess the risk of misuse of their services for CSAM dissemination (similar to DSA risk assessments)
2. **Risk mitigation**: Providers must implement mitigation measures including age verification, safety-by-design for children, and content moderation for CSAM
3. **Detection orders**: National coordinating authorities can request judicial/administrative authorities to issue detection orders requiring providers to deploy automated detection technologies for known CSAM, new CSAM, and grooming
4. **Reporting**: Providers must report detected CSAM to the EU Centre on Child Sexual Abuse (EUCSA) and to national law enforcement
5. **Removal orders**: Competent authorities can order removal within 24 hours
6. **Blocking orders**: For CSAM that cannot be removed at source, blocking access
7. **EU Centre on Child Sexual Abuse (EUCSA)**: New decentralised agency to coordinate implementation, maintain hash/indicator databases, receive reports, forward to Europol and national authorities

### Detection Order Mechanism (Commission Proposal)

- National coordinating authority (CA) reviews provider's risk assessment
- If significant risk of misuse persists despite mitigation, CA requests judicial/administrative authority to issue detection order
- Provider must deploy automated content-recognition technologies
- Provider chooses technology but must use the least privacy-intrusive option
- Detection orders: max 2 years for CSAM, max 1 year for grooming
- Provider given at least 3 months and max 1 year to start detection
- Provider reports detected CSAM to EUCSA
- EUCSA forwards to law enforcement and verifies provider has removed content

### Parliament Position (LIBE Report, November 2023)

Key differences from the Commission proposal:

| Issue | Commission | Parliament |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Scope | Hosting services + interpersonal communications | Extended to search engines and AI systems |
| E2E encryption | Not explicitly addressed | Encrypted communications excluded from scanning; limited to metadata analysis |
| Grooming detection | Mandatory via detection orders | Restricted approach; technology not mature enough |
| EUCSA seat | To be decided | Aligned with CJEU case law on agency location |
| Victims' rights | Basic provisions | Victims' Rights and Survivors' Consultative Forum at EUCSA |
| Prevention | Limited provisions | Reinforced: safety-by-design for children, age assurance, enhanced parental controls |
| Voluntary detection | Would terminate | Preserved alongside mandatory regime |
| Online games | Not specifically addressed | Included in scope |

### Council Position (November 2025)

The Council achieved its common position on 26 November 2025 under the Danish Presidency, after failed attempts under Belgian, Hungarian, and Polish presidencies. The Council position is generally closer to the Commission proposal but with some modifications.

## EDPS and EDPB Positions

### EDPB-EDPS Joint Opinion (2022)

- Serious concerns about proportionality and interference with communication confidentiality
- Risk mitigation measures do not meet legal certainty and foreseeability criteria
- Proposal risks generalised and indiscriminate scanning
- Technology for detecting new CSAM and grooming not mature enough
- Interference with Articles 7 and 8 of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights

### EDPS Opinion 7/2026 (on the extension)

- Extension must address shortcomings and prevent indiscriminate scanning
- Processing must be targeted, not general
- Any solution detecting illegal content must be carefully considered against privacy rights
- "Temporary measures must not bypass fundamental rights" (Supervisor Wiewiórowski)
- Consistent emphasis on targeted rather than blanket approaches

## Commission Guidelines on Protection of Minors (DSA Art. 28)

Published 14 July 2025, these guidelines implement DSA Article 28(1) requiring platforms to ensure "a high level of privacy, safety and security for minors online."

Key requirements for platforms:
- Set minor accounts to private by default
- Enable blocking, muting, and explicit consent for group additions
- Prohibit screenshot/download features for minor-created content
- Modify recommender systems to reduce harmful content exposure
- Disable addictive features (streaks, autoplay, push notifications) by default for minors
- Remove persuasive design targeting engagement
- Prevent exploitation of limited commercial literacy
- Restrict manipulative practices (virtual currencies, loot boxes)
- Implement age assurance methods for adult content access
- Improve reporting mechanisms with prompt feedback
- Establish minimum parental control standards

Note: Following these guidelines remains voluntary and does not guarantee DSA compliance, but the Commission will reference them during enforcement assessments.

## Special Panel on Child Safety Online

On 5 March 2026, the Commission held the first meeting of the Special Panel on Child Safety Online, hosted by President von der Leyen. The Panel provides expert recommendations on:

## Detection Technologies

### Known CSAM
- Hash-based detection (PhotoDNA, Microsoft): extracts digital fingerprint, compares to database of known hashes
- Very high accuracy rate; minimal false positives
- No human oversight needed as illegality already verified
- Does not access message content, only compares hash values

### New/Unknown CSAM
- AI-based image classification
- Higher false positive rate
- Requires human oversight for verification
- Technology maturity questioned by EPRS complementary impact assessment

### Grooming Detection
- Text-based pattern detection (natural language processing)
- Microsoft reports 88% accuracy rate for one tool
- Significant privacy concerns: requires accessing message content
- Cannot reliably differentiate consensual adolescent conversations from grooming

## Impact on Platforms

### Hosting Services
- Must conduct CSAM risk assessments (similar to DSA systemic risk)
- Must implement mitigation measures
- Subject to detection orders from national CAs
- Must report CSAM to EUCSA within defined timeframe
- Must remove CSAM within 24 hours of removal order

### Interpersonal Communications Services (e.g. Discord, WhatsApp, Telegram)
- Same obligations as hosting services
- Additional controversy: scanning private communications
- E2E encryption creates fundamental technical and legal tension
- Parliament position: encrypted communications excluded from content scanning
- App stores must implement age verification to prevent children downloading high-risk apps

### Penalties
- Member States to set penalties
- Must be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive
- Expected to align with DSA-level fines (up to 6% global annual turnover)
