# Fusion for Energy (F4E), ITER and Europe's industrial fusion programme

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Fusion for Energy (F4E) -- the European Joint Undertaking for ITER and the Development of Fusion Energy
- Legal basis: Council Decision 2007/198/Euratom (27 March 2007), adopted under the Euratom Treaty (1957), not the TFEU
- Mandate: 35 years from establishment (2007 to 2042)
- Headquarters: Barcelona, Spain
- Other F4E-linked sites: Cadarache / Saint-Paul-lez-Durance (France, co-located with the ITER Organization); Garching (Germany, co-located with EUROfusion/IPP); Escuzar, Granada (Spain, IFMIF-DONES construction site)
- Role: F4E is the European Domestic Agency for ITER -- it procures, manufactures and delivers the EU/Euratom in-kind contribution to the project
- ITER share: the EU/Euratom contributes roughly 45% of ITER's costs, the largest single share among the 7 parties (the other 6 -- China, India, Japan, Korea, Russia, USA -- each contribute in-kind shares of broadly comparable, smaller size). Figures vary by source depending on whether host-country benefits are counted; verify the live percentage on fusionforenergy.europa.eu before quoting a precise number.
- ITER site: Cadarache, southern France (host country France, EU/Euratom party)
- ITER new baseline (agreed by the ITER Council, November 2024): Research Operation start 2034, Full magnetic energy 2036, start of Deuterium-Tritium Operation 2039. This replaces the 2016 baseline and represents roughly a 4-year slip on the D-T milestone, plus a significant cost increase. **Hedge hard**: ITER schedules have slipped repeatedly since the project's 2006 founding; treat any date beyond the next ITER Council meeting as provisional.
- Broader Approach with Japan: JT-60SA satellite tokamak (Naka, Japan) achieved first plasma 23 October 2023, the world's largest operating superconducting tokamak; undergoing upgrades toward its first full experimental campaign expected in 2026. IFMIF-DONES (International Fusion Materials Irradiation Facility -- DEMO Oriented Neutron Source), a materials-irradiation facility for post-ITER reactor materials, is under construction at Escuzar near Granada, Spain (construction phase started 2023).
- DEMO: the planned post-ITER demonstration fusion power plant (target ~500 MW continuous), still at design stage, coordinated at EU level through the EUROfusion consortium under the Euratom Research and Training Programme.
- EUROfusion: the EU consortium (member organisations across EU Member States plus UK, Switzerland and Ukraine as associated countries) that runs Europe's coordinated fusion research programme, distinct from F4E, which is the ITER procurement/construction body.
- Fusion investment (19 March 2026, per SMR Strategy guide): EU announced EUR 330 million to accelerate fusion energy and support nuclear technologies/skills under the Euratom research programme.
- Status: F4E is, by construction spend, the most expensive body operating under the Euratom Treaty.

## Overview

Fusion for Energy (F4E) is the EU body responsible for delivering Europe's contribution to ITER, the International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor under construction at Cadarache in southern France, and for coordinating Europe's side of the EU-Japan Broader Approach fusion agreement. F4E was established in 2007 by Council Decision 2007/198/Euratom under the Euratom Treaty, the 1957 treaty that sits alongside (but legally separate from) the TFEU and governs the EU's nuclear competences. Unlike most EU agencies and joint undertakings, which report primarily through Horizon Europe or DG structures, F4E's founding legal base is Euratom, and its 35-year mandate runs to roughly 2042 -- a horizon set deliberately long because ITER construction and operation stretch across decades.

F4E is headquartered in Barcelona, with staff also based at Cadarache (alongside the ITER Organization itself) and Garching, Germany (alongside EUROfusion and the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics). Its core function is that of "European Domestic Agency": each of the seven ITER parties (China, the EU/Euratom, India, Japan, Korea, Russia and the United States) delivers its contribution not as cash into a common pot but as manufactured components, buildings and in-kind services, procured and managed by that party's own domestic agency. F4E is the EU's domestic agency, and because the EU/Euratom holds host-party status and the largest procurement share (commonly cited as around 45% of total project value, though the precise figure depends on accounting convention), F4E is comfortably the largest and most expensive of the seven domestic agencies, and the single most expensive body established under the Euratom Treaty.

## Why this matters for EU industrial policy

ITER is frequently covered in the press as a scientific mega-project, but for EU policy professionals the more relevant lens is industrial: F4E channels billions of euros of EU/Euratom procurement into a dense supply chain of European engineering, superconducting-magnet, vacuum, and precision-manufacturing firms across France, Germany, Italy, Spain and beyond, and its work sits alongside (and increasingly overlaps with) the EU's separate push on Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) and broader nuclear/Euratom industrial strategy (see `smr_strategy_nuclear.md`). Fusion is also the subject of a fast-growing private-sector investment wave (Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Tokamak Energy, and others), and Brubru users researching "fusion energy" frequently mean the private-sector story; this guide gives the distinct EU institutional and industrial-policy reading -- public, treaty-based, procurement-driven -- as opposed to venture-backed private fusion.

## ITER schedule: the 2024 rebaseline

At the 35th ITER Council meeting (November 2024), the ITER Organization presented, and the Council accepted, a new project baseline replacing the reference schedule that had been in place since 2016. Headline milestones under the new baseline:

| Milestone | New baseline date |
|---|---|
| Start of Research Operation | 2034 |
| Full magnetic energy | 2036 |
| Start of Deuterium-Tritium Operation | 2039 |

ITER's Director-General attributed the rebaseline to a combination of COVID-19 disruption, quality issues found during manufacturing and construction, and overly optimistic planning for a genuinely first-of-a-kind engineering project. The new baseline also changed technical choices (for example, tungsten rather than beryllium for the first wall) and increased planned plasma-heating capacity relative to the 2016 design. Commentary at the time (including trade press) described the associated cost increase as running into billions of euros; **do not cite a specific cost figure without verifying it against the current ITER Organization or F4E published position**, since cost estimates have been revised multiple times since the project's 2006 founding and are politically sensitive across all seven parties.

## Broader Approach with Japan

Alongside ITER, the EU/Euratom and Japan run the "Broader Approach" Agreement, a parallel fusion R&D programme intended to complement ITER and prepare for DEMO. F4E coordinates the European side. Two flagship facilities:

- **JT-60SA**: a superconducting tokamak at Naka, Japan, jointly built and operated by Japan (QST) and Europe. It achieved first plasma on 23 October 2023, making it briefly the world's largest operating superconducting tokamak. It has since undergone upgrades and enhancement work, with a first full experimental campaign expected from 2026 (verify live, as commissioning campaigns for first-of-a-kind machines routinely slip).
- **IFMIF-DONES** (International Fusion Materials Irradiation Facility -- DEMO Oriented Neutron Source): a materials-irradiation facility under construction at Escuzar, near Granada, Spain, since the construction phase formally began in 2023. It will generate a high-flux neutron source to test how candidate structural materials behave under the neutron bombardment expected inside a DEMO-class fusion reactor -- a capability ITER itself cannot fully provide.

## DEMO and the post-ITER pathway

DEMO (DEMOnstration Power Plant) is the planned successor to ITER: a reactor intended to demonstrate net electricity generation from fusion, targeted at continuous output in the hundreds-of-megawatts range. DEMO remains at the design stage; European DEMO design work is coordinated by the EUROfusion consortium (not F4E directly) under the Euratom Research and Training Programme, drawing on data expected from ITER operation and from IFMIF-DONES materials testing. There is no confirmed construction start date for DEMO; any date circulating in press coverage should be treated as indicative only and verified against EUROfusion's own published roadmap.

## Institutional landscape

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| Fusion for Energy (F4E) | EU/Euratom Domestic Agency for ITER; procures and delivers the EU's in-kind ITER contribution; coordinates EU side of the Broader Approach |
| ITER Organization | International body based in Cadarache, France, that builds and will operate ITER itself, drawing on in-kind contributions from all 7 domestic agencies |
| EUROfusion | EU consortium coordinating Europe's fusion research programme (device operation, materials science, DEMO design) under the Euratom Research and Training Programme |
| DG RTD | Commission Directorate-General with policy oversight of the Euratom Research and Training Programme |
| DG ENER | Commission DG with broader nuclear/Euratom energy-policy oversight (see `smr_strategy_nuclear.md`) |
| QST (Japan) | Japan's domestic agency for ITER and Broader Approach counterpart to F4E |
| Euratom Supply Agency (ESA) | Separate Euratom-Treaty body on nuclear fuel supply security (not directly involved in fusion procurement) |

## Funding

F4E's multiannual budget is set through EU/Euratom budgetary procedures tied to the Multiannual Financial Framework, and running costs of ITER construction over the current MFF period run into the billions of euros; **do not state a precise multiannual F4E budget figure without verifying it live** against F4E's own published budget documents or the relevant MFF/Euratom programme regulation, since these figures are periodically revised alongside ITER rebaselining exercises. Separately, the EU announced (19 March 2026, per the SMR Strategy guide) EUR 330 million to accelerate fusion energy and support nuclear technologies and skills through the Euratom research programme -- a figure that sits alongside, not instead of, F4E's ITER-specific procurement budget.
