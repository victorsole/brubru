# STEP: Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal basis**: Regulation (EU) 2024/795 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 February 2024 establishing the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (STEP) and amending several other regulations (**CELEX 32024R0795**). Proposed by the Commission on 20 June 2023 (COM(2023)335) as a fast-track response to the US Inflation Reduction Act and China's industrial subsidies; agreed under an accelerated ordinary legislative procedure; entered into force in March 2024.
- **Three eligible technology domains**: (1) digital technologies and deep tech (AI, semiconductors and microelectronics, quantum, cloud/edge computing, advanced connectivity/6G, robotics); (2) clean and resource-efficient technologies (net-zero and renewable tech, energy storage and grids, sustainable mobility, resource and material efficiency); (3) biotechnologies (biomanufacturing, medical biotech, industrial and agri-biotech).
- **11 existing programmes topped up or amended** by the founding Regulation: InvestEU (Reg (EU) 2021/523), Horizon Europe (Reg (EU) 2021/695), the European Defence Fund (Reg (EU) 2021/697 -- **plus EUR 1.5 billion in genuinely new money**), the Innovation Fund (under the ETS Directive 2003/87/EC), the European Regional Development Fund (Reg (EU) 2021/1058), the Just Transition Fund (Reg (EU) 2021/1056), the European Social Fund Plus (Reg (EU) 2021/1057), the Common Provisions Regulation 2021/1060 (plus legacy Regs 1303/2013 and 223/2014 for closing out 2014-2020 cohesion programmes), and the Recovery and Resilience Facility (Reg (EU) 2021/241). EU4Health and the Digital Europe Programme also carry STEP-relevant calls in practice (via work programmes and STEP Seal evaluation), even though the founding Regulation's amending articles centre on the list above.
- **Sovereignty Seal (official name: "STEP Seal")**: an EU quality label automatically awarded to STEP-relevant project proposals that clear every evaluation threshold (eligibility, exclusion, award) under five EU funding programmes -- the Digital Europe Programme, the European Defence Fund, EU4Health, Horizon Europe, and the Innovation Fund. It is widely referred to in press and industry commentary as the "Sovereignty Seal". It differs sharply from the **Seal of Excellence**: the STEP Seal is automatic for qualifying, evaluated-as-excellent proposals under STEP-relevant calls (whether funded or not), while the Seal of Excellence targets high-scoring Research & Innovation proposals that were NOT funded through the initial call and remain on a reserve list. Both seals boost visibility and help attract alternative public/private investment, but only the STEP Seal is explicitly tied to the Union's strategic-autonomy objectives. STEP Seal validity runs for the life of the project and lapses if the project relocates outside the EU or fails to start within five years of the award.
- **STEP Portal**: `strategic-technologies.europa.eu` -- the Commission's public showcase of STEP Seal-labelled projects and open STEP-relevant calls (example live call: a EUR 100 million EIC STEP Defence Scale Up call). Offers dedicated entry points for project promoters, managing authorities (national/regional bodies implementing cohesion funds) and investors, with cross-links to the InvestEU Portal and Dealroom.
- **Total mobilisation**: the Commission's 2023 proposal targeted **up to EUR 160 billion** in new investment by 2030, of which only around **EUR 10 billion** is genuinely new EU money; the rest comes from reprioritisation within existing envelopes and leverage (e.g. a roughly 10x multiplier turning EUR 7.5 billion of the InvestEU guarantee into an estimated EUR 75 billion of total investment). Actual progress reported by the Commission: **over EUR 15 billion** mobilised in STEP's first year, rising to **EUR 29 billion** by STEP's second anniversary (30 March 2026) -- split roughly EUR 14 billion from Commission-managed programmes (Innovation Fund, Digital Europe Programme, European Defence Fund, EU4Health, Horizon Europe) and EUR 15 billion from cohesion policy funds deployed by 20 Member States and their regions.
- **Precursor to the European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)**: STEP is explicitly framed by the Commission as the pilot/proof-of-concept for the ECF proposed for the **MFF 2028-2034** (COM(2025)555, 16 July 2025), which would consolidate 14 existing funding instruments into a single framework. The ECF is paired with a proposed **Competitiveness Coordination Tool (CCT)** to align EU and national investment in the same strategic technologies STEP already targets. See `fp10_ecf_competitiveness.md` (existing Brubru guide covering the ECF/CCT in detail) and `mff_2028_2034.md` for the wider budget negotiation.

## What STEP is and why it exists
The Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (STEP) is not a new spending programme with its own budget line. It is a **coordination and top-up mechanism** layered onto 11 existing EU funding instruments, designed to redirect unspent or reprioritised money -- plus a modest amount of genuinely new funding -- towards technologies the Commission judges critical to European strategic autonomy and competitiveness.

STEP was proposed on 20 June 2023, explicitly as the EU's answer to two external shocks: the United States' Inflation Reduction Act (which offered large tax credits for clean-tech and semiconductor investment on US soil) and China's state-backed industrial subsidies in batteries, solar and critical raw materials. Rather than create a brand-new fund from scratch (which would have required protracted MFF negotiation), the Commission chose a faster route: amend the rules of programmes already running under the 2021-2027 MFF so they could earmark and fast-track money for the same critical technologies. The founding Regulation (EU) 2024/795 was agreed and entered into force within roughly nine months of the original proposal, an unusually fast timeline for EU co-legislation, reflecting the political urgency attached to the file.

STEP sits alongside, and is often read together with, other 2023-2024 sovereignty-industrial instruments: the EU Chips Act (semiconductors, see `eu_chips_act.md`), the Net-Zero Industry Act and the Critical Raw Materials Act. STEP is broader in scope than any single sectoral act because it spans three whole technology domains rather than one product category.

## The three technology domains
1. **Digital technologies and deep tech**: artificial intelligence, microelectronics and semiconductors, quantum technologies, advanced connectivity (5G/6G), cloud and edge computing infrastructure, cybersecurity, and robotics/advanced manufacturing.
2. **Clean and resource-efficient technologies**: net-zero and renewable energy technologies, energy storage and smart grids, sustainable and low-carbon mobility, and technologies that improve resource and material efficiency (aligned with the EU's climate and Net-Zero Industry Act objectives).
3. **Biotechnologies**: biomanufacturing, medical biotechnology (including gene and cell therapies), industrial biotech, and agricultural biotech.

A project only needs to fall within one of the three domains, and must additionally demonstrate either (a) an innovative, cutting-edge element with significant Single Market economic potential, or (b) a contribution to reducing or preventing the Union's strategic dependencies (on third-country suppliers, inputs or technology).

## The 11 (plus) funding programmes folded in
STEP works by inserting "STEP-relevant" earmarks, calls, and evaluation criteria into programmes that already exist, rather than creating a parallel structure:
- **InvestEU** -- budgetary guarantee redirected to leverage private capital into STEP domains (the single largest multiplier in the EUR 160 billion figure).
- **Horizon Europe** -- STEP-relevant calls under Clusters and the European Innovation Council.
- **European Defence Fund (EDF)** -- the only programme to receive genuinely new money under the founding Regulation: **EUR 1.5 billion**, on top of its existing envelope.
- **Innovation Fund** (financed by ETS auction revenue, under Directive 2003/87/EC) -- clean-tech STEP calls.
- **European Regional Development Fund (ERDF)**, **Just Transition Fund (JTF)**, **European Social Fund Plus (ESF+)** -- shared-management cohesion funds, amended via the Common Provisions Regulation 2021/1060 (with legacy Regs 1303/2013 and 223/2014 covering the 2014-2020 programme closeout) to let Member States redirect cohesion money towards STEP priorities with lighter selection procedures.
- **Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF)** -- Member States can add a STEP chapter to their national recovery and resilience plans, unlocking additional flexibility (reporting suggests up to EUR 30 billion potentially available this way).
- In practice, **EU4Health** and the **Digital Europe Programme** also run STEP-relevant calls and are among the five programmes under which the STEP Seal is awarded, even though they are not amended by name in the founding Regulation's articles.

## Sovereignty Seal (STEP Seal) vs Seal of Excellence
The STEP Seal (informally the "Sovereignty Seal") is a certification, not a funding decision in itself. To qualify, a project must: (a) have the applicant, or at least one consortium member, established in an EU Member State or an associated programme country; (b) be evaluated under a call for proposals explicitly flagged as "STEP-relevant"; and (c) score above the evaluation thresholds for every relevant criterion (eligibility, exclusion, award) under one of five programmes -- Digital Europe Programme, European Defence Fund, EU4Health, Horizon Europe, Innovation Fund.

The Seal functions as a "passport": once awarded, the European Commission proactively promotes the project to national and regional managing authorities, and the project becomes eligible for streamlined access to cohesion funds (ERDF, JTF, ESF+) and RRF top-ups without having to compete through a fresh full evaluation. It can also be showcased on the STEP Portal to signal credibility to private investors (linked to the InvestEU Portal and Dealroom).

This is fundamentally different from the **Seal of Excellence**, which the Commission has run for years under Research & Innovation calls (chiefly Horizon Europe/EIC): the Seal of Excellence goes to proposals that scored above threshold but were NOT funded because the call was oversubscribed, giving them a "quality stamp" to seek funding elsewhere (regional funds, national schemes, private investors). The STEP Seal, by contrast, is awarded automatically to qualifying STEP-relevant proposals (funded or not) specifically because of their strategic-autonomy relevance. Coordinators of both seal types get a 30-day window to decide whether their project is listed publicly.

## STEP Portal
`strategic-technologies.europa.eu` is the Commission's dedicated public site for STEP. It functions as:
- A directory of **STEP Seal-labelled projects** across the three technology domains, with individual project profiles (examples cited by the Commission include battery production, gene-therapy biotech, and advanced manufacturing projects).
- A live listing of **open STEP-relevant calls** across the five participating programmes (for example, a EUR 100 million EIC STEP Defence Scale Up call).
- Three audience-specific sections: **project promoters** (how to apply, how the Seal helps), **managing authorities** (how to fast-track cohesion/RRF co-funding for Sealed projects), and **investors** (how to source Sealed projects via the InvestEU Portal and Dealroom).

## Relationship to the European Competitiveness Fund (MFF 2028-2034)
The Commission repeatedly frames STEP as the pilot for a much larger successor: the **European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)**, proposed as part of the MFF 2028-2034 package (COM(2025)555, 16 July 2025). Where STEP works by bolting earmarks onto 11 pre-existing programmes, the ECF would consolidate roughly 14 existing instruments into a single fund, alongside Horizon Europe's proposed successor (FP10, EUR 175bn), for a combined competitiveness-technology envelope the Commission has cited at EUR 400bn+ under Heading 2. A proposed **Competitiveness Coordination Tool (CCT)** would extend STEP's coordination logic (aligning EU and national investment in the same priority technologies) across the whole ECF, and STEP's own Seal mechanism is the direct template for the ECF's proposed "Competitiveness Seal". Brubru already covers the ECF/CCT/FP10 negotiation in full in `fp10_ecf_competitiveness.md`, and the wider MFF 2028-2034 budget battle in `mff_2028_2034.md`; this guide is the STEP-specific companion to both.

## Brubru engagement angles
- **De facto sovereignty filter**: any client applying to Horizon Europe, Digital Europe Programme, EDF, EU4Health, the Innovation Fund, or seeking cohesion/RRF co-funding for a digital/clean-tech/biotech project should check whether their call carries a STEP flag -- it changes both the evaluation framing (strategic dependency reduction / cutting-edge Single Market potential) and the post-award funding pathway.
- **Tenderator angle**: STEP-relevant calls across the five seal-eligible programmes are a natural filter/tag inside Tenderator's funding-opportunity view (My EU Bubble Section 1.25 Tender Docs), alongside the existing EIC lens.
- **EU Law Comply / compliance angle**: for defence-adjacent and dual-use technology clients, the EUR 1.5 billion EDF top-up and the STEP Seal's defence eligibility are directly relevant to compliance and funding-eligibility questions.
- **Position Analysis / Predictions angle**: STEP's role as ECF pilot makes it a live proof-point in the MFF 2028-2034 negotiation -- cite STEP's EUR 29 billion two-year mobilisation figure whenever a client asks whether the Commission's EUR 160 billion or ECF EUR 234bn+ ambitions are credible.
- **Chat/knowledge angle**: STEP is frequently confused with the EIC's separate "STEP Scale Up" instrument (see `eic_step_scaleup_process.md`) -- the acronym overlaps but the two are legally and administratively distinct; a chat answer must disambiguate before answering.
