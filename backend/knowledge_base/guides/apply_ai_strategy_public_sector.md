## Three Flagship Actions for Public Administrations

From Strategy Section 2.11 (public sector subsection):

1. **AI Toolbox for Public Administrations**: repository of practical tools/solutions + a pathway ("Public Sector AI and Interoperability Readiness Pathway", PAIR Pathway) to guide public administrations through AI adoption.
2. **Generative AI Adoption**: scalable, replicable GenAI solutions in public administrations via a call for proposals under GenAI4EU (call DIGITAL-2025-AI-08 "Apply AI: GenAI for the public administrations", published 22 July 2025). Includes a technical and policy toolkit for GenAI + agentic AI.
3. **European Interoperability Framework (EIF) revision**: incorporating guidance supporting AI-first policy.

## AI Adoption Framework for Public Administrations (JRC143539)

The JRC proposes three core activities:

### 1. ANCHORING AI (in EU policies, regulations and principles)
- Align AI adoption with AI Act, GDPR, Data Act, Interoperable Europe Act
- Embed people/public-value orientation
- Continuous monitoring of impact and risks

### 2. ADAPTING (capabilities of public administrations)
- **AI literacy** per Article 4 of the AI Act (mandatory for all public administration staff working with AI)
- Role-specific training (strategic planning, procurement, risk management, legal compliance, operational supervision)
- Leverage Digital Competence Framework 3.0 and AI Literacy Framework for Primary and Secondary Schools
- Use EDIHs and the AI skills academy
- AI Board sub-configuration on the AI Innovation Ecosystem is the coordination forum

### 3. APPLYING (AI in high-impact domains creating public value)
JRC identifies specific public-value use cases, including:

| Use case | Description | JRC example |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| AI for digital twins (extreme weather) | Destination Earth leverages ML/deep learning on Earth system data | Norwegian Met Institute + ECMWF, PSTW-2382 |

## Apply AI Strategy Roadmap (2018-2025)

Three phases in EU AI policymaking (per JRC Science for Policy brief, Rodriguez Müller et al., 2026):

1. **Foundational (2018-2020)**: First AI Communication COM(2018) 237; High-Level Expert Group on AI; Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI; White Paper on AI (2020)
2. **Regulatory (2020-2024)**: AI Act negotiation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Coordinated Plan on AI, Data Act, Data Governance Act
3. **Adoption / Apply AI (2025-)**: Competitiveness Compass, AI Continent Action Plan, Apply AI Strategy, GenAI4EU, InvestAI Initiative

## Five Policy Recommendations (JRC143539)

For Member States and public administrations:

### Recommendation 1: Recognise public sector as strategic domain in national AI strategies
- National AI strategies should frame public sector as structured, accountable driver of AI adoption (not just beneficiary)
- Include governance arrangements, dedicated resources, responsibilities across levels
- Continuous monitoring of pace, public value, risk management, AI Act compliance, organisational readiness

### Recommendation 2: Ensure adequate AI literacy (per AI Act Article 4)
- Particular focus on staff in strategic planning, procurement, risk management, legal compliance, operational supervision
- Role-specific training with curated resources
- Peer exchange via Commission's AI literacy practices repository

### Recommendation 3: Engage with EU initiatives, funding instruments, guidance
- Leverage EDIHs, PSTW, EU GovTech catalogue, AI on Demand platform, GenAI4EU
- Access expertise, repositories of use cases, trusted providers
- Reduce fragmentation; accelerate replication

### Recommendation 4: Develop structured pathways for public-value-driven AI
- Assessment of where AI can be most effectively applied
- Integrate technology with skills, process reengineering, awareness raising
- Validate pilots before scaling

### Recommendation 5: Use public sector demand strategically
- Strengthen European GovTech ecosystem, EU digital sovereignty
- Reduce dependency on non-EU providers
- Align procurement with emerging European GovTech solutions
- Help EU AI startups scale through demand

## AI Act Obligations for Public Administrations

Public administrations are deployers of AI under the AI Act and often operate high-risk systems (Article 6 + Annex III):
- Biometric identification (subject to Article 5 prohibitions and Article 6 high-risk thresholds)
- Critical infrastructure management
- Education/vocational training admissions and assessment
- Access to essential public services and benefits
- Law enforcement (profiling, polygraph, evidence evaluation)
- Migration, asylum, border control
- Administration of justice and democratic processes

Compliance obligations: fundamental rights impact assessment (FRIA) under Article 27 for deployers of Annex III systems; AI literacy under Article 4; register use cases in EU database; human oversight; transparency to affected persons.
