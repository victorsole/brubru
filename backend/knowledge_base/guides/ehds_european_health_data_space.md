## Why it matters

EHDS is the first sector-specific European data space to move from concept into binding law. It gives every natural person the right to access their own electronic health data, download it in a common exchange format, and have it follow them across borders. It sets up two cross-border infrastructures: **MyHealth@EU** for primary use (healthcare delivery) and **HealthData@EU** for secondary use (research, policymaking, regulation, statistics). It certifies **EHR systems** the same way the Medical Device Regulation certifies medical devices. It fines misuse (re-identification, extraction from secure environments) at up to **€20M or 4% of worldwide turnover**.

The Regulation amends Directive 2011/24/EU (patient rights in cross-border healthcare) and Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act) to align cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements classified as EHR systems.

## Chapter I — Definitions worth memorising (Article 2)

- **Personal electronic health data:** data concerning health and genetic data, processed in electronic form.
- **Electronic health data:** personal or non-personal electronic health data.
- **Primary use:** processing for provision of healthcare (assess, maintain, restore health of the natural person the data relate to), including prescription/dispensation and reimbursement services.
- **Secondary use:** processing for the purposes set out in Chapter IV (research, policymaking, statistics, education, care improvement), other than the initial purpose the data were collected for.
- **EHR (electronic health record):** collection of electronic health data related to a natural person and collected in the health system, processed for provision of healthcare.
- **EHR system:** software (or hardware + software) that stores, intermediates, exports, imports, converts, edits or views personal electronic health data in the priority categories for primary use, intended by the manufacturer for healthcare providers or patients.
- **European interoperability software component for EHR systems:** the software component of the EHR system that provides/receives priority-category data in the European electronic health record exchange format.
- **European logging software component for EHR systems:** the software component that provides logging information related to access by health professionals to priority-category data (format defined in Annex II point 3.2).
- **Wellness application:** software intended by the manufacturer for a natural person, for processing electronic health data, providing information on health or delivery of care for purposes other than provision of healthcare.
- **Health data holder:** any natural or legal person, public authority or body in the healthcare/care sectors (or acting as controller/joint controller processing personal electronic health data for healthcare, public health, reimbursement, research, innovation, policymaking, statistics, patient safety, regulatory purposes) or able to make non-personal electronic health data available through control of technical design.
- **Health data user:** natural or legal person, including Union institutions, granted lawful access to electronic health data for secondary use pursuant to a data permit, health data request approval, or access approval by an authorised participant in HealthData@EU.
- **Data permit:** administrative decision issued by a health data access body to a health data user, authorising processing of specific electronic health data for specific secondary-use purposes.
- **Dataset:** structured collection of electronic health data.
- **Dataset of high impact for secondary use:** dataset the reuse of which is associated with significant benefits due to its relevance for health research.
- **Data quality and utility label:** graphic diagram (including a scale) describing data quality and conditions of use of a dataset.
- **CE marking of conformity:** marking by which the manufacturer indicates the EHR system conforms to EHDS + other applicable Union law under Regulation (EC) No 765/2008.
- **Serious incident:** any malfunction or deterioration in an EHR system that leads or could lead to death, serious harm to health, serious prejudice to rights, or serious disruption of critical health-sector infrastructure.

## Chapter II — Primary use of personal electronic health data

### The six rights (Articles 3-10)

Every natural person in the Union has:

1. **Right of access** (Art 3): free, immediate, in easily readable/consolidated format, in the European electronic health record exchange format on request. Covers at least the six priority categories.
2. **Right to insert information** in own EHR (Art 5): distinguishable from health-professional entries; patient cannot alter health-professional entries.
3. **Right to rectification** (Art 6): online, in accordance with Article 16 GDPR.
4. **Right to data portability** (Art 7): all or part of personal electronic health data to another healthcare provider — immediately, free of charge, without hindrance. Cross-border transmission is via MyHealth@EU in the European exchange format.
5. **Right to restrict access** (Art 8): patient can restrict all or parts of their electronic health data; the restriction itself is not visible to healthcare providers.
6. **Right to obtain information on access** (Art 9): automatic notifications; log retained at least **three years**. Must include who accessed, when, which data.

Member States may (Article 10) provide a right to opt out from access to personal electronic health data registered in an EHR system, provided the opt-out is reversible and vital-interest overrides remain possible.

### Priority categories for primary use (Article 14)

Six categories, expandable by delegated act:

1. Patient summaries
2. Electronic prescriptions
3. Electronic dispensations
4. Medical imaging studies and related imaging reports
5. Medical test results (laboratory and other diagnostic results and related reports)
6. Discharge reports

Main characteristics are set out in **Annex I**. Member States may add categories in national law; Commission may lay down cross-border specifications for those.

### European electronic health record exchange format (Article 15)

By 26 March 2027 the Commission shall lay down (implementing acts) technical specifications for the six priority categories, setting out the European electronic health record exchange format. It must be commonly used, machine-readable, support structured and unstructured health data, and include harmonised datasets, coding systems, and interoperability specifications.

### Governance (Articles 19-22)

- Each Member State designates one or more **digital health authorities** (notified by 26 March 2027) responsible for implementing Chapter II + Chapter III at national level.
- Authorities publish a biennial activity report (Art 20) covering percentage of natural persons with EHR access, requests handled, healthcare providers connected to MyHealth@EU, cross-border data volumes, non-compliance cases.
- Complaints route to the digital health authority (Art 21); if the complaint concerns rights under Articles 3, 5-10 (which mirror GDPR rights), it goes to the GDPR supervisory authority.
- GDPR supervisory authorities remain competent for Articles 3, 5-10 (Art 22).

### Cross-border infrastructure — MyHealth@EU (Article 23)

- The Commission establishes MyHealth@EU as the central interoperability platform for digital health.
- Each Member State designates one **national contact point for digital health** (by 26 March 2027).
- All healthcare providers must connect to their national contact point; pharmacies must be able to dispense electronic prescriptions issued in other Member States and report the dispensation back through MyHealth@EU.
- National contact points act as joint controllers; the Commission acts as processor.
- Third countries or international organisations may become authorised participants subject to Commission compliance checks.

## Chapter III — EHR systems and wellness applications (Articles 25-49)

EHR systems are treated as regulated products, along the CE-marking pattern used for medical devices.

- **Article 25:** EHR systems must include the two harmonised software components — European interoperability + European logging — set out in Annex II.
- **Article 26:** placing on the market / putting into service requires compliance with Chapter III; in-house systems developed and used within Union health institutions are considered "put into service".
- **Article 27:** manufacturers of **medical devices**, **in vitro diagnostic medical devices** (Regulations 2017/745 and 2017/746), and providers of **high-risk AI systems** (Regulation 2024/1689) that claim interoperability with EHR systems must prove compliance with Annex II Section 2.
- **Chapter III Section 3** (Arts 36-42) is a full CE-marking regime: common specifications (Art 36), technical documentation (Art 37), information sheet (Art 38), EU declaration of conformity (Art 39), European digital testing environment (Art 40), CE marking (Art 41).
- **Article 40:** Commission develops a **European digital testing environment** (open source); Member States operate national digital testing environments; results feed into the technical documentation.
- **Article 47:** wellness applications claiming interoperability with EHR systems must carry a **label** issued by the manufacturer, valid for max **3 years**.
- **Article 48:** interoperability of wellness applications does not entail automatic data sharing; the natural person must consent per Article 5.
- **Article 49:** Commission establishes a public **EU database for registration of EHR systems and wellness applications**; manufacturers register before placing on market.

## Chapter IV — Secondary use of electronic health data (Articles 50-81)

### Who is covered (Article 50)

Health data holders that are individual researchers (natural persons) or microenterprises (as defined in Recommendation 2003/361/EC Annex Art 2(3)) are exempt from Chapter IV obligations; Member States may extend the obligations to them by national law.

### Minimum 17 categories for secondary use (Article 51)

Health data holders must make these categories available:

1. Electronic health data from EHRs
2. Data on factors impacting health (socioeconomic, environmental, behavioural determinants)
3. Aggregated data on healthcare needs, resources, provision, access, expenditure, financing
4. Data on pathogens that impact human health
5. Healthcare-related administrative data (dispensations, reimbursement claims and reimbursements)
6. Human genetic, epigenomic and genomic data
7. Other human molecular (omics) data — proteomic, transcriptomic, metabolomic, lipidomic
8. Personal electronic health data automatically generated through medical devices
9. Data from wellness applications
10. Data on professional status, specialisation, institution of health professionals treating a natural person
11. Data from population-based health data registries (public health registries)
12. Data from medical registries and mortality registries
13. Data from clinical trials, clinical studies, clinical investigations, performance studies (under Reg 536/2014, Reg 2024/1938, Regs 2017/745 and 2017/746)
14. Other health data from medical devices
15. Data from registries for medicinal products and medical devices
16. Data from research cohorts, questionnaires and surveys after first publication of related results
17. Health data from biobanks and associated databases

Member States may add categories. For genetic/genomic (f), omics (g), wellness (i) and biobank (q) data, they may introduce stricter measures and additional safeguards.

### Purposes for which secondary use is allowed (Article 53)

Six purposes only:

- (a) public interest in public/occupational health (surveillance, quality and safety of care, medicinal products and medical devices)
- (b) policymaking and regulatory activities to support public sector bodies or Union institutions
- (c) statistics as defined in Regulation (EC) No 223/2009
- (d) education or teaching in health/care sectors at vocational or higher-education level
- (e) scientific research (including development, innovation, training/testing/evaluation of algorithms, including AI systems and digital health apps)
- (f) improvement of delivery of care, optimisation of treatment, provision of healthcare based on health data of other natural persons

Access under (a), (b), (c) is reserved for public sector bodies and Union institutions.

### Prohibited secondary uses (Article 54)

- (a) decisions detrimental to a natural person or group with legal, social or economic effects
- (b) discrimination in job offers, goods/services, insurance/credit exclusions, modified premiums
- (c) advertising or marketing activities
- (d) developing products/services that may harm individuals/public health/society (illicit drugs, alcohol, tobacco, nicotine, weapons, addiction-inducing products)
- (e) activities contrary to national ethical provisions

### Health data access bodies (Article 55)

Each Member State designates one or more **health data access bodies** (notified by 26 March 2027). They decide on data permits, provide access in secure processing environments, monitor compliance, request data from health data holders. The Commission acts as the Union health data access service when Union institutions are the health data holders (Art 56).

### Access mechanisms

- **Data permit** (Art 68): administrative decision issued after a health data access application (Art 67). Data provided in a **secure processing environment** (Art 73).
- **Health data request** (Art 69): applicant only receives an **anonymised statistical response** — no access to underlying data.
- **Trusted health data holder** (Art 72): simplified procedure — Member States may designate holders meeting technical, expertise and guarantee conditions; they pre-assess applications for their data.
- **Secure processing environment** (Art 73): technical + organisational safeguards; only anonymised data can leave; downloads and copies of raw data are prohibited.

### Right to opt out from secondary use (Article 71)

Every natural person has an unconditional, reversible right to opt out at any time from processing of their personal electronic health data for secondary use. Member States must provide accessible, easily understandable opt-out mechanisms. Exceptions are possible only for public-interest research/public-health tasks by public sector bodies where the data cannot be obtained by alternative means (Art 71(4)).

### Fines (Article 64)

- Up to **EUR 10 000 000** or **2 % of worldwide annual turnover** — infringements of duties of holders (Art 60) or users (Art 61(1), (5), (6))
- Up to **EUR 20 000 000** or **4 % of worldwide annual turnover** — health data users processing data for prohibited uses (Art 54), extracting personal electronic health data from secure processing environments, **re-identifying or attempting to re-identify** natural persons, non-compliance with enforcement measures

Whichever is higher applies.

### Cross-border infrastructure — HealthData@EU (Article 75)

- Each Member State designates one **national contact point for secondary use** (notified by 26 March 2027).
- The Union health data access service is the contact point for Union institutions.
- National contact points connect to HealthData@EU, the Union-level cross-border infrastructure for secondary use.
- Third countries and international organisations may become authorised participants subject to Commission compliance decisions (Art 75(5), applicable from 26 March 2035).

### Data quality and utility (Section 5, Arts 77-80)

- Dataset catalogue: publicly available, standardised, machine-readable (Art 77).
- Data quality and utility label (Art 78): mandatory for datasets funded with Union or national public money; covers documentation, technical quality, quality management, coverage, access, modifications.
- EU dataset catalogue connecting national catalogues (Art 79).
- Minimum specifications for **datasets of high impact for secondary use** (Art 80).

## Chapter VI — European governance (Articles 92-96)

- **European Health Data Space Board (EHDS Board)** — Article 92: composed of two representatives per Member State (one from digital health authority, one from health data access body) + Commission. Chaired by Commission. Advises on implementation, coordinates cross-border operations, promotes common standards.
- Article 93: sub-groups for primary use and secondary use.
- Article 94: joint controllership arrangements for MyHealth@EU and HealthData@EU.
- Article 96: dispute-resolution mechanism between health data access bodies of different Member States.

## Chapter VIII — Penalties, compensation, evaluation

- **Article 99:** each Member State lays down national penalties (effective, proportionate, dissuasive); notified to Commission by 26 March 2027.
- **Article 100:** right to compensation for material or non-material damage.
- **Article 101:** representation by not-for-profit bodies with public-interest objectives.
- **Article 102:** targeted evaluation by 26 March 2033; overall evaluation by 26 March 2035; annual progress report by Commission to Council until full application.
- **Article 103:** Article 14 of Directive 2011/24/EU (eHealth Network) deleted with effect from 26 March 2031.
- **Article 104:** amends Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act) to align conformity assessment for EHR systems classified as products with digital elements.

## Chapter IX — Deferred and staggered application (Article 105)

- **Entry into force:** 25 March 2025 (20 days after OJ publication of 5 March 2025).
- **General application:** from 26 March 2027.
- **Priority categories (a), (b), (c)** — patient summaries, e-prescriptions, e-dispensations — Articles 3-15, 23(2)-(6), 25-27, 47-49 apply **from 26 March 2029**.
- **Priority categories (d), (e), (f)** — imaging, test results, discharge reports — same articles apply **from 26 March 2031**.
- **Chapter III (EHR systems put into service in the Union, Art 26(2)):** from **26 March 2031**.
- **Chapter IV (secondary use):** from **26 March 2029**.
- **Article 51(1) categories (b), (f), (g), (m), (p)** — determinants, genetic, omics, clinical trials, cohorts — from **26 March 2031**.
- **Article 75(5)** — third-country participation in HealthData@EU — from **26 March 2035**.
- Article 55(6), Art 70, Art 73(5), Art 75(1) and (12), Art 77(4), Art 78(6) apply from 26 March 2027 (to enable early implementing acts).

- 9 Chapters, 105 Articles, 115 recitals, 4 Annexes

- 5-year maximum exclusion of a non-compliant health data user (Art 63(3))

## Lineage and family tree

EHDS sits at the intersection of Union health law and Union data-governance law:

- **Foundation:** Articles 16 and 114 TFEU (data protection + internal market).
- **Data-governance stack:** GDPR (2016/679), Data Governance Act (2022/868), Data Act (2023/2854).
- **Cybersecurity + AI stack:** NIS 2 Directive (2022/2555), Cyber Resilience Act (2024/2847, amended by EHDS Art 104), AI Act (2024/1689).
- **Medical device stack:** MDR (2017/745), IVDR (2017/746) — interoperability claims covered by EHDS Chapter III.
- **Cross-border healthcare:** Directive 2011/24/EU — Article 14 (eHealth Network) deleted from 26 March 2031 by EHDS Art 103.
- **eIDAS 2:** Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 — used for identification of natural persons and health professionals.
- **First European Health Union proposal** delivered on the Commission's promise following the COVID-19 pandemic response.
