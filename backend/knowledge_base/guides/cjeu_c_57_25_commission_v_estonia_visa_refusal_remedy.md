# Court of Justice, Case C-57/25, Commission v Estonia: a court remedy against short-stay visa refusals (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Ruling:** judgment of the Court of Justice (**Eighth Chamber**) of **8 October 2026** in **Case C-57/25, European Commission v Republic of Estonia** (CELEX 62025CJ0057), an infringement action under **Article 258 TFEU** brought on 29 January 2025. Language of the case: Estonian. The Court decided **without an Advocate General's opinion**.
- **Holding:** by providing that a **decision refusing, annulling or revoking a short-stay visa** (transit or stays of up to **90 days in any 180-day period**), and a decision taken on an **administrative appeal** against it, **cannot be challenged before a court within the meaning of EU law, Estonia failed to fulfil its obligations** under **Article 32(3), Article 34(7) and Article 35(7) of the Visa Code (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, as amended by Regulation (EU) No 610/2013), read in the light of Article 47 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights** (right to an effective remedy and to a fair trial).
- **The Commission's wider claim:** it also relied on **Article 19(1) TEU**; the operative part rests on the Visa Code provisions read with Article 47 of the Charter.
- **Costs:** Estonia bears its own costs and those of the Commission.
- **Consequence:** the Commission asked only for the declaration and for costs, **no penalty payment**; under Article 260(1) TFEU Estonia must now take the measures needed to comply, that is give third-country nationals a court remedy against such visa decisions.
- **Basis of this guide:** the operative part of the judgment as published on EUR-Lex was read; the Court's reasoning (grounds) was not, so state no reasoning, no legal principle and no earlier case beyond what is written here.
- **Scope limit:** short-stay (Schengen) visas under the Visa Code only; it does not concern long-stay national visas or residence permits.

## How to answer
"Can a visa refusal be challenged in court in the EU?": yes for short-stay visas, since the Court held on 8 October 2026 (C-57/25) that Member States must provide a judicial remedy, not only an administrative appeal.
