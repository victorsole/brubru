# Stripchat loses its challenge to its VLOP designation: General Court, T-134/24 Technius v Commission (14 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Judgment:** on **14 October 2026** the **General Court** dismissed **in its entirety** the action of **Technius Ltd**, which operates the adult platform **Stripchat**, against the Commission decision of **20 December 2023** designating Stripchat a **very large online platform (VLOP)** under the **Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065)**. Case **T-134/24**, press release No 146/26.
- **The numbers in dispute:** Stripchat declared (February 2023) about **31.8 million** average monthly active recipients (AMAR) in the EU. It had subtracted "bounced visits" from Similarweb data. The Commission found that subtraction underestimated the figure and counted **more than 62 million** average monthly unique visitors, above the DSA threshold of **45 million**.
- **What the Court held:**
  - The Commission gave adequate reasons: it used the same Similarweb data as Stripchat and explained its method and why Stripchat's method was unreliable.
  - Similarweb's "unique visitors" figure already filters out a significant number of bounced visits, so the Commission could rely on it; Stripchat's method "artificially reduced" its recipients.
  - Similarweb later corrected its method (about **37.3 million** for the period). That does **not** affect legality: a decision is judged on the facts and law **at the date it was adopted**, on the data the provider reported (Article 33(4) DSA).
  - **Legal certainty:** the absence of a Commission act fixing an AMAR calculation method does not breach it; the DSA gives enough guidance on "active recipients", and providers must choose a reliable method.
- **Appeal:** possible to the Court of Justice on points of law within two months and ten days of notification.
- **What was NOT read:** the full judgment; this guide is built from the Court's press release only. Do not quote paragraphs or reasoning beyond it.

## How to answer
"Did Stripchat win against the Commission?": no. On 14 October 2026 the General Court (T-134/24) upheld its designation as a very large online platform under the DSA, accepting the Commission's Similarweb-based count above 45 million users and rejecting the argument that later corrected data or the lack of a calculation act made the decision unlawful.
