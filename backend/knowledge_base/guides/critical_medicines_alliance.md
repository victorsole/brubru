# Critical Medicines Alliance (CMA)

## QUICK FACTS

- **What it is**: the Critical Medicines Alliance is a voluntary, multi-stakeholder consultative mechanism, not a legal or legislative body. It brings together EU Member States, EU institutions and agencies, industry, patient organisations, healthcare-professional bodies, civil society and academia to identify priorities and propose solutions for strengthening the EU's pharmaceutical supply chain and preventing and mitigating medicine shortages.
- **Coordinated by**: DG HERA (Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority) together with DG SANTE, with the European Medicines Agency (EMA) closely involved; HaDEA (Health and Digital Executive Agency) provides operational support.
- **Launch**: open call for membership issued 16 January 2024; first plenary meeting 24 April 2024 (Brubru cites 24 April 2024 as launch date). Both "established January 2024" and "launched April 2024" appear in sources -- they refer to different milestones.
- **Membership**: open, voluntary, no fixed cap; commonly described as 250-300+ organisations, spanning Member States, EU institutions/agencies, pharmaceutical and generic/API manufacturers, trade associations (EFPIA and generics/biosimilars bodies), patient organisations, healthcare-professional bodies, NGOs and academia. Members sign the Alliance Declaration; can join at any stage.
- **Governance**: (1) **Forum** -- full plenary, consulted at key milestones; (2) **Steering Board** -- strategic direction (Member States, industry, patient organisations, EMA, Commission staff); (3) **Working Groups** -- (a) EU manufacturing capacity for critical medicines/APIs, (b) international-partnership diversification.
- **Key output**: Strategic Report, 28 February 2025 -- recommendations on investment in manufacturing, demand-side/procurement measures, supply-chain diversification and stockpiling, and shortage monitoring.
- **Direct legislative impact**: the Strategic Report fed directly into the Critical Medicines Act proposal, COM(2025) 102 final, tabled 11 March 2025 (procedure 2025/0102(COD)). See guide `critical_medicines_act`.
- **Distinguish carefully**: the Alliance (consultative forum, no legal personality) is NOT the Critical Medicines Act (a proposed Regulation) nor the Critical Medicines Coordination Group (a formal Commission/Member-State-only body under the Act, once adopted). "CMA" is ambiguous between Alliance and Act -- always spell out which is meant.
- **Links to**: Union List of Critical Medicines (EMA/HERA, Dec 2023, updated Dec 2024, 276 substances); IPCEI Med; the pharma-package reform (2023/0131(COD) + 2023/0132(COD)); the European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP).
- **Official source**: `health.ec.europa.eu/health-emergency-preparedness-and-response-hera/overview/critical-medicines-alliance_en`
- **Why it matters**: the Steering Board and Working Groups are the earliest, most direct channel for shaping EU critical-medicines policy, well ahead of OEIL. Primary lever for influencing the Act's implementing measures (Strategic Projects criteria, EU-preference thresholds, contingency-stock rules).

## Overview

The Critical Medicines Alliance is the European Commission's flagship consultative platform for addressing the EU's structural vulnerability in the supply of critical medicines, established in the wake of COVID-19 shortages and mounting concern over EU dependence on a small number of non-EU suppliers (notably China and India) for active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and finished medicines.

Unlike a legislative body, the Alliance has no formal decision-making powers. It functions as a structured dialogue and recommendation-generating forum: the Commission (via DG HERA and DG SANTE) convenes stakeholders, channels their input through a Forum-Steering Board-Working Group architecture, and translates the resulting consensus recommendations into concrete Commission proposals -- most visibly the Critical Medicines Act.

This model mirrors similar EU sectoral alliances (for example the European Raw Materials Alliance or the European Battery Alliance): a fast, informal, non-legislative coordination mechanism that lets the Commission move quickly on an urgent industrial-policy problem while a full legislative process runs in parallel.

## Governance in detail

**The Forum** is the plenary body: all signatories of the Alliance Declaration participate in periodic Forum meetings, held at key milestones (launch, publication of the Strategic Report, review of Working Group progress). It is the widest consultation layer and the main venue where the Commission communicates policy direction back to the full membership.

**The Steering Board** provides strategic direction between Forum meetings. Its composition blends public and private-sector voices deliberately: Member State representatives (national health and industrial-policy authorities), industry representatives (originator and generic/API manufacturers, trade associations), patient organisations, the European Medicines Agency, and European Commission staff (DG HERA, DG SANTE). The Steering Board sets the agenda for the Working Groups and reviews their outputs before they are folded into Commission policy documents.

**The two Working Groups** carry out the substantive technical work:
1. **EU manufacturing capacity** -- mapping current EU production of critical medicines and their active substances, identifying investment gaps, and proposing incentive mechanisms (this workstream is the direct precursor of the Critical Medicines Act's Strategic Projects mechanism, which offers fast-track permitting and funding access to recognised manufacturing investments).
2. **International partnerships and diversification** -- assessing where the EU is overly dependent on a single third-country source and proposing strategic partnerships to diversify supply (this workstream feeds Chapter VI of the Critical Medicines Act, which requires the Commission to pursue international strategic partnerships).

## The 28 February 2025 Strategic Report

The Alliance's Strategic Report is its principal output to date. Its recommendations cluster around four themes that map almost one-to-one onto the structure of the Critical Medicines Act proposed two weeks later:

1. **Investment in manufacturing**: a European investment plan combining EU funding instruments (EU4Health, Horizon Europe, the Digital Europe Programme, IPCEI Med) with national State aid, plus fast-track regulatory and permitting routes for manufacturing projects.
2. **Demand-side / procurement measures**: moving Member State procurement away from lowest-price-only tendering toward criteria that reward security of supply and EU-based manufacturing (the "most economically advantageous tender" approach, and what became the Act's "EU preference"), alongside more joint and collaborative procurement to aggregate demand.
3. **Supply-chain diversification and stockpiling**: a harmonised, proportionate approach to contingency stocks and closer coordination on where the EU sources non-EU inputs, to reduce concentration risk.
4. **Shortage monitoring**: better, faster, more harmonised data on emerging shortages, building on the European Shortages Monitoring Platform (ESMP) and the Union List of Critical Medicines.

These recommendations were explicitly cited by the Commission as the evidentiary and political basis for the Critical Medicines Act proposal, COM(2025) 102 final (11 March 2025). For the Act's full legal content, legislative timeline (EP first reading 20 January 2026, Council general approach 2 December 2025, provisional inter-institutional agreement 11-12 May 2026) and chapter-by-chapter breakdown, see guide `critical_medicines_act`.

## Relationship to the wider critical-medicines architecture

- **Union List of Critical Medicines**: maintained jointly by EMA and HERA (first published December 2023, updated December 2024, 276 critical active substances); defines the scope of medicines the Alliance and the Act both focus on.
- **IPCEI Med**: the heavy industrial-investment complement to the Alliance's recommendations, mobilising cross-border public and private investment in EU health manufacturing and R&D value chains on the IPCEI model (alongside DG GROW); approved individually via the State Aid decisions register on a rolling basis.
- **Critical Medicines Coordination Group**: once the Critical Medicines Act is formally adopted, this new formal body (Commission plus Member State representatives only, no industry or civil society seats) takes over strategic coordination on Strategic Project financing and collaborative procurement -- a narrower, statutory successor function to parts of what the Alliance currently does informally. The Alliance itself is expected to continue as the broader stakeholder-consultation layer alongside the Coordination Group, not be replaced by it.
- **EU pharmaceutical legislation reform**: the wider "pharma package" (2023/0131(COD) Regulation and 2023/0132(COD) Directive) addresses shortage-prevention obligations on marketing-authorisation holders; the Alliance and the Critical Medicines Act instead address the industrial and supply-chain side. See guide `eu_pharmaceutical_legislation_reform`.
- **DG HERA's three pillars** (Preparedness, Crisis Response, Industrial Readiness): the Alliance sits under the Industrial Readiness pillar, alongside EU FAB (vaccine manufacturing network) and the HERA Industry Hub. See guide `dg_hera_overview`.

## How Brubru users should engage with the Alliance

**Pharmaceutical and API manufacturers**: the Steering Board and the two Working Groups are the highest-leverage venues for shaping how "critical" and "vulnerable" medicines get defined, and how Strategic Project recognition criteria under the Critical Medicines Act will work in practice. Join via the Alliance Declaration; monitor Working Group outputs as leading indicators of forthcoming implementing measures.

**Trade associations (EFPIA and generics/biosimilars bodies)**: represented on the Steering Board; use Forum meetings to track how Commission thinking is evolving between formal legislative milestones.

**Patient organisations and healthcare-professional bodies**: hold seats on the Steering Board; the Alliance is a direct channel to ensure shortage-mitigation measures reflect patient-access priorities, particularly for the orphan-medicines scope added to the Critical Medicines Act during trilogues.

**Public-affairs and government-affairs professionals**: track the Alliance as an early-warning system for Critical Medicines Act implementing measures -- Working Group recommendations typically precede formal Commission proposals by months. Cross-reference Alliance outputs against the Act's legislative timeline in guide `critical_medicines_act`.

**Member State authorities**: participate via national health and industrial-policy representatives on the Steering Board; the Alliance is the venue to flag national manufacturing capacity and supply-chain vulnerabilities before they surface in formal Coordination Group discussions post-adoption.
