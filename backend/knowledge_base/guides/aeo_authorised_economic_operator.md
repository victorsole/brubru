# AEO: Authorised Economic Operator status

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Authorised Economic Operator (AEO), the EU customs trusted-trader status
- Legal basis: Union Customs Code (UCC), Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, Articles 38-41; detailed criteria in the UCC Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/2446 and the UCC Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447
- EUR-Lex UCC: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32013R0952
- Responsible DG: DG TAXUD (Taxation and Customs Union)
- Granting authority: national customs administrations of the 27 Member States (not the Commission directly)
- Three status types:
  - **AEOC** (Customs Simplifications): faster access to customs simplifications, no security/safety component
  - **AEOS** (Security and Safety): faster clearance through customs controls relating to security and safety, no simplifications component
  - **AEOF** (Full/Combined): AEOC + AEOS together, the most common and most valuable status for large importers/exporters
- Core benefits: fewer physical and document-based controls, priority treatment if selected for control, choice of place of control, easier access to customs simplifications, reduced or waived guarantees, indirect benefit of being pre-notified before certain controls, and a reputational "trusted trader" signal to supply-chain partners
- Eligibility criteria assessed by national customs (Article 39 UCC):
  1. Absence of serious infringement or repeated infringement of customs and tax rules, including no record of serious criminal offences relating to the applicant's economic activity
  2. A satisfactory system of managing commercial and, where appropriate, transport records, allowing appropriate customs controls
  3. Financial solvency, proven for the past three years
  4. Practical standards of competence or professional qualifications directly related to the activity carried out (required for AEOC)
  5. Appropriate security and safety standards, including physical integrity, access control, logistical processes, personnel, and business-partner security (required for AEOS)
- Application channel: eAEO application via the EU Customs Trader Portal / Customs Decisions System (CDS), submitted to the customs authority of the Member State where the applicant's main customs-relevant records are held
- Standard processing time: customs authorities must decide within 120 days of accepting the application (extendable), status is valid indefinitely unless suspended, revoked, or the operator no longer meets the criteria (subject to periodic monitoring/reassessment)
- Mutual recognition: the EU has AEO Mutual Recognition Agreements/Arrangements (MRAs) with a number of trading partners, giving reciprocal facilitation to each other's trusted traders. Confirmed EU MRA partners historically include the United States (C-TPAT), Japan, China, Switzerland, Norway, Canada, and the United Kingdom (via the EU-UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement framework). **Verify the current, complete MRA list live on the DG TAXUD AEO page**: new agreements and reviews are added periodically and this guide should not be treated as an exhaustive real-time list
- Interaction with reform: the pending Union Customs Code reform (COM(2023)258, procedure 2023/0156(COD)) proposes a new **EU Customs Authority** and a centralised **EU Customs Data Hub**, and envisages a reinforced "Trust and Check" trader status building on the AEO concept for the most reliable operators. Also interacts with the **Import Control System 2 (ICS2)** for advance cargo information/safety-and-security declarations. See `eu_customs_reform.md` and `union_customs_code_reform.md` for the reform's status; treat any "Trust and Check" detail as evolving until the reform is adopted

Trusted-trader status under EU customs law, granted by national customs authorities and recognised across all 27 Member States and, via mutual recognition, by a growing list of non-EU trading partners.

## What AEO is

AEO status certifies that a business's customs-related operations, financial standing, and (where relevant) security processes meet EU standards of reliability. It is voluntary, not mandatory, but a large share of the EU's largest importers, exporters, forwarders, and logistics operators hold it because of the trade-facilitation benefits and the competitive signal it sends to partners and customs authorities alike.

AEO sits within the World Customs Organization's SAFE Framework of Standards, which is why mutual recognition with non-EU customs authorities is possible: a foreign customs administration that also implements a SAFE-aligned trusted-trader programme can agree to extend reciprocal benefits to EU AEOs, and vice versa.

## The three AEO types

| Type | Focus | Typical holder |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| AEOC | Customs simplifications (e.g. self-assessment, simplified declarations, centralised clearance) | Importers/exporters wanting faster administrative processing |
| AEOS | Security and safety (fewer security-related controls, priority treatment at the border) | Logistics operators, carriers, freight forwarders |
| AEOF | Combined AEOC + AEOS | Large multinational traders wanting both sets of benefits |

An operator can apply for AEOC, AEOS, or both at once (which is processed and certified as AEOF). Holding AEOF does not require two separate certificates; it is a single combined authorisation.

## Eligibility criteria in detail

National customs authorities assess applicants against the criteria set out in Article 39 UCC and detailed further in the Delegated and Implementing Regulations:

1. **Compliance record**: no serious or repeated infringements of customs legislation or taxation rules, and no serious criminal record connected to the applicant's economic activity, over a period usually covering the last three years.
2. **Record-keeping system**: a satisfactory system of managing commercial and transport records that allows customs to carry out effective controls, including audit trail and traceability of goods movements.
3. **Financial solvency**: demonstrated financial solvency over the preceding three years, or evidence that solvency can reasonably be assumed going forward.
4. **Practical competence or professional qualifications**: relevant only for AEOC, this criterion looks at the applicant's practical standards of competence or professional qualifications directly related to the activity carried out.
5. **Security and safety standards**: relevant only for AEOS, covering physical site security, access control to premises, cargo handling procedures, personnel security (including vetting of staff in security-sensitive posts), and security requirements imposed on business partners in the supply chain.

Applicants must generally have been established in the EU customs territory for at least three years, though shorter-established businesses can sometimes be assessed on other available evidence.

## Application process

1. **Self-assessment**: applicants typically complete a self-assessment questionnaire covering the relevant criteria before formally applying, since customs authorities will audit the same areas.
2. **Submission**: the application is filed electronically through the EU Customs Trader Portal, feeding into the EU-wide Customs Decisions System (CDS), which all Member States use to process and record AEO (and other customs) authorisations.
3. **Consultation**: the customs authority handling the application must consult customs authorities in other Member States where the applicant has operations, to ensure a consistent EU-wide assessment.
4. **Site visits and document checks**: authorities typically carry out on-site verification of premises, IT systems, and record-keeping, particularly for AEOS/AEOF applications.
5. **Decision**: the authority must decide within 120 days of accepting the application (this period can be extended in complex cases, with the applicant informed of the extension and reasons).
6. **Monitoring**: once granted, AEO status is subject to ongoing monitoring; authorities can reassess, suspend, or revoke status if the operator ceases to meet the criteria, and operators must notify customs of relevant changes to their business.

## Benefits in practice

- **Fewer controls**: statistically and procedurally, AEOs are subject to fewer physical inspections and fewer document-based checks than non-AEO traders.
- **Priority treatment**: if an AEO's consignment is selected for control, it is generally prioritised for faster processing.
- **Choice of location**: AEOs can sometimes request that a control take place at a specific location, reducing disruption to their supply chain.
- **Easier access to simplifications**: AEOC status is a prerequisite or fast-track for several other customs simplifications and authorisations under the UCC (e.g. centralised clearance, self-assessment).
- **Reduced guarantees**: AEOs can benefit from reduced levels of comprehensive guarantee, or in some cases a guarantee waiver, for customs debts that may be incurred.
- **Pre-notification**: AEOs are often notified in advance (where feasible and where it does not compromise the effectiveness of the control) that a consignment has been selected for control.
- **Mutual recognition benefits**: where an MRA is in force, the EU AEO's benefits extend into the partner country's customs procedures, and vice versa for the partner's certified traders entering the EU.

## Mutual Recognition Agreements/Arrangements (MRAs)

Mutual recognition means a non-EU country's trusted-trader programme and the EU's AEO programme agree to treat each other's certified operators favourably, reducing duplicate vetting for supply chains that cross both jurisdictions. The EU has negotiated MRAs or mutual-recognition arrangements with a number of partners over time, historically including the United States (whose domestic programme is C-TPAT), Japan, China, Switzerland, Norway, Canada, and a UK-EU facilitation arrangement following Brexit. New MRAs and periodic reviews of existing ones are managed by DG TAXUD; always check the current DG TAXUD AEO page for the live, authoritative list before citing specific countries in client-facing material, since agreements can be added, suspended, or renegotiated.

## Interaction with the Union Customs Code reform

The pending UCC reform (COM(2023)258, procedure 2023/0156(COD), see `union_customs_code_reform.md` and `eu_customs_reform.md`) proposes establishing a new **EU Customs Authority** and a centralised **EU Customs Data Hub** to replace fragmented national IT systems. The reform also floats a reinforced "Trust and Check" trader status intended to build on the AEO concept for the most reliable, highest-volume operators, offering even lighter-touch customs treatment in exchange for near-real-time data sharing. As of the file's current status (awaiting Council's first-reading position after Parliament's first reading), the detail of how "Trust and Check" will relate to existing AEO status has not been finalised; treat this as an evolving area of the reform rather than settled law.

AEO status also interacts operationally with the **Import Control System 2 (ICS2)**, the EU's advance cargo information system for safety-and-security risk analysis before goods arrive: AEOS/AEOF holders can benefit from a reduced dataset requirement for certain pre-arrival declarations. See `eu_customs_electronic_systems.md` for ICS2 and the other EU customs IT systems.

## Who typically needs AEO status

- Importers and exporters with regular, high-volume EU customs declarations
- Customs agents, freight forwarders, and customs representatives acting on behalf of others
- Carriers (road, rail, air, maritime) moving goods across the EU external border
- Warehouse keepers and logistics operators handling customs-supervised storage
- Manufacturers with complex international supply chains seeking to reduce clearance delays and demonstrate supply-chain security to business partners (a de facto expectation in some security-conscious sectors such as aerospace, defence, and pharmaceuticals)
