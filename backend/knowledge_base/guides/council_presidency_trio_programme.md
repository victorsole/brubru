## What a Rotating Presidency Does

The presidency chairs all **Council of the EU** configurations except the Foreign Affairs Council. In practice this means:

- **Chairing Council meetings and working parties.** The presidency sets the agenda for each Council formation, runs the technical working parties where line-by-line negotiation happens, and steers the hundreds of dossiers moving through the system in parallel.
- **Conducting interinstitutional negotiations.** In ordinary legislative procedure, the presidency leads Council's side in trilogue negotiations with the European Parliament and the Commission. The presidency chair sits at the table; their objective is to reach a deal that satisfies both Council and Parliament within the six-month window.
- **Setting the pace of files.** The presidency decides which dossiers to push, which to park, and when to call for a general approach (Council position) or a mandate for trilogues. A file that the presidency does not prioritise typically stalls until the next presidency.
- **Issuing Council conclusions and presidency conclusions.** On non-legislative matters (foreign policy positions, macro-economic guidance, political declarations), the presidency drafts and brokers the text.
- **Representing the Council externally.** The presidency represents the Council before the Parliament and in some third-party forums, though it does so in close coordination with the Secretary-General of the Council.

The rotating presidency does **not** have an autonomous political programme; it is constitutionally required to act in the general interest of the Union, not to advance its national agenda. In practice, however, the choice of which files to accelerate reflects real national and political priorities.

## The Trio Mechanism

The Trio system was introduced with the Lisbon Treaty to prevent abrupt shifts in legislative priorities every six months. It works as follows:

1. **Three consecutive presidencies** form a group at least 18 months before the first of them takes office.
2. They negotiate a **joint 18-month programme** (the Trio programme), setting overarching political objectives and a shared approach to the legislative agenda. This document is presented to the General Affairs Council for endorsement shortly before the first presidency begins.
3. Each presidency then produces its own **six-month programme**, which is more detailed and operationally specific, nested inside the Trio framework.

The Trio programme is a political declaration, not a binding legal act. It signals priorities to the Parliament, the Commission, and stakeholders. Files explicitly named in the Trio programme have a higher probability of advancing during the 18-month window.

### Practical mechanics
- The three incoming presidency teams begin working together well before the first term opens. Staff from Ireland, Lithuania and Greece were already meeting in 2025 on the joint IE-LT-GR programme.
- A country that holds the second or third slot of a Trio can pick up files left incomplete by the first presidency. This is the continuity benefit the Trio provides.
- When a file slips, presidency teams brief their incoming Trio partner in a formal "handover" process in the final weeks of each term.

## What the Presidency Cannot Chair

Three Council-adjacent bodies have permanent chairs independent of the rotation:

| Body | Permanent Chair | Basis |
|------|----------------|-------|
| **Foreign Affairs Council** | High Representative / Vice-President (currently Kaja Kallas, since December 2024) | Article 18(3) TEU |
| **Eurogroup** | Elected president, 2.5-year renewable term (currently Kyriakos Pierrakakis, elected 11 December 2025) | Protocol 14 TFEU |
| **European Council** | President (currently António Costa, since 1 December 2024) | Article 15(5) TEU |

The **European Council** (heads of state/government) is a separate institution from the **Council of the EU** (ministers). The rotating presidency applies to the Council of the EU, not to the European Council. See `european_council_and_council_personnel.md` for full detail on this distinction.

## Practical File-Timing for Brubru Users

Understanding the presidency calendar is the first step in predicting when a file will move. Key principles:

**Files need 2--4 presidencies to reach adoption.** A Commission proposal published in early 2026 might achieve a Council general approach in H1 2027 (Lithuania) and conclude trilogue under H2 2027 (Greece) or later.

**Presidency priorities are a filter.** If a file is not named in the Trio programme and not a legacy obligation, expect it to stall. Competitiveness files under COMPET, for example, moved faster under the Polish presidency given Poland's declared priority of economic growth.

**End-of-term pressure creates windows.** Presidencies push hard in May--June and November--December to close deals before handover. Trilogues often accelerate sharply in these months. The last four weeks of a term frequently see more movement than the preceding three months combined.

**New Trio, new priorities.** At every 18-month Trio transition (like the current PL-DK-CY to IE-LT-GR shift), some files are de-prioritised and others accelerated. Advocacy strategies need re-calibration against the incoming Trio programme.

**Enlargement files track the presidency's home politics.** Presidencies from enlargement-friendly states (e.g. Czech Republic, Poland) have historically pushed accession files harder. Read the presidency programme's language on enlargement carefully.

## How to Read a Presidency Programme Document

Every incoming presidency publishes a programme document. Structure is typically:

1. **Political introduction** -- framing statement from the presidency's head of government or foreign minister. Read for tone and political emphasis; it signals which pillar will get real attention.
2. **Thematic sections** -- aligned to the Trio pillars. Look for named legislative files (explicit dossier references), not just thematic language. Named files are the ones the presidency has committed to advance.
3. **Council configurations** -- some programmes list priorities by Council formation (COMPET, TTE, JHA, etc.). This is the most operationally useful section.
4. **Indicative calendar** -- major informal ministerial meetings planned in the presidency country. These generate political momentum and media attention.

When a file you are tracking is not mentioned in the programme, that is information: you need to assess whether it will be picked up under operational pressure or will wait for the next presidency.

## National Presidency Website Pattern

Every presidency runs a dedicated portal at:

```
https://[country-name]-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/
```

Examples:
- `https://irish-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/`
- `https://cyprus-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/`
- `https://polish-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/`

These sites carry the six-month programme, event calendar, press releases, and links to Council meeting documents. They are active only during the presidency term and for a short archival period after.

The canonical Council presidency page, listing all past and future presidencies, is at:

```
https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/council-eu/presidency-council-eu/
```

## Irish Presidency (1 July -- 31 December 2026)

Ireland assumed the Presidency of the Council of the EU on **1 July 2026** for the **eighth time** (previous: 2013). President of the European Council **António Costa** travelled to Dublin on 1 July for the opening ceremony. The programme was published at `irish-presidency.consilium.europa.eu`.

- **National motto**: **"Ní neart go cur le chéile" -- "Strength with unity."**
- **Three pillars**: **Competitiveness · Values · Security.**
- **Competitiveness**: deliver on the timelines of the **One Europe, One Market Roadmap** (agreed by the three institutions in April 2026); drive the **simplification / better-regulation agenda** in the General Affairs Council (GAC).
- **Values**: uphold the **rule of law** across the Union and in candidate countries; strengthen democratic resilience; advance the EU's accession to the ECHR; children's rights and digital justice (DigitalJustice@2030).
- **Security**: continued **support for Ukraine** (political, financial, humanitarian, accession) and pressure on Russia through additional sanctions and anti-circumvention measures; Middle East; enhance the EU's capacity to act.
- **Enlargement**: an explicit ambition to **"prepare the ground for the first accession of new Member States since 2013"** -- steering candidate-country files (Montenegro's accession is the most advanced; see the June 2026 financial package Communication COM(2026) 334).
- **Trade files under the Irish gavel**: Council decisions on the **EU-Indonesia CEPA/IPA** (proposals tabled 29 June 2026, see `eu_indonesia_cepa`), plus ongoing FTA and trade-defence dossiers.
- **Numbers (official)**: 2 Leaders' Summits, ~22 informal ministerial meetings, 250+ events in Ireland.
- **Trio context**: Ireland opens the incoming Trio (Ireland H2 2026 · Lithuania H1 2027 · Greece H2 2027; Council doc ST-10392-2026-INIT). What the presidency does **not** chair is unchanged: Foreign Affairs Council (HR/VP Kaja Kallas), Eurogroup (Kyriakos Pierrakakis), European Council (António Costa).

Source: `irish-presidency.consilium.europa.eu/en/programme/programme-of-the-irish-presidency`; Council press (1 July 2026).
