## QUICK ANSWERS

**What is the Animal Health Law?** It is the single EU framework regulation for preventing and controlling transmissible animal diseases. It replaced around 40 older directives, regulations and decisions (most repealed from 21 April 2021) with one principles-based rulebook covering listing of diseases, responsibilities, notification, surveillance, eradication, disease control, registration and approval of establishments, traceability, movements within the Union, entry from third countries, pet movements and emergency measures.

**When did it start to apply?** It entered into force on 20 April 2016 but applied from 21 April 2021, to allow the Commission time to adopt the necessary delegated and implementing acts.

**Which diseases does it name directly?** Five in Article 5(1): foot-and-mouth disease, classical swine fever, African swine fever, highly pathogenic avian influenza, and African horse sickness. Other listed diseases are set out in Annex II and amendable by delegated act.

**What is the A to E categorisation?** Article 9 sorts listed diseases into five management categories: A (eradicate immediately, e.g. foot-and-mouth disease, classical swine fever); B (compulsory Union-wide eradication, e.g. brucellosis); C (optional eradication, e.g. infectious bovine rhinotracheitis); D (movement and entry restrictions); E (surveillance). A disease can fall into more than one category. The detailed assignment is set by Commission implementing rules.

**Who is responsible for animal health?** Operators bear primary responsibility (animal health, biosecurity, prudent use of veterinary medicines); supported by animal professionals, pet keepers, veterinarians and aquatic animal health professionals, Member State competent authorities, and a network of Union and national reference laboratories.

**How are animals identified and traced?** Member States run identification-and-registration systems with computer databases. Bovine animals carry individual physical identification plus an identification document; ovine and caprine animals a physical means plus a movement document; equine animals a unique code plus a single lifetime identification document (equine passport); porcine animals establishment-level identification. Cross-border movements are notified through TRACES, the EU integrated computerised veterinary system.

**What about moving pets?** Up to five pet animals of the Annex I Part A species (dogs, cats, ferrets) may move in a single non-commercial movement (with an exception for competitions and exhibitions). Pets must be microchipped, meet preventive measures (notably rabies vaccination) and carry an identification document, the EU pet passport. Special bilateral arrangements continue for San Marino, the Vatican, Monaco, Andorra, Norway, the Faroe Islands and Greenland.

**How does it deal with imports?** Animals, germinal products and products of animal origin may enter the Union only from listed third countries, from approved establishments, meeting requirements as stringent as intra-Union rules, and accompanied by an animal health certificate from the third-country official veterinarian (electronic via TRACES).

**Is it still in force?** Yes. It is the foundational framework; its operative detail is filled in by a large body of Commission delegated and implementing regulations (the "animal health package").

## OVERVIEW

The Animal Health Law is the consolidated EU framework for transmissible animal diseases, built on the One Health principle and aligned with the WTO SPS Agreement and World Organisation for Animal Health (OIE/WOAH) standards, with risk assessment informed by EFSA. It is structured in nine Parts: general rules and definitions (Part I); notification, surveillance, eradication and disease-free status (Part II); disease awareness, preparedness and control, including contingency plans, vaccine banks and restricted zones (Part III); registration, approval, traceability and movements for terrestrial and aquatic animals (Part IV); entry into the Union and export (Part V); non-commercial pet movements (Part VI); emergency measures (Part VII); common provisions including comitology and penalties (Part VIII); and transitional and final provisions including the repeals (Part IX).

It rests on three TFEU legal bases (agriculture, internal market, public health), was adopted on 9 March 2016, entered into force on 20 April 2016 and applied from 21 April 2021. It repealed roughly 40 prior acts (mostly from 21 April 2021), including the cattle-and-swine trade Directive 64/432/EEC, the aquatic-animal Directive 2006/88/EC, the sheep-and-goat identification Regulation (EC) 21/2004 and the pet-movement Regulation (EU) 576/2013.

## STRUCTURE

- **Part I (Articles 1 to 17):** subject matter and aim, scope, 56 definitions, listing of diseases (the five named diseases + Annex II) and the A to E categorisation, responsibilities of operators, vets, Member States and laboratories.
- **Part II (Articles 18 to 42):** notification and reporting (ADIS), surveillance, compulsory and optional eradication programmes, disease-free status of Member States, zones and compartments.
- **Part III (Articles 43 to 83):** contingency plans and simulation exercises, veterinary medicinal products, Union antigen/vaccine/diagnostic reagent banks, and the disease-control machinery (investigation, confirmation, killing/disposal, restricted/protection/surveillance zones, emergency vaccination, wild animals).
- **Part IV (Articles 84 to 228):** registration and approval of establishments and transporters, record-keeping, identification and traceability, movements within the Union, animal health certificates and TRACES notification, for terrestrial animals (Title I), aquatic animals (Title II) and other animals (Title III).
- **Part V (Articles 229 to 243):** entry into the Union from listed third countries, approved establishments, certificates, and export.
- **Part VI (Articles 244 to 256):** non-commercial pet movements (max five Annex I Part A animals, identification document, preventive measures).
- **Part VII (Articles 257 to 262):** emergency measures by Member States and the Commission.
- **Part VIII (Articles 263 to 269):** delegated and implementing acts, the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed, penalties, national measures.
- **Part IX (Articles 270 to 283):** repeals, transitional provisions, entry into force and application.
- **Annexes:** I (pet species, Parts A and B), II (listed diseases), III (ungulates), IV (categorisation criteria), V (correlation table).

## KEY NUMBERS

- 283 articles, 9 Parts, 179 recitals, 5 Annexes.
- 5 diseases named directly (Article 5(1)); 5 management categories A to E (Article 9).
- ~40 prior acts repealed (Article 270), most from 21 April 2021.
- Entry into force 20 April 2016; application 21 April 2021.
- Maximum 5 pet animals (dogs, cats, ferrets) per non-commercial movement (Article 246).
- Records retained at least 3 years (Articles 102 to 105).
- Penalties notified by 22 April 2022; Commission evaluation by 22 April 2026.
