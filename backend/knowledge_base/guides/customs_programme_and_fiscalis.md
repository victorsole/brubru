# Customs Programme + Fiscalis: TAXUD's funding instruments

## QUICK FACTS
- **Three distinct DG TAXUD funding instruments**, all under the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework, all managed by the Directorate-General for Taxation and Customs Union (DG TAXUD):
  1. **Customs Programme** -- Regulation (EU) 2021/444 (CELEX: 32021R0444), budget **EUR 950 million** (2021-2027, current prices). Funds the Trans-European electronic customs systems (TARIC, NCTS, ICS2, CDS, SURV3, REX and others -- see `eu_customs_electronic_systems`), joint actions between national customs administrations (seminars, workshops, expert teams, working visits), and EU-level training (eLearning modules, educational videos).
  2. **Customs Control Equipment Instrument (CCEI)** -- Regulation (EU) 2021/1077 (CELEX: 32021R1077), part of the Integrated Border Management Fund, budget **EUR 1,006,407,000 (~EUR 1 billion)** (2021-2027). Buys, maintains and upgrades physical customs control equipment at the EU's external borders: scanners, automated number-plate/container-recognition systems, sniffer-dog teams, mobile sample-analysis laboratories.
  3. **Fiscalis Programme** -- Regulation (EU) 2021/847 (CELEX: 32021R0847), budget **EUR 269 million** (2021-2027). Funds tax-administration cooperation: electronic tax systems (VIES, Eurofisc data-sharing tools), joint audits and risk-management actions, eLearning/nano-learning modules, expert networks and seminars between national tax authorities.
- **Beneficiaries are national customs and tax authorities, NOT private companies.** A business asking "is there EU money for our customs software/scanner/compliance tooling" is asking the wrong question of the wrong instrument: these three programmes fund the public administrations that run the Customs Union and tax cooperation, not private-sector vendors or importers/exporters directly (equipment manufacturers may bid as suppliers into a Member State's CCEI-funded procurement, but the grant beneficiary is the Member State authority).
- **Eligible countries beyond the 27 Member States**: acceding countries, candidate countries, potential candidates, and European Neighbourhood Policy (ENP) countries with sufficient legislative alignment can participate in Customs Programme and Fiscalis actions. Confirmed participants include Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Kosovo, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Turkey (Customs Programme) and Ukraine.
- **EU Funding & Tenders Portal prefixes**: `CUST` (Customs Programme) and `FISC` (Fiscalis Programme) -- use these to find live calls for proposals/tenders on the Funding & Tenders Portal.
- **TADEUS** (Tax Administration EU Summit): the forum of Heads and Deputy Heads of EU Member States' tax administrations, created June 2018 in Thessaloniki, meeting at least twice a year with the Commission to coordinate reforms and operational cooperation (fed into DAC7 preparatory work and the Eurofisc Advisory Board). TADEUS is the governance/coordination layer that Fiscalis-funded joint actions and IT systems support -- not itself a funding instrument.
- **Related systems these programmes finance or connect to**: AEO (Authorised Economic Operator) authorisation infrastructure, UCC (Union Customs Code) electronic systems, EMCS (Excise Movement and Control System), VIES (VAT Information Exchange System), Import Control System 2 (ICS2).
- **Distinguish from substantive policy guides**: `eu_customs_reform` and `union_customs_code_reform` cover the SUBSTANTIVE legal overhaul of EU customs (EU Customs Authority, EU Customs Data Hub, e-commerce de-minimis abolition). `eu_corporate_tax_policy_2026` and `vat_in_the_digital_age_2026` cover SUBSTANTIVE tax policy and VAT rules. This guide (`customs_programme_and_fiscalis`) covers only the FUNDING INSTRUMENTS that pay for the IT systems, equipment, training and cooperation underpinning that substantive law -- it does not itself set customs or tax rules.

## The three instruments and their envelopes

| Instrument | Regulation | CELEX | Budget 2021-2027 | Fund family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customs Programme | Regulation (EU) 2021/444 | 32021R0444 | EUR 950 million | Standalone programme |
| Customs Control Equipment Instrument (CCEI) | Regulation (EU) 2021/1077 | 32021R1077 | EUR ~1.006 billion | Integrated Border Management Fund |
| Fiscalis Programme | Regulation (EU) 2021/847 | 32021R0847 | EUR 269 million | Standalone programme |

All three run for the same 2021-2027 period as the current Multiannual Financial Framework and are managed directly by DG TAXUD (not shared management with Member States in the way structural funds are).

## Customs Programme (Regulation (EU) 2021/444)

Adopted 11 March 2021. Successor to the 2014-2020 Customs 2020 programme. Finances:
- **Trans-European IT systems**: development, maintenance and operation of the central customs electronic systems that Member States plug into -- see `eu_customs_electronic_systems` for the full system inventory (TARIC, NCTS Phase 5, ICS2, CDS, SURV3, QUOTA2, REX, EBTI, PoUS, GUM). Under the pending Customs Reform, the programme would also fund build-out of the EU Customs Data Hub.
- **Joint actions**: seminars, workshops, expert teams, working visits, and monitoring activities that bring together customs officials from different Member States (and participating third countries) to align practice.
- **Human competency building**: EU-level training curricula, eLearning modules and educational videos for customs officials.
- **Innovation**: studies, communication activities, pilot projects and prototyping of new customs approaches.

## Customs Control Equipment Instrument (Regulation (EU) 2021/1077)

Adopted 6 July 2021, part of the Integrated Border Management Fund (alongside the Border Management and Visa Instrument). Runs 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2027 with a financial envelope of EUR 1,006,407,000 (current prices).

Finances the purchase, maintenance and upgrading of customs control equipment used at the EU's external borders and at points of entry/exit, including:
- Scanning equipment (container, vehicle, luggage, postal-parcel scanners)
- Automated number-plate and container-code recognition systems
- Detection dog teams
- Mobile laboratories for sample analysis
- Related preparation, monitoring, audit and evaluation activities

The CCEI is the instrument DG TAXUD points to when businesses or equipment manufacturers ask about EU customs-equipment money -- but the grant beneficiary is always the Member State customs authority procuring the equipment, not the manufacturer. Equipment manufacturers can only participate as suppliers responding to a national authority's procurement, which is why DG TAXUD ran information sessions for equipment manufacturers in October and December 2021 to explain how to position for those national tenders.

## Fiscalis Programme (Regulation (EU) 2021/847)

Adopted 20 May 2021. Successor to the 2014-2020 Fiscalis 2020 programme. Finances:
- **European electronic tax systems**: development and operation of systems such as VIES (VAT Information Exchange System) and the data-sharing infrastructure behind Eurofisc (the EU's VAT-fraud early-warning network).
- **Collaborative activities**: seminars, workshops, expert networks, joint audits and joint risk-management actions between national tax administrations.
- **Training**: eLearning modules, educational videos, nano-learning courses for tax officials.
- **Innovation**: studies, communication, pilots and prototyping of new cooperation tools.

Fiscalis money underwrites the operational/IT layer that supports EU tax-cooperation initiatives such as DAC (Directive on Administrative Cooperation) amendments, VAT in the Digital Age (see `vat_in_the_digital_age_2026`) reporting infrastructure, and the TADEUS coordination forum below.

## Who is eligible (the recurring misunderstanding)

Both the Customs Programme and Fiscalis are grant/procurement instruments aimed at **public administrations**, not businesses:
- Primary beneficiaries: EU Member State customs and tax authorities.
- Extended eligibility: acceding countries, candidate countries, potential candidates, and ENP countries with sufficient legislative alignment (confirmed participants: Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Kosovo, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Turkey, Ukraine, varying by programme and year).
- A private company cannot apply directly for a Customs Programme or Fiscalis grant to fund its own compliance software or customs brokerage. It can bid as a contractor/supplier if a Member State authority runs a public procurement using CCEI or Customs Programme funds, or it can respond to DG TAXUD's own service tenders (published on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal under the `CUST` and `FISC` programme codes, and listed on the DG TAXUD tenders-and-grants page).

## TADEUS (Tax Administration EU Summit)

Created June 2018 at a meeting in Thessaloniki. Brings together the Heads and Deputy Heads of Member States' tax administrations with the Commission, meeting at least twice a year. TADEUS has fed into preparatory work for DAC7 (Directive (EU) 2021/514, tax transparency rules for digital platforms) and into governance reform of the Eurofisc network (including setting up an Advisory Board). TADEUS itself does not disburse funding -- it is the political/administrative coordination layer whose recommendations are then implemented through Fiscalis-funded joint actions and IT systems.

## Trans-European systems funded

Systems whose EU-level development, hosting and maintenance the Customs Programme (customs side) or Fiscalis (tax side) pays for:
- **Customs side**: TARIC, NCTS Phase 5, ICS2, CDS, SURV3, QUOTA2, REX, EBTI, PoUS, GUM (full detail in `eu_customs_electronic_systems`), and prospectively the EU Customs Data Hub under the Customs Reform.
- **Tax side**: VIES (VAT Information Exchange System), Eurofisc data-sharing tools, and the electronic systems underpinning EMCS (Excise Movement and Control System, itself established under separate excise legislation but operationally supported through Fiscalis cooperation).

## Cross-link to substantive customs/tax guides

- `eu_customs_reform` -- the SUBSTANTIVE legal overhaul (EU Customs Authority, EU Customs Data Hub, e-commerce de-minimis abolition, EUR 3 flat duty). The Customs Programme is the funding vehicle that would finance the Data Hub build-out if/when the reform is adopted; it does not itself change customs rules.
- `union_customs_code_reform` -- the SUBSTANTIVE Union Customs Code framework being reformed.
- `eu_customs_electronic_systems` -- the full inventory of Trans-European customs IT systems that Customs Programme money funds.
- `eu_corporate_tax_policy_2026` -- SUBSTANTIVE corporate tax policy (BEFIT, Pillar Two, etc), unrelated to Fiscalis funding mechanics.
- `vat_in_the_digital_age_2026` -- SUBSTANTIVE VAT reform whose reporting infrastructure Fiscalis money helps build and operate.
- `eu_commission_funding_programmes_map` -- where these three instruments sit within the wider EU funding-programmes landscape.
- `aeo_authorised_economic_operator` (planned) -- AEO status is a customs facilitation status for traders, administered using systems the Customs Programme funds, but AEO itself is a substantive customs-law status, not a funding instrument.

## Brubru engagement angles

Users asking variants of "is there EU money to help my company with customs compliance / buy scanning equipment / digitalise our tax reporting" need a redirect, not a grant list: these three DG TAXUD instruments fund national AUTHORITIES, not businesses. The correct answer distinguishes:
1. If the user is a national customs/tax authority or an ENP/candidate-country administration: point them to the Customs Programme, CCEI or Fiscalis calls on the Funding & Tenders Portal (`CUST`/`FISC` prefixes) and the DG TAXUD tenders-and-grants page.
2. If the user is a private business: there is no direct grant here. The relevant angle is (a) becoming a supplier into a Member State's CCEI-funded equipment procurement, or (b) tracking the trans-European systems (ICS2, NCTS, EU Customs Data Hub) these programmes build, since businesses must integrate with them regardless of who paid for them.
3. If the user is asking about substantive customs or tax rule changes (not funding), redirect to `eu_customs_reform`, `union_customs_code_reform`, `eu_corporate_tax_policy_2026` or `vat_in_the_digital_age_2026` -- this guide is about who pays for the plumbing, not what the plumbing does.
