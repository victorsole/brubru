## How SANT came to be: the 10th-term split

Until the end of the 9th parliamentary term (2019-2024), health and environment were handled by a single committee: ENVI (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety). The combined remit made ENVI one of the largest and most overburdened committees in the Parliament, covering everything from the European Green Deal to COVID-19 recovery legislation and the pharmaceutical framework.

When MEPs were elected in June 2024 and the new Parliament constituted itself in July, the Conference of Presidents and the Committee on Constitutional Affairs agreed to split the health dossier into a dedicated subcommittee. SANT was formally established under Annex VI of the Rules of Procedure as a subcommittee of ENVI, with its own chair, bureau and rapporteurs but remaining formally subordinate to ENVI for procedural purposes. In practice, SANT operates with considerable autonomy: it holds its own hearings, adopts its own reports and opinions, and maintains its own work programme.

The rationale was straightforward. The pharmaceutical legislation reform alone (two files: a recast directive and a new regulation replacing the medicines code from 2001 and the centralised procedure regulation from 2004) ran to hundreds of amendments over two years. The EHDS, the Critical Medicines Act, the Cancer Plan, and the cardiovascular and mental health strategies further crowded the calendar. A focused subcommittee allows MEPs with health expertise to dedicate their full attention to this legislative load.

Adam Jarubas (EPP, Poland) was elected Chair of SANT in the committee constitutive session in July 2024.

## Mandate and formal remit

Under the 10th-term Rules of Procedure (Annex VI), SANT's remit covers:

- **Public health policy** in the broad sense: health promotion, disease prevention, health inequalities
- **Pharmaceuticals and medical devices**: authorisation, pharmacovigilance, pricing and reimbursement (to the extent the EP has a role), shortages and security of supply
- **Health security**: pandemic preparedness, HERA (Health Emergency preparedness and Response Authority), Joint Action on Health Security, cross-border health threats regulation
- **EU4Health programme** (2021-2027): implementation monitoring, annual work programmes, financial execution
- **EU Beating Cancer Plan**: monitoring, flagship initiative delivery, Parliament's scrutiny role
- **European Health Data Space**: secondary use of health data, patient access rights, data governance
- **Rare diseases and orphan medicinal products**: incentive framework, COMP (Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products at EMA), unmet need assessments
- **Antimicrobial resistance**: One Health action plan, AMR voucher schemes in the pharma reform, ECDC contributions
- **Mental health**: EU Comprehensive Approach to Mental Health (June 2023), implementation tracking
- **Cardiovascular health**: EU Cardiovascular Health Plan (February 2024), related screening and prevention measures

Food safety proper (EFSA, pesticide maximum residue levels, novel foods, food additives, contaminants) remains with ENVI, as does the environmental chapter of health (air quality, water quality, chemicals). The line is not always sharp: the Carcinogens and Mutagens Directive spans both SANT (health outcomes) and ENVI (environmental exposure thresholds).

## Current legislative files

### Pharmaceutical legislation reform
Two interlinked files: a recast Directive (2023/0132(COD)) replacing Directive 2001/83/EC and a new Regulation (2023/0131(COD)) replacing Regulation (EC) 726/2004. Commission proposals adopted 26 April 2023. A provisional trilogue agreement was reached 11 December 2025; formal adoption in the Council and Parliament was pending as of June 2026. Key changes include: revised data exclusivity (8 years data protection + 1 year market protection, maximum 11 years with bonuses); a transferable data exclusivity voucher for priority antimicrobials; streamlined centralised procedure; regulatory sand-box for innovative medicines. See `eu_pharmaceutical_legislation_reform`.

### Critical Medicines Act
Legislative proposal to reduce strategic dependencies in the supply of critical medicines, active pharmaceutical ingredients and key starting materials. Builds on the Critical Medicines Alliance work and the shortages framework. See `critical_medicines_act`.

### European Health Data Space (EHDS)
Regulation establishing a specific health-data ecosystem for primary use (patient access, cross-border care) and secondary use (research, policy, public interest). Trilogue concluded; final text under formal adoption process. Involves joint work with LIBE on data protection safeguards. See `european_health_data_space`.

### Europe's Beating Cancer Plan -- implementation monitoring
The Cancer Plan (COM(2021) 44) is a Commission initiative; SANT's role in this term is monitoring delivery of the 10 flagship initiatives and the EUR 4 billion commitment. A SANT draft report on implementation was under consideration in April 2026 (PE786.738v01-00). See `eu_beating_cancer_plan`.

### EU Cardiovascular Health Plan
Commission initiative (February 2024) addressing cardiovascular disease as the leading cause of death in the EU. SANT monitors implementation and may initiate own-initiative reports on screening targets and prevention frameworks. See `eu_cardiovascular_health_plan`.

### Mental health
The Commission's Comprehensive Approach to Mental Health (June 2023) set out flagship actions on prevention, access to care and recovery. SANT scrutinises funding under EU4Health and coordinates with EMPL on workplace mental health.

### EU4Health 2021-2027
The EUR 5.1 billion health programme is in its final implementation years. SANT has budgetary oversight alongside BUDG and scrutinises annual work programmes under comitology. See `eu4health_2027_priorities`.

### Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)
The EU One Health AMR Action Plan runs to 2027. SANT coordinates with the Commission on the EMA/ECDC joint AMR report, monitors the AMR voucher mechanism introduced in the pharma reform, and liaises with the WHO Global Action Plan. See `antimicrobial_resistance_eu`.

### Rare diseases
The Regulation on orphan medicinal products (Regulation (EC) 141/2000) is under revision as part of the broader pharma reform. SANT also covers the EU Rare Diseases policy framework, the EUCERD/ECRD platform recommendations and EMA's COMP role. See `eu_rare_diseases_policy`.

## Coordination with other committees

### SANT and ENVI
As formal parent committee, ENVI retains authority over the committee bureau for joint meetings. In practice the two committees coordinate via regular bureau contacts. Files that straddle both remits (cancer and environmental carcinogens, One Health AMR, food safety aspects of pharmaceutical packaging) trigger joint SANT-ENVI consideration. ENVI maintains full ownership of the Carcinogens and Mutagens Directive, REACH amendments affecting health, and climate-health nexus initiatives.

### SANT and EMPL
Occupational health is led by EMPL. When SANT files (for example, pharmaceutical workforce regulation, workplace mental health) touch employment conditions, EMPL provides an associated opinion. The Carcinogens and Mutagens Directive (occupational exposure) is an EMPL lead with SANT associated.

### SANT and LIBE
EHDS secondary data use triggers a mandatory LIBE opinion on data protection and fundamental rights compliance. Any AI-in-healthcare file with implications for medical confidentiality similarly routes through LIBE. SANT and LIBE maintain a dialogue on the AI Act's Article 6 classification of high-risk AI in healthcare, though IMCO leads on the AI Act proper.

### SANT and IMCO
AI Act provisions for medical devices classified as high-risk AI (Class IIb and Class III under the MDR/IVDR) are led by IMCO; SANT gives an opinion. Medtech market access and notified bodies (under MDR/IVDR) sit at the intersection of both committees.

### SANT and HERA
The Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA), established under Commission Decision C(2021) 6712, sits inside DG SANTE. SANT scrutinises HERA's annual reports, budget execution and pandemic preparedness exercises. See `dg_hera_overview`.

## Brubru engagement angles

**Pharmaceutical and biotech companies**: SANT is the primary EP contact point for the pharma reform, Critical Medicines Act, EHDS secondary use and AMR incentives. Engagement includes SANT hearings, shadow rapporteur bilateral meetings and written contributions to draft reports.

**Medical devices and diagnostics (medtech)**: AI Act and MDR/IVDR intersection is handled jointly between SANT and IMCO. Companies seeking to influence classification criteria and post-market surveillance requirements engage both committees.

**Patient organisations and public-health NGOs**: SANT regularly invites patient groups to hearings. The Cancer Plan, rare diseases framework and mental health strategy are the most active dossiers for non-governmental stakeholders.

**Health ministries and Member State delegations**: Although the Council's Health Council configuration is the government-side interlocutor, SANT rapporteurs frequently engage national officials and DG SANTE bilaterally. Permanent representations in Brussels monitor SANT committee votes closely.

**Research institutions and academia**: EU4Health and Horizon Europe health missions (Cancer Mission, Rare Diseases) are scrutinised by SANT. EPRS commissions studies directly relevant to research funders and universities.
