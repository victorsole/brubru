# ENISA: European Union Agency for Cybersecurity

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) -- the acronym is retained from the agency's original 2004 name (European Network and Information Security Agency)
- **Founding act:** Regulation (EU) 2019/881 of 17 April 2019 (the Cybersecurity Act) gave ENISA a **permanent mandate** and a new role in EU cybersecurity certification; CELEX 32019R0881
- **Predecessor mandate:** Regulation (EC) No 460/2004 (established the agency, 2004); most recently renewed under Regulation (EU) No 526/2013 before the 2019 Cybersecurity Act made the mandate permanent and repealed 526/2013
- **Seat:** Legally seated in **Heraklion, Crete**; agency headquarters and most operational staff are based in the **Athens** metropolitan area under a Seat Agreement with the Hellenic Republic, with a branch office retained in Heraklion (research, analysis, administrative functions) and a liaison office in Brussels
- **Executive Director:** Juhan Lepassaar (Estonia), in post since October 2019; mandate renewed for a second five-year term in 2024
- **Governance:** Management Board (one representative per Member State + two Commission representatives), Executive Board (a smaller steering subset), National Liaison Officers Network, and a permanent stakeholders' group (the ENISA Advisory Group) representing industry, consumer and academic interests
- **Core mandate areas:** EU cybersecurity policy support, capacity building in Member States, operational cooperation (CSIRTs Network secretariat, EU-CyCLONe support), the European Cybersecurity Certification Framework, market surveillance/knowledge consolidation, and research/foresight on emerging threats
- **EU cybersecurity certification framework (ECCF):** ENISA prepares candidate certification schemes for the Commission and the EU Cybersecurity Certification Group; the first scheme adopted was EUCC (Common Criteria-based, ICT products), followed by EUCS (cloud services) work
- **CSIRTs Network:** ENISA provides the secretariat for the network of national Computer Security Incident Response Teams under NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555); CERT-EU (the EU institutions' own CERT) participates as an observer
- **NIS Cooperation Group:** ENISA supports (without formal membership) the strategic cooperation body bringing together Member State representatives, the Commission and ENISA on NIS2 implementation
- **ENISA Threat Landscape (ETL):** flagship annual report, most recent edition ENISA Threat Landscape 2025 (published October 2025), analysing thousands of reported incidents over the preceding 12 months; also publishes sectoral threat landscapes (e.g. public administration, health, transport)
- **Cyber Europe exercises:** ENISA has organised the biennial "Cyber Europe" cross-border crisis-management exercise series since 2010, alongside smaller exercises such as BlueOLEx for executive-level crisis coordination
- **Related bodies ENISA works alongside:** ECCC (European Cybersecurity Industrial, Technology and Research Competence Centre, Bucharest, Regulation (EU) 2021/887) runs cybersecurity funding programmes; CERT-EU protects EU institutions; ENISA itself has no funding-disbursement role
- **Underpins:** NIS2 Directive implementation, the Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, product security), the Cyber Solidarity Act (Regulation (EU) 2025/38, in force 4 February 2025, European Cybersecurity Alert System of national/cross-border cyber hubs), and DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, financial-sector operational resilience)
- **Cybersecurity Act 2 (pending):** Commission proposal COM(2026)11, published 20 January 2026, to revise the 2019 Cybersecurity Act, including ENISA's mandate; procedure 2026/0011(COD); EP lead committee ITRE, rapporteur Marketa Gregorova (Greens/EFA, Czechia) -- see `cybersecurity_act` guide for the full legislative file

ENISA is the EU's standing technical agency for cybersecurity. It does not regulate directly (it has no enforcement powers over companies or Member States) but it is the operational backbone that makes NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, the Cyber Solidarity Act and the EU cybersecurity certification framework function day to day: running the CSIRT secretariat, publishing the threat intelligence the rest of the EU cybersecurity architecture relies on, drafting certification schemes, and coordinating cross-border exercises.

## Legal Basis and Mandate

- **Regulation (EU) 2019/881** (the "Cybersecurity Act") is a two-part instrument: Title I re-establishes ENISA with a permanent mandate (replacing the previous time-limited renewals), and Title II creates the EU cybersecurity certification framework.
- Legal basis: Article 114 TFEU (internal market).
- The 2019 Act repealed the prior mandate regulation, Regulation (EU) No 526/2013.
- A 2025 amendment, **Regulation (EU) 2025/37**, extended ENISA's certification remit to **managed security services** (applied from 15 January 2025).
- Consolidated text: CELEX 02019R0881-20250204.
- ENISA's mandate is under review as part of the pending "Cybersecurity Act 2" proposal (COM(2026)11); see `cybersecurity_act` guide for procedure detail, rapporteur and status.

## What ENISA Actually Does

### 1. Policy and legislative support
ENISA advises the Commission, Council and Parliament on cybersecurity legislation, drafts implementing guidance, and produces technical input feeding into files such as NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the AI Act's cybersecurity-of-AI dimension.

### 2. Operational cooperation
- **Secretariat of the CSIRTs Network** (Article 15 of NIS2, Directive (EU) 2022/2555): the operational layer where national Computer Security Incident Response Teams exchange information on incidents, vulnerabilities and near misses, and can coordinate cross-border response. CERT-EU participates as observer.
- Support to **EU-CyCLONe** (the European cyber crisis liaison network organisation for large-scale incident and crisis management at operational/political level).
- Support to the **NIS Cooperation Group**, the strategic body steering NIS2 implementation across Member States.

### 3. EU Cybersecurity Certification Framework (ECCF)
Title II of the Cybersecurity Act tasks ENISA with preparing candidate European cybersecurity certification schemes at the request of the Commission or the EU Cybersecurity Certification Group (composed of national certification authorities). Certified schemes create EU-wide recognised certificates for ICT products, services and processes, replacing fragmented national schemes. The first scheme adopted under this framework was **EUCC** (Common Criteria-based scheme for ICT products); work on a cloud services scheme (**EUCS**) and, since the 2025 amendment, managed security services has followed.

### 4. Capacity building
Training, awareness campaigns (European Cybersecurity Month, run jointly with the Commission each October), support for national cybersecurity strategies, and skills initiatives connected to the Commission's Cybersecurity Skills Academy.

### 5. Knowledge consolidation and foresight
- **ENISA Threat Landscape (ETL):** the agency's flagship annual report, drawing on open-source intelligence, anonymised Member State data and the ENISA Cyber Threat Landscape methodology to map the year's incident trends (e.g. ransomware, hacktivism, supply-chain attacks, AI-enabled phishing).
- Sectoral threat landscape reports (public administration, health, transport, telecom, space).
- Foresight work on emerging technologies (AI, post-quantum cryptography, 5G/6G).

### 6. Exercises
- **Cyber Europe:** biennial, large-scale, cross-border cyber crisis simulation series since 2010, involving public and private-sector participants from the EU and EFTA states plus EU institutions/agencies.
- **BlueOLEx:** shorter executive-level exercise testing EU cybersecurity crisis-management coordination at the political/strategic tier.

## Governance and Structure

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Management Board | One representative per Member State + two Commission representatives; sets strategic direction, adopts the work programme and budget |
| Executive Board | Smaller steering subset of the Management Board, prepares decisions |
| Executive Director | Day-to-day management and legal representation of the agency; currently Juhan Lepassaar (Estonia) |
| National Liaison Officers Network | One nominated contact per Member State, coordinates with national authorities |
| ENISA Advisory Group | Stakeholder body (industry, consumer groups, academia) providing input on the work programme |
| EU Cybersecurity Certification Group | National certification authorities; works with ENISA on certification schemes (formally a Commission expert group, not part of ENISA's own governance, but central to Title II implementation) |

## ENISA in the Wider EU Cybersecurity Architecture

| Body | Role | Relationship to ENISA |
|------|------|----------------------|
| ENISA | EU cybersecurity agency: threat intelligence, certification, CSIRT secretariat, exercises | -- |
| CERT-EU | Protects EU institutions', bodies' and agencies' own networks | Observer in the CSIRTs Network ENISA runs the secretariat for; operational cooperation partner |
| ECCC (Bucharest) | Disburses EU cybersecurity funding (Digital Europe Programme, Horizon Europe Cluster 3, CEF Digital cybersecurity strand) | Funding counterpart; ENISA has no disbursement role |
| National Coordination Centres (Article 6, Regulation (EU) 2021/887) | Member State contact points for ECCC funding | Coordinate with ENISA's National Liaison Officers on capacity building |
| EU Cybersecurity Certification Group | Approves/steers certification schemes | ENISA prepares the technical candidate schemes this group adopts |

## Why This Matters for Advocacy and Compliance

- **Certification strategy:** companies planning to seek an EU cybersecurity certificate (EUCC, EUCS, managed security services) should track ENISA's scheme-preparation work, not just the Commission's adoption decisions -- ENISA runs the technical consultations.
- **Incident reporting:** organisations covered by NIS2 report to national CSIRTs, which sit inside the ENISA-serviced CSIRTs Network; understanding this architecture clarifies who actually receives and acts on a mandatory incident notification.
- **Threat intelligence:** the annual ENISA Threat Landscape report is a primary, citable EU source for sector risk assessments, board papers and compliance justifications -- more authoritative for EU-specific claims than private-sector vendor reports.
- **Cybersecurity Act 2 file:** ENISA's own mandate, budget and certification remit are being renegotiated in Parliament (ITRE, rapporteur Gregorova) -- stakeholders with a certification or CSIRT-cooperation interest should track this file directly (see `cybersecurity_act` guide).
- **Cyber Solidarity Act rollout:** the European Cybersecurity Alert System (national and cross-border cyber hubs) is a newer ENISA-adjacent structure (Regulation (EU) 2025/38, in force since 4 February 2025) relevant to critical-infrastructure operators seeking EU-level threat-sharing access.
