## Why This Reform

The EU humanitarian aid system faces three structural pressures driving the 2026 reform:

1. **Scale:** Global humanitarian needs reached USD 54.9 billion in 2025 (UN OCHA), a 10-fold increase since 2010. EU humanitarian budgets have not kept pace.
2. **Politicisation:** Humanitarian access is increasingly weaponised — particularly in Gaza (Israeli restrictions), Sudan (warring factions blocking aid), and Myanmar. EU principles of impartiality and neutrality come under pressure.
3. **Coordination:** The EU humanitarian system operates through 200+ partner NGOs, UN agencies, and Red Cross/ICRM. Fragmented implementation reduces efficiency and accountability.

## Key Measures in the 2026 Communication

The Communication "Defending values, driving reform, delivering impact" proposes:

### 1. Principled Humanitarian Action
- Reaffirm the EU's commitment to International Humanitarian Law (IHL), particularly the principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence
- Strengthen mechanisms to ensure aid reaches affected populations regardless of political context (including conflict zones with contested access)
- Dedicated dialogue track with humanitarian organisations on IHL compliance by all parties

### 2. Operational Reform
- Streamline ECHO partner accreditation and grant management (reduce admin burden for NGOs)
- Expand multi-year funding for protracted crises (currently most ECHO grants are 12-month; move toward 36-month for chronic crises like DRC, Sahel, Rohingya)
- Strengthen the EU Civil Protection Mechanism integration with humanitarian response (rescEU + ECHO coordination)

### 3. Impact Measurement
- New EU Humanitarian Aid Monitoring Framework with standardised KPIs
- Mandatory 3% accountability allocation (for evaluations, learning, complaints mechanisms)
- Annual public impact report to EP and Council

### 4. Strategic Funding
- Call for increased overall ECHO budget under MFF 2028-2034
- New "Crisis Reserve" within NDICI-Global Europe for shock-responsive funding (faster disbursement than standard grant cycles)
