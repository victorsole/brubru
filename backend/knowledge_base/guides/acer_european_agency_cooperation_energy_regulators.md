# ACER: European Union Agency for the Cooperation of Energy Regulators

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: European Union Agency for the Cooperation of Energy Regulators (ACER)
- Founding regulation: Regulation (EC) No 713/2009 (13 July 2009, Third Energy Package)
- Current legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2019/942 of 5 June 2019 (recast), CELEX 32019R0942
- Amended by: Regulation (EU) 2024/1106 (REMIT II), in force 7 May 2024, which strengthened ACER's cross-border investigatory powers
- Headquarters: Ljubljana, Slovenia (Trg republike 3)
- Type: EU decentralised agency, separate legal personality from the Commission
- Governance: Director (day-to-day management), Board of Regulators (regulatory policy, one senior representative per national regulatory authority), Administrative Board (9 members: 2 Commission, 2 Parliament, 5 Council), Board of Appeal (independent appeals body)
- Core mandate: electricity and gas network codes and guidelines; binding decisions on cross-border infrastructure access terms/conditions/methodologies; oversight of ENTSO-E and ENTSOG (and, since 2024, ENNOH for hydrogen); wholesale energy market monitoring and enforcement support under REMIT
- REMIT: Regulation (EU) No 1227/2011 on wholesale energy market integrity and transparency, revised by REMIT II (Regulation (EU) 2024/1106, in force 7 May 2024) which gave ACER direct investigatory powers (on-site inspections, information requests, statements) in cross-border market-abuse cases; ACER's own cross-border investigations begin Q4 2026; sanctioning remains with national regulatory authorities (NRAs)
- Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas Market Package (May 2024): Directive (EU) 2024/1788 + Regulation (EU) 2024/1789 extended ACER's mandate to dedicated hydrogen infrastructure; ACER reviews the statutory documents of ENNOH (European Network of Network Operators for Hydrogen) and issues an opinion to the Commission
- Ten-Year Network Development Plans (TYNDPs): ACER opines on and monitors the EU-wide electricity and gas TYNDPs prepared by ENTSO-E and ENTSOG
- Responsible DG: DG ENER (Energy)
- Key stakeholders: 27 National Regulatory Authorities (NRAs), ENTSO-E, ENTSOG, ENNOH, European Commission, EP ITRE committee

## Overview

ACER is the EU's decentralised regulatory agency for electricity and gas markets. It was created by the Third Energy Package (Regulation (EC) No 713/2009) to close the gap between national energy regulators and give the internal energy market a genuinely European regulatory layer. Regulation (EU) 2019/942 recast and strengthened ACER's powers as part of the Clean Energy for All Europeans package, giving it a stronger role in cross-border infrastructure decisions and regional coordination.

ACER does not regulate energy companies directly. Its core method is coordinating and, where Member States cannot agree, taking binding decisions among the 27 National Regulatory Authorities (NRAs) that form the Board of Regulators. It also drafts and monitors implementation of EU-wide network codes and guidelines that the Commission adopts as binding legislation, and it is the market-monitoring authority for wholesale electricity and gas trading under REMIT.

Two 2024 legislative developments substantially widened ACER's remit: REMIT II gave the agency its own cross-border investigatory powers in wholesale market-abuse cases (a first for ACER, which previously only supported NRA-led enforcement), and the Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas Market Package extended ACER's oversight into dedicated hydrogen infrastructure and the new hydrogen network operators' body, ENNOH.

## Governance Structure

| Body | Composition | Role |
|------|-------------|------|
| Director | Single head, appointed by Administrative Board | Legal representative; day-to-day management; adopts individual decisions (with Board of Regulators approval where required) |
| Board of Regulators | Senior representative from each of the 27 NRAs, plus non-voting members from EEA EFTA states, the Commission and the EFTA Surveillance Authority | Decides regulatory policy; approves opinions, recommendations, framework guidelines and the work programme; decisions require a two-thirds majority of members present; members must act independently of national governments |
| Administrative Board | 9 members and 9 alternates: 2 appointed by the Commission, 2 by the European Parliament, 5 by the Council | Appoints the Director; adopts the budget; general administrative oversight; adopts rules of procedure |
| Board of Appeal | Independent members, own rules of procedure | Hears appeals against ACER decisions before any onward appeal to the EU Court of Justice |

## Core Functions

### 1. Network Codes and Guidelines
ACER drafts framework guidelines and reviews the network codes developed by ENTSO-E (electricity) and ENTSOG (gas), which the Commission then adopts as directly applicable EU regulations covering capacity allocation, congestion management, balancing, connection rules and system operation.

### 2. Cross-Border Infrastructure Decisions
Where NRAs cannot agree on terms, conditions or methodologies for cross-border infrastructure (interconnectors, capacity calculation regions), ACER takes a binding decision. These decisions are appealable to ACER's own Board of Appeal.

### 3. Market Monitoring and REMIT Enforcement Support
ACER collects and analyses wholesale electricity and gas transaction data (via the REMIT Data Reference Centre and Inside Information Access Point) to detect insider trading and market manipulation. Since REMIT II (May 2024), ACER can itself investigate cross-border cases affecting at least two Member States, conducting on-site inspections, requesting information and taking statements, while sanctioning powers stay with NRAs. ACER's own cross-border investigations are scheduled to begin in Q4 2026.

### 4. Regional and System Planning Oversight
ACER opines on the biennial EU-wide Ten-Year Network Development Plans (TYNDPs) for electricity and gas, and coordinates Regional Coordination Centres that manage cross-border system operation.

### 5. Hydrogen and Decarbonised Gas (new since 2024)
Under Directive (EU) 2024/1788 and Regulation (EU) 2024/1789, ACER reviews the statutory documents, membership and rules of procedure of ENNOH, the new European Network of Network Operators for Hydrogen, and issues an opinion to the Commission before ENNOH's formal establishment.

## Decision Instruments

- **Opinions**: Non-binding but influential input to the Commission and co-legislators
- **Recommendations**: Guidance to NRAs, ENTSO-E, ENTSOG and ENNOH
- **Framework guidelines**: Steer the content of network codes before Commission adoption
- **Decisions**: Binding on cross-border infrastructure terms, conditions and methodologies, and (since 2024) in cross-border REMIT investigations

## Relevance for Advocacy

ACER is the single most important non-Commission actor in EU electricity and gas market design, and its expanding hydrogen and REMIT mandates make it increasingly central to energy-transition and market-integrity files. Entry points:

- Public consultations on draft network codes, framework guidelines and ENNOH statutory documents (published on ACER's website)
- National Regulatory Authority engagement (NRAs sit on the Board of Regulators and shape ACER positions from the national level)
- ENTSO-E, ENTSOG and ENNOH stakeholder processes that feed directly into ACER review
- Market Monitoring Reports (annual and thematic, including the hydrogen markets report) as evidence sources for policy submissions
- EP ITRE committee scrutiny of ACER's annual report and work programme
- Board of Appeal for entities directly affected by an ACER decision
