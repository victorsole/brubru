# EU Mobile Satellite Services — New Authorisation Proposal (2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Wednesday 27 May 2026 — COMMISSION PROPOSAL)**: The European Commission proposed a new authorisation framework for **mobile satellite services (MSS)** in the EU (27 May 2026). EVP Virkkunen stated: the proposal strengthens EU "resilience and competitiveness" in satellite connectivity. The proposal creates a streamlined, EU-wide licensing regime for mobile satellite operators, replacing the current fragmented national authorisation processes. Source: EC Press Corner IP_26_1170 + Virkkunen speech, 27 May 2026.
- Initiative: Commission proposal on mobile satellite services authorisation
- Responsible EVP: Henna Virkkunen (Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy)
- Responsible DG: DG CNECT (Communications Networks, Content and Technology)
- Policy context: Part of the broader Tech Sovereignty Package (3 June 2026, tbc) and the Digital Networks Act (DNA, COM(2026) 0016)
- Objective: Single EU authorisation for MSS operators (replace 27 national regimes), improve spectrum management, ensure service continuity for aviation/maritime/IoT use cases
- Relation to IRIS2: The proposal complements IRIS2 (EU secure connectivity satellite constellation, part of the EU Space Programme), which provides government-grade secure satcom; MSS authorisation covers commercial operators (Starlink, OneWeb, Amazon Kuiper, Inmarsat, Viasat, etc.)
- Spectrum: MSS operators use L-band (1.5/1.6 GHz) and S-band (2 GHz); the S-band was harmonised under EU Decision 626/2008/EC; this proposal updates the authorisation layer
- BEREC role: The Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications (BEREC) will play a coordination role in the new single-authorisation process

## Why This Matters

Satellite connectivity is increasingly critical for EU resilience — covering maritime routes, aviation, remote areas, and emergency communications. The current patchwork of 27 national authorisations creates regulatory friction: an operator must obtain licences in every Member State to offer EU-wide MSS coverage. This disadvantages EU operators vis-à-vis US competitors (SpaceX/Starlink operates under FCC authority across the US) and slows down the deployment of next-generation Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite networks.

The proposal is part of the Commission's broader push for "Tech Sovereignty" — ensuring European connectivity infrastructure does not depend entirely on non-EU providers. It links to:
- The **Digital Networks Act** (COM(2026) 0016) — which addresses terrestrial connectivity infrastructure
- **IRIS2** — the EU's own secure satellite constellation (public-private consortium, expected launch 2027-2030)
- **European Chips Act** — satellite chipsets are part of the sovereign semiconductor stack

## Key Players in MSS

Current major MSS operators active in the EU:
- **Inmarsat** (UK/US) — L-band, aviation/maritime/IoT
- **Viasat** (US) — Ka-band, broadband
- **Eutelsat/OneWeb** (FR/UK) — LEO, broadband; Eutelsat is the main EU-based player
- **SpaceX Starlink** (US) — LEO, consumer broadband; largest by subscriber count
- **Amazon Kuiper** (US) — LEO, expected commercial launch 2026-2027
