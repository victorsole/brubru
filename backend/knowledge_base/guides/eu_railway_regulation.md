## Overview

EU railway regulation aims to create a Single European Railway Area where train operators can run services across borders with a single safety certificate and vehicle authorisation. The regulatory framework has been built through four successive Railway Packages over 25 years, culminating in the 4th Railway Package (2016) which completed the legal framework for market opening and established the EU Agency for Railways (ERA) as a one-stop shop for safety and interoperability.

The rail sector is central to the EU's climate and mobility goals. Rail accounts for less than 1% of transport emissions while carrying 7% of freight and 7% of passengers. The European Green Deal and Sustainable and Smart Mobility Strategy target a doubling of high-speed rail traffic by 2030 and a tripling of rail freight by 2050.

## 4th Railway Package (2016)

### Technical Pillar (adopted April 2016)

| Act | Subject | Replaces |
|-----|---------|----------|
| Regulation (EU) 2016/796 | EU Agency for Railways (ERA) | Regulation (EC) 881/2004 |
| Directive (EU) 2016/797 | Railway interoperability | Directive 2008/57/EC |
| Directive (EU) 2016/798 | Railway safety | Directive 2004/49/EC |

Key changes:
- **Single safety certificate**: ERA issues a single EU-wide safety certificate, replacing separate national certificates
- **Vehicle authorisation**: ERA is the single EU-wide authority for vehicle type authorisation and placing on market
- **One-stop shop (OSS)**: ERA's digital portal for applications (safety certificates, vehicle authorisations, ERTMS trackside approvals)
- **Reduced costs**: Eliminates duplication of national procedures, cutting administrative burden for cross-border operators

### Market Pillar (adopted December 2016)

| Act | Subject | Replaces/Amends |
|-----|---------|----------------|
| Regulation (EU) 2016/2338 | Public service contracts for domestic passenger rail | Amends Regulation (EC) 1370/2007 |
| Directive (EU) 2016/2370 | Market opening and infrastructure governance | Amends Directive 2012/34/EU |
| Regulation (EU) 2016/2337 | Railway accounting normalisation | Repeals Regulation (EEC) 1192/69 |

Key changes:
- **Open access**: General operating rights for railway undertakings to run domestic passenger services across the EU
- **Competitive tendering**: Mandatory tendering for public service contracts (PSCs) from December 2023, with transition period until 2033
- **Infrastructure independence**: Strengthened independence requirements for infrastructure managers from railway operators
- **Regulatory bodies**: Enhanced powers for national regulatory bodies to ensure non-discriminatory access

## EU Agency for Railways (ERA)

ERA (established 2004, reformed 2016) is the EU's railway regulator, based in Valenciennes, France. Under Regulation (EU) 2016/796, its mandate covers:

1. **Safety and interoperability**: Develops and updates Technical Specifications for Interoperability (TSIs)
2. **Vehicle authorisation**: Single EU authority for placing railway vehicles on the market
3. **Safety certification**: Issues single safety certificates valid across the EU
4. **ERTMS**: System authority for the European Rail Traffic Management System
5. **Monitoring**: Collects and analyses safety data, publishes biennial railway safety reports
6. **Recommendations**: Issues opinions and recommendations to the Commission on railway safety and interoperability

## ERTMS (European Rail Traffic Management System)

ERTMS is the EU's flagship signalling and train control system, designed to replace 20+ incompatible national systems. It comprises:
- **ETCS** (European Train Control System): harmonised cab signalling and speed control
- **GSM-R / FRMCS**: radio communication between train and trackside

The Third ERTMS Work Plan (February 2026) sets deployment targets. Full ERTMS deployment on the TEN-T core network is targeted for 2040. The system is mandatory for new infrastructure and major upgrades on the TEN-T network.

## Previous Railway Packages

| Package | Year | Key achievement |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| 1st | 2001 | Separated infrastructure management from operations; opened freight corridors |
| 2nd | 2004 | Full opening of rail freight market; established ERA; common safety framework |
| 3rd | 2007 | Open access for international passenger services; passenger rights (Regulation 1371/2007) |
| 4th | 2016 | Domestic passenger market opening; ERA as single EU authority; one-stop shop |

## Recent Developments

- **High-speed rail plan** (November 2025): Commission strategy to better connect Europe by high-speed rail, supporting climate targets and competitiveness
- **Data sharing** (February 2026): Implementing Regulation on harmonised EU specifications for rail transport data sharing
- **ERTMS Third Work Plan** (February 2026): Updated deployment monitoring and targets
- **Rail freight corridors**: 11 European rail freight corridors under Regulation (EU) 913/2010

## Relevance for Advocacy

Railway regulation is a key domain for infrastructure companies, rolling stock manufacturers, train operators, passenger rights groups, and freight logistics providers. Entry points:
- ERA consultations on TSIs and safety targets
- EP TRAN committee hearings
- DG MOVE consultations on market opening implementation
- National regulatory body proceedings on track access and capacity allocation
- ERTMS deployment planning (national implementation plans)
- TEN-T corridor forums (cross-border infrastructure projects)
