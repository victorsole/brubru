# EU Film Financing and Support for Cinema from Smaller Member States

- Primary programme: Creative Europe (2021-2027), MEDIA strand for film/audiovisual
- Creative Europe regulation: Regulation (EU) 2021/818 (CELEX: 32021R0818), OJ L 189, 28.5.2021
- Creative Europe total budget 2021-2027: EUR 2.44 billion (EUR 1.64B for MEDIA strand, 67%)
- MEDIA strand focuses on: film production, distribution, training, audience development, innovation
- Lead DG: DG CNECT (Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology), Unit I.2 "Creative Europe"
- Responsible Commissioner: Glenn Micallef (Intergenerational Fairness, Youth, Culture and Sport, Malta)
- EP committee: CULT (Culture and Education); lead MEP: Nela Riehl (Greens/EFA, Germany) chair of CULT
- Eurimages (Council of Europe, separate fund): EUR 27.2 million in 2025, 39 member states, co-production support
- EPRS At a Glance (14 April 2026): "Support for films from smaller Member States: successes, challenges and the way forward" -- findings: smaller MS films struggle with distribution despite quality, MEDIA Slate Funding reform proposed. Ref: EPRS_UNK_5b4e7a981726
- EPRS At a Glance (14 April 2026): "Looking ahead to the future of film financing" -- platforms now account for 45% of audiovisual content consumption in EU, need for structural rebalancing. Ref: EPRS_UNK_fa898dca33b0

## Smaller Member States: The Structural Challenge

**Smaller MS in film context:** Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania), Malta, Cyprus, Slovenia, Slovakia, Luxembourg, Bulgaria, Croatia, Ireland, Czechia (some definitions include Portugal, Greece, Hungary)

**Issues identified by EPRS:**
- Limited domestic market restricts recoupment
- Difficulty competing for MEDIA selective funding against large-market incumbents (France, Germany, Italy)
- "Low capacity" bonus: MEDIA offers enhanced co-funding rates (up to 70%) for countries with low audiovisual capacity
- Eurimages co-production bonus helps but does not fully compensate
- Platforms (Netflix, Amazon Prime, Disney+) under-commission from smaller markets despite AVMSD Article 13 30% European works quota

## Future of Film Financing: Policy Debate (EPRS April 2026)

**Trends:**
- Platforms now 45% of EU audiovisual consumption (up from 25% in 2020)
- Traditional theatrical revenues down 18% vs pre-COVID baseline
- AI-generated content emerging as cost challenge and opportunity
- Private equity entering as financing partners

**Policy questions under debate:**
- Should Creative Europe MFF 2028-2034 be doubled for MEDIA strand? (CULT committee INI in progress)
- Should AVMSD Article 13 quota enforcement be stricter? Platform reporting obligations?
- Should there be an EU-level cultural investment levy on streaming subscriptions? (precedent: France, Italy national levies)
- Should Creative Europe fund AI-assisted production and archival restoration?

## Member State Cinema Support (complementary)

- France: CNC levy and automatic/selective support (EUR 700M+ annual)
- Germany: FFA + regional Filmforderung (EUR 300M+)
- Italy: DG Cinema MiC tax credit (up to 40% production credit)
- Spain: ICAA selective and automatic support
- UK (post-Brexit): BFI + Audio-Visual Expenditure Credit (25.5% for film)
- Poland: PISF selective support
- Nordic countries: coordinated Nordisk Film & TV Fond + national institutes

## Brubru User Implications

- **Independent producers and co-producers:** Creative Europe MEDIA calls open rolling; check single project development deadlines
- **Small MS film policy professionals:** advocate for MEDIA MFF increase in 2028-2034 trilogue
- **Cinema network operators:** Europa Cinemas membership enables Films on the Move participation
- **Platforms operating in EU:** AVMSD Article 13 30% quota, increasing scrutiny by national regulators (ARCOM, AGCOM, CNC, ERC)
- **Distributors:** selective distribution support is the workhorse for smaller MS titles in larger markets
- **CULT committee stakeholders:** Creative Europe evaluation report due Q2 2026, MFF negotiations decisive
