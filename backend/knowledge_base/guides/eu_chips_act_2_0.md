# Chips Act 2.0 (COM(2026) 504)

## QUICK FACTS
- **ADOPTED by the College on 3 June 2026** as part of the European Technological Sovereignty Package (with the Cloud and AI Development Act / CADA, the EU Open Source Strategy, and the Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy).
- Full name: Proposal for a Regulation on a framework of measures for strengthening the Union's semiconductor ecosystem, repealing Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2.0).
- Reference **COM(2026) 504 final**; procedure **2026/0139(COD)** (proposal CELEX 52026PC0504). Accompanying: ANNEXES I-VII; Impact Assessment SWD(2026) 504 (Parts 1-3); IA executive summary SWD(2026) 505; SEC(2026) 504.
- **Repeals Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (the first Chips Act)** — confirmed in the title, the enacting terms and the Annex VII correlation table. NOTE: the two impact-assessment SWD title lines carry a clerical "Regulation (EU) 2023/1782"; the authoritative repeal target is **2023/1781**.
- Status: PROPOSAL (ordinary legislative procedure). Goes to the EP (lead committee ITRE expected) + Council. Lead: EVP Henna Virkkunen; DG CNECT. Legal bases: Articles 173(3) + 114 TFEU. Entry into force the day after OJ publication (Art 60).
- **Why:** the EU produces **less than 10% of global semiconductors** and is almost entirely dependent on the US and Asia for advanced/leading-edge chips below 5nm (including AI chips). Semiconductors are the world's 3rd most-traded commodity (USD 700.9bn in 2025, >USD 1tn by 2026, USD 1.6tn by 2030). The first Chips Act mobilised **>EUR 52bn** under Pillar II and set up 5 pilot lines + the European Semiconductor Board (ESB), but the lab-to-fab gap, weak private capital, fragmented demand and limited supply-chain intelligence remain. Draghi report + Semicon Coalition Declaration (27 Member States, 29 Sept 2025) basis.
- **Two overarching objectives:** (1) increase the competitiveness of the European semiconductor value chain to improve technological sovereignty and resilience (accelerate industrial deployment of R&I; security of supply; reduce strategic dependencies in cutting-edge AND mature chips); (2) enhance crisis preparedness to ensure security of supply. **Three specific objectives:** SO1 capacity/security/competitiveness across the value chain incl. leading-edge AI chips; SO2 a strong user market; SO3 intelligence for crisis preparedness.
- **Shift to demand-side:** the first Act was supply-driven; 2.0 adds demand-side levers (innovation procurement, grand challenges, demand forum/accelerators, security-of-supply criterion in public procurement of European chips).
- **Three pillars (continued from the first Act):**
  - **Pillar I — Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0** (Chapter II): six operational objectives — (1) advanced design capacities (virtual design platform); (2) pilot lines + testbeds; (3) quantum chips; (4) competence centres (>=1 per Member State); (5) **photonics & photonic integrated circuits (NEW)**; (6) **Chips Fund** (start-ups/scale-ups/SMEs via the EIC Accelerator + InvestEU blending + EIB). Plus **grand challenges** (coordinated with CADA + EuroHPC JU), **demand accelerators**, **demand forum**, **chip innovation procurement**. Funded by Horizon Europe + Digital Europe Programme; implemented by the **Chips Joint Undertaking**.
  - **Pillar II — Security of Supply and Demand** (Chapter III): **European semiconductor technology initiatives (ESTIs)** — successors to the first Act's "integrated production facilities" + "open EU foundries", carried out by domestic undertakings, must be **first-of-a-kind**, must join the B2B Platform + accept priority-rated orders; status NOT conditional on public support. **Strategic projects** (six recognition criteria; Union entities, derogation for third countries; single consortium if multi-site) get the **competitiveness seal under the European Competitiveness Fund**; designation runs through the Chips Joint Undertaking. **Art 19: a Union-based open foundry for advanced semiconductor manufacturing is treated with the highest priority.** **First-of-a-kind** scope clarified to the whole value chain. **European Semiconductor Region of Excellence label** + Region Investment Plan + Network. Fast-track permitting (**<=12 months**, one-stop shop, single access portal using **European Business Wallets**). Public-procurement security-of-supply declaration for NIS2 Annex I/II sectors; risk-prone sectors (25% cost-difference presumption for disproportionate cost). The "European Chips Infrastructure Consortium (ECIC)" is **deleted** (never used).
  - **Pillar III — Monitoring and Crisis Response** (Chapter IV): strategic mapping + early-warning indicators; **Business-to-Business Semiconductor Supply Chain Platform** (a "digital twin", domestic undertakings only, participants exempt from pre-crisis info requests); key market actors; **crisis stage** activated by the **Council (qualified majority), max 12 months**; emergency toolbox — Art 41 information requests, **Art 42 priority-rated orders**, **Art 43 common purchasing**. An **EU Blueprint for semiconductor crisis management** is due by Q2 2027. Platform set-up ~EUR 70m.
- **Governance (Chapter V):** the **European Semiconductor Board (ESB)** (Member States + Commission chair) continues; national competent authorities + a national single point of contact; the **Industrial Alliance for Semiconductors** (takes over the Industrial Alliance on Processors and Semiconductor Technologies).
- **Penalties (Art 51):** fines up to EUR 300,000 (incorrect/incomplete info), EUR 150,000 (failure to report a third-country obligation), SMEs capped at EUR 50,000; periodic penalty payments up to 1.5% of daily turnover (0.5% for SMEs) for breaching priority-rated orders.
- **Annex II strategic-project priority areas (with indicative investment):** (1) **European Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing** — leading-edge node + chiplet + 2.5D/3D packaging, **EUR 20-40bn** public+private (+EUR 3-4bn for fabless design); pilot production 2030-2033; (2) **AI chips and systems** for EU compute infrastructure; (3) **automotive application processor** for autonomous driving (RISC-V); (4) **European memory fabrication facility** (DRAM/NAND/HBM), **EUR 15-30bn**; (5) **leading-edge chip design** (IP/architecture/verification, 2nm and below), **EUR 8-12bn**; (6) **strengthening key value-chain segments** (OSAT/advanced packaging, mainstream chips, materials/equipment — Nexperia case).
- Funding: Horizon Europe + Digital Europe (Pillar I backbones); European Competitiveness Fund (ECF) + FP10 + National and Regional Partnership Plans (next MFF 2028-2034); IPCEI on Advanced Semiconductor Technologies; STEP; complementary to the Critical Raw Materials Act. Evaluation 4-5 years after application.

## Overview

The Chips Act 2.0 (COM(2026) 504, 2026/0139(COD), adopted by the College on 3 June 2026) is the Commission's second-generation semiconductor industrial policy. It repeals and replaces the first European Chips Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781) with a single, more ambitious framework, keeping the three-pillar structure but rebalancing it toward demand and industrial deployment.

The driver is that the first Act, while mobilising over EUR 52 billion and building EU-level pilot lines, competence centres and a crisis board, did not close the gap: the EU still makes under 10% of the world's chips and remains almost entirely dependent on the US and Asia for advanced nodes below 5nm, including AI chips. The Draghi report and the Semicon Coalition Declaration (signed by all 27 Member States in September 2025) called for a reinforced Chips Act 2.0. Semiconductors are now the world's third most-traded commodity, with the market heading toward USD 1.6 trillion by 2030.

Chips Act 2.0 pursues two objectives: raising the competitiveness, technological sovereignty and resilience of the EU value chain, and strengthening crisis preparedness. It does so through three pillars: a reinforced Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0 (Pillar I), a security-of-supply-and-demand framework with strategic projects and a flagship advanced-manufacturing open foundry (Pillar II), and an upgraded monitoring and crisis-response mechanism built around a B2B supply-chain digital twin (Pillar III). It is a core component of the European Technological Sovereignty Package adopted the same day.

## Complete Chapter-by-Chapter Overview

### Chapter I - General Provisions (Arts 1-2)
- **Art 1 Subject matter:** continues the Chips for Europe Initiative (now 2.0); sets criteria for European semiconductor technology initiatives + strategic projects; enhances the monitoring/crisis coordination mechanism. Two general objectives (competitiveness/innovation; crisis preparedness/internal market).
- **Art 2 Definitions:** semiconductor, chip, quantum chip, pilot line, first-of-a-kind initiative, front-end/back-end, **domestic undertaking** (EU seat + Union ownership/control; OR owned by a FTA/customs-union/strategic-partnership country; OR a WTO GPA signatory), ownership = 50%+, control = Reg 2021/697, strategic project, Chips Joint Undertaking.

### Chapter II - Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0 (Pillar I, Arts 3-12)
- **Art 4 six operational objectives:** (1) advanced design capacities; (2) pilot lines + testbeds; (3) quantum chips; (4) competence centres; (5) **photonic integrated circuits (new)**; (6) **Chips Fund** (EIC Accelerator, InvestEU).
- **Art 5** virtual design platform, pilot lines, quantum-chip facilities, competence-centre tasks, photonics design libraries, Chips Fund access to finance.
- **Art 6** European network of competence centres (>=1 per Member State, built on EDIHs).
- **Art 7 grand challenges** (AI chips, energy-efficient compute, leading-edge capacities) coordinated with CADA grand challenges + EuroHPC JU.
- **Art 8 demand accelerators** + **Art 9 demand forum** (run by the Industrial Alliance for Semiconductors) + **Art 10 chip innovation procurement**.
- **Art 12** implementation entrusted to the Chips Joint Undertaking; delegated acts amend Annex I + III.

### Chapter III - Security of Supply and Demand (Pillar II, Arts 13-32)
- **Art 13** ESTIs + strategic projects are in the public interest; Member States/Commission may grant support (ECF, State aid).
- **Art 14-15 European semiconductor technology initiatives:** by domestic undertakings; first-of-a-kind; spill-over impact; B2B Platform participation if a production facility; preferential pilot-line access. Commission decides within 3 months.
- **Art 16-18 strategic projects:** six criteria (Union added value; domestic undertaking; reduces supply-chain dependence; cross-border; resilience; tech sovereignty); Union entities (third-country derogation); designated via the Chips Joint Undertaking; competitiveness seal under the ECF.
- **Art 19 advanced semiconductor manufacturing:** a Union-based **open foundry** for advanced manufacturing treated with the highest priority.
- **Art 21-25 permitting:** <=12 months from a complete application; one-stop shop (45-day completeness check); single access portal using **European Business Wallets**; online information.
- **Art 26-29 European Semiconductor Regions of Excellence:** label (Commission, on regional application + Member State endorsement), Region Investment Plan, Network.
- **Art 30-32 supply-chain resilience:** security-of-supply declaration in procurement for NIS2 Annex I/II + critical sectors; further actions; risk-prone sectors (25% cost-difference may be presumed disproportionate; mitigation: dual sourcing, stockpiling, diversification).

### Chapter IV - Monitoring and Crisis Response (Pillar III, Arts 33-43)
- **Art 33 strategic mapping** + early-warning indicators; **Art 34 B2B Semiconductor Supply Chain Platform** (digital twin, domestic undertakings only, stress tests, dashboard); **Art 35 monitoring**; **Art 36 key market actors**.
- **Art 37 alerts + preventive action**; **Art 38 preventive information gathering** (Platform participants exempt); **Art 39 crisis-stage activation** (Council by qualified majority, max 12 months, prolongable).
- **Art 40 emergency toolbox**; **Art 41 information gathering in crisis**; **Art 42 priority-rated orders** (override contracts, fair price, liability exemption); **Art 43 common purchasing** (Commission as central purchasing body for >=2 Member States).

### Chapter V - Governance (Arts 44-49)
- **Art 44-46 European Semiconductor Board** (Member States + Commission chair; one vote per Member State); **Art 47 international cooperation + strategic partnerships**; **Art 48 national competent authorities + single point of contact**; **Art 49 Industrial Alliance for Semiconductors**.

### Chapters VI-VIII - Final (Arts 50-60)
- **Art 50 confidentiality**; **Art 51-54 penalties** (fines EUR 300k/150k, SME 50k; periodic payments 1.5%/0.5% of daily turnover) + limitation periods + right to be heard; **Art 55-56 delegated/implementing acts** + Semiconductor Committee; **Art 57 evaluation** (4-5 years after application); **Art 59 repeal** of Reg (EU) 2023/1781 (transitional for existing facilities); **Art 60 entry into force** (day after OJ).

## Key Innovations (vs the first Chips Act, Reg (EU) 2023/1781)

| Area | First Chips Act (2023/1781) | Chips Act 2.0 (COM(2026) 504) |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | Supply-driven | Demand + supply (innovation procurement, demand forum/accelerators) |
| Strategic Projects | Not a distinct instrument | EU-level Strategic Projects (Annex II), competitiveness seal under the ECF |
| Flagship | Integrated production facilities / open EU foundries | Highest-priority open foundry for advanced manufacturing (Art 19) |
| Initiative scope | 5 operational objectives | 6 (adds photonics) + grand challenges + Chips Fund |
| Regional attractiveness | None | "Semiconductor Region of Excellence" label + Network |
| Supply-chain intelligence | Voluntary info-sharing | B2B Platform "digital twin" + EU crisis Blueprint (Q2 2027) |
| Permitting | General | <=12 months + one-stop shop + European Business Wallets |
| FOAK scope | Narrower | Whole value chain (design, materials, equipment, PCBs, packaging) |
| Simplification | ECIC concept | ECIC deleted (never used) |

## Impact Assessment Key Figures (SWD(2026) 504 / 505)
- EU produces <10% of global semiconductors; dependent on US + Asia below 5nm.
- Semiconductors = 3rd most-traded commodity; USD 700.9bn (2025) -> ~USD 1.6tn (2030).
- First Chips Act: >EUR 52bn committed under Pillar II; 11 First-of-a-Kind projects approved by end-2025; 5 pilot lines.
- EU permitting ~7.5 months longer than competitors (~3.125% extra cost; ~EUR 625m on a EUR 20bn fab).
- RSB: negative opinion 28 Jan 2026 -> positive with reservations 30 March 2026. Preferred option PO2 (vertical/proactive industrial policy via strategic projects under the ECF).

## Stakeholder Reactions
Broad support for a reinforced EU approach (Semicon Coalition Declaration, 27 Member States). Stakeholders flag overdependence on non-EU suppliers for high-performance chips, weak domestic demand from hyperscalers, skills shortages (decade-long pipelines), higher EU fab construction/operating costs, longer permitting, and late-stage financing gaps (no real Capital Markets Union).
