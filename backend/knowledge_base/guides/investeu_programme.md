# InvestEU: the EU Guarantee Programme for Strategic Investment

## QUICK FACTS
- **Legal base**: Regulation (EU) 2021/523 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 24 March 2021 establishing the InvestEU Programme (CELEX 32021R0523), adopted 24 March 2021, applicable for the 2021-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF).
- **EU budget guarantee**: EUR 26.2 billion (precisely EUR 26,152,310,073 in current prices), provisioned at 40 percent, designed to mobilise **more than EUR 372 billion** in additional public and private investment across the Union over 2021-2027. As of end 2025, the Commission reports around EUR 397 billion already mobilised, ahead of the original target.
- **Four policy windows** (the thematic areas the InvestEU Fund supports):
  1. Sustainable infrastructure
  2. Research, innovation and digitisation
  3. Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), including small mid-caps
  4. Social investment and skills (this window absorbed the former EaSI, the EU Programme for Employment and Social Innovation, as one of its financial instruments)
- **Three components (pillars) of the programme**:
  1. **InvestEU Fund**: the investment vehicle backed by the EU guarantee, deployed by implementing partners.
  2. **InvestEU Advisory Hub**: a single-entry advisory point connecting project promoters and intermediaries with advisory partners, helping projects reach financing stage.
  3. **InvestEU Portal**: an EU-wide, publicly accessible database matching investors with project promoters seeking funding.
- **Implementing partners**: the **European Investment Bank (EIB) Group** (EIB plus the European Investment Fund, EIF) is the main and privileged partner, implementing **75 percent of the EU guarantee** (EUR 19.65 billion) under the March 2022 Guarantee Agreement; within the EIB Group, the **EIF** takes the larger share of financing aimed at smaller companies. The remaining **25 percent** is shared among **14 or more national promotional banks and institutions (NPBIs)** and other international financial institutions with EU Member State majority shareholding, including **KfW** (Germany), **Cassa Depositi e Prestiti (CDP)** (Italy), **Bpifrance** (France) and **Instituto de Crédito Oficial (ICO)** (Spain).
- **Successor to EFSI**: InvestEU replaced and consolidated the European Fund for Strategic Investments (EFSI, the "Juncker Plan", 2015-2020) and more than a dozen other EU financial instruments into a single guarantee architecture.
- **STEP top-up**: the Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (Regulation (EU) 2024/795) reinforces InvestEU (alongside Horizon Europe, the Innovation Fund, the European Defence Fund and cohesion funds) with targeted top-ups and a new **sovereignty compartment of the EIC Fund** for larger individual-company investments (above EUR 15 million), expected to help unlock up to EUR 160 billion of new investment in critical technologies.
- **Additionality and state aid**: operations backed by InvestEU must not duplicate or crowd out private financing or distort competition in the internal market; projects must be technically and economically viable and address genuine market failures or sub-optimal investment situations that would otherwise go unfunded.
- **Successor programme in the next MFF**: the Commission's proposed **European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)** (COM(2025) 555, part of the 2028-2034 MFF package) would fold InvestEU, Horizon Europe, the Innovation Fund and several other instruments into a larger, more flexible pot (headline figures in the EUR 400-450 billion range across sub-windows including Defence and Space, Digital, Health and Biotech, and Clean Transition). Council's negotiating position (2026) has floated raising the maximum InvestEU-style guarantee ceiling from EUR 70 billion to EUR 150 billion within the new architecture. Treat ECF figures as provisional until the co-legislators reach a final MFF deal.

InvestEU is the EU's flagship financial-instrument architecture: a single budget guarantee that lets public and private financing partners take on investment risk the market alone would not absorb, at a scale roughly 14 times the guarantee itself.

## What InvestEU is

InvestEU is not a grant programme. It is a **guarantee mechanism**: the EU budget backs a pool of financing risk, so implementing partners (led by the EIB Group) can extend loans, guarantees, equity and quasi-equity to projects and companies that are viable but face a financing gap or higher risk profile than commercial lenders alone would accept. The guarantee absorbs first losses, which lets partners crowd in private capital that would otherwise stay on the sidelines.

It runs for the 2021-2027 MFF period and consolidated more than a dozen pre-existing EU financial instruments (including EFSI, the InnovFin family, the Connecting Europe Facility debt instrument, COSME loan guarantees, the Cultural and Creative Sectors Guarantee Facility, and EaSI's financial instruments) into one framework with one set of rules, one risk-management approach and one governance structure.

## The four policy windows

InvestEU channels investment through four thematic windows, each with its own indicative guarantee allocation and eligibility rules:

1. **Sustainable infrastructure**: transport, energy, digital connectivity, water, waste, climate adaptation and mitigation, biodiversity, circular economy, social infrastructure. At least 30 percent of overall InvestEU investment targets European Green Deal / climate objectives; within this window the climate and environment share of financing can reach around 60 percent.
2. **Research, innovation and digitisation**: scaling up research results and innovative technologies, deployment of digital technologies, supporting Horizon Europe-adjacent activity that markets alone would not finance at sufficient scale.
3. **SMEs**: access to finance for small and medium-sized enterprises and small mid-caps, largely delivered through the EIF and NPBIs via guarantees to local banks and funds.
4. **Social investment and skills**: microfinance, social enterprise finance, skills and education, social infrastructure (housing, healthcare, long-term care), integration of vulnerable groups. This window absorbed the financial-instrument strand of the former EaSI programme.

## The three components

**InvestEU Fund.** The guarantee-backed financing vehicle itself. Implementing partners submit investment proposals; an independent **Investment Committee** assesses each proposal against EU policy objectives and additionality criteria before approval.

**InvestEU Advisory Hub.** A single access point pooling advisory initiatives and technical assistance previously scattered across separate programmes (the European Investment Advisory Hub, Fi-Compass, and others). It helps project promoters, especially smaller and less experienced ones, structure bankable projects and reach financing stage. The Advisory Hub received an additional EUR 40 million top-up in the 2026 budget cycle.

**InvestEU Portal.** A free, EU-wide online matchmaking database where project promoters seeking financing (typically EUR 500,000 or above) can list projects and investors can browse them. Listing on the Portal does not guarantee EU support; it is a visibility tool, not a funding application.

## Implementing partners

The **EIB Group** (European Investment Bank plus European Investment Fund) is the main and privileged implementing partner, formalised in the March 2022 Guarantee Agreement covering **75 percent of the EUR 26.2 billion guarantee** (EUR 19.65 billion) and the same share of the Advisory Hub budget. Within the Group, the EIF typically handles the SME-window financing delivered through intermediary banks and funds, while the EIB handles larger infrastructure, research and innovation operations directly.

The remaining **25 percent** of the guarantee is available to other eligible counterparts, chiefly **national promotional banks and institutions (NPBIs)**, for the first time able to access the EU guarantee directly rather than only via the EIB. Fourteen or more NPBIs and international financial institutions with EU Member State majority shareholding participate, including **KfW** (Germany), **Cassa Depositi e Prestiti** (Italy), **Bpifrance** (France) and **Instituto de Crédito Oficial** (Spain), alongside institutions such as the EBRD. NPBI participation lets national expertise and local market knowledge complement the EIB Group's pan-European reach.

## How a project becomes InvestEU-backed

1. A project promoter (company, public authority, fund manager) approaches an implementing partner directly, or lists the project on the InvestEU Portal, or seeks structuring help via the Advisory Hub.
2. The implementing partner conducts its own due diligence and risk assessment, then submits the operation for coverage under the EU guarantee.
3. The independent Investment Committee reviews each proposal against the relevant policy window's objectives, additionality (would this happen without the guarantee, or would it happen but on worse terms or a smaller scale) and compliance with state-aid and competition rules.
4. If approved, the guarantee is allocated and the implementing partner disburses the financing (loan, guarantee, equity or quasi-equity) directly to the final recipient, often alongside co-investors.
5. There is no single "InvestEU application form": promoters apply through whichever implementing partner (EIB, EIF, or a participating NPBI) is active in the relevant window and geography.

## EFSI legacy

InvestEU is the direct successor to the **European Fund for Strategic Investments (EFSI)**, the "Juncker Plan" (2015-2020), which pioneered the EU-guarantee-plus-EIB-leverage model and mobilised roughly EUR 500 billion against an initial EUR 21 billion guarantee (later increased). InvestEU generalised and simplified that model, folding EFSI and a dozen-plus other sectoral financial instruments into a single rulebook, a single Investment Committee and a single Advisory Hub, while opening direct guarantee access to NPBIs for the first time.

## STEP top-up

The **Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform (STEP)**, established by Regulation (EU) 2024/795, is not a standalone fund but a **label and booster mechanism** layered on top of existing programmes, InvestEU chief among them. STEP combines reprioritisation of existing budget lines with targeted top-ups across roughly eleven EU programmes (InvestEU, Horizon Europe, the Innovation Fund, the European Defence Fund, cohesion funds, and others) to steer investment toward critical and emerging technologies: deep tech, clean tech, biotech and digital/AI. Within InvestEU specifically, STEP created a new **sovereignty compartment of the EIC Fund** targeting larger individual-company tickets above EUR 15 million. STEP is estimated to help unlock up to **EUR 160 billion** of new investment. See `step_strategic_technologies_for_europe` for the dedicated guide.

## Additionality and state aid

Every InvestEU-backed operation must satisfy the **additionality test**: it must address market failures or sub-optimal investment situations, and must not have been deliverable by the market on similar terms without EU support. The regulation is explicit that operations "should not duplicate or crowd out private financing or distort competition in the internal market." Projects must also be technically and economically viable. This additionality requirement is the key discipline distinguishing InvestEU from ordinary commercial lending, and it is also the hinge point for **state-aid compatibility**: because the EU guarantee itself is not classified as state aid, but downstream terms passed to final recipients can trigger state-aid scrutiny if they confer a selective advantage beyond what the market would offer.

## Successor: the European Competitiveness Fund

For the 2028-2034 MFF, the Commission has proposed folding InvestEU, alongside Horizon Europe, the Innovation Fund and several other instruments, into a single **European Competitiveness Fund (ECF)** (COM(2025) 555), pitched as the EU's principal response to the Draghi report's call for an "investment shock." Headline sub-window figures floated in the proposal include Horizon Europe (~EUR 175 billion), Resilience and Security/Defence Industry and Space (~EUR 131 billion), Digital leadership, and Health/Biotech/Agriculture, with the overall Fund's scope discussed in the EUR 400-450 billion range depending on which sub-programmes are counted inside versus alongside it. Council's 2026 negotiating position has discussed raising the maximum guarantee ceiling for the InvestEU-style instrument inside the new architecture from EUR 70 billion to EUR 150 billion. None of this is final: the MFF 2028-2034 negotiation is ongoing between Parliament and Council, and the ECF's exact structure, budget split and governance could still change materially. See `european_competitiveness_fund` and `mff_2028_2034` for the negotiation track.

## Brubru engagement angles

InvestEU backing is a strong signal of institutional-grade due diligence and co-investment appetite, useful for:

- **Company research**: firms that have received InvestEU-backed financing (loans, guarantees, equity) via the EIB Group, EIF or an NPBI have already cleared additionality and viability screening, useful context for partnership, procurement or investment due diligence.
- **Policy monitoring**: tracking how the four policy windows evolve, how STEP top-ups reshape sectoral allocations, and how the European Competitiveness Fund negotiation could restructure the guarantee architecture from 2028.
- **Funding-readiness advisory**: helping SMEs and mid-caps understand which window and which implementing partner (EIB direct, EIF via intermediary bank, or a national promotional bank) fits their financing need, and how the Advisory Hub and Portal can be used before approaching a partner directly.
- **Cross-referencing with EIC Fund and BlueInvest**: InvestEU's SME and research/innovation windows overlap with dedicated equity vehicles such as the EIC Fund (see `eic_fund_equity_investment`) and sector-specific facilities like BlueInvest (blue economy) and EFSD+ (external action guarantee, see `efsd_plus_external_action_guarantee`), so distinguishing which vehicle backs which type of financing avoids conflating grant, guarantee and equity instruments.
