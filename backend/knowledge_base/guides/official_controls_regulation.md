## QUICK ANSWERS

**What is the Official Controls Regulation?** The EU's single framework for how Member States police the entire agri-food chain. It sets harmonised rules for the official controls and other official activities that competent authorities perform to verify compliance with the whole body of EU agri-food chain legislation, from farm to fork.

**What areas does it cover (Article 1(2))?** Ten areas: food and food safety (including food-contact materials); GMOs for food and feed production; feed and feed safety; animal health; animal by-products; animal welfare; plant health (pests); plant protection products and sustainable pesticide use; organic production and labelling; and protected designations of origin, geographical indications and traditional specialities (PDO/PGI/TSG).

**What did it replace?** It consolidated a patchwork of older laws, repealing the 2004 Official Controls Regulation (EC) No 882/2004 and the meat-controls Regulation (EC) No 854/2004, plus seven Council Directives and Decisions, and integrating plant-health, residues and animal-by-product controls into one framework.

**What is the difference between official controls and other official activities?** Official controls verify that operators comply and that animals or goods meet requirements (including for certification). Other official activities are everything else authorities do under the rules: disease and pest surveillance, eradication and containment, granting authorisations and approvals, issuing certificates and attestations.

**How are controls organised?** Member States designate competent authorities for each area. Controls are risk-based, at appropriate frequency, generally without prior notice, documented in writing, with operators required to cooperate. Tasks can be delegated to ISO-accredited bodies or to natural persons. Samples go to official laboratories accredited to EN ISO/IEC 17025; operators have a right to a second expert opinion at their own expense; authorities may use anonymous "mystery shopping" for online trade.

**How does the border-control system work?** Animals and goods entering the EU from third countries are checked at designated border control posts (BCPs) before release for free circulation, with documentary, identity and physical checks at risk-based frequencies. Each consignment travels with a Common Health Entry Document (CHED) for prior notification, recording the control outcome and obtaining customs clearance.

**Who pays for the controls?** Competent authorities collect mandatory fees or charges for certain controls and for border controls, at cost or at the Annex IV amounts. Fees must cover but not exceed costs and must be transparent. Member States may reduce fees for low-throughput operators, traditional methods, geographically constrained regions and operators with a good compliance record.

**What is IMSOC?** The Information Management System for Official Controls, the single computerised system that integrates and upgrades TRACES, the Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed (RASFF), the animal-disease notification system (under the Animal Health Law) and the plant-pest notification system.

**What are the penalties?** Member States set effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties (Article 139); for fraud, financial penalties must reflect at least the operator's economic advantage or a percentage of turnover. The Regulation also requires whistleblower-reporting mechanisms with protection against retaliation, and lets the Commission take safeguard measures where a Member State's control system seriously breaks down.

## OVERVIEW

The Official Controls Regulation is the enforcement backbone of the EU's "farm to fork" acquis. It does not set the substantive food-safety, animal-health, plant-health or welfare standards (those live in sectoral law); instead it harmonises how those standards are checked and enforced across all Member States, at home and at the border, with common rules on competent authorities, financing, laboratories, certification, cross-border assistance, Commission audits and penalties. It is the companion enforcement instrument to the Animal Health Law and the Plant Health Law.
