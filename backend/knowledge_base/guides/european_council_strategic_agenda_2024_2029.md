# European Council Strategic Agenda 2024-2029

## QUICK FACTS
- **What this is**: the **political compass** adopted by EU Heads of State and Government at the European Council on 27 June 2024 -- the overarching mandate that guides the EU institutions for the 2024-2029 legislative cycle.
- **Adopted**: 27 June 2024 by the European Council; replaces the 2019-2024 Strategic Agenda.
- **Three pillars**: (1) A free and democratic Europe; (2) A strong and secure Europe; (3) A prosperous and competitive Europe.
- **European Council President**: Antonio Costa (took office 1 December 2024; succeeds Charles Michel).
- **Commission President re-elected**: Ursula von der Leyen (confirmed by EP 18 July 2024; College of Commissioners took office 1 December 2024).
- **High Representative / Vice-President (HR/VP)**: Kaja Kallas (foreign and security policy).
- **EP President re-elected**: Roberta Metsola (re-elected 16 July 2024 for a new 2.5-year term).
- **Legal function**: the Strategic Agenda is not a legislative act; it is a political orientations document. It forms the basis for the Commission's Political Guidelines, the Mission Letters to Commissioners, and successive College Work Programmes.
- **Canonical URL**: `https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/european-council/strategic-agenda-2024-2029/`
- **Full text PDF**: available via the Council website in all 24 EU official languages.

## Why the Strategic Agenda matters for Brubru users

Every Commission proposal and every Council Presidency work programme traces back to one of the three pillars and their sub-objectives. Knowing the Strategic Agenda lets advocates frame their position papers against the political priorities of Heads of Government, not just technical DG agendas. Brubru Chat can surface which pillar a given file sits under so users calibrate advocacy accordingly.

## How the Strategic Agenda is produced

The European Council sets the EU's general political direction and priorities (Treaty on European Union, Article 15). The Strategic Agenda is typically negotiated and adopted at a summit just after the EP elections in June:

1. **Draft by the President and General Secretariat**: the outgoing European Council President (Michel in 2024) circulates a draft based on informal consultations with Member State leaders and Commission input.
2. **Leaders' debate**: the June European Council summit debates the text in light of the election result and the incoming institutional landscape.
3. **Adoption by consensus**: the European Council adopts the text without a formal vote (Article 15(4) TEU: the European Council does not exercise legislative functions and normally acts by consensus).
4. **Mandate cascade**: the adopted Agenda sets the brief for the incoming Commission President's Political Guidelines, which the EP then votes on before the College hearing process.

The 2024 Agenda was unusual in that European elections (6-9 June 2024) produced a broadly centrist result, allowing the Agenda to be finalised quickly. The 2019 Agenda by contrast required two attempted June summits before agreement was reached.

## The three pillars in detail

### Pillar 1: A free and democratic Europe

Protecting democracy, the rule of law, and fundamental rights across the Union and in the wider neighbourhood. Key themes under this pillar:

- Defending democratic institutions from external interference and disinformation.
- Upholding judicial independence and the rule of law in Member States (linked to the Rule of Law Regulation / conditionality mechanism).
- Enlargement: continuing accession processes for Western Balkans and the Eastern Partnership countries (Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia). The 2024 Agenda marks a significant political commitment to enlargement as a geopolitical tool.
- EU's global role: neighbourhood policy, strategic partnerships, multilateral engagement.

### Pillar 2: A strong and secure Europe

Security, defence, and geopolitical resilience -- the most significant shift relative to the 2019-2024 Agenda, driven by Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine (February 2022):

- European defence: achieving genuine European defence capabilities, developing the defence industrial base, closing critical capability gaps. Translates directly into the European Defence Union, EDIP (European Defence Industry Programme), and the ReArm Europe / SAFE instruments.
- External borders and migration: a robust borders policy, including the New Pact on Migration and Asylum (adopted April 2024).
- Energy security: reducing energy dependence; sustained emphasis on the energy transition.
- Cyber and hybrid threats: resilience of critical infrastructure, hybrid warfare countermeasures.
- Ukraine support: a standing commitment to Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

### Pillar 3: A prosperous and competitive Europe

Growth, competitiveness, and the green and digital transitions -- the economic counterpart to security:

- Competitiveness: explicit reference to Europe's declining productivity gap with the US and China; basis for the Draghi Report (published September 2024) and the Letta Report on the Single Market (April 2024). Translates into the Competitiveness Compass (January 2025) and the Clean Industrial Deal.
- Digital transformation: AI governance, digital infrastructure, data economy; builds on the DSA/DMA/AI Act baseline.
- Climate and energy: completing the Green Deal legislative corpus while managing industrial competitiveness (Clean Industrial Deal, Affordable Energy Action Plan).
- Social dimension: cohesion, fair labour markets, housing affordability (European Affordable Housing Plan).
- Single Market deepening: removing remaining barriers, particularly in services and capital markets (Capital Markets Union / Savings and Investments Union).

## Cross-reference to Commission priorities 2024-2029 and Mission Letters

The Strategic Agenda does not list seven Commission priorities -- that level of detail is the Commission President's prerogative. Von der Leyen translated the three-pillar Agenda into **seven Commission priorities** set out in her Political Guidelines of 18 July 2024 (see `eu_commission_priorities_2024_2029.md`).

Mission Letters: each Commissioner received a Mission Letter from von der Leyen stating their mandate, flagships, and expected deliverables within those seven priorities. Mission Letters cross-reference the Agenda pillars. The letters are public and searchable on the Commission website.

The relationship is:

```
EUCO Strategic Agenda (3 pillars, political)
    --> Commission Political Guidelines (7 priorities, programmatic)
        --> Commission Work Programme (annual, legislative)
            --> Individual proposals + Mission Letter deliverables
```

## How the Strategic Agenda is implemented

The Strategic Agenda is not self-executing. Implementation runs through four channels:

**College Work Programme**: published each autumn, the CWP translates the Agenda into concrete legislative and non-legislative initiatives for the coming year. Each initiative is tagged to a Commission priority and traceable to a pillar.

**Annual European Council Conclusions**: each June summit (and each October/December summit) includes conclusions that call on the Commission or Council to act on specific items. These are de facto mini-mandates that can accelerate or redirect the Agenda between Work Programmes.

**Council Presidency programmes**: each Presidency (six-month rotation) publishes a work programme that selects which elements of the Agenda to prioritise in the Council's legislative calendar for that semester.

**Sectoral Council configurations**: the Agriculture and Fisheries Council, the Competitiveness Council, the ECOFIN, the Employment Council, and so on each adopt conclusions that advance the Agenda in their domain. The Competitiveness Council debate (see `eu_competitiveness_council_debate.md`) is the main vehicle for Pillar 3 priorities.

## Comparison to the 2019-2024 Strategic Agenda

| Dimension | 2019-2024 Agenda | 2024-2029 Agenda |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| Core framing | Climate + digital + social | Security + competitiveness |
| Headline flagship | European Green Deal | Competitiveness Compass / Clean Industrial Deal |
| Defence prominence | Low (pre-war) | Very high (post-February 2022) |
| Enlargement | Conditional and deferred | Elevated as a strategic priority |
| Rule of law | Stated as a priority | Reaffirmed, conditionality tool now live |
| Geopolitical posture | "Geopolitical Commission" aspiration | Structural -- HR/VP Kallas mandate, EDIP, SAFE |

The 2024 Agenda does not abandon the green transition -- it reframes it through the lens of industrial competitiveness and energy security. The Clean Industrial Deal is the direct successor to elements of the Green Deal.
