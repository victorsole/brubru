# Novel Food Regulation and Edible Insects in the EU

## QUICK FACTS
- Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 (Novel Food Regulation), CELEX: 32015R2283
- Replaces: Regulation (EC) 258/97 (original 1997 Novel Food Regulation)
- Entry into force: 1 January 2018
- Lead DG: DG SANTE (Health and Food Safety)
- Safety authority: EFSA (European Food Safety Authority)
- Pre-market authorisation required for all novel foods
- 4 insect species approved as novel food (2021-2023), 1 UV-treated variant (2025)
- 8+ applications under EFSA evaluation (including honeybee drone broods, black soldier fly)
- Labelling: mandatory disclosure of insect content + allergen warnings (crustacean/dust mite cross-reactivity)
- Farm to Fork Strategy: positions insects as sustainable alternative protein source
- EPRS briefing: "What if insects were on the menu in Europe?" (EPRS_ATA(2020)641551, Samuel Gregory-Manning, July 2020)
- Source: https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/authorisations/approval-insect-novel-food_en

Guide covering the EU's Novel Food Regulation framework, authorised insect species, EFSA safety assessment process, labelling requirements, and how to sell insect-based food products in the EU.

## What is a Novel Food?

A "novel food" is any food that was not consumed to a significant degree in the EU before 15 May 1997. This includes:
- New or intentionally modified molecular structure foods
- Foods from micro-organisms, fungi, algae, or cell/tissue cultures
- Foods from new production processes not previously used
- Insects and insect-derived products

All novel foods require **pre-market authorisation** via EFSA safety assessment before they can be placed on the EU market.

## Authorisation Process

### Step-by-step for applicants

1. **Application submission** to the European Commission via the e-submission system
2. **Validity check** by the Commission (completeness, fees)
3. **EFSA safety assessment** (9-month deadline, extendable):
   - Compositional analysis, nutritional profile
   - Toxicological data, allergenicity assessment
   - Contaminant levels, microbiological safety
   - Production process assessment
4. **Commission decision** (7 months after EFSA opinion):
   - Proposal to Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF)
   - Comitology procedure (examination)
5. **Publication** in Official Journal + Union List update
6. **Data protection** period: 5 years of exclusive market access for the applicant (novel food with proprietary data)

### Timeline
Typical authorisation takes **18-24 months** from application to market. EFSA assessment is the rate-limiting step.

### Two Pathways

1. **Standard novel food application** (Articles 10-12 of Regulation 2015/2283): Full dossier with safety data. Most common for insects.
2. **Traditional food notification** (Articles 14-20): Simplified procedure for foods consumed safely in a third country for 25+ years. Less common for insects.

### Generic Authorisation

Once an insect species is authorised, the authorisation is **generic**: any operator can market the same species in the same forms and conditions without a new application. Exception: products with **data protection** (5-year exclusivity for the original applicant if proprietary safety data was submitted). Operators using different specifications must submit a new application or extension.

### Transitional Measures (Article 35.2)

Products lawfully marketed in a Member State before 1 January 2018 could continue to be sold during a transition period. The CJEU ruling in case **C-526/19** clarified that transitional benefits apply where qualifying conditions existed. This created divergent Member State interpretations before the centralised EU procedure became the sole pathway.

## Labelling Requirements

Insect-containing foods **must** display:
- Common name of the insect species in the ingredient list
- Warning that the product may cause allergic reactions in consumers allergic to **crustaceans, dust mites, and molluscs** (cross-reactivity)
- Standard nutrition labelling per Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 (Food Information to Consumers)
- If the food is a novel food ingredient, the specific designation prescribed in the authorisation regulation

## How to Sell Insects as Food in the EU

### For producers/importers

1. **Check if your product is already authorised**: Consult the EU Novel Food Catalogue and the Union List (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2470)
2. **If not authorised**: Submit a novel food application (or traditional food notification if consumed outside EU for 25+ years)
3. **If authorised but by another applicant**: Wait for data protection period to expire (5 years) or obtain permission from the authorisation holder
4. **Comply with food safety law**: Regulation (EC) 178/2002 (General Food Law), hygiene regulations (852/2004, 853/2004)
5. **Labelling**: Follow FIC Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 + specific conditions in the authorisation regulation
6. **Traceability**: Full farm-to-fork traceability required

### For retailers

- Can sell any authorised insect novel food without additional permits
- Must ensure correct labelling including allergen warnings
- Country-specific rules may apply for food supplements

## Policy Context

### Farm to Fork Strategy (2020)

The EU Farm to Fork Strategy (COM(2020) 381) identifies insects as a key **alternative protein source** for sustainable food systems:
- Lower environmental footprint than conventional livestock (less land, water, feed, GHG)
- Contribution to circular economy (insects can be reared on organic waste streams)
- Food security diversification

## Feed Regulations

Insects are also regulated as **animal feed**:
- Regulation (EU) 2017/893 authorises processed insect proteins as feed for aquaculture and poultry
- Feed substrates are largely limited to vegetable-based materials
- Live insects, processed animal proteins, and insect-derived fats/oils are the three permitted forms
- Scientific literature notes that "insect-specific limits for hazards" are not yet fully defined -- current legal limits apply but do not account for insect-specific circumstances (source: Animal journal, 2025)

## Safety and Allergenicity

- EFSA assessments confirm safety under proposed conditions of use
- **Cross-reactivity risk**: insect proteins share allergens with crustaceans (tropomyosin), dust mites, and molluscs
- Mandatory allergen labelling required on all insect food products
- Contaminant levels (heavy metals, mycotoxins, pesticides) assessed per batch
- Microbiological safety: thermal processing required (no raw insect consumption)
- Insect welfare: ethical and welfare requirements for farmed insects increasingly discussed in scientific literature

## Market Data

- EU insect protein market estimated at EUR 1.3 billion by 2030 (IPIFF projections)
- International Platform of Insects for Food and Feed (IPIFF): industry association representing EU insect producers, develops administrative guidelines and coordinates with EU authorities
- Main producing countries: France, Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Finland
- Products: whole insects (snacks), insect flour/powder (baking, pasta, protein bars), insect oil
- Market penetration remains limited despite growing authorisations
