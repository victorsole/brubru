# Rules for Arbitration of EU Grant Disputes

## QUICK FACTS

- **Applies when**: Contractual disputes concerning interpretation, application or validity of Horizon Europe grant agreements (MGA Article 47)
- **Appointing authority**: Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) Secretariat or mutually acceptable neutral authority if parties cannot compose the panel
- **Seat of arbitration**: The Hague, Netherlands
- **Language of proceedings**: English (evidence may be in other languages if parties agree)
- **Procedural rules**: 2012 PCA Arbitration Rules with EU-specific modifications
- **Panel size**: Three arbitrators for claims EUR 500,000 and above; one arbitrator for claims below EUR 500,000
- **Award is final and binding**: Parties may request review by The Hague Court of Appeal under national law
- **Costs**: Arbitrators' fees capped at EUR 30,000 per arbitrator (below EUR 1m) or 9% of contested amount (above EUR 1m, max EUR 300,000 per arbitrator)
- **Alternatives available**: Amicable settlement is first step; mediation available before formal arbitration
- **General Court vs arbitration**: If EU law interpretation required, arbitration stays and references a preliminary ruling request to the Court of Justice via the President of the Paris High Court

## When Arbitration Applies Under the MGA

Arbitration under MGA Article 47 applies when a contractual dispute concerning the interpretation, application or validity of a grant agreement cannot be settled amicably. This forum resolves disputes between beneficiaries and the granting authority that have exhausted informal resolution channels.

Arbitration is mandatory only if the parties have agreed to it in the grant agreement. It is the dispute settlement mechanism specified in Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreements for cases where amicable settlement fails.

## Appointment of Arbitrators

The appointment rules differ based on the claim value:

### For Claims EUR 500,000 or Above

A three-arbitrator panel is appointed:

1. Each party appoints one arbitrator within 40 calendar days after notice of arbitration is sent
2. The two party-appointed arbitrators then appoint a third arbitrator to serve as presiding arbitrator
3. If the panel is not composed within 80 calendar days after notice, either party may request the PCA Secretariat or another mutually acceptable neutral authority to appoint the necessary arbitrator(s)

### For Claims Below EUR 500,000

A single arbitrator shall hear the case, unless the parties agree otherwise.

## Procedure: Request, Exchange of Pleadings, Hearings and Award

Arbitration follows the 2012 PCA Arbitration Rules with the following EU-specific modifications:

### Excluded Procedures

- Interim measures (emergency orders) are not permitted
- Third-party interventions are excluded
- Amicus curiae interventions (submissions from non-parties) are excluded

### Confidentiality and In Camera Hearings

If either party requests that information be treated as confidential, the panel issues a reasoned order after hearing the other party's observations (10 calendar days to respond). The panel weighs the reasons for confidentiality, the nature of the information, and the right to effective judicial protection. The panel may:

- Impose specific undertakings on disclosure, or
- Decline disclosure but require a non-confidential version or summary containing essential content to ensure the other party can meaningfully participate

If either party requests an in camera (closed) hearing, the panel decides after hearing the other party's objections (10 calendar days).

### EU Law Interpretation

If the panel is requested to interpret or apply European Union law, proceedings are stayed. The panel requests the President of the Paris High Court (Président du Tribunal de grande instance de Paris) to refer a preliminary ruling to the Court of Justice of the European Union under Article 267 TFEU. The arbitration resumes once the juge d'appui makes its decision. Both the Court of Justice decision and the juge d'appui decision are binding on the panel.

### The Arbitral Award

The award is final and binding on both parties, and must be carried out without delay. Either party may request review by The Hague Court of Appeal under applicable Dutch national law. The award is not considered final until this review procedure is concluded (if initiated). The reviewing court's decision is binding on the panel.

## Language, Seat and Costs

### Language

The language of proceedings is English. Evidence may be produced in other languages if both parties agree.

### Seat

The seat of arbitration is The Hague, Netherlands. This provides a neutral location and access to international arbitration infrastructure.

### Costs of Arbitration

Arbitration costs consist of:

- Fees and reasonable expenses of arbitrators
- Reasonable costs of experts and witnesses approved by the panel
- Fees and expenses of the PCA Secretariat (room, interpretation, administrative support)

### Arbitrators' Fee Caps

Arbitrators' fees shall not exceed:

- EUR 30,000 per arbitrator if the contested amount is below EUR 1,000,000
- 9% of the contested amount if above EUR 1,000,000 (capped at EUR 300,000 per arbitrator overall)

### Cost Allocation

Costs of arbitration shall be borne by both parties equally, unless they agree otherwise. Each party bears its own legal representation costs and other costs incurred in relation to the arbitration.

## Alternatives: Amicable Settlement and Mediation

Before resorting to formal arbitration, MGA Article 47 requires an attempt at amicable settlement. This informal negotiation phase allows both parties to resolve disputes without triggering formal proceedings and associated costs and timelines.

If amicable settlement fails, parties may pursue:

- **Mediation**: An informal, confidential process facilitated by a neutral third party, allowing the parties to explore mutually acceptable solutions without binding procedures
- **Conciliation**: Similar to mediation but with more structured procedures and recommendations from a conciliator

Only after exhausting amicable approaches does arbitration commence. This tiered approach encourages cost-effective resolution whilst preserving rights to formal adjudication if needed.

## Relationship to General Court Actions

Arbitration panels may not review the General Court's jurisdiction or the Commission's discretionary decisions outside the scope of the MGA. However, when arbitration concerns the interpretation of EU law itself, the panel must stay proceedings and seek a preliminary ruling from the Court of Justice via the President of the Paris High Court (Article 1460 and 1505 of the French Civil Procedural Code, with reference under Article 267 TFEU).

The arbitration award, once final, operates independently of General Court jurisdiction. A party dissatisfied with an arbitral award may seek review only through The Hague Court of Appeal under Dutch national law, not through EU courts. Conversely, allegations that the Commission breached EU principles of proportionality or procedural fairness in the grant administration itself fall outside the scope of arbitration and may require separate General Court challenge under Article 263 TFEU.

Arbitration therefore complements but does not replace judicial review of Commission action. The seat in the Netherlands and reference to Dutch law reflect the choice to use international commercial arbitration principles for contractual disputes, whilst reserving EU law interpretation for EU courts.

## Privileges and Immunities

The agreement to pursue arbitration under the 2012 PCA Arbitration Rules does not constitute a waiver of privileges or immunities to which any party is entitled. The European Union and its institutions retain all privileges and immunities under international law and EU law, even during arbitration proceedings.
