# Cedefop: European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** European Centre for the Development of Vocational Training (Cedefop) -- the EU's agency for vocational education and training (VET), skills intelligence and qualifications policy
- **Founded:** 1975, under Council Regulation (EEC) No 337/75
- **Current legal basis:** **Regulation (EU) 2019/128** of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 January 2019 establishing Cedefop and repealing Regulation (EEC) No 337/75; CELEX **32019R0128**. A recast, not a new agency -- it consolidates and modernises the founding text rather than creating a fresh mandate.
- **Seat:** Thessaloniki (Thermi), Greece -- based there since 1995
- **Executive Director:** Jurgen Siebel
- **Deputy Director:** Mara Brugia
- **Agency type:** a **tripartite-plus-Commission** decentralised EU agency -- its Management Board seats representatives of national governments, employers' organisations and employees' organisations (trade unions) from each participating country, alongside the European Commission, following the same tripartite governance model as Eurofound and EU-OSHA (Cedefop covers VET and skills; Eurofound covers living and working conditions).
- **What it does:** provides the EU's evidence base, data and tools on vocational education and training, skills anticipation, qualifications frameworks and lifelong learning -- it is a knowledge and data agency, not a funding body or a regulator.
- **Flagship skills-intelligence tools:** the **Skills Forecast** (projections to 2035 across around 40 sectors and occupations), the **European Skills and Jobs Survey (ESJS)** (survey of tens of thousands of adult workers across the EU), **Skills-OVATE** (online job-vacancy analysis by occupation and sector), the **European Skills Index** (comparative country performance on skills systems), and a wider **skills intelligence** indicator set.
- **Qualifications tools:** coordinates the **European Qualifications Framework (EQF)** at EU level and runs the **National Qualifications Frameworks (NQFs) online tool**, which maps how national qualifications systems reference to the EQF's eight levels across dozens of countries.
- **Other tools:** the **VET in Europe** database (country-by-country VET system descriptions), an information hub on **validation of non-formal and informal learning**, apprenticeship databases, a **mobility scoreboard** tracking conditions for VET learner mobility, and **VET policy dashboards**.
- **Europass:** Cedefop has historically run the day-to-day operation, templates and quality control of the **Europass** platform (the EU's CV, skills-passport and learning-mobility documentation service) jointly with the European Commission -- confirm the current operational split before citing it as Cedefop-exclusive, since Europass governance has evolved since its 2004/2018 legal basis.
- **Distinguish from:** **Eurofound** (living and working conditions, industrial relations -- Dublin), the **ETF** (European Training Foundation, VET support for EU candidate and neighbourhood countries outside the Union -- Turin), and **EACEA** (the Commission executive agency that actually disburses Erasmus+ funding; Cedefop supplies evidence and mobility data, it does not run the funding programme).
- **Underpins:** the **Union of Skills** flagship initiative, the **Pact for Skills**, the **European Education Area 2021-2030** strategic framework, and the skills-matching layer feeding instruments such as the **EU Talent Pool**.

Cedefop is the EU's standing knowledge and data agency for vocational education, training and skills, in the same institutional family as Eurofound and EU-OSHA. It does not fund training or set binding VET policy itself; it builds the evidence, forecasts and comparability tools -- the EQF, the Skills Forecast, Skills-OVATE, the European Skills Index -- that the Commission, Member States and social partners use to design, target and evaluate EU skills and VET policy.

## Legal Basis and Mandate

- **Regulation (EU) 2019/128** is the current, recast founding act. It repealed and replaced the original 1975 founding regulation (Council Regulation (EEC) No 337/75), consolidating decades of amendments into a single modernised text and updating Cedefop's tasks, governance and working methods in line with the EU's Common Approach on decentralised agencies.
- Treaty basis: EU competence to support, coordinate and supplement Member State action in vocational training (Article 166 TFEU) and, for the skills-intelligence and labour-market dimension, employment policy coordination (Article 149 TFEU).
- Core statutory tasks (Article 2, Regulation (EU) 2019/128): to assist the Commission, Member States and social partners in developing and implementing EU VET and skills policy, through research, analysis, technical support and knowledge-sharing; to collect, analyse and disseminate data, statistics and comparable information on VET systems, skills needs and qualifications; and to promote a common European approach to skills and qualifications.
- Cedefop's mandate is periodically widened by sectoral initiatives that assign it specific data or coordination tasks -- for example the Council Recommendation on individual learning accounts, the Council Recommendation on micro-credentials, and the Union of Skills flagship -- without necessarily amending Regulation (EU) 2019/128 itself. Check the sectoral instrument for the precise task before assuming it sits inside the founding regulation.

## What Cedefop Actually Does

### 1. Skills intelligence and anticipation
Cedefop's **Skills Forecast** projects labour-market and skills trends out to 2035 across dozens of sectors and occupations. **Skills-OVATE** mines online job vacancies to show which skills and occupations employers are actually requesting, by sector and country. The **European Skills and Jobs Survey (ESJS)** is a large-scale survey of adult workers capturing skills mismatch, skills use and training participation directly from the workforce. The **European Skills Index** aggregates these and other indicators into a comparative score of how well each country's skills system performs.

### 2. Qualifications frameworks
Cedefop coordinates work on the **European Qualifications Framework (EQF)**, the EU-level reference framework of eight qualification levels that lets national qualifications be compared across borders. Its **National Qualifications Frameworks (NQFs) online tool** tracks how each participating country's own qualifications framework references to the EQF, including development stage and qualification-type comparisons.

### 3. Validation and recognition
Cedefop maintains an information hub on the **validation of non-formal and informal learning** -- how skills and knowledge gained outside formal education (on the job, through volunteering, self-study) can be formally recognised -- and tracks **micro-credentials** policy as EU Member States implement the 2022 Council Recommendation on a European approach to micro-credentials.

### 4. VET systems, mobility and apprenticeships
The **VET in Europe** database gives country-by-country descriptions of how vocational education and training systems are structured, financed and governed. A **mobility scoreboard** monitors the conditions VET learners face when training or working abroad (relevant to Erasmus+/ErasmusPro mobility, though Cedefop itself does not administer that funding). Apprenticeship databases and **VET policy dashboards** track reform and policy developments across countries.

### 5. Europass support
Cedefop has historically operated the technical infrastructure, templates and quality control of **Europass**, the EU's platform for CVs, skills passports, diploma supplements and learning-mobility documents, working jointly with the European Commission, which holds the overall legal and policy responsibility for the platform under the Europass Decision.

## Governance and Structure

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Management Board | Representatives of national governments, employers' organisations and employees' organisations (trade unions) from participating countries, plus European Commission representatives -- the tripartite-plus-Commission model; sets strategic direction, adopts the work programme and budget |
| Executive Board | A smaller steering group within the Management Board, chaired on a rotating basis among the constituent groups (government/employers/employees/Commission) |
| Executive Director | Day-to-day management and legal representation; currently Jurgen Siebel, with Mara Brugia as Deputy Director |
| Social partners | EU-level employer and trade union organisations sit on the Management Board directly, giving Cedefop a formal social-dialogue channel that most Commission-side agencies lack |

## Cedefop in the Wider EU Skills and VET Architecture

| Body | Role | Relationship to Cedefop |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| Cedefop | VET, skills intelligence and qualifications evidence base | -- |
| Eurofound | Living and working conditions, industrial relations | Sister tripartite agency; different policy scope (working conditions vs. VET/skills) |
| ETF (European Training Foundation) | VET support for EU candidate countries and neighbourhood partners | Same VET domain, but external-action focus outside the EU; Turin-based |
| EACEA | Disburses Erasmus+ and other education/culture funding | Commission executive agency; runs the money, Cedefop supplies the evidence and mobility data |
| European Commission (DG EMPL, DG EAC) | Sets VET, skills and education policy; proposes legislation | Principal user of Cedefop data and forecasts; co-runs Europass |
| ESCO / EURES | EU classification of skills/occupations; EU job-mobility portal | Cedefop aligns its qualifications and skills tools with both |

## Why This Matters for Advocacy and Compliance

- **Evidence, not enforcement:** Cedefop does not inspect, certify or sanction. Organisations should treat its Skills Forecast, ESJS survey and European Skills Index data as citable, authoritative EU evidence for workforce and reskilling submissions, not as a source of legal obligations.
- **Skills-shortage arguments:** sectors making a case for labour or skills shortages (for talent-mobility instruments such as the EU Talent Pool, or for sectoral skills partnerships under the Pact for Skills) should ground the argument in Cedefop's Skills Forecast and Skills-OVATE vacancy data, which the Commission itself uses.
- **Qualifications recognition:** organisations handling cross-border recruitment or professional recognition should reference the EQF and the NQF online tool to establish how a given national qualification compares -- this is the same comparability layer EU institutions use.
- **Micro-credentials and validation:** as Member States implement micro-credentials and non-formal/informal learning validation, Cedefop's guidance is the practical reference point for what "EU-aligned" implementation looks like.
- **Funding vs. evidence:** when a stakeholder asks about VET or apprenticeship funding, redirect the funding question to EACEA (Erasmus+) or the ESF+ managing authority; redirect the evidence, forecasting or qualifications-comparison question to Cedefop.
