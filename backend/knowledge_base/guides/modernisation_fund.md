# EU Modernisation Fund

## QUICK FACTS
- **LATEST (Thursday 2 July 2026 — EUR 2.5 billion disbursed to 51 projects)**: The European Commission and the **European Investment Bank (EIB)** announced the disbursement of **EUR 2.5 billion from the Modernisation Fund** to support **51 energy-related projects in 11 beneficiary Member States**. This round brings the **total Modernisation Fund funding made available to EUR 23.2 billion since January 2021**. Beneficiaries this round: **Czechia (EUR 516.8m), Estonia (EUR 44.8m), Greece (EUR 233.9m), Croatia (EUR 109m), Latvia (EUR 40m), Lithuania (EUR 169m), Hungary (EUR 552.3m), Poland (EUR 180m), Portugal (EUR 81.4m), Romania (EUR 636.9m), Slovenia (EUR 20.2m)**. The 51 projects focus on renewable electricity generation, deployment of renewable energy sources, modernisation of energy networks, and energy efficiency. Next submission deadlines: **11 August 2026** (non-priority investments) and **8 September 2026** (priority investments; priority accounts for over 90% of the portfolio). Source: EC/EIB joint press release IP/26/1501, 2 July 2026 (DG CLIMA + DG ENER).
- **What it is**: an ETS-revenue-funded instrument that helps **lower-income EU Member States** meet their 2030 energy and climate targets by modernising energy systems and improving energy efficiency, including in the transport sector. Complements Cohesion Policy, the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF), and the Just Transition Fund.
- **Legal basis**: **Article 10d of the EU ETS Directive 2003/87/EC** (CELEX 32003L0087), as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/959 (Fit for 55 ETS revision). At least **70% of resources ("priority investments")** must go to the priority areas listed in Article 10d(2).
- **Funding source**: revenues from auctioning **2% (raised toward ~2.5% after the 2023 ETS revision) of total ETS allowances** for 2021-2030, plus any additional allowances transferred voluntarily by beneficiary Member States. Expected to provide **up to ~EUR 48 billion by 2030** depending on the carbon price.
- **Beneficiary Member States (13)**: original 10 — **Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, Slovakia**; plus **3 added from 2024** after the strengthened ETS deal — **Greece, Portugal, Slovenia**.
- **Governance**: operates under the **responsibility of the beneficiary Member States** in close cooperation with the **European Commission** (DG CLIMA policy lead, DG ENER energy dimension) and the **European Investment Bank (EIB)**. Investment proposals must be presented by the Member State where the action is located. No minimum co-financing rate; public or private beneficiaries allowed.
- **First year (2021)**: EUR 898 million made available to eight beneficiary countries.
- **Distinct from the Innovation Fund**: the **Innovation Fund** (see `innovation_fund`) is EU-wide, managed by CINEA, and funds breakthrough net-zero technologies via competitive calls. The **Modernisation Fund** is restricted to the 13 lower-income beneficiary Member States and funds energy-system modernisation/efficiency at national level. Both draw on ETS auction revenues but are separate instruments with different scope and governance.
- **Lead DG**: DG CLIMA (Climate Action), with DG ENER (Energy) on the energy-systems dimension.
- **Cross-links**: `eu_ets_review_2026`, `innovation_fund`, `temporary_decarbonisation_fund`, `cohesion_policy_midterm_review`, `eu_just_transition_fund`.

## Priority areas (Article 10d(2) ETS Directive)
At least 70% of Modernisation Fund resources must be invested in:
- Generation and use of electricity from **renewable sources** (wind, solar, hydropower, hydrogen)
- **Energy efficiency** improvements (industry, transport, buildings, agriculture, waste)
- **Energy storage** and the **modernisation of energy networks**, including district heating, electricity grids, and cross-border interconnections
- **Just Transition** support in carbon-dependent regions

Non-priority areas (up to 30%) can include broader energy-system modernisation not listed above.

## How disbursement works
1. Beneficiary Member States submit investment proposals to the **Investment Committee** and the EIB, split into **priority** and **non-priority** streams.
2. The EIB confirms whether a proposal qualifies as a priority investment (Article 10d(2) area).
3. The Investment Committee issues recommendations; the Commission adopts disbursement decisions.
4. Twice-yearly submission windows; the 2026 windows close **11 August 2026** (non-priority) and **8 September 2026** (priority).

## Why this matters for advocacy
- The Modernisation Fund is one of the largest ETS-revenue recycling channels and a key co-financing source for national energy-transition projects in Central, Eastern and Southern Europe.
- Firms and public bodies in the 13 beneficiary states can access it for renewables, grid, storage, efficiency, and clean-transport projects — but the proposal must be routed through the Member State.
- The instrument's size scales with the carbon price, so ETS Review 2026 outcomes (see `eu_ets_review_2026`) directly affect its future envelope.
