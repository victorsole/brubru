# Industrial Accelerator Act (COM(2026)100)

## Overview

On 4 March 2026, the European Commission presented the **Industrial Accelerator Act** -- a proposal for a Regulation establishing measures for industrial capacity and decarbonisation in strategic sectors. The proposal is part of the **Clean Industrial Deal** package (COM(2025)85) and the **Competitiveness Compass** (COM(2025)30).

- **Reference:** COM(2026)100
- **Legal form:** Regulation (directly applicable in all Member States)
- **Legal basis:** Article 114 TFEU (internal market) and Article 192 TFEU (environment)
- **Responsible DG:** DG GROW (Internal Market, Industry, Entrepreneurship and SMEs)
- **Responsible Commissioner:** Stephane Sejourne (Executive Vice-President for Prosperity and Industrial Strategy)
- **Supporting documents:** SWD(2026)70 (Subsidiarity Grid), SWD(2026)71 (Impact Assessment), SWD(2026)72 (Executive Summary)

## Key Measures

### 1. Permitting Acceleration
- Streamlined and digitalised permitting procedures for industrial decarbonisation projects
- Addresses permitting bottlenecks not already covered by the Net-Zero Industry Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1735, CELEX 32024R1735)
- Commission may use AI to help accelerate the permit-granting process
- Member States urged to improve administrative capacities for timely permit processing

### 2. Low-Carbon Product Labels
- Introduction of voluntary carbon intensity labels for EU-made clean products
- Initial scope: **steel**, then **cement**
- Labels based on clean, resilient, circular, and cybersecure criteria
- Aim: create transparency for buyers and incentivise low-carbon production

### 3. Public Procurement
- Minimum proportion requirements for clean products in public contracts
- Sustainability and resilience criteria for public and private procurement
- European preference in strategic sectors (aligned with Single Market Strategy revision scheduled for 2026)

### 4. Lead Markets for EU-Made Clean Products
- Create lead markets for European clean, resilient industrial technologies and products
- Support production of EU-made clean products through regulatory framework
- Linked to the future Circular Economy Act (scheduled 2026)

### 5. State Aid and Financing
- Conditions certain state aid subsidies on sustainability criteria
- Clean Industry State Aid Framework (CISAF, adopted 25 June 2025) provides the backdrop
- Carbon Contracts for Difference (CCfDs) as a key instrument for hard-to-abate sectors
- Industrial Decarbonisation Bank to mobilise EUR 100 billion
- Innovation Fund and InvestEU as financing vehicles
- IPCEI framework made more accessible to SMEs (EUR 50 million threshold for GBER)

### 6. Strategic Sectors
The Act targets sectors central to decarbonisation and strategic autonomy:
- Steel and metals
- Cement
- Chemicals
- Clean technology manufacturing (solar, wind, batteries, heat pumps, electrolysers)
- Automotive
- Sustainable transport
- Aerospace and alternative fuels (per EP request)

### Relationship to Net-Zero Industry Act
The NZIA (Regulation (EU) 2024/1735, CELEX 32024R1735) focuses on manufacturing capacity for net-zero technologies. The Industrial Accelerator Act complements it by addressing:
- Industrial decarbonisation (demand side, not just supply of clean tech)
- Permitting bottlenecks beyond NZIA scope
- Low-carbon product labelling and procurement (market creation)
- Broader strategic sector coverage (steel, cement, chemicals)

## EP Resolution: Comparative Analysis

On 19 June 2025, the European Parliament adopted resolution 2025/2656(RSP) on the Clean Industrial Deal. This resolution directly shaped the Industrial Accelerator Act.

### Resolution Details
- **Rapporteur:** Tom Berendsen (EPP / NL)
- **Committee:** ITRE (Industry, Research and Energy)
- **Shadow rapporteurs:** Nicolas Gonzalez Casares (S&D), Isabella Tovaglieri (PfE), Daniel Obajtek (ECR), Christophe Grudler (Renew), Sara Matthieu (Greens/EFA), Hanna Gedin (The Left)
- **Motions:** B10-0277/2025 and B10-0278/2025

### What the EP Asked vs What the Commission Proposed

| EP Resolution Request | Commission Response (Nov 2025) | Industrial Accelerator Act (Mar 2026) |
|----------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Industrial Decarbonisation Bank** (EUR 100bn) | Welcomed; pointed to Innovation Fund and InvestEU as vehicles | Included as financing framework |
| **Carbon Contracts for Difference** for hard-to-abate sectors | Committed to CCfD guidance; "auctions-as-a-service" via Innovation Fund | CCfDs as key instrument, CISAF framework in place |
| **Simplified permitting** and digitalised procedures | Committed to IAA measures beyond NZIA scope; AI for permit-granting | Core measure: permitting acceleration with digitalisation |
| **Lead markets** for EU-made clean, circular products with carbon intensity labels | Announced IAA would create lead markets and regulatory framework | Low-carbon labels (steel, cement), public procurement criteria |
| **Accessible IPCEI** for SMEs | Highlighted GBER EUR 50mn threshold; SME participation rising to 60% | SME accessibility maintained |
| **Sector-specific action plans** (automotive, steel, chemicals, transport) | Confirmed Strategic Dialogues ongoing; plans for all requested sectors | Strategic sectors explicitly covered |
| **Aerospace and alternative fuels** inclusion | Not explicitly addressed in November response | To be confirmed in final text |
| **FSR enforcement** against unfair foreign subsidies | Committed to vigorous enforcement; record 33 investigations in 2024 | Complementary trade defence measures |
| **CBAM extension** to downstream products | Legislative proposal for steel/aluminium downstream by Q4 2025 | Linked via CBAM anti-circumvention strategy |
| **CO2 transport and infrastructure** framework | New legislative initiative for EU CO2 market planned for 2026 | Related but separate initiative |
| **Simplified procurement** with sustainability/resilience criteria | Single Market Strategy revision (2026) to include European preference | Public procurement provisions included |
| **Skills and VET** for green transition | VET Strategy and European Alliance for Apprenticeships ongoing | Linked to Union of Skills initiative |

### Key Alignment Areas
The Industrial Accelerator Act broadly aligns with the EP resolution. The Parliament's core demands -- permitting acceleration, lead markets, low-carbon labels, CCfDs, and SME accessibility -- are all addressed. This suggests strong potential for cross-party support during the legislative procedure.

### Areas of Potential Friction
- **Scope of European preference** in public procurement (trade policy sensitivity)
- **Stringency of low-carbon criteria** (industry vs environmental ambition)
- **Funding adequacy** (EP called for EUR 100bn; Commission points to existing instruments)
- **CBAM extension timeline** (EP wanted faster action on downstream products)

### Prediction Basis

1. **EP Resolution alignment:** The CID resolution (June 2025) was adopted with broad cross-party support. The IAA directly implements Parliament's own requests, creating political momentum for adoption.
2. **Industrial competitiveness framing:** The proposal is framed as competitiveness (not just green regulation), which attracts broader political support including centre-right.
3. **Clean Industrial Deal package:** Part of a flagship Commission initiative with strong political backing from EVP Sejourne.
4. **Net-Zero Industry Act precedent:** NZIA was adopted with 361 votes to 121 (75%) in February 2024. The IAA has a similar industrial policy profile.
