## What the proposal does

Article 20(2)(c) TFEU and the Charter Article 46 guarantee every EU citizen the right to consular protection in a third country where their own Member State is not represented. **Council Directive 2015/637** translated this into operational rules — Member State consulates abroad must protect citizens of any other EU Member State on the same conditions as their own nationals.

The 2023 revision (COM(2023) 930) updates the 2015 framework for the lessons learned from:
- **COVID-19 mass repatriations** (March-May 2020) — over 600,000 EU citizens repatriated from third countries via consular cooperation
- **Afghanistan evacuation** (August 2021)
- **Ukraine evacuation** (February-March 2022)
- **Sudan evacuation** (April 2023)
- **Lebanon / Israel-Gaza evacuations** (October 2023 - 2024-2025 cycles)
- **Niger / Sahel evacuations** (2023-2025)

Key changes proposed:
1. **EU Delegations as default consular interface** in third countries where Member State coverage is thin
2. **Crisis preparedness obligations** for Member State consulates — mandatory contingency plans, exercises, communications protocols
3. **Financial reimbursement mechanism** harmonisation — currently fragmented, often invoiced months after evacuation
4. **Digital consular ID** — interoperability of national consular cards + ETIAS pre-clearance for evacuation flights
5. **Information rights** — EU citizens to be able to register travel digitally before departure (one stop, via the **Single Digital Gateway**); proactive crisis alerts via the registry
6. **Family-member protection** — extended to third-country-national family members of EU citizens travelling together
7. **Reporting and transparency** — annual Commission report to EP + Council on consular-protection cases
