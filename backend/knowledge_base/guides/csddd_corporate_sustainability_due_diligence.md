## Overview

CSDDD is the EU's mandatory horizontal due diligence law. It requires large companies operating in the Union to identify, prevent, mitigate, remedy and publicly account for adverse human rights and environmental impacts arising from their own operations, the operations of their subsidiaries, and the operations of their business partners in the "chain of activities" (upstream + limited downstream). It also obliges those companies to adopt and put into effect a climate transition plan compatible with the 1.5°C target of the Paris Agreement and the 2050 climate neutrality objective of the European Climate Law.

Whereas CSRD (Directive (EU) 2022/2464) forces companies to REPORT sustainability information, CSDDD forces them to DO due diligence, to act on findings, and to compensate victims when they don't. Together with CSRD, the SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088) and the Taxonomy Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2020/852), CSDDD closes the EU sustainable-finance and corporate-conduct framework.

CSDDD is heavily contested. The originally-adopted 2024 text was already narrower than the 2022 Commission proposal (COM(2022) 71 final). Stop-the-clock (Directive (EU) 2025/794) postponed transposition and Wave 1 application by one year in April 2025, and a substantive Omnibus I proposal tabled by the Commission on 26 February 2025 (COM(2025) 81 final and COM(2025) 82 final) proposes to narrow scope further — limit routine due diligence to own operations and direct suppliers, delete or soften Article 22 climate transition plan enforcement, and delete the Article 29 harmonised civil-liability regime. The substantive Omnibus is still under co-legislator negotiation in mid-2026.


## Omnibus I scope reduction (2026)

The Commission's Omnibus I "simplification" package narrowed the CSDDD scope
sharply. Updated SOMO datahub figures (April 2026):

- **974 EU corporate groups** now in scope, a fall of about 71% against the
  original CSDDD scope
- **473 non-EU parent groups** in scope through their EU subsidiaries
- By Member State: **Germany 280**, with sharp drops across all the large
  member states
- By third country: **US 182**, **UK 69**, **Switzerland 47**, **China 13**
- **Review clause: July 2031** — scope is revisited at that point

Source: Andreas Rasche (Copenhagen Business School), SOMO datahub, 15 April
2026, commenting that "due diligence cannot remain a 'big players only'
exercise".

Policy implication: at 974 EU groups, 280 of them German, the effect of the
CSDDD on supply chains and human-rights outcomes is materially diluted against
the original 2024 scope. The July 2031 review is the next natural window to
broaden it.


## Structure

The Directive has 39 Articles across 5 chapters + one Annex:

- **Chapter I: General provisions** (Articles 1-4) — subject matter, scope, definitions, level of harmonisation.
- **Chapter II: Due diligence obligations of companies** (Articles 5-16) — the operational core. Article 5 lays out the 8-step process; Articles 6-16 elaborate each step.
- **Chapter III: Support measures** (Articles 17-20) — Commission guidelines, single portal, financial support for SMEs.
- **Chapter IV: Enforcement** (Articles 21-29) — climate transition plan (Article 22), authorised representative (Article 23), supervisory authorities (Articles 24-25), substantiated concerns (Article 26), penalties (Article 27), European Network of Supervisory Authorities (Article 28), civil liability (Article 29).
- **Chapter V: General and final provisions** (Articles 30-39) — whistleblower protection (Article 30), public support/procurement (Article 31), review + reporting (Article 36), transposition (Article 37), entry into force (Article 38).
- **Annex I:** the list of protected human rights (Part I Section 1) + international instruments (Part I Section 2) + environmental prohibitions (Part II).

## Substantive core

### Scope (Article 2)

The Directive applies to:

**EU companies:**
- (a) more than **1,000 employees** on average AND net worldwide turnover **above €450 million** in the last financial year;
- (b) ultimate parent of a group meeting (a) on a consolidated basis;
- (c) franchising/licensing with royalties **above €22.5 million** in the Union AND turnover **above €80 million**.

**Third-country companies (extraterritorial reach):**
- (a) net turnover **above €450 million in the Union** in the financial year preceding the last;
- (b) ultimate parent of a group meeting (a);
- (c) franchising/licensing threshold applied to Union turnover.

Thresholds must be met for **two consecutive financial years** to trigger scope; scope drops after two consecutive years below. Pure holding companies can be exempted where a designated Union subsidiary fulfils Articles 6-16 and 22 on their behalf. AIFs and UCITS are excluded.

### The 8-step due diligence process (Article 5)

Member States must require companies to conduct risk-based human rights and environmental due diligence by:
1. **Integrating** due diligence into policies + risk management systems (Article 7)
2. **Identifying and assessing** actual and potential adverse impacts (Article 8)
3. **Prioritising** actual and potential adverse impacts where full simultaneous coverage is not feasible (Article 9)
4. **Preventing and mitigating** potential adverse impacts (Article 10) and **bringing actual adverse impacts to an end** or minimising their extent (Article 11)
5. **Providing remediation** for actual adverse impacts (Article 12)
6. **Meaningful engagement** with stakeholders (Article 13)
7. Notification mechanism + **complaints procedure** (Article 14)
8. **Monitoring** effectiveness (Article 15) + **publicly communicating** on due diligence (Article 16)

Companies must **retain documentation** proving compliance for at least 5 years.

### "Chain of activities" (Article 3(g))

CSDDD does not use "value chain" as CSRD does. Instead:
- **Upstream activities**: design, extraction, sourcing, manufacture, transport, storage and supply of raw materials, products and parts.
- **Downstream activities**: distribution, transport and storage of the company's product BUT ONLY where the business partner carries out those activities for the company or on its behalf. Excludes distribution/transport/storage of products subject to Regulation (EU) 2021/821 export controls, weapons, munitions or war materials once export has been authorised.

The downstream restriction is narrower than the 2022 Commission proposal and was contested during trilogues. Financial services distribution / lending are excluded from downstream due diligence.

### Climate transition plan (Article 22)

Every in-scope company must **adopt and put into effect** a transition plan for climate change mitigation compatible with 1.5°C and 2050 climate neutrality. The plan must contain:
- (a) **time-bound targets** for 2030 and in **5-year steps to 2050**, including absolute GHG reduction for scope 1, scope 2 and (where relevant) scope 3 in each significant category;
- (b) decarbonisation levers and key actions;
- (c) investment and funding quantification;
- (d) role of admin/management/supervisory bodies.

**CSRD deemer clause:** a company that reports a transition plan under CSRD Article 19a/29a/40a of Directive 2013/34/EU is **deemed** to have complied with CSDDD Article 22(1). Updated every **12 months**.

### Penalties (Article 27)

Member States must set effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties. Minimum:
- **Pecuniary penalties**: maximum cap **not less than 5% of net worldwide turnover** of the last financial year (consolidated turnover for group-parent cases).
- **Public statement** naming the non-compliant company where a pecuniary penalty is not paid on time.
- Penalty decisions published, kept available at least 5 years, sent to the European Network of Supervisory Authorities.

### Civil liability (Article 29)

Member States must ensure that a company can be held liable for damage where:
- (a) the company **intentionally or negligently** failed to comply with Articles 10 or 11 obligations aimed at protecting the person, AND
- (b) that failure caused damage to the person's legally protected interests.

Key mechanics:
- A company **cannot be held liable** for damage caused only by a business partner (concurrent-cause carve-out).
- **Full compensation** — no punitive, multiple or "over-compensation" damages.
- **Limitation period at least 5 years**, not starting until infringement ceases and claimant reasonably knows of the harm and infringer.
- Costs must not be **prohibitively expensive**; injunctive relief and evidence-disclosure orders available.
- **Trade unions, NGOs and national human rights institutions** may bring representative actions on the injured party's authorisation.
- **Overriding mandatory application** — applies even where the applicable law is not that of a Member State (Rome II carve-out).

Article 29 civil liability is the most politically contested provision. The Omnibus I substantive proposal would replace it with a discretionary Member-State option.

### Phased application (Article 37) — as postponed by Stop-the-clock

Directive (EU) 2025/794 postponed both the transposition deadline and the Wave 1 date by one year. Current schedule:

| Wave | Original date | Postponed date | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transposition | 26 July 2026 | **26 July 2027** | Member States adopt national law |
| Wave 1 apply | 26 July 2027 | **26 July 2028** | >5,000 employees + >€1.5bn worldwide turnover (EU + non-EU); Article 16 from FY starting 1 Jan 2029 |
| Wave 2 apply | 26 July 2028 | **26 July 2029** | >3,000 employees + >€900m worldwide turnover; Article 16 from FY 2030 |
| Wave 3 apply | 26 July 2029 | **26 July 2030** | All other in-scope companies (Article 2(1)(a-c), 2(2)(a-c)) plus franchising companies; Article 16 from FY 2030 |

## Key Numbers

- Scope threshold: **>1,000 employees + €450 million** net worldwide turnover (EU companies).
- Third-country threshold: **€450 million net turnover in the Union**.
- Franchising threshold: **€22.5 million royalties + €80 million turnover**.
- Penalty cap: **≥5% of net worldwide turnover** as minimum ceiling.
- Civil-liability limitation period: **at least 5 years**.
- Documentation retention: **at least 5 years**.
- Transition-plan update: **every 12 months**.
- Directive length: **39 Articles + Annex I** across 54 OJ pages.
- Approximately **6,000 EU companies + ~900 non-EU companies** are estimated in scope at full roll-out (Commission Impact Assessment 2022 estimate, may fall further if the substantive Omnibus is adopted).

## Omnibus I (February 2025)

Commission Omnibus I proposal (COM(2025) 81 final, 26 February 2025) proposes to:
- **Narrow due diligence** by default to own operations + direct (Tier 1) suppliers; look beyond Tier 1 only on "credible information";
- **Delete the harmonised civil-liability regime** of Article 29, leaving liability to national law;
- **Soften Article 22 climate transition plan** enforcement, dropping the "put into effect" obligation;
- **Postpone transposition** (this postponement was actually adopted separately as Directive (EU) 2025/794 in April 2025 — the "Stop-the-clock" file);
- Extend the review period from every 12 months to less frequent updates.

The **Stop-the-clock is already law**; the **substantive Omnibus is still under negotiation** in mid-2026. Do not conflate them.
