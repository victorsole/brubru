# EU Multilingual, Translation and Content Localisation Law

Guide for professionals working on content, translation, localisation and multilingual compliance in the EU. Covers the legal framework governing language requirements, content accessibility, audiovisual rules, and sector-specific translation obligations.

## Foundational Language Regime

### Regulation No 1/1958 (EEC Council)
The foundational regulation determining EU official and working languages. Currently 24 official languages. All EU regulations and documents of general application are drafted and published in all official languages. Each language version is equally authentic.

CELEX: 31958R0001

### Charter of Fundamental Rights - Article 22
"The Union shall respect cultural, religious and linguistic diversity." This underpins all EU multilingual policy.

## Content and Digital Services

### Digital Services Act (DSA) - Regulation (EU) 2022/2065
CELEX: 32022R2065

Content-relevant provisions:
- **Art. 14**: Terms of conditions must be available in all official languages of Member States where the service is offered
- **Art. 16**: Notice-and-action mechanisms must be accessible in the language of the Member State
- **Art. 27**: Transparency of recommender systems -- platforms must explain content curation in user's language
- **Art. 33-34**: Risk assessments must consider linguistic diversity and content moderation in all EU languages
- **Art. 42**: Codes of conduct for online advertising -- multilingual transparency requirements

### Digital Markets Act (DMA) - Regulation (EU) 2022/1925
CELEX: 32022R1925

- Gatekeepers must ensure interoperability and transparency across linguistic markets
- Anti-steering provisions apply regardless of language version of the service

### e-Commerce Directive - Directive 2000/31/EC
CELEX: 32000L0031

- **Art. 5**: Information society services must provide basic information in an accessible manner (including language considerations)
- **Art. 10**: Contract terms and general conditions must be made available in a way that allows storage and reproduction

## Audiovisual and Media Content

### Audiovisual Media Services Directive (AVMSD) - Directive 2018/1808/EU
CELEX: 32018L1808

Key content and localisation provisions:
- **Art. 13**: Video-on-demand services must ensure at least 30% European works in their catalogues and give them prominence
- **Art. 7**: Accessibility requirements -- services must be continuously and progressively made more accessible, including through subtitling, audio description, and sign language
- **Art. 28a-28b**: Video-sharing platform provisions -- content moderation must address linguistic diversity
- Promotes linguistic diversity in European audiovisual content
- Member States may impose financial contributions for local/regional content production

### European Media Freedom Act (EMFA) - Regulation (EU) 2024/1083
CELEX: 32024R1083

- Protects editorial independence and media pluralism across all EU languages
- **Art. 20**: Audience measurement systems must be transparent and non-discriminatory across linguistic markets
- Cross-border media services protected from discriminatory national restrictions

## Consumer Protection and Language Requirements

### Consumer Rights Directive - Directive 2011/83/EU
CELEX: 32011L0083

- **Art. 6-7**: Pre-contractual information must be provided in a clear and comprehensible manner; Member States may require the language of their territory
- **Art. 8**: Distance contracts -- information must be legible and in plain, intelligible language
- Member States commonly require consumer contracts in the national language(s)

### Unfair Commercial Practices Directive - Directive 2005/29/EC
CELEX: 32005L0029

- Misleading practices include providing information in a language the consumer cannot understand when this affects their purchasing decision
- Marketing content must not exploit linguistic confusion

### Product Safety: General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) - Regulation (EU) 2023/988
CELEX: 32023R0988

- **Art. 9**: Safety information, warnings and instructions must be in the language(s) of the Member State where the product is placed on the market
- Applies to all consumer products

## Accessibility and Language

### European Accessibility Act (EAA) - Directive 2019/882/EU
CELEX: 32019L0882

- Requires digital products and services to be accessible, which includes language-related requirements:
  - Screen reader compatibility (affects multilingual content structure)
  - Captioning and subtitling for audiovisual content
  - Plain language requirements for user interfaces
- Applies to: e-commerce, banking, transport, e-books, and more
- Deadline: 28 June 2025 for compliance

### Web Accessibility Directive - Directive 2016/2102/EU
CELEX: 32016L2102

- Public sector websites and mobile apps must meet WCAG 2.1 AA
- Language identification requirements (HTML lang attributes)
- Content must be perceivable regardless of language
- Accessibility statements must be provided in the national language(s)

## Sector-Specific Translation Requirements

### Medical Devices Regulation (MDR) - Regulation (EU) 2017/745
CELEX: 32017R0745

- **Art. 10(11)**: Labels, instructions for use, and implant cards must be in the official language(s) of the Member State where the device is made available
- Member States determine which languages are required on their territory

### In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation (IVDR) - Regulation (EU) 2017/746
CELEX: 32017R0746

- Same language requirements as MDR for IVD-specific documentation

### Regulation on Food Information to Consumers - Regulation (EU) 1169/2011
CELEX: 32011R1169

- **Art. 15**: Mandatory food labelling must be in a language easily understood by consumers in the Member State where the food is marketed
- Member States may require one or more official EU languages
- Allergen information must be clearly indicated in the local language

### Cosmetics Regulation - Regulation (EC) 1223/2009
CELEX: 32009R1223

- **Art. 19**: Product labelling (function, precautions, ingredients list) must be in the official language(s) of the Member State where the product is made available to the end user

### Pharmaceutical Legislation - Directive 2001/83/EC
CELEX: 32001L0083

- **Art. 63**: Labelling and package leaflets must be in the official language(s) of the Member State where the medicinal product is placed on the market
- Multilingual packaging permitted under certain conditions

## Data Protection and Language

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) - Regulation (EU) 2016/679
CELEX: 32016R0679

- **Art. 12**: Information to data subjects must be provided in a concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language
- **Art. 13-14**: Privacy notices must be comprehensible -- in practice, this means in the local language
- **Recital 58**: Particularly relevant for online and children's services -- information should be in the language of the target audience
- Cross-border data processing: communications with supervisory authorities in the language of the Member State

## AI and Language

### AI Act - Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
CELEX: 32024R1689

- **Art. 13**: Transparency requirements -- high-risk AI systems must provide instructions for use in a language easily understood in the Member State
- **Art. 50**: Transparency for general-purpose AI -- users must be informed when interacting with AI, in the relevant language
- Implications for machine translation, content generation, and automated localisation systems

## Public Procurement and Translation

### Public Procurement Directives
- Directive 2014/24/EU (classic sectors) - CELEX: 32014L0024
- Directive 2014/25/EU (utilities) - CELEX: 32014L0025

Tender documents may be required in the language of the contracting authority. Cross-border tenders raise practical language barriers that the Single Market Act seeks to reduce.

## Translation Industry Specific

### EN 17100:2015 (European Standard)
Not EU legislation but the European standard for translation services. Establishes requirements for translation service providers, including competence, qualifications, and quality assurance. Referenced in EU procurement for translation contracts.

### DG Translation (European Commission)
Largest translation service in the world (~2,300 staff). Translates ~2 million pages annually. Uses eTranslation (machine translation tool available to EU institutions and public administrations).

### eTranslation
EU's machine translation service, available free to public administrations and SMEs through the Digital Europe Programme. Supports all 24 official EU languages plus Icelandic, Norwegian and Ukrainian.
