# Chips JU (ex-KDT/ECSEL) and the Chips for Europe Initiative

## QUICK FACTS
- **Chips Joint Undertaking (Chips JU)**: the EU body implementing Pillar 1 (Chips for Europe Initiative) of the European Chips Act. Brussels-based, operates under the Joint Undertakings framework of Horizon Europe.
- Legal basis: **Council Regulation (EU) 2023/1782** of 25 July 2023 (CELEX 32023R1782), amending the Single Basic Act **Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085** (CELEX 32021R2085) which establishes the Joint Undertakings under Horizon Europe. Entered into force 21 September 2023, alongside the first Chips Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781).
- **Institutional lineage**: ECSEL JU (Electronic Components and Systems for European Leadership, Horizon 2020 era) -> Key Digital Technologies Joint Undertaking (KDT JU, early Horizon Europe) -> **renamed and expanded into the Chips Joint Undertaking** by Regulation (EU) 2023/1782. The Chips JU continues KDT JU's ongoing research and innovation activities and adds the Chips for Europe Initiative on top.
- **Governance model**: tripartite public-private partnership. Members are the European Union (represented by the Commission), Participating States (EU Member States plus associated third countries that opt in), and three Private Members industry associations: **AENEAS** (electronic components and systems), **EPoSS** (smart systems integration), and **INSIDE Industry Association** (nanoelectronics, successor of the former AENEAS/ARTEMIS-linked structures for this JU cycle).
- **Budget**: the EU contribution to the Chips JU was raised from EUR 1.8 billion (KDT JU baseline) to **EUR 4.175 billion** under the Chips Act. Including Participating States' matching contributions and private industry co-investment, the Chips JU's **grand total budget is close to EUR 11 billion (2023-2027)**.
- **Pillar 1 building blocks** (Article 5-14 of the first Chips Act, Regulation (EU) 2023/1781): the **virtual/Cloud-based Design Platform** (shared IP libraries and EDA tool access for fabless SMEs and start-ups); **Pilot Lines** (open-access lines to prototype and test chip designs at leading-edge nodes without a company having to own a fab); a **network of national Competence Centres** (at least one per Participating State, hub-and-spoke, built partly on existing European Digital Innovation Hubs); **quantum chip actions**; and **skills and training actions**.
- **The five Pilot Lines launched under the Multiannual Work Programme 2023-2027**:
  - **NanoIC** -- advanced/leading-edge node and gate-all-around technologies beyond 2nm; led by imec (Belgium); the largest of the five, EUR 2.5 billion total investment (EUR 700 million from Chips JU, EUR 700 million from the Flemish government, plus private/other public co-funding); opened February 2026.
  - **FAMES** -- FD-SOI (fully depleted silicon-on-insulator) ultra-low-power technologies; led by CEA-Leti (France); EUR 830 million, split equally between Chips JU and Participating States.
  - **APECS** -- Advanced Packaging and Heterogeneous Integration for Electronic Components and Systems (chiplets, advanced packaging, system integration); led by the Fraunhofer Group for Microelectronics / Research Fab Microelectronics Germany (FMD); EUR 730 million over roughly four and a half years.
  - **PIXEurope** -- integrated photonics / photonic integrated circuits (PICs); imec-led consortium of 20 partner entities from 11 countries; roughly EUR 400 million over 10 years.
  - **Wide Bandgap (WBG) Pilot Line** -- power semiconductors based on silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) for energy-efficient power electronics (EVs, renewables, grid infrastructure).
- **Note on the popular pilot-line labelling**: some shorthand summaries describe FAMES as the "advanced node" line and NanoIC as the "advanced packaging" line. Verified against imec and CEA-Leti sources, it is the reverse: **NanoIC = advanced node / gate-all-around**, **APECS = advanced packaging / heterogeneous integration**; FAMES is FD-SOI low-power, not advanced-node.
- **Competence Centres**: a structured network co-financed by the EU and national contributions; first wave (2024-2025 calls) reached 27+ Competence Centres across 24+ Member States plus Norway and Iceland, with a second call extending coverage toward all 27 Member States. First-wave investment exceeded EUR 170 million. Centres give SMEs access to Pilot Lines, the Design Platform, and local training.
- **Skills**: delivered mainly through the Competence Centres' training services and a dedicated Coordination and Support Action; the companion Chips Act guide (`eu_chips_act`) references the **European Semiconductor Education and Training (RESET)** network. A separately branded "Chips Skills Academy" is referenced in some secondary summaries of Pillar 1 skills actions but was not independently confirmed against a primary Chips JU source at the time of writing; treat as unverified until confirmed on chips-ju.europa.eu.
- **Chips Act 2.0 continuity (from COM(2026) 504, adopted 3 June 2026)**: the Chips JU keeps implementing Pillar 1, now "Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0", with six operational objectives -- design capacities, pilot lines/testbeds, quantum chips, competence centres, a **new photonics/PIC objective**, and a **new Chips Fund** (EIC Accelerator plus InvestEU blending for start-ups, scale-ups and SMEs). The Chips JU also gains a role designating "strategic projects" and "European semiconductor technology initiatives" under Pillar 2 of Chips Act 2.0. See `eu_chips_act_2_0` for full detail.
- **Recent state aid link**: 23 June 2026, the Commission approved EUR 76 million of German State aid for QuantumDiamonds GmbH (Munich) to build a first-of-a-kind semiconductor testing/metrology facility, framed as strengthening the value chain the Chips JU pilot lines feed into (EC press release IP/26/1393).
- **2026 activity level**: as of 8 July 2026, the Chips JU's live calls totalled roughly EUR 300 million across 16 open calls (figure moves; check chips-ju.europa.eu/Calls/ for the current snapshot).

## Overview

The Chips Joint Undertaking is the delivery arm of Pillar 1 of the European Chips Act, the EU's response to the 2021-2022 semiconductor shortage and to its long-standing dependency on Taiwanese and South Korean foundries for advanced logic chips. Where the Chips Act itself (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781, guide `eu_chips_act`) sets the legal framework across three pillars (research and innovation, manufacturing capacity, monitoring and crisis response), the Chips JU is the operational body that actually spends the research and innovation money: it runs calls, selects and co-funds consortia, and manages the shared infrastructure (pilot lines, design tools, competence centres) that individual companies, especially SMEs and start-ups, could not build alone.

The Chips JU did not appear from nothing. It is the latest link in a chain of EU semiconductor and electronics public-private partnerships going back to Horizon 2020: **ECSEL JU** (Electronic Components and Systems for European Leadership) ran from 2014, itself a merger of the earlier ENIAC and ARTEMIS Joint Undertakings. Under Horizon Europe (2021-2027), ECSEL JU's mandate was carried forward by the **Key Digital Technologies Joint Undertaking (KDT JU)**, established under the Single Basic Act for Joint Undertakings, Council Regulation (EU) 2021/2085. When the Chips Act package was adopted in 2023, the Council did not create a brand-new body: it amended Regulation (EU) 2021/2085 via **Council Regulation (EU) 2023/1782**, which renamed and substantially expanded KDT JU into the Chips Joint Undertaking, giving it the new Chips for Europe Initiative mandate on top of KDT JU's ongoing research and innovation portfolio. This lineage matters for anyone tracing multi-year projects: a project that began life as an ECSEL or KDT JU grant may now report under the Chips JU without having changed its underlying consortium.

Governance is tripartite. The European Union (via the Commission) sits alongside "Participating States" (EU Member States and Horizon Europe-associated third countries that choose to co-fund the JU) and three private industry members: **AENEAS**, **EPoSS**, and **INSIDE Industry Association**. This structure mirrors other Horizon Europe Joint Undertakings (e.g. Clean Hydrogen JU, EuroHPC JU): public money is matched by Participating States and leveraged further by private co-investment in each individual project, so the headline budget figure is always larger than the EU appropriation alone.

## Budget

| Component | Amount | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| EU contribution (post-Chips Act) | EUR 4.175 billion | Raised from the KDT JU baseline of EUR 1.8 billion |
| Participating States' matching contributions | Included in grand total | Varies by call and consortium |
| Private industry co-investment | Included in grand total | AENEAS/EPoSS/INSIDE members and consortium partners |
| **Grand total (2023-2027)** | **~EUR 11 billion** | Public plus private, across the full Multiannual Work Programme |
| Chips JU share of the five Pilot Lines | Roughly half of each Pilot Line's total budget | E.g. NanoIC: EUR 700m of EUR 2.5bn; FAMES: 50% of EUR 830m |
| Competence Centres (first wave) | >EUR 170 million | Co-financed EU plus national contributions |

The Chips JU budget sits alongside, and should not be confused with, the much larger Pillar 2 figures (state aid for Integrated Production Facilities and Open EU Foundries) or the EUR 43 billion aggregate target for the whole Chips Act. The Chips JU's ~EUR 11 billion is specifically the research, pilot-line, design and skills money channelled through Horizon Europe and the Digital Europe Programme, not fab-construction subsidies.

## The five Pilot Lines

Pilot Lines are the Chips JU's flagship Pillar 1 instrument: open-access fabrication and testing capacity where companies (especially SMEs and start-ups without their own fab) can prototype and validate chip designs at leading-edge process nodes, at advanced packaging level, or in specialised technologies, without the capital cost of owning the equipment.

| Pilot Line | Focus | Lead partner | Budget |
|------------|-------|---------------|--------|
| NanoIC | Advanced/leading-edge node, gate-all-around, beyond-2nm technologies | imec (Belgium) | EUR 2.5bn total (EUR 700m Chips JU + EUR 700m Flemish government + other) |
| FAMES | FD-SOI (fully depleted silicon-on-insulator) ultra-low-power technologies | CEA-Leti (France) | EUR 830m (50/50 Chips JU / Participating States) |
| APECS | Advanced packaging and heterogeneous integration (chiplets, system integration) | Fraunhofer Group for Microelectronics / FMD (Germany) | EUR 730m over ~4.5 years |
| PIXEurope | Integrated photonics / photonic integrated circuits | imec-led consortium, 20 partners, 11 countries | ~EUR 400m over 10 years |
| Wide Bandgap (WBG) | Power semiconductors: silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) | Multi-partner consortium | Figure not separately verified; part of the ~EUR 11bn envelope |

NanoIC, the largest of the five, opened in February 2026 and was described in coverage as Europe's largest Chips Act pilot line to date. Note the common mislabelling: NanoIC is the advanced-node line, not the advanced-packaging line; APECS is the advanced-packaging line. FAMES is a low-power specialist line, not an advanced-node line, despite some secondary summaries conflating the two.

## Design Platform, Competence Centres, skills, quantum

**Cloud-based Design Platform**: gives fabless SMEs and start-ups shared access to IP libraries and electronic design automation (EDA) tools that would otherwise be prohibitively expensive to license individually. It is one of the six operational objectives carried into Chips Act 2.0 Pillar 1.

**Competence Centres**: a network intended to reach at least one hub per Participating State (built in part on the existing European Digital Innovation Hub infrastructure). First-wave selection (2024-2025) covered more than 27 centres across 24+ Member States plus Norway and Iceland; a second call extended the ambition toward full 27-Member-State coverage. Centres provide local technical expertise, access to the Design Platform and the Pilot Lines, and training tailored to regional industry needs, with SMEs as the priority audience.

**Skills**: training and education actions delivered mainly through the Competence Centres and a dedicated Coordination and Support Action (CSA) connecting the network. The companion Chips Act guide references a European Semiconductor Education and Training (RESET) network as the vehicle for this. Some secondary sources refer to a "Chips Skills Academy" as a Pillar 1 skills brand; this was not independently confirmed against a primary Chips JU source and should be treated as unverified pending direct confirmation on chips-ju.europa.eu.

**Quantum chip actions**: cover superconducting, photonic, quantum-dot and ion-trap qubit chip pilot activities, positioning the Chips JU as an enabling infrastructure layer for the EU's broader quantum technology ambitions (linking to the Quantum strategy and, on the compute side, to EuroHPC JU).

## Relationship to the wider Chips Act architecture

| Instrument | What it covers | Relationship to Chips JU |
|------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Chips Act, Pillar 1 (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781, Arts 5-14) | Legal mandate for the Chips for Europe Initiative | Chips JU is the implementing body |
| Chips Act, Pillar 2 (IPFs and OEFs, state aid) | Manufacturing capacity, fab construction | Separate from Chips JU; Member State/Commission state-aid track, though Chips Act 2.0 gives Chips JU a designation role for "strategic projects" |
| Chips Act, Pillar 3 (monitoring, crisis response) | European Semiconductor Board, crisis measures | Separate governance track (national competent authorities plus Commission), not run by Chips JU |
| IPCEI Microelectronics and Communication Technologies (ME/CT) | Member State-coordinated Important Projects of Common European Interest, cleared under State aid rules | Complements Chips JU-funded pre-competitive R&I with closer-to-market, Member State-driven investment |
| Chips Act 2.0 (COM(2026) 504) | Repeals and replaces the first Chips Act, adds photonics + Chips Fund to Pillar 1, adds strategic projects/ESTIs to Pillar 2 | Chips JU keeps implementing (now-expanded) Pillar 1 and gains designation functions under Pillar 2 |
| STEP Regulation (EU) 2024/795 | EUR 29 billion pan-EU strategic technologies platform (cohesion, innovation, Digital Europe funds) | Complements, does not replace, Chips JU funding; both feed semiconductor investment |
| Digital Europe Programme / Horizon Europe | Underlying EU budget lines | Source of the Chips JU's EU contribution |
