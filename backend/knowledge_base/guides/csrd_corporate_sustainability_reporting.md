## Overview

CSRD is the EU's mandatory corporate sustainability reporting mandate. It amends four separate instruments simultaneously — Directive 2013/34/EU (Accounting), Directive 2004/109/EC (Transparency), Directive 2006/43/EC (Statutory Audit), and Regulation (EU) No 537/2014 (Audit of PIEs) — to require large undertakings, listed SMEs, and large third-country undertakings with EU operations to disclose standardised sustainability information as a dedicated section of the management report, prepared in accordance with mandatory European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), assured by an independent third party, and digitally tagged in ESEF.

It replaces the earlier Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD, Directive 2014/95/EU) which had covered only ~11,700 large public-interest entities with more than 500 employees, was voluntary as to reporting framework, and had no assurance requirement. CSRD expands the scope by roughly 5x, mandates a Union-wide standard, mandates assurance (initially limited, ultimately reasonable), and mandates digital machine-readability.

CSRD is the single reporting instrument in a wider EU sustainable-finance and corporate-responsibility framework that includes SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088) governing financial-market participants' own disclosures, the Taxonomy Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2020/852) providing the classification of sustainable economic activities, and CSDDD (Directive (EU) 2024/1760) mandating due diligence over own operations, subsidiaries, and value-chain business partners.

## Structure

The Directive has 8 Articles:
- **Article 1** — amends Directive 2013/34/EU (Accounting). The bulk of the substantive content. Introduces new Articles 19a (single-undertaking sustainability reporting), 29a (consolidated sustainability reporting), 29b (Commission-adopted ESRS), 29c (SME ESRS), 29d (single electronic reporting format), 40a-40d (third-country reporting), and amends Article 34 (assurance) and Article 49 (delegated-acts empowerment).
- **Article 2** — amends Directive 2004/109/EC (Transparency): extends reporting-format requirements to sustainability information for listed issuers.
- **Article 3** — amends Directive 2006/43/EC (Statutory Audit): introduces the statutory auditor's role in assuring sustainability reporting (unless Member States open the market to independent assurance service providers).
- **Article 4** — amends Regulation (EU) No 537/2014 (Audit of PIEs): allows statutory auditors to provide sustainability-reporting assurance without pre-approval by the audit committee, and clarifies non-audit services caps.
- **Article 5** — transposition. Deadline 6 July 2024. Phased application by wave (see "Key numbers" below).
- **Article 6** — review clause. Commission report by 30 April 2029 (and every 3 years); assurance-market concentration report by 31 December 2028.
- **Article 7** — entry into force (5 January 2023). Article 4 applies from 1 January 2024.
- **Article 8** — addressees (Member States; Article 4 directly applicable).

## Substantive core — new Article 19a (sustainability reporting)

Sustainability information must be presented in a **dedicated identifiable section of the management report**. It must cover both:
- the undertaking's impacts on sustainability matters (impact materiality), and
- how sustainability matters affect the undertaking's development, performance and position (financial materiality).

The requirement to consider both perspectives is the **double materiality principle** — codified explicitly in the operative text.

Mandatory content areas (Article 19a(2)):
- (a) business model and strategy, including resilience to sustainability risks, opportunities, and the undertaking's **transition plan** compatible with 1.5°C warming (Paris Agreement) and climate neutrality by 2050 (European Climate Law), with disclosure of exposure to coal, oil and gas
- (b) time-bound targets, including at minimum **absolute greenhouse-gas emission reduction targets for 2030 and 2050**
- (c) governance — role of administrative, management, and supervisory bodies
- (d) sustainability policies
- (e) incentive schemes linked to sustainability matters
- (f) due diligence process; principal actual/potential adverse impacts across own operations, products/services, business relationships and supply chain; remediation actions
- (g) principal risks (including dependencies) and risk management
- (h) indicators relevant to (a)-(g)

Information must be forward-looking AND retrospective; qualitative AND quantitative; cover short-, medium- and long-term horizons; and cover the **whole value chain** — with a 3-year grace period during which unavailable value-chain information can be explained rather than reported.

## Consolidated sustainability reporting — new Article 29a

Parent undertakings of large groups must prepare **consolidated sustainability reporting** at group level. Subsidiary undertakings included in a parent's consolidated report are exempt from single-undertaking reporting, subject to a set of publicity conditions (linking to the parent report, indicating the exemption). Large listed subsidiaries are NOT exempt (investor protection).

## European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) — new Article 29b

The Commission adopts ESRS by **delegated acts**, based on technical advice from **EFRAG** (European Financial Reporting Advisory Group). ESRS must specify what to disclose across all sustainability matters (environmental, social, human rights, governance). Commission consults Member State Expert Group on Sustainable Finance, Accounting Regulatory Committee, ESMA/EBA/EIOPA (2-month opinion window), EEA, FRA, ECB, CEAOB, Platform on Sustainable Finance.

**First set of general ESRS:** adopted 31 July 2023 by **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772** — 12 standards: 2 cross-cutting (ESRS 1 general requirements + ESRS 2 general disclosures) + 5 environmental (E1 climate change, E2 pollution, E3 water and marine, E4 biodiversity and ecosystems, E5 resource use and circular economy) + 4 social (S1 own workforce, S2 workers in the value chain, S3 affected communities, S4 consumers and end-users) + 1 governance (G1 business conduct).

**Sector-specific ESRS:** original mandate to adopt by 30 June 2024. **Deferred to 30 June 2026** by Directive (EU) 2023/2775 ("Stop-the-clock" for standards adoption).

**SME ESRS (VSME + LSME):** Article 29c mandates a proportionate voluntary standard for non-listed SMEs (VSME) and a mandatory standard for listed SMEs (LSME). VSME draft published by EFRAG December 2024.

## Digital tagging — new Article 29d

Sustainability information must be prepared in **single electronic reporting format** (ESEF) per Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/815. Undertakings must **mark up (tag)** sustainability information according to a digital taxonomy the Commission adopts by delegated act. The tagging obligation applies from financial years starting on or after 1 January 2026 (Article 5(2), point (14) derogation).

## Third-country undertakings — new Articles 40a-40d

A third-country undertaking with **net turnover in the Union above €150 million** in each of the last two consecutive financial years, and at least one EU subsidiary that is a large or listed undertaking or one EU branch with net turnover above €40 million, must publish an EU-territory-only sustainability report through that subsidiary or branch. Report standards to be adopted by delegated act by 30 June 2024. **Application: financial years starting on or after 1 January 2028** (wave 4).

## Assurance — amended Article 34

Reporting undertakings must obtain an assurance opinion on their sustainability reporting.
- **Limited assurance** is required from wave 1.
- The Commission may adopt **limited-assurance standards by 1 October 2026** and **reasonable-assurance standards by 1 October 2028** (by delegated acts).
- Assurance is performed by the **statutory auditor** by default (Article 34), or by an **independent assurance services provider** (IASP) accredited under Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 — Member States may choose to open the market to IASPs.
- Where the statutory auditor performs the assurance, the Regulation (EU) 537/2014 non-audit-services fee cap excludes sustainability-assurance work.

## Sanctions

Amendments to Article 51 of Directive 2013/34/EU require Member States to lay down effective, proportionate, dissuasive **sanctions** for infringements of the national provisions adopted pursuant to CSRD.

## Key Numbers

- Reporting entities in scope (as originally designed, pre-Omnibus): approximately **50,000 undertakings** across the Union (up from ~11,700 under NFRD).
- Transposition deadline: **6 July 2024** (18 months after entry into force).
- Wave 1 (FY starting on or after 1 January 2024, first reports in 2025): large public-interest entities (PIEs) with >500 employees on average during the financial year — the group already reporting under NFRD.
- Wave 2 (FY starting on or after 1 January 2025, first reports in 2026): all other large undertakings meeting 2 of 3 thresholds (>€25M balance sheet, >€50M net turnover, >250 employees).
- Wave 3 (FY starting on or after 1 January 2026, first reports in 2027): listed SMEs (except micro), small non-complex credit institutions, captive (re)insurance undertakings — with a Member-State-optional opt-out until financial years starting before 1 January 2028.
- Wave 4 (FY starting on or after 1 January 2028): third-country undertakings with EU turnover >€150M via EU subsidiary or branch.
- Value-chain grace period: **3 years** during which an undertaking may explain unobtained value-chain information rather than report it.
- Commission review report: by **30 April 2029** and every 3 years thereafter.
- Assurance-market concentration review: by **31 December 2028**.
- OJ publication length: **66 pages** (L 322, 16.12.2022, p. 15-80).
- Amends **4 instruments** (1 Regulation + 3 Directives).

## Omnibus I (2025) — the wave shift and the scope narrowing

CSRD's original wave schedule has been amended twice by the Omnibus I sustainability-simplification package, tabled by the Commission on 26 February 2025 (COM(2025) 81 final and COM(2025) 82 final):

- **Stop-the-clock Directive (EU) 2025/794**, adopted 14 April 2025 and published OJ L, 2025/794, 16 April 2025. Postpones Wave 2 and Wave 3 by **two years**: Wave 2 slips from FY2025 to FY2027 (first reports in 2028) and Wave 3 slips from FY2026 to FY2028 (first reports in 2029). Wave 1 (large PIEs >500 employees, already reporting since FY2024) is unchanged.
- **Substantive Omnibus proposal** — still under co-legislator negotiation as of mid-2026. Key proposed changes: raise the "large undertaking" employee threshold from 250 to **1,000**, cutting in-scope firms by roughly 80% (to ~7,000 undertakings). Simplify ESRS. Make sector-specific ESRS voluntary. Cap SME data requests. These changes are NOT yet law and depend on the political outcome.

**Do not conflate the two.** Stop-the-clock only postponed dates; the substantive Omnibus which changes thresholds and scope is a separate, contested file.
