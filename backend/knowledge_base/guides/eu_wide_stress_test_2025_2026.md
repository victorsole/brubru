# EU-wide Bank Stress Test: Methodology, Coverage, Results

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: EU-wide bank stress test, biennial resilience exercise coordinated by the European Banking Authority (EBA)
- Coordinators: EBA (leads the exercise and sets the methodology), European Central Bank / Single Supervisory Mechanism (ECB/SSM, runs a parallel exercise for banks outside the EBA sample), European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB, designs the adverse macro-financial scenario), national competent authorities
- 2025 EBA sample: 64 banks from 17 EU and EEA countries, covering 75% of EU banking sector assets (51 of the 64 are euro area banks under direct ECB supervision)
- 2025 ECB/SSM parallel sample: 45 additional medium-sized euro area banks assessed separately by the ECB using EBA templates and scenarios, adjusted for proportionality; combined coverage across both exercises is around 96 institutions
- Methodology: constrained bottom-up exercise (banks project their own losses under common scenarios and assumptions set by supervisors), static balance-sheet assumption (no management actions or new business assumed), three-year projection horizon
- 2025 adverse scenario: sharp deterioration in the global macro-financial environment driven by resurgent geopolitical tensions, entrenched trade fragmentation and higher tariffs, persistent supply shocks, simultaneous and prolonged recessions in the EU and globally, high unemployment, and sharp falls in asset prices
- 2025 results (published 1 August 2025): aggregate losses of EUR 547 billion in credit, market and operational risk; average CET1 capital depletion of 370 basis points; end-of-scenario CET1 ratio of 12% (transitional basis, reflecting CRR3 transitional arrangements)
- NOT a pass/fail test: there is no minimum capital threshold to "pass". Results feed the Pillar 2 Guidance (P2G) set under the Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP), plus wider supervisory dialogue on capital and distribution plans
- Next full EU-wide stress test: 2027 round already announced, planned sample of 63 banks from the EU and Norway (47 euro area), covering around 75% of EU banking sector assets; draft methodology and templates published June 2026, with a simplified framework reducing data requirements and integrating climate-related risks
- Off years: EBA runs lighter transparency exercises and thematic or exploratory scenarios (for example climate risk, counterparty credit risk) between the biennial full exercises

## What the exercise is for

The EU-wide stress test measures how resilient the EU banking sector is to a severe but plausible economic shock. It is not a market-facing "pass or fail" test like the post-financial-crisis US stress tests were originally designed to be. Instead, it is a supervisory tool: results are one of the quantitative inputs supervisors use to calibrate each bank's Pillar 2 Guidance (P2G), the additional capital buffer a bank is expected to hold on top of its binding Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 Requirement (P2R) minimums under the Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) framework.

Because P2G is not itself a legally binding requirement (unlike P2R), a bank that falls short of its P2G does not automatically breach the Maximum Distributable Amount (MDA) trigger, but supervisors expect banks to operate above it and will discuss capital and dividend plans with any bank that repeatedly falls short.

## Institutional roles

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| European Banking Authority (EBA) | Designs and coordinates the overall methodology, publishes bank-by-bank results for its sample, sets common assumptions and templates |
| European Central Bank / Single Supervisory Mechanism (ECB/SSM) | Runs the EBA-sample banks it directly supervises through the same exercise; separately stress-tests additional medium-sized euro area banks outside the EBA sample using the same templates and scenarios, adjusted for proportionality |
| European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB) | Designs the adverse macro-financial scenario in cooperation with the ECB and national central banks; the baseline scenario typically follows the latest official EU macroeconomic projections |
| National competent authorities (NCAs) | Quality-assure bank submissions, participate in the supervisory challenge process, and use results in their own SREP assessments for non-SSM banks |

## Methodology

- **Constrained bottom-up approach**: participating banks calculate their own projected losses and capital trajectories under the common scenarios, but supervisors constrain or override certain projections (notably net interest income and some market risk components) to ensure comparability across banks and prevent overly optimistic self-assessment.
- **Static balance sheet**: banks are assumed to keep the size and composition of their balance sheet broadly constant over the three-year horizon; no new business growth, deleveraging or management actions (such as raising capital or cutting dividends) are factored in. This makes the exercise a stress on the existing book, not a forecast of what banks would actually do in a real downturn.
- **Two scenarios**: a baseline scenario (aligned with official macroeconomic projections) and an adverse scenario (a severe but plausible tail-risk narrative designed by the ESRB). Results are reported for both, with the gap between them (capital depletion) being the key resilience metric.
- **Three-year horizon**: losses and capital ratios are projected year by year across the full exercise period.
- **Risk coverage**: credit risk, market risk, counterparty credit risk, operational and conduct risk, net interest income, and net fee and commission income.

## Results and interpretation

The 2025 exercise found EU and EEA banks in the sample would, in aggregate, absorb EUR 547 billion of losses under the adverse scenario, depleting average CET1 capital by 370 basis points to an end-of-scenario ratio of 12% on a transitional basis. The EBA's headline conclusion was that the banking sector, in aggregate, would remain adequately capitalised even under a severe geopolitical and trade-fragmentation shock, though results vary significantly bank by bank and are published at individual institution level.

Because the exercise is bank-specific, the most useful reading for any single institution is not the aggregate figure but its own capital depletion relative to peers, which supervisors use directly in setting that bank's P2G for the following supervisory cycle.

## Timeline and what to track next

- **2025 round**: launched Q1 2025, results published 1 August 2025.
- **2027 round**: already announced with a planned sample of 63 EU and Norwegian banks (47 euro area), draft methodology and templates published June 2026. The 2027 framework is explicitly designed to reduce data requirements on participating banks and integrates climate-related risk considerations more directly into the scenario design.
- **Between rounds**: watch for EBA transparency exercises (lighter-touch, wider-sample disclosure exercises) and any thematic or exploratory stress tests (for example on climate risk or counterparty credit risk), which can affect supervisory expectations even in years without a full EU-wide test.
