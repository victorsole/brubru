# Unit Cost Decision: Travel

## QUICK FACTS
- **Applies to:** Horizon Europe, EIC, CEF (Connecting Europe Facility), DIGITAL Europe, and all 2021-2027 EU funding programmes under direct or indirect management
- **Optional use:** Beneficiaries may choose unit costs OR actual costs (but not both for the same trip category)
- **Three cost categories:** International air/rail travel (400+ km), land travel (50-399 km), daily subsistence, accommodation per night
- **Zoning system:** Distance bands for air travel (400-600 km, 601-1600 km, up to 10001+ km); country-by-country rates for land travel; per-country accommodation and subsistence ceilings
- **Rate-setting:** Based on representative market prices (2023 study: 131,000 flight tickets, 21,000+ connections, 800+ train ticket prices)
- **Latest update:** Commission Decision C(2024) 5405 (31 July 2024)
- **Green incentive:** Unit costs promote rail travel up to 600 km as a sustainable alternative to flights; air travel <600 km capped at EUR 340 return
- **Documentation:** Travel reports and proof of attendance required; ex-post verification accepted (no pre-approval needed)
- **Audit simplification:** Reduces administrative burden and risk of error compared to actual-cost claims
- **Auto-update:** Accommodation and subsistence costs automatically align with EU Staff Regulations changes (Article 13 of Annex VII)

## What this decision allows (unit-cost travel claims)

Commission Decision C(2024) 5405 authorises beneficiaries of EU-funded actions (grants under direct or indirect management) to declare travel, accommodation and subsistence costs using standardised unit cost amounts rather than submitting itemised receipts. This simplification reduces administrative burden for both beneficiaries and granting authorities.

**Key principle:** When unit costs are used to reimburse any category of travel, accommodation, or subsistence, no additional costs in those categories may be claimed. The unit cost is an all-in amount.

**Optional nature:** Beneficiaries are not required to use unit costs. They may instead claim actual costs incurred if they retain supporting documentation. However, once a choice is made for an individual trip, they cannot mix unit costs with actual costs for that journey.

**Who may use them:** Recipients of Union funds implementing actions or work programmes under 2021-2027 multiannual financial framework appropriations. This includes:
- Horizon Europe and its constituent programmes (EIC, MSCA, etc.)
- Digital Europe Programme
- Connecting Europe Facility (CEF)
- Erasmus+
- Justice Programme
- Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values (CERV)
- Creative Europe
- And other 2021-2027 programmes in direct or indirect management

## Categories of travel covered (intercity, international, daily)

### Air travel and combined air/rail journeys
Travel by air for distances of 400 km or more (one-way). Unit costs apply to return trips. The amount covers the flight itself plus any associated land-based travel (bus or train) to/from the airport.

**Combined air/rail:** When a beneficiary uses rail for part of a journey and air for another, the air travel unit cost applies (which already factors in the land connection component).

### Rail travel
Land travel by train within the EU and to/from neighbouring countries. Rail journeys of 50-399 km one-way are covered by intra-Member State or inter-Member State unit costs. Rail journeys of 400-600 km have dedicated higher rates to encourage sustainable travel. Rail travel above 600 km may also be reimbursed using actual costs if no unit cost is specified in the Decision.

### Land travel (train, bus, car)
Travel distances between 50 and 399 km one-way using trains, buses or private vehicles. Unit costs exist for:
- **Intra-Member State travel:** One fixed amount per Member State for distances 50-399 km; some larger EU countries (DE, ES, FI, FR, IT, PL, SE) have additional unit costs for 400-600 km distances
- **Inter-Member State travel:** Specific amounts for journeys between pairs of Member States
- **Cross-border travel:** Journeys between EU Member States and neighbouring third countries (Liechtenstein, Switzerland, Serbia, Turkey, etc.)

**Private car reimbursement:** Where necessary, costs for using a private car are reimbursed at the same rate as other land travel, but not exceeding the unit cost for land travel up to 399 km.

### Accommodation
Hotel or equivalent lodging for one night in any EU Member State, EEA/EFTA country, candidate country, UK, or countries under European Neighbourhood Policy. Unit costs are expressed per night and differ by country.

### Subsistence (daily allowance)
A 24-hour daily allowance covering meals and incidental expenses (such as local public transport) during the trip. Amount depends on destination country.

## When to use unit cost vs actual cost

### Use unit costs when:
1. Administrative simplification is a priority (especially for consortia with many small trips)
2. Travel patterns are diverse or difficult to predict in detail
3. The beneficiary can meet the documentation requirement (travel report and proof of attendance, checked ex-post)
4. The unit cost reasonably reflects expected market prices for the destination and distance

### Use actual costs when:
1. A specific journey deviates significantly from the standard unit cost (e.g. a flight booked last-minute at double the normal price)
2. Travel is not covered by any unit cost (e.g. internal travel over 600 km without a specified unit cost, or travel to destinations not listed in the accommodation/subsistence tables)
3. The beneficiary prefers to retain and submit itemised invoices and boarding passes

**Important:** Once a choice is made for a trip, do not mix methods. If unit cost is claimed for air fare, subsistence and accommodation for that trip must also be claimed using unit costs (or not at all). If actual costs are submitted for the air fare, actual costs must be submitted for subsistence and accommodation too.

**Exception:** For rail travel above 600 km or not covered by the unit costs, and for air travel in exceptional situations (e.g. from an island where land travel is impractical), actual costs may be reimbursed even if other trip categories use unit costs.

## Documentation required (travel report, attendance proof)

### For all claims (unit cost or actual):
- **Travel report:** A brief statement documenting the journey (departure point, arrival point, purpose, dates)
- **Proof of attendance:** Evidence that the travel was linked to the implementation of the action (participation list, meeting agenda, email confirmation, etc.)
- **No itemised receipts required for unit costs:** A boarding pass or travel itinerary is sufficient to confirm the trip took place and the distance travelled

### For actual costs (when chosen instead of unit costs):
- Itemised invoices or receipts (airline tickets, train tickets, hotel bills, meal receipts)
- Each document must show the beneficiary name, date, amount, and nature of the expense
- For accommodation and subsistence, the dates of stay must be clear

### Distance measurement:
Use the official calculator provided by the European Commission at https://ec.europa.eu/info/calculate-unit-costs-eligible-travel-costs_en to determine the one-way distance for air and rail travel. This calculator uses standard routes and is binding for unit cost calculations.

### Audit verification:
Auditors will check that:
- The trip was genuinely necessary and linked to the action
- The distance measured by the calculator corresponds to the actual route
- No double reimbursement (the same trip claimed under multiple cost categories)
- Accommodation and subsistence rates do not exceed those set in the Decision for the destination country
- If actual costs were claimed, invoices are original and itemised

## Common audit issues

1. **Mixing unit costs with actual costs:** Claiming a unit cost for the flight but then adding actual meal receipts or a higher hotel invoice. Not permitted; must be consistent across all trip costs.

2. **Over-claiming accommodation or subsistence:** Submitting a hotel bill for EUR 180/night in Belgium when the unit cost ceiling is EUR 137. Difference is ineligible.

3. **Incorrect distance band:** Measuring distance incorrectly (e.g. straight-line "as the crow flies" instead of routable distance), leading to a lower unit cost being claimed than justified. Always use the EC calculator.

4. **Missing proof of attendance:** Claiming travel costs without evidence that the trip was required for the action. A travel report alone is not sufficient; link to meeting minutes, email invitations, or project activities is needed.

5. **Claiming subsistence for fewer than 24 hours:** The unit cost is a full-day allowance. If travel is part-day, actual costs may be claimed, or the unit cost may apply if the beneficiary was away overnight.

6. **Inadequate documentation of exceptional air travel:** When claiming actual costs for air travel under 400 km in exceptional circumstances (e.g. from an island), the exceptional nature must be explained and justified in the travel report.

7. **Accommodation in outermost regions or OCTs:** These regions no longer have separate unit costs; use the rate for the relevant Member State as a proxy. If the beneficiary incurs higher costs, actual costs may be claimed but must be documented.

8. **Dual funding:** Beneficiaries may not claim travel costs both under the unit cost scheme and from another funding source (e.g. a co-financing organisation) for the same trip.
