# CLP Regulation: Classification, Labelling and Packaging

## What CLP does

CLP is the operative EU regulation that determines how a substance or mixture is classified as hazardous, and how that hazard must be communicated through labels and packaging before the product can be placed on the EU market. It is built on three pillars:

1. **Classification**: manufacturers, importers and downstream users must classify substances and mixtures against harmonised criteria covering physical hazards (explosivity, flammability, oxidising properties), health hazards (acute toxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, sensitisation) and environmental hazards (aquatic toxicity, ozone-layer effects).
2. **Labelling**: classified substances and mixtures must carry standardised labels with GHS pictograms, a signal word ("Danger" or "Warning"), hazard statements (H-statements) and precautionary statements (P-statements).
3. **Packaging**: hazardous products must use packaging that prevents leakage, resists the contents, and -- where required -- includes child-resistant fastenings and tactile hazard warnings.

CLP replaced the older EU Dangerous Substances Directive and Dangerous Preparations Directive to align the EU with the UN GHS, making EU hazard classification broadly consistent with major trading partners (though the EU applies its own harmonised classifications on top of the UN framework).

## Harmonised classification and labelling (CLH)

For substances of particular concern -- carcinogens, mutagens, reproductive toxicants (CMR), respiratory sensitisers, and active substances under pesticide/biocide law -- classification is not left to company self-assessment. A Member State authority or a manufacturer/importer submits a CLH dossier to ECHA. ECHA's Risk Assessment Committee (RAC) issues a scientific opinion, and the Commission adopts the classification as a legally binding entry in **CLP Annex VI**. For other hazard classes, CLH remains optional but companies can still request it to secure a single EU-wide classification rather than relying on self-classification.

## The 2023 new hazard classes

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/707 (19 December 2022, CELEX 32023R0707) introduced six new hazard categories into CLP Annex I, closing a long-standing gap where CLP had no dedicated criteria for endocrine disruption or for persistence/mobility hazards in the environment:

| Hazard class | Scope |
|---|---|
| Endocrine disruptor for human health (ED-HH) | Substances that interfere with the endocrine system and cause adverse effects in humans |
| Endocrine disruptor for the environment (ED-ENV) | Substances that interfere with the endocrine system and cause adverse effects in non-target organisms |
| PBT / vPvB | Persistent, bioaccumulative and toxic / very persistent, very bioaccumulative substances |
| PMT / vPvM | Persistent, mobile and toxic / very persistent, very mobile substances -- targets "forever chemicals" that migrate readily through water, including many PFAS |

These criteria became applicable to substances from 1 May 2025 and to mixtures from 1 May 2026, giving industry a staged transition to reclassify portfolios. They feed directly into REACH: a harmonised ED, PBT/vPvB or PMT/vPvM classification is a route to SVHC (Substance of Very High Concern) identification.

## The December 2024 CLP revision (Regulation (EU) 2024/2865)

This is the first substantive revision of the CLP Regulation's operative text since 2008, responding to the growth of online chemical sales and gaps in the labelling regime. Key elements:

- **Online and distance sales**: sellers marketing hazardous chemicals to EU consumers online -- including from outside the EU -- must display the required hazard information clearly on the sales listing and, where based outside the EU, ensure a person established in the EU is responsible for compliance.
- **Refill stations**: new labelling and packaging rules for substances and mixtures dispensed without packaging (for example fuel and cleaning-product refill points).
- **Digital labelling**: suppliers may provide supplemental (non-mandatory) label information via a digital label accessed through a data carrier (such as a QR code) affixed to the physical label or packaging; the mandatory core label elements must still appear physically.
- **Fold-out and group labelling**: more flexible label formats for small packaging and for groups of similar products.
- **Classification of complex substances (UVCBs) and nanoforms**: hazard assessment must be based on the individual known components of a complex substance rather than only its overall composition, closing a route by which hazardous components could be diluted out of a classification; specific criteria are introduced for nanoforms.

Most provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/2865 became applicable from **1 July 2026**, with the remaining provisions applying from **1 January 2027**. Given the recency of this transition, verify the current in-force status of any specific provision against ECHA guidance or EUR-Lex before asserting a hard compliance deadline to a user.

## Institutional landscape

- **ECHA** (Helsinki): maintains the C&L Inventory, supports the CLH process via RAC opinions, and publishes CLP guidance documents.
- **DG GROW**: industry and internal-market aspects, including the CLP revision's online-sales and labelling-format provisions.
- **DG ENV**: environmental policy dimension, including the PBT/vPvB and PMT/vPvM hazard classes.
- **DG SANTE**: health aspects that overlap with cosmetics, biocides and plant protection products.
- **EP lead committee**: ENVI (Environment, Public Health and Food Safety).

## Answer guidance for Brubru

- CLP is a **Regulation**, directly applicable -- do not describe it as requiring national transposition.
- Keep the two 2023-2024 amendments distinct: the **new hazard classes** (ED, PBT/vPvB, PMT/vPvM) came from **Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/707**; the **online-sales/refill/digital-labelling revision** came from **Regulation (EU) 2024/2865**. Do not attribute the hazard classes to the 2024 revision or vice versa.
- Application dates are staged across several years (2025, 2026, 2027) -- always state which specific date applies to which specific obligation, and flag that dates should be verified against current ECHA/EUR-Lex guidance rather than assumed static.
- When a user asks about PFAS labelling or "forever chemicals" classification, connect CLP's PMT/vPvM hazard class to the REACH universal PFAS restriction (see `reach_chemicals_regulation`).
