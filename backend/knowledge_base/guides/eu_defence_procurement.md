## Overview

EU defence procurement has historically been a national prerogative, exempted from single market rules under Article 346 TFEU (essential security interests). This has produced a deeply fragmented European defence market: 27 separate procurement regimes, duplicative weapon systems, short production runs, and an inability to achieve economies of scale. The result is that Europe spends roughly one-third of what the United States spends on defence but gets far less capability per euro.

Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, the EU has moved with unprecedented speed to address this structural weakness. Three major instruments now form the backbone of EU joint defence procurement: EDIP (grants for joint procurement), EDF (R&D co-funding), and SAFE (loans for national defence investment). Together with the European Defence Industrial Strategy (EDIS), they represent the most ambitious attempt in EU history to create a genuine European defence market.

## Joint Procurement Instruments

### EDIP -- European Defence Industry Programme (EUR 1.5 billion, 2025-2027)

The European Defence Industry Programme is the EU's first dedicated instrument for incentivising joint procurement of defence equipment. Adopted as Regulation (EU) 2025/2643, it provides grants to consortia of at least three Member States that jointly procure defence equipment from EU-based manufacturers.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Legal basis | Article 173 TFEU (industrial policy) |
| Budget | EUR 1.5 billion (2025-2027) |
| Minimum consortium | 3 Member States |
| EU co-financing rate | Up to 20% of procurement value |
| Eligible items | All categories of defence equipment, including ammunition, vehicles, aircraft, naval vessels, cyber tools |
| European content | Minimum 65% EU content required |
| Management | Commission (DG DEFIS) with EDA advisory role |
| Predecessor | EDIRPA (EUR 310 million, 2023-2025) |

EDIP builds on the lessons of its predecessor, EDIRPA (European Defence Industry Reinforcement through Common Procurement Act), which demonstrated that EU co-financing can overcome national "buy national" reflexes. EDIRPA's first calls attracted 15 joint procurement projects involving 23 Member States.

Key EDIP innovations compared to EDIRPA:
- Higher budget (EUR 1.5B vs EUR 310M)
- Broader scope (not limited to Ukraine-related urgency)
- Stronger European preference (65% EU content requirement)
- Integration with EDIS industrial strategy objectives
- Linkage with SAFE loan conditionality

### EDF -- European Defence Fund (EUR 7.95 billion, 2021-2027)

The European Defence Fund is the EU's instrument for co-financing collaborative defence research and capability development. It operates upstream of procurement, funding the R&D phase that produces the technologies and prototypes later procured through EDIP or national programmes.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Legal basis | Article 173 TFEU + Article 182 TFEU (research) |
| Budget | EUR 7.95 billion (2021-2027 MFF) |
| Two pillars | Research (EUR 2.65B) + Development (EUR 5.3B) |
| Minimum consortium | 3 entities from 3 Member States |
| EU co-financing | Up to 100% for research, up to 80% for development |
| Eligible entities | Defence companies, research organisations, SMEs established in EU/EEA |
| Third-country participation | Restricted (no access to classified projects) |
| Management | Commission (DG DEFIS) |

By March 2026, EDF has launched five annual work programmes, funding projects across all major capability domains: land systems, air combat, naval defence, cyber, space, and emerging disruptive technologies. Notable projects include the Future Combat Air System (FCAS) components, the European Patrol Corvette, and next-generation ground combat systems.

### SAFE -- Security Action For Europe (EUR 150 billion in loans)

The SAFE instrument, adopted under Article 122 TFEU, enables the Commission to borrow on capital markets and on-lend to participating Member States for defence investment. Unlike EDIP and EDF (which are grants), SAFE provides loans at below-market rates with 30-45 year maturities.

SAFE's European preference clause incentivises Member States to procure EU-manufactured equipment when using SAFE loans, creating a powerful demand-side pull for the European defence industrial base.

For full details on SAFE, see the dedicated guide: **safe_rearm_europe**.

## European Defence Industrial Strategy (EDIS)

The European Defence Industrial Strategy, presented by the Commission in March 2024 (COM(2024) 150), provides the policy framework within which EDIP, EDF, and SAFE operate. Its core objectives:

1. **Collaborative procurement**: Increase the share of collaborative EU defence procurement from 18% to 35% by 2030, and 40% by 2035.

2. **Intra-EU procurement**: Increase the share of defence procurement from EU sources from 62% to 85% by 2030 (reducing dependency on US and other third-country suppliers).

3. **Industrial consolidation**: Encourage mergers, joint ventures, and cross-border supply chains to reduce the fragmentation of Europe's 2,500+ defence companies.

4. **Innovation**: Ensure EU DTIB access to emerging technologies (AI, quantum, hypersonics, autonomous systems, space).

5. **Readiness**: Maintain adequate stockpiles and production capacity for sustained high-intensity operations.

This fragmentation means shorter production runs, higher unit costs, incompatible systems, and duplicative R&D. The EU effectively pays more for less capability.

## The 35% Collaborative Procurement Target

EDIS sets a headline target of 35% collaborative procurement by 2030 (up from 18% in 2023). Achieving this target requires:

- **Demand aggregation**: Member States pooling requirements through EDA-coordinated capability planning
- **Financial incentives**: EDIP grants (up to 20% co-financing) making joint procurement cheaper than going alone
- **SAFE conditionality**: European preference clauses steering loan-funded procurement toward EU industry
- **Standardisation**: Common technical standards and interoperability requirements reducing barriers to joint programmes
- **Political will**: Overcoming "strategic autonomy" concerns at national level and the instinct to protect national champions

Progress as of March 2026 is mixed. EDIRPA demonstrated strong demand (23 Member States participating), and early EDIP calls have attracted significant interest. However, structural barriers remain: different operational requirements, varying procurement timelines, and the political economy of defence industrial employment.

## Industrial Base Challenges

The EU Defence Technological and Industrial Base (DTIB) faces several structural challenges:

### Fragmentation
Europe's defence industry remains organised along national lines. While consolidation has occurred at the top (Airbus, Leonardo, Thales, Rheinmetall, MBDA), the mid-tier and supply chain remain fragmented across 27 national markets.

### Production Capacity
The Ukraine war exposed critical shortages in ammunition, air defence systems, and armoured vehicles. EU production capacity for 155mm artillery shells has increased from 500,000/year (2022) to 2 million/year (2026), but remains below the estimated 3-4 million/year needed for sustained high-intensity operations.

### Third-Country Dependency
Despite the 85% intra-EU procurement target, significant dependencies remain on US platforms (F-35, Patriot, HIMARS), Israeli systems (Iron Dome components), and South Korean equipment (K2 tanks, K9 howitzers ordered by Poland). Replacing these dependencies requires decades of R&D investment.

### SME Integration
Defence supply chains are increasingly complex, with SMEs providing critical components (sensors, electronics, software, advanced materials). Ensuring SME access to EDIP and EDF funding, and integrating them into cross-border supply chains, is a policy priority.

### Workforce
Europe faces a skilled workforce shortage in defence manufacturing, particularly in ammunition production, shipbuilding, and aerospace engineering. Several Member States have launched retraining programmes, but the challenge remains acute.

## EPRS Analysis (March 2026)

The European Parliamentary Research Service briefing "EU joint defence procurement" (13 March 2026) provides an independent assessment of the EU's procurement framework. Key findings:

- EDIP represents a significant step forward but its EUR 1.5 billion budget is modest compared to the scale of the procurement challenge (EU Member States collectively spend over EUR 80 billion/year on defence equipment).

- The European preference clause in SAFE is politically sensitive, with some Member States arguing it restricts their sovereign procurement choices, particularly for urgent operational needs.

- The 35% collaborative procurement target lacks a robust monitoring and enforcement mechanism. EDIS relies on voluntary commitments and peer pressure rather than binding obligations.

- EDF has demonstrated its value in stimulating cross-border defence R&D collaboration, but the transition from R&D to procurement remains a "valley of death" that EDIP alone may not bridge.

- The institutional architecture (Commission, EDA, NATO) requires better coordination to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity between EU and NATO capability planning.

## Military Equipment Transport Framework (March 2026)

The SEDE subcommittee published a joint draft report (PE784.420, 17 March 2026) on a proposed framework for military equipment transport across the Union. Co-rapporteurs: Michal Szczerba and Roberts Zile. Amendment deadline: 9 April 2026.

This proposal addresses a critical capability gap: the EU currently lacks harmonised rules for moving military equipment across Member State borders, causing delays of days or weeks for cross-border military logistics. The framework aims to standardise permits, customs procedures, and infrastructure requirements for military mobility -- complementing NATO's military mobility initiative.

## AGILE -- Programme for Agile and Rapid Defence Innovation (EUR 115 million, 2027)

Adopted by the College of Commissioners on 25 March 2026. On 26 March 2026, DG DEFIS published EUR 115 million AGILE call details. The AGILE Programme targets "New Defence" players -- SMEs, start-ups, and tech innovators from both defence and civilian sectors -- to accelerate the deployment of disruptive technologies to European armed forces.

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Full name | Programme for agile and rapid defence innovation (AGILE) |
| Proposal | COM(2026) 135, 25 March 2026 |
| Procedure | 2026/0078(COD), ordinary legislative procedure |
| Legal basis | Article 173 TFEU (competitiveness of EU defence industry) |
| Budget | EUR 115 million (1 January 2027 -- 31 December 2027) |
| Projects | 20-30 projects funded |
| Funding rate | Up to 100% of eligible costs |
| Time-to-grant | 4 months (unprecedented speed) |
| Deployment timeline | Technologies reach defence forces in 1-3 years |
| Retroactive clause | Companies can claim expenses from 3 months before call closing |
| Commissioner | Andrius Kubilius (Defence and Space) + Henna Virkkunen (EVP, Tech Sovereignty) |
| Lead DG | DG DEFIS |
| Next MFF | Expected to continue with larger resources (2028-2035) |

### Work Programme
Two main types of activities:
1. **Mission-driven development**: Disruptive defence products and technologies aligned with Member States' most pressing needs
2. **Market delivery**: Helping defence solutions reach actual markets and armed forces

### Key Innovation Areas
- Artificial Intelligence (AI)
- Quantum technologies
- Robotics
- Cyber defence
- Space technologies
- Autonomous systems
- Drones and counter-drone systems

### Context
Russia's war against Ukraine demonstrated that modern warfare depends on short innovation cycles -- developing, testing, and deploying new technologies in weeks or months. Commissioner Kubilius: "Since we are able to use only EUR 115 million for 2027, we can expect that only 20-30 European SMEs will be able to enjoy the benefits. If we shall manage to assist those companies to become a new European 'Helsing SE' or 'Frankenburg Technologies', that would be a great success."

### Relationship to Other Instruments
- **EDF** (EUR 7.95B): Upstream R&D. AGILE bridges the gap between EDF research outputs and rapid deployment.
- **EDIP** (EUR 1.5B): Joint procurement of mature systems. AGILE targets earlier-stage disruptive innovation.
- **SAFE** (EUR 150B loans): National defence investment. AGILE complements by funding the innovation that SAFE-procured systems will incorporate.

## EDIP Work Programme Adopted (31 March 2026)

On 31 March 2026, the Commission adopted the first **EDIP work programme** worth EUR 1.5 billion, with first calls for proposals published on the EU Funding & Tenders portal. The programme covers:

- **Production of key defence components**: Scaling EU defence manufacturing capacity
- **Ukraine cooperation**: Support to rebuild Ukraine's defence industry through joint procurement and technology sharing
- **Common procurement**: Co-financing joint procurement of defence equipment by consortia of 3+ Member States
- **Defence start-ups**: Dedicated funding stream for innovative defence SMEs and start-ups

This work programme operationalises the EDIP Regulation (EU) 2025/2643 adopted in 2025. Combined with the AGILE programme (EUR 115M for rapid innovation, calls opened 26 March 2026), the EU has now deployed EUR 1.615 billion in defence industrial grants for 2025-2027.
