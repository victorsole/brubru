## QUICK ANSWERS

**What does this Regulation do?** Imposes definitive countervailing duties on imports of woven and/or stitched glass fibre fabrics (GFF) from China and Egypt. It also amends Regulation (EU) 2020/492 (the parallel anti-dumping regulation on the same product) and discontinues import registration under Regulation (EU) 2020/44.

**Who pays what?**

| Country / Company group | CVD rate | TARIC additional code |
|---|---|---|
| China — CNBM Group (Jushi, Hengshi, Taishan) | **30.7 %** | C531 |
| China — PGTEX China / Chongqing Tenways | **17.0 %** | C532 |
| China — other co-operating in both AS and AD | **24.8 %** | See Annex I |
| China — other co-operating in AD only | **30.7 %** | See Annex II |
| China — all other companies | **30.7 %** | C999 |
| Egypt — Jushi Egypt / Hengshi Egypt | **10.9 %** | C533 |
| Egypt — all other companies | **10.9 %** | C999 |

**Combined CVD + AD rates** (recital 1122 of 2020/776 read with amended Reg 2020/492):

- CNBM Group (China): 30.7 % + 69.0 % = **99.7 %**
- Yuntianhua Group (China): 17.0 % + 37.6 % = **54.6 %**
- Egypt CNBM: 10.9 % + 20.0 % = **30.9 %**

**Did provisional duties get collected?** **No.** The Commission decided on 24 January 2020 not to impose provisional measures. Article 3 of 2020/776 discontinues the registration set up by Reg 2020/44 and confirms that no duty is collected retroactively for registered imports.

**Why are Egyptian exports also countervailed?** This is the landmark element of the case. The Commission attributed Chinese government subsidies to production located in the China-Egypt Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone (SETC-Zone) by invoking Article 11 of the International Law Commission's Articles on State Responsibility ("acknowledgement and adoption"). The legal hook is the Cooperation Agreement signed on 21 January 2016 between MOFCOM (China) and the General Authority for the Suez Canal Economic Zone (Egypt). Articles 4 and 5 of that Agreement commit China to provide "relevant policy support" and to "encourage relevant financial institutions to provide financial facility" to companies operating in the zone.

**Is this still in force?** Yes, with the normal anti-subsidy framework. The Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation sets a 5-year duration for definitive measures (subject to expiry review). The five-year clock runs from 16 June 2020.

## OVERVIEW

This is the first EU trade-defence case to use the doctrine of cross-border subsidy attribution — countervailing in Country A subsidies granted by Country B to producers operating in Country A. It established the SETC-Zone precedent that has since shaped EU practice on third-country manufacturing financed by Chinese state-owned capital.

The investigation followed a 1 April 2019 complaint by Tech-Fab Europe, the European association of GFF producers, representing more than 25 % of Union production. The Commission initiated on 16 May 2019 (notice OJ C 167/11). Verification visits were partly disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic; the Commission accepted that constraint and proceeded on the basis of remote verification where on-site visits were not possible (recital 116 of 2020/776).

The Commission found the Union GFF industry suffered material injury over 2015 to 2018: market share fell from 73.7 % to 63.4 % (-10.3 percentage points), unit ex-works prices fell 7 % while unit costs of production rose 3 %, profitability swung from +2.5 % to -3.4 %, return on investment fell from +15 % to -17 %, and cash flow collapsed by 86 % (Table 11 of recital 1016-1017). Egyptian import volumes grew from 108 tonnes in 2015 to 15,334 tonnes during the IP, directly coinciding with the commissioning of SETC-Zone capacity (Table 3 of 2020/776).

## SUBSTANTIVE ELEMENTS

### Product scope (Article 1(1))

Fabrics of woven and/or stitched continuous filament glass fibre rovings and/or yarns, with or without other elements. The product is used primarily as a structural reinforcement in composite materials, notably in wind turbine blades and nacelles.

CN codes: ex 7019 39 00, ex 7019 40 00, ex 7019 59 00, ex 7019 90 00.
TARIC suffixes: 7019390080, 7019400080, 7019590080, 7019900080.

Three main product types within scope: woven rovings (WR), non-crimp fabrics (NCF), complex materials (CM).

Excluded: products which are impregnated or pre-impregnated (pre-preg); open-mesh fabrics with cells of more than 1.8 mm in both length and width weighing more than 35 g/m^2.

### Sampling

- **Union industry:** 16 producers in total. Four sampled (Owens Corning, Chomarat, Saertex, Ahlstrom), representing more than 40 % of Union GFF production (recital 83).
- **PRC:** eight Chinese groups came forward; two sampled, representing more than 79 % of reported Chinese exports during the IP (recital 88): CNBM Group (CNBM, China Jushi, Jushi Group, Hengshi Group, Taishan Fiberglass, Sinoma Science and Technology) and Yuntianhua Group (Yunnan Yuntianhua, CPIC, PGTEX China, CTM).
- **Egypt:** both known producers included without formal sampling (Jushi Egypt and Hengshi Egypt, both CNBM Group subsidiaries) — together 100 % of Egyptian GFF exports during the IP (recital 107).

### Subsidisation — PRC findings

The Commission countervailed several categories:

- **Preferential lending.** 36 financial institutions provided financing to the sampled groups; 29 were state-owned (recital 222). Only China Export-Import Bank submitted to verification (recital 228); the Commission applied facts available under Article 28 of the Basic Regulation for the rest. Both groups were assigned a BB (non-investment grade) credit rating (recitals 306, 334). The market benchmark was constructed from US BB-rated corporate bond yields adjusted by the US-China sovereign spread. Resulting benefits: CNBM 7.39 % ad valorem, Yuntianhua 2.53 % (recital 344).
- **Grants and subsidies** from central and local governments.
- **Provision of inputs at less than adequate remuneration** (LTAR): electricity, natural gas, energy, land-use rights.
- **Income tax preferences** under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, including the 15 % reduced rate for high-technology enterprises.
- **Import duty exemptions** on capital equipment.
- **Other indirect tax exemptions and reductions.**

Aggregate PRC subsidy rates (recital 645 read with Article 1(2)): CNBM Group 30.73 %, Yuntianhua Group 17.02 %, other cooperating in both AS and AD 24.85 %, other cooperating in AD only 30.73 %, all other 30.73 %.

### Subsidisation — Egypt findings (the SETC-Zone doctrine)

The SETC-Zone (Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone) is a special economic zone near Port Said, 7.34 km^2 in area (recital 647), established under Egyptian Law 83/2002 and operated by China-Africa TEDA Investment Co. (90 % control) in joint venture with Egyptian state participation (recital 654).

The legal anchor for attribution is the Cooperation Agreement signed on 21 January 2016 between MOFCOM (PRC) and the General Authority for the Suez Canal Economic Zone (recital 656):

- Article 4 commits China to provide "relevant policy support and facilitation" to the zone (recital 676).
- Article 5 commits China to "support" the zone by "encouraging relevant financial institutions to provide financial facility" to companies operating there (recital 676).

The Commission invoked Article 11 of the ILC Articles on State Responsibility (acknowledgement and adoption) to find that both the GOC and the GOE adopted as their own the subsidy arrangements operating inside the zone (recital 676 and surrounding analysis).

Findings against the GOC (attributed to Egypt-located production):
- Preferential financing channelled through Chinese state-owned banks and policy banks.
- Other support under the Cooperation Agreement.

Findings against the GOE:
- Land within the SETC-Zone at less than adequate remuneration (initial allocation at less than USD 1 per square metre, recital 818), under Article 38(bis A) of Law 83/2002 as amended in 2015.
- Tax exemptions and reduced charges applicable to zone-based manufacturers.

Combined Egyptian subsidy rate: 10.9 % (recital 934).

### Injury, causation, Union interest

- Union industry: 16 producers, 1,197 employees, production almost 115,000 tonnes during the IP (recital 936).
- Combined import market share: 22.6 % (2015) to 31.4 % (IP), +8.8 percentage points (Table 3, recital 949). Of which PRC 22.3 %, Egypt 9.1 % during the IP.
- Price undercutting: PRC weighted average 32.3 % (range 15.4 % to 55.8 %); Egypt weighted average 31.5 % (range 30.5 % to 54.9 %) (recital 966).
- Union industry market share: 73.7 % to 63.4 % (-10.3 percentage points) (Table 6, recital 994).
- Union ex-works price: EUR 2.20/kg to EUR 2.05/kg (-7 %); unit cost: EUR 2.18/kg to EUR 2.25/kg (+3 %) (Table 8, recital 1001).
- Profitability: +2.5 % to -3.4 %; return on investment: +15 % to -17 %; cash flow EUR 8.93 million to EUR 1.26 million (-86 %) (Table 11, recitals 1016-1017).
- Cumulative assessment under Article 8(3) of the Basic Regulation: conditions met (recital 966 area).
- Non-attribution analysis dismissed: imports from India (1.8 % of consumption), Russia (1.4 %), demand conditions (consumption grew +9.8 %), raw material costs, wind-turbine sourcing arguments. GFF represents only 0.1 % to 2 % of total wind park costs (recital 1087).
- Union interest: passes (recital 1115). Kitting industry employs more than 2,000 persons (recital 1106) but duty pass-through impact on wind park economics found very limited.

### No lesser duty rule

Regulation (EU) 2018/825 amended the Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation to remove the obligation to apply the lesser duty rule in anti-subsidy proceedings. Accordingly, duties are imposed at the full subsidy rate, not capped by the injury elimination level (recital 1115).

### No undertakings accepted

No undertaking offers were accepted in this case. The operative text contains no undertaking annex.

## NOTABLE NOVELTY: CROSS-BORDER SUBSIDY ATTRIBUTION

This regulation is a landmark in EU trade-defence law. It is the first time the Commission countervailed subsidies granted by Country A (PRC) to production located in Country B (Egypt), and it did so by treating both the granting government (GOC) and the host government (GOE) as having acknowledged and adopted the subsidy arrangements under Article 11 of the ILC Articles on State Responsibility. The doctrine has since informed subsequent EU investigations involving Belt-and-Road financing of overseas production capacity.
