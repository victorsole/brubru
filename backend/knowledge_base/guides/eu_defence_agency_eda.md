# European Defence Agency (EDA): Mandate, Capability Development, PESCO/CARD/EDF Coordination

## QUICK FACTS
- Topic: European Defence Agency (EDA) -- the EU's intergovernmental hub for defence capability coordination
- Full name / seat: European Defence Agency, Brussels
- Established: 12 July 2004 (Council Joint Action 2004/551/CFSP); current legal basis is the recast Council Decision (CFSP) 2015/1835
- Legal status: An agency of the Council of the European Union under the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) -- NOT a Commission body and not an EU institution in the Article 13 TEU sense. Reports to the Council, guided by the Head of the Agency and the Steering Board
- Treaty basis for its tasks: Article 42(3) TEU and Article 45 TEU (identifying capability objectives, promoting harmonisation of operational needs, proposing multilateral projects, supporting defence R&T, strengthening the European defence technological and industrial base)
- Head of the Agency: High Representative/Vice-President Kaja Kallas (ex officio role, since December 2024)
- Chief Executive: André Denk (German Major General), appointed by Member States 5 May 2025, took office 16 May 2025 -- first serving military officer to lead EDA; previously EDA Deputy Chief Executive from February 2023, where he led joint procurement of 155mm ammunition for Ukraine/stock replenishment
- Governance: Steering Board, composed of Member States' defence ministers (or national armaments/capability directors in specialised formations), chaired by the Head of the Agency
- Participating Member States: all 27 EU Member States on a voluntary, project-by-project basis (Denmark ended its CSDP opt-out after the June 2022 referendum and rejoined EU defence cooperation, including PESCO, in 2023)
- Third-country partners with Administrative Arrangements (participate in projects, no voting rights): Norway (2006), Switzerland (2012), Serbia (2013), Ukraine (2015); a framework arrangement with the US Department of Defense was signed April 2023
- Four core functions (per Council Decision (CFSP) 2015/1835 and EDA's own mandate pages): (1) capability development, (2) defence research and technology (R&T), (3) armaments cooperation and industry/SME engagement, (4) support to CSDP operations and the ministerial-level defence agenda
- Capability Development Plan (CDP): EDA's flagship output, periodically revised with Member States and EU military bodies; sets EU Capability Development Priorities used to steer national planning, PESCO projects and EDF calls
- Coordinated Annual Review on Defence (CARD): launched by Member States May 2017; EDA is CARD Secretariat jointly with the EU Military Staff (EUMS); synchronises national defence planning cycles and surfaces collaborative opportunities
- Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO): established December 2017 (Council Decision (CFSP) 2017/2315), Article 46 TEU + Protocol No 10; EDA is PESCO Secretariat jointly with the European External Action Service (EEAS)
- European Defence Fund (EDF): operational since January 2021, EUR 7.953 billion budgeted 2021-2027; managed by the Commission (DG DEFIS), with EDA contributing technical/capability expertise, not fund management
- EDA does NOT manage EDIP (European Defence Industry Programme) or SAFE (Security Action for Europe) -- those are Commission (DG DEFIS) instruments; EDA's role there is capability-planning input and coordination, not disbursement. See `safe_rearm_europe.md` and `eu_defence_procurement.md` for those instruments
- Verify live: Chief Executive and Steering Board composition can change; check `eda.europa.eu/who-we-are` before citing names in time-sensitive outputs

EDA is the standing technical and planning layer underneath the EU's post-2022 defence integration push. Where the Commission's DG DEFIS controls the money (EDF, EDIP, SAFE) and the EEAS/Council control the political and operational CSDP dimension, EDA is the body that tells Member States what capability gaps exist, secretariats the two main coordination mechanisms (PESCO, CARD), and translates collective capability priorities into projects Member States can actually join. It predates the 2022 defence-readiness surge by 18 years but has become significantly more central to it.

## Governance and Structure

- **Head of the Agency**: an ex officio role held by the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy/Vice-President of the Commission (currently Kaja Kallas). Sets the Agency's political direction and chairs the Steering Board when it meets at ministerial level.
- **Chief Executive**: runs the Agency day to day, proposed by the Head of the Agency and appointed by the Steering Board (unanimity of participating Member States). André Denk (Germany) has held the post since May 2025.
- **Steering Board**: the Agency's principal decision-making body, bringing together defence ministers (or capability/armaments/R&T directors in specialised configurations) of participating Member States. Approves the Agency's budget, work programme and admission of new projects.
- **Four directorates/functional pillars**: Capability, Armament & Technology (CAT); Industry, Synergies & Enablers (ISE); Resources & Coordination; and Corporate Support -- the exact directorate names have been reorganised over the Agency's history, so verify current org chart on `eda.europa.eu` before citing internal structure in detail.
- EDA decisions on individual projects generally require the participating Member States' consent; the Agency itself does not have autonomous procurement or spending power over national defence budgets. Its influence is coordinating and advisory, backed by EU co-funding instruments run elsewhere (EDF, EDIP).

## Core Functions

1. **Capability development** -- Identifying Member States' military capability shortfalls (air, land, maritime, cyber, space) and turning them into the Capability Development Plan and EU Capability Development Priorities, which in turn feed PESCO project selection and EDF/EDIP call topics.
2. **Research and technology (R&T)** -- Coordinating collaborative defence research through the Overarching Strategic Research Agenda, ahead of and alongside EDF-funded research.
3. **Armaments cooperation and industry engagement** -- Supporting collaborative procurement (for example joint ammunition procurement during the Ukraine war), standardisation, certification, military airworthiness, test and evaluation, and facilitating SME access to defence markets and EU funding tools.
4. **CSDP and ministerial agenda support** -- Preparing capability-related input for Council defence ministerial meetings and supporting CSDP missions and operations with capability advice.

## Coordination Role: PESCO, CARD, CDP, EDF

| Mechanism | What it is | EDA's role |
|---|---|---|
| Capability Development Plan (CDP) | The EU's periodically revised full capability picture, translating military needs into shared priorities | EDA produces and revises the CDP with Member States and EU military bodies |
| Coordinated Annual Review on Defence (CARD) | Biennial review synchronising national defence planning cycles, launched May 2017 | Joint CARD Secretariat with the EU Military Staff (EUMS) |
| Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) | Treaty-based framework (Article 46 TEU) for binding commitments and joint capability projects, launched December 2017 | Joint PESCO Secretariat with the EEAS |
| European Defence Fund (EDF) | EU budget instrument co-financing collaborative defence research and capability development, EUR 7.953bn 2021-2027 | Commission (DG DEFIS) manages; EDA provides technical/capability input, not fund management |
| European Defence Industry Programme (EDIP) / SAFE | Commission instruments from the 2025-2026 defence-readiness surge (ReArm Europe) | Not managed by EDA; capability-planning input only -- see `safe_rearm_europe.md` |

## EDA vs Other Defence-Relevant Bodies

| Body | Institutional home | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| EDA | Council (CFSP agency) | Capability planning, PESCO/CARD secretariat, armaments cooperation |
| DG DEFIS (Commission) | European Commission | Manages EDF, EDIP, SAFE funding instruments; defence industrial policy |
| EEAS / EU Military Staff (EUMS) | Council/HR-VP structure | Strategic and operational planning, CSDP missions, joint PESCO/CARD secretariat with EDA |
| EU Military Committee (EUMC) | Council | Highest military body in the Council framework, advises PSC and HR/VP |
| NATO | Separate international organisation | Collective defence (Article 5), capability targets that EDA/CDP work aims to complement, not duplicate |

EDA is not one of the Commission's executive agencies (compare `eu_commission_executive_agencies.md`, which covers bodies like EACEA or HaDEA that implement Commission-managed spending programmes under delegation). EDA sits on the Council/CFSP side of the institutional map and has no equivalent delegation relationship with the Commission.
