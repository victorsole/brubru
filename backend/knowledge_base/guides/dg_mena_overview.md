# DG MENA: Middle East, North Africa and Gulf Directorate-General

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full official name:** Directorate-General for the Middle East, North Africa and the Gulf (DG MENA)
- **Operational since:** 1 February 2025, under the Von der Leyen II Commission (took office 1 December 2024)
- **Commissioner:** Dubravka Suica, Commissioner for the Mediterranean
- **Director-General:** Michael Karnitschnig (Acting) as at June 2026 per the official Commission about page; Stefano Sannino (former EEAS Secretary-General) was named at the DG's launch in February 2025 -- verify current postholder via EU Who-is-Who at `op.europa.eu/en/web/who-is-who`
- **Predecessor DG:** DG NEAR (Neighbourhood and Enlargement Negotiations), which was split on 1 February 2025; the enlargement and eastern neighbourhood work became DG ENEST (see `dg_enest_overview`)
- **Geographic scope:** Three sub-regions -- (1) North Africa: Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Mauritania, Morocco, Tunisia; (2) Middle East: Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, Syria; (3) Gulf: Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates, Yemen
- **Flagship initiative:** Pact for the Mediterranean -- presented 16 October 2025, launched 28 November 2025 in Barcelona (30th anniversary of the Barcelona Declaration), first Action Plan published 17 April 2026; second Action Plan expected autumn 2026
- **Primary funding instrument:** NDICI-Global Europe (Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument), specifically the Southern Neighbourhood geographic programming window, plus the EFSD+ blended-finance guarantee and Global Gateway
- **Staff:** More than 500, split between Brussels headquarters (office code MERODE) and EU Delegations across the region
- **Main portal:** `https://north-africa-middle-east-gulf.ec.europa.eu/index_en`
- **Commission about page:** `https://commission.europa.eu/about/departments-and-executive-agencies/middle-east-north-africa-and-gulf_en`
- **Commission switchboard:** +32 2 299 11 11
- **Relationship to EEAS:** DG MENA coordinates Commission-side policy tools and funding; the European External Action Service (EEAS) and High Representative handle the diplomatic, CFSP, and EU Delegation chain; the two work in tandem (see `eeas_institution_hrvp_kallas`)
- **Relationship to DG INTPA:** DG INTPA (International Partnerships) handles sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America; DG MENA holds the lead for North Africa and the broader Southern Neighbourhood

## How DG MENA Fits in the Commission's External-Action Architecture

The European Commission manages external relations through a combination of dedicated policy DGs and the Secretariat-General. Until 1 February 2025, the southern neighbourhood, eastern neighbourhood, and enlargement pipeline were all handled by a single body -- DG NEAR. President von der Leyen's second mandate separated these into two specialised DGs:

- **DG MENA** covers the southern arc from Morocco to Yemen. It is the Commission's primary coordination hub for all policy instruments -- trade, investment, energy, migration, digital cooperation, and humanitarian response -- directed at these three sub-regions.
- **DG ENEST** (Enlargement and Eastern Neighbourhood) covers the EU accession pipeline (Western Balkans, Türkiye, Ukraine, Moldova, Georgia) and eastern partners (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus).

The split reflects a strategic shift: the Mediterranean, Gulf, and broader southern arc present a distinct set of priorities (energy security, migration management, post-conflict stabilisation, Gulf investment partnerships) that warrant dedicated Commission-level capacity, separate from the enlargement and eastern-partnership logic.

DG MENA's legal mandate sits under the Commission's general powers in external action as set out in Title V of the Treaty on European Union (TEU). Its work programmes reference the NDICI-Global Europe Regulation (EU 2021/947) as the primary operational funding base.

For an overview of all Commission DGs and services, see `eu_commission_services_overview` and `commission_guide`.

## Geographic Scope

DG MENA operates across three distinct sub-regions:

**North Africa (Maghreb + Egypt)**
Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Mauritania, Morocco, Tunisia. These countries are all members of the Arab Maghreb Union (AMU) or linked through the Union for the Mediterranean (UfM). Relations are governed largely through Association Agreements (Euro-Mediterranean Agreements) and Action Plans.

**Middle East (Mashriq + Levant)**
Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, Syria. The region presents the EU's most acute humanitarian and political challenges: the ongoing Lebanon crisis, the situation in Gaza, the Syrian reconstruction file, and Jordanian stability. DG MENA coordinates with the EU Special Representative for the Middle East Peace Process and ECHO (humanitarian aid).

**Gulf States**
Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates, Yemen. EU-Gulf relations are managed primarily through the EU-Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) framework. The Gulf states represent major energy suppliers, sovereign wealth fund investors, and increasingly significant partners in clean-energy transition. Yemen is covered for humanitarian and diplomatic coordination purposes.

## Pact for the Mediterranean

The Pact for the Mediterranean is the flagship political framework of the Von der Leyen II mandate for the southern neighbourhood. Key milestones:

- **16 October 2025:** Commission and High Representative formally presented the Pact
- **28 November 2025:** Official launch in Barcelona, co-hosted by Spain and the EU, coinciding with the 30th anniversary of the 1995 Barcelona Declaration
- **17 April 2026:** First Action Plan published, comprising 21 co-designed actions across three pillars: People; Stronger, More Sustainable and Integrated Economies; Security, Preparedness and Migration Management
- **Autumn 2026:** Second Action Plan expected

The Pact does not replace existing bilateral Association Agreements or the Union for the Mediterranean (UfM) track. It functions as an overarching political commitment to co-create specific, measurable actions with partner governments, civil society, youth, and the private sector.

Key actions in the first Action Plan include the Trans-Mediterranean Renewable Energy and Clean-Tech Cooperation Initiative (T-MED), a Mediterranean University Initiative, a Youth Parliamentary Assembly, a European Firefighting Hub in Cyprus, and border-management cooperation (Secure Borders). The Action Plan references mobilising "billions in European investment" through NDICI-Global Europe, Global Gateway, and Member State bilateral channels, but does not publish a consolidated envelope figure.

## Funding Instruments

DG MENA does not hold a separate budget line. It programmes and coordinates through existing EU external-financing instruments:

**NDICI-Global Europe (Regulation EU 2021/947)**
The main instrument for 2021-2027 with a total envelope of EUR 79.5 billion. The Southern Neighbourhood geographic programme (within NDICI-Global Europe) is the principal vehicle for DG MENA bilateral and regional programming. It covers economic development, governance, migration, energy, and digital connectivity.

**EFSD+ (European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus)**
A blended-finance guarantee facility embedded within NDICI-Global Europe. EFSD+ allows the Commission to blend grants with guarantees from the European Investment Bank (EIB) and European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD) to de-risk private investment in the region.

**Global Gateway**
The EU's alternative to Belt and Road for infrastructure investment in third countries. DG MENA coordinates Global Gateway pipelines for the MENA and Gulf regions, including submarine cable projects, renewable energy interconnections (T-MED), and transport corridors.

**EU Regional Trust Fund in Response to the Syrian Crisis and EU Emergency Trust Fund for Africa**
Older multi-donor instruments that remain active for Syria-related displacement and Sahel/North Africa migration management, respectively.

**TAIEX, Twinning, SIGMA**
Technical-assistance instruments for regulatory approximation and administrative capacity, available to Southern Neighbourhood partners under the neighbourhood policy framework.

## Coordination with Institutional Partners

**European External Action Service (EEAS)**
The EEAS, led by High Representative / Vice-President Kaja Kallas, manages EU diplomatic representations (Delegations) in the region and steers the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP). DG MENA staff may be deployed to or seconded at EU Delegations in MENA countries, but the administrative chain of Delegations runs through the EEAS. Policy coherence between the two is maintained through Joint Programming documents and regular inter-service coordination. See `eeas_institution_hrvp_kallas`.

**DG ENEST**
Covers enlargement candidates and eastern partners. For countries geographically at the border of the two mandates (e.g. Türkiye, which has neighbourhood links to both eastern and southern arcs), the two DGs coordinate on cross-cutting files such as energy and migration. See `dg_enest_overview` (planned).

**DG INTPA**
DG INTPA (International Partnerships) holds the Commission's lead on sub-Saharan Africa, Asia-Pacific, the Americas, and global thematic programmes (democracy, gender, civil society). For cross-regional issues such as the Sahel, MENA migration routes, and African Union coordination, DG MENA and DG INTPA run joint programming cycles. See `dg_intpa_overview` (planned).

**DG ECHO (Humanitarian Aid)**
DG ECHO coordinates EU humanitarian response in conflict zones across the DG MENA geographic perimeter (Gaza, Lebanon, Syria, Yemen, Libya). DG MENA manages the development-diplomacy side; ECHO retains its independent humanitarian mandate.

**DG TRADE**
Trade aspects of EU-Gulf Cooperation Council negotiations and bilateral Association Agreements remain primarily under DG TRADE competence. DG MENA provides regional context and political steering.

Commissioner Suica's meetings with interest representatives are disclosed on the Commission Transparency Register portal:
`https://ec.europa.eu/transparencyregister/public/consultation/displaylobbyist.do`

Search under "Dubravka Suica" or "DG MENA" to see disclosed meetings. All meetings between Commissioners (and their cabinets) and registered interest representatives are published within two weeks of the meeting.
