## Overview

EU occupational health and safety law protects over 170 million workers across the EU. The Framework Directive 89/391/EEC establishes general principles: employer duty to ensure worker safety, risk assessment obligation, preventive measures, worker information and training, health surveillance. Specific "daughter directives" cover particular risks: chemical agents, carcinogens, biological agents, display screen equipment, manual handling, noise, vibration, asbestos, and more.

## Carcinogens and Mutagens Directive (CMD)

The CMD is the main instrument protecting workers from occupational cancer, which causes an estimated 100,000 deaths per year in the EU. It has been revised six times since 2004:

| Revision | Year | Key Changes |
|----------|------|-------------|
| CMD1 | 2017/2398 | Added 11 substances (chromium VI, hardwood dust, etc.) |
| CMD2 | 2019/130 | Added 5 substances (formaldehyde, cadmium, etc.) |
| CMD3 | 2019/983 | Added 5 substances (nickel, acrylonitrile, benzene) |
| CMD4 | 2022/431 | Extended scope to reprotoxic substances; added lead, diisocyanates |
| CMD5-6 | Ongoing | Further substance additions, lowering existing OELs |

### Current EMPL Vote (April 2026)
The EMPL committee is voting on an update to EU rules protecting workers from hazardous chemical exposure. Key elements:
- New or revised binding occupational exposure limits (OELs) for specific substances
- Enhanced provisions for lead exposure (construction, battery manufacturing, recycling)
- Diisocyanates: widely used in polyurethane production, construction foams, automotive coatings
- Strengthened risk assessment and substitution obligations

## EU Strategic Framework on OSH 2021-2027

Three objectives:
1. **Anticipating change**: addressing risks from digitalisation, green transition, demographic change
2. **Improving prevention**: "Vision Zero" approach to work-related deaths
3. **Increasing preparedness**: lessons from COVID-19 pandemic response

Key actions:
- Review of workplace directive (89/654/EEC) and display screen equipment directive (90/270/EEC)
- EU-OSHA campaigns on digitalisation and psychosocial risks
- Asbestos exposure: Directive (EU) 2023/2668 lowering OEL for asbestos (CELEX 32023L2668)

## Key Statistics
- 2.9 million non-fatal work accidents per year in EU
- ~3,400 fatal work accidents per year
- ~100,000 occupational cancer deaths per year
- Economic cost: 3.3% of EU GDP (EUR 476 billion annually)
- Sectors most affected: construction, manufacturing, agriculture, mining, healthcare

## Psychosocial risks: the 2026 legislative-initiative request

On Wednesday **2 September 2026** the European Parliament's Committee on Employment and Social Affairs
(EMPL) adopted a **legislative-initiative request under Article 225 TFEU**, by **41 votes to 12 with 4
abstentions**, calling on the Commission to bring forward a law on work-related psychosocial risks.
Rapporteur: **Estelle Ceulemans (S&D, Belgium)**.

**Status, stated plainly.** This is a request for a proposal. There is no directive, no number and no
Commission text. Parliament is expected to vote on it at the **October I 2026 plenary**, after which the
Commission has **three months** either to say what steps it will take or to give reasons for refusing.
The report also asks that any proposal be preceded by an impact assessment covering existing legislation.

**What MEPs asked for:**

- **Binding obligations on employers** to protect mental and physical health and ensure safe and dignified
  working conditions.
- **Recognition that work-related psychosocial risks give rise to occupational diseases**, by both the
  Commission and Member States.
- **Violence and harassment policies**: employers to adopt and implement policies defining and prohibiting
  violence, harassment, bullying and discriminatory behaviour that undermines dignity or physical or
  mental health, with early-detection mechanisms and risk-based prevention protocols for third-party
  violence, and timely support for workers exposed.
- **Regular psychosocial risk assessments** evaluating factors such as excessive workload and high work
  intensity, with action plans setting out concrete measures.
- **A prior assessment before change**: significant restructuring or reorganisation — including
  teleworking arrangements and the introduction of automated decision-making or automated monitoring
  systems — should be subject to a psychosocial risk assessment beforehand, carried out with workers and
  their representatives.
- **Limits on algorithmic management**: introducing algorithmic systems must not impose excessive burden
  or monitoring on workers, and **human oversight must always be ensured**. The report reiterates the
  **right to disconnect**.
- **Return to work**: workers absent because of work-related psychosocial risks should have a right to a
  supported and sustainable return, with individual return-to-work plans adjusting working time, work
  organisation, tasks or workload.
- **Reversed burden of proof**: where a worker establishes injury or damage to health from exposure to
  work-related psychosocial risk factors, it is **presumed to be work-related unless the employer
  demonstrates** the injury is not linked to working conditions.

**Figures cited in support:** more than **840,000 deaths a year** worldwide from health conditions linked
to psychosocial risks (International Labour Organisation), and **EUR 100 billion a year** in the EU for
work-related depression.
