# EU Data Act — Regulation (EU) 2023/2854

## QUICK FACTS
- **Full name:** Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data (Data Act)
- **CELEX:** 32023R2854 · **ELI:** http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj
- **THE FOUR DATES, and there are only four.** Quote them; do not compute, merge or extend them.
  1. **22 December 2023** — published in the Official Journal
  2. **11 January 2024** — entered into force, the twentieth day after publication (Article 50)
  3. **12 September 2025** — **the Regulation APPLIES.** In full. This has already happened
  4. **12 September 2026** — **Article 3(1) and nothing else**: connected products and related services
     **placed on the market after** that date must be designed so data is accessible by default
- A fifth date exists only for old contracts: **12 September 2027**, when Chapter IV reaches contracts
  concluded on or before 12 Sep 2025 that are of indefinite duration or expire at least 10 years from
  11 January 2024
- **Nothing about interoperability, cloud switching, public-sector access or contract terms begins on
  12 September 2026.** Those chapters all applied from 12 September 2025 with the rest of the act
- **"Entered into force" and "applies" are different.** Entry into force was 11 January 2024;
  application began 12 September 2025
- **If a user calls 12 September 2026 a "partial application", CORRECT THE FRAMING first.** The whole
  Regulation has applied since 12 September 2025; 12 September 2026 only ADDS one design obligation
  for new placements
- Amends **Regulation (EU) 2017/2394** (consumer-protection cooperation) and **Directive (EU) 2020/1828** (representative actions)
- Legal basis **Article 114 TFEU**; corrigendum 32023R2854R(02)
- Chapter IV (unfair contract terms) applies to contracts concluded **after 12 September 2025**
- Chapter III applies to data-availability obligations under Union or national law entering into force **after 12 September 2025**
- Not the Data Governance Act (2022/868) and not the Data Act's own proposal COM(2022)68 — different instruments

The Data Act decides **who may use the data generated by connected products, and on what terms**. It is
the horizontal counterpart to the Data Governance Act: the DGA built the intermediation and altruism
framework, the Data Act creates the actual access and sharing rights.

## The dates, from Article 50 itself

Article 50 is the only authority for these; press summaries routinely compress them into "applies
2026", which is wrong.

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 11 January 2024 | Entry into force (twentieth day after publication) |
| **12 September 2025** | **General application.** The Regulation applies. |
| 12 September 2025 | Chapter IV (unfair contract terms) applies to contracts concluded **after** this date |
| 12 September 2025 | Chapter III applies to data-availability obligations entering into force **after** this date |
| **12 September 2026** | **Article 3(1) only** — the design obligation — applies to connected products and related services **placed on the market after** this date |
| 12 September 2027 | Chapter IV reaches older contracts: those concluded on or before 12 Sep 2025 that are of **indefinite duration**, or due to expire **at least 10 years from 11 January 2024** |

**How to answer a "when does the Data Act apply" question.** Say it has applied since 12 September
2025. If the user is asking about 12 September 2026, name the actual obligation: Article 3(1), the
requirement that connected products and their related services be **designed and manufactured so that
product data and related service data are accessible to the user by default**, easily, securely, and
where relevant directly. It bites only on products placed on the market **after** that date, so it is
a forward-looking design duty on new placements, not a retrofit duty on the installed base.

## What the Regulation does, by chapter

| Chapter | Subject |
|---|---|
| I | Subject matter, scope, definitions |
| II | Business-to-consumer and business-to-business data sharing from connected products |
| III | Obligations for data holders legally obliged to make data available |
| IV | Unfair contract terms on data access and use between enterprises |
| V | Making data available to public-sector bodies on exceptional need |
| VI | **Switching between data-processing services** (cloud) |
| VII | International governmental access and transfer safeguards |
| VIII | Interoperability |
| IX | Implementation and enforcement |
| X | Sui generis database right |
| XI | Final provisions, including Article 50 |

## Points that get answered wrongly

- **It is not only about cloud switching.** Chapter VI is the most-covered part in the trade press, but
  the connected-product access rights in Chapter II are the broader change.
- **It does not override the GDPR.** Where product data includes personal data, the GDPR continues to
  apply in full; the Data Act adds access rights, it does not create a lawful basis that displaces them.
- **The sui generis database right** (Chapter X) does not apply to databases containing data obtained
  from or generated by a connected product — this is a genuine narrowing of Directive 96/9/EC.
- Do not confuse it with the **Data Governance Act** (Regulation (EU) 2022/868) or with the
  **European Health Data Space** (Regulation (EU) 2025/327).
