# Court of Justice, Case C-482/24 P, Global 8 Airlines v Commission: inclusion on the aircraft flight-ban list can be challenged (8 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **Judgment of 8 October 2026**, Case **C-482/24 P | Global 8 Airlines v Commission**, Court of Justice (Court press release No 143/26). An appeal against an **order of the General Court of 29 April 2024 (T-277/23)**.
- **Holding:** the Commission's inclusion of aircraft on a **flight-ban list** is an act that may be challenged before the EU Courts by an action for annulment. Whether an act can be challenged is assessed **objectively, on its substance** and the intention of its author, even if the institution lacked the powers to adopt it. Whether the act changes the applicant's legal position is a **separate** question about standing under Article 263(4) TFEU.
- **What the Court did:** it **set aside** the General Court's order (which had dismissed the action as inadmissible) and **referred the case back to the General Court**.
- **What the Court has NOT decided:** whether the listing of the two aircraft was lawful. The merits and the remaining admissibility questions are still to be examined by the General Court. Do not say the airline won its case.
- **Background:** under the restrictive measures adopted after Russia's military aggression against Ukraine, the EU prohibited certain aircraft linked to Russian persons or entities from landing, taking off or overflying EU territory. Global 8 Airlines, a **Kyrgyz** airline, bought two aircraft from a Russian company; after Eurocontrol rejected a flight plan for one of them, the company learned both appeared on a list of aircraft subject to a flight ban that the Commission had sent to Eurocontrol.
- **Why the Court set the order aside:** the General Court did not examine the content of the decision and did not ask the Commission to produce it; the case file showed the Commission's leading role in putting the aircraft on the list. The Court rejected the grounds of inadmissibility that the decision did not exist, was not a challengeable act and was not attributable to the Commission.
- Eurocontrol is an intergovernmental organisation separate from the EU; as network manager of the single European sky it processes flight plans and takes the Commission's information on banned aircraft into account.
