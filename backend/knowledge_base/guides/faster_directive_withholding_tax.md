# FASTER: Faster and Safer Tax Excess Relief Directive

## QUICK FACTS
- Name: FASTER (Faster and Safer Relief of Excess Withholding Taxes)
- Legal act: Council Directive (EU) 2025/50 of 10 December 2024
- CELEX: 32025L0050 -- Official Journal: OJ L, 2025/50, published 10 January 2025
- Legal basis: Article 115 TFEU (direct-tax harmonisation, unanimity in Council)
- Lead: DG TAXUD (Taxation and Customs Union)
- Procedure: special legislative procedure, Council unanimity, European Parliament consulted (non-binding opinion)
- Transposition deadline: 31 December 2028
- Application date: national rules apply from 1 January 2030
- Scope: cross-border withholding tax relief on dividends (mandatory) and, where a Member State's system covers them, interest payments from publicly-traded securities
- Pillar 1: common EU digital Tax Residence Certificate (eTRC), automated issuance, one certificate usable for multiple refund claims in the same calendar year
- Pillar 2: two fast-track relief procedures Member States must offer as alternatives to slow standard-refund procedures -- relief at source (correct treaty/domestic rate applied at the moment of payment) and/or quick refund (refund granted within 50 days of the payment date)
- Pillar 3: national registers of certified financial intermediaries (large intermediaries mandatory, smaller ones optional), linked via a proposed EU Certified Financial Intermediary Portal, with standardised reporting obligations to combat withholding-tax fraud (Cum/Ex, Cum/Cum schemes)
- Political agreement: ECOFIN Council, 14 May 2024. Formal adoption: 10 December 2024
- Origin: part of the Commission's 2022 Capital Markets Union tax package, feeding into the broader EU capital-markets integration and Savings and Investments Union agenda
- Standard procedures unaffected: Member States keep existing standard-refund systems; FASTER adds fast-track alternatives, it does not abolish the status quo
- Small Member State carve-out: jurisdictions with a market capitalisation of listed shares below a set threshold and no central securities depository may request a temporary derogation from parts of the fast-track obligations

FASTER modernises how cross-border portfolio investors reclaim tax withheld in excess of the applicable double-taxation-treaty rate on dividends from EU-listed shares (and, in participating Member States, interest from EU-listed bonds). It replaces slow, paper-heavy national refund procedures that could take months or years with a digital residence certificate and two harmonised fast-track relief options, while tightening anti-fraud controls through a certified-intermediary chain of custody.

## Why FASTER exists

Standard withholding-tax refund procedures across the EU have historically been slow (often 6 months to several years), paper-based, and inconsistent between Member States. This created two problems the Commission set out to fix:

1. **Cost and friction for investors.** Cross-border investors routinely face double taxation in practice, even where a tax treaty entitles them to relief, because the refund process is too slow or too costly relative to the amount at stake. This discourages cross-border investment, working against the EU's capital-markets integration goals.
2. **Fraud exposure.** The same fragmented, paper-based procedures were exploited in large-scale "Cum/Ex" and "Cum/Cum" withholding-tax reclaim schemes, in which the same tax refund was claimed multiple times or claimed by parties not entitled to it. Estimates cited during the legislative process put EU-wide revenue losses from such schemes in the tens of billions of euro over the past two decades.

FASTER responds to both: it speeds up legitimate relief while embedding stronger identity and ownership verification (via the eTRC and certified intermediaries) to close the loopholes that enabled reclaim fraud.

## The three pillars

### 1. Digital Tax Residence Certificate (eTRC)

Member States must put in place an automated process to issue a digital Tax Residence Certificate to any natural person or entity resident for tax purposes in their jurisdiction. Once issued, a single eTRC can be used to support multiple withholding-tax relief claims within the same calendar year, removing the need to request a fresh paper certificate for every claim. The eTRC must contain standard identifying information (name, address, tax identification number, and, where available, a European Unique Identifier or Legal Entity Identifier) and can be invalidated if the holder ceases to be tax-resident in the issuing state, or if it was obtained on the basis of incorrect or fraudulent information.

### 2. Fast-track relief procedures

Member States whose existing standard-refund procedure exceeds a set processing-time threshold must offer at least one of two fast-track alternatives:

- **Relief at source**: the correct, treaty- or domestic-law-compliant withholding rate is applied directly when the dividend (or interest) is paid, so the investor never overpays and never needs to claim a refund.
- **Quick refund**: where relief at source is not applied, the excess amount withheld must be refunded within 50 days of the payment date, replacing standard procedures that can take far longer.

Member States retain their existing standard-refund systems in parallel; FASTER does not remove those, it adds faster options investors and intermediaries can use instead.

### 3. Certified financial intermediaries

Member States operating the fast-track procedures must establish a national register of certified financial intermediaries. Large intermediaries active in the relevant market (credit institutions, investment firms and central securities depositories performing custody activities, above a defined size threshold) must register; smaller intermediaries may register voluntarily. Certified intermediaries take on due-diligence and reporting duties: verifying investor eligibility for relief, collecting and passing on the eTRC data, and reporting payment and ownership information to national tax authorities, creating a documented chain of custody that underpins fraud detection. The Directive also provides for a proposed EU-level Certified Financial Intermediary Portal, giving a single access point to the national registers.

## Scope and carve-outs

FASTER applies to withholding tax relief on dividends paid on publicly-traded shares issued within the EU, where the investor is resident in a jurisdiction other than the source Member State. Coverage of interest on publicly-traded bonds is at Member State discretion. Member States whose domestic capital market is very small (measured by the market capitalisation of listed shares against a threshold set in the Directive) and that do not operate a central securities depository may request a temporary derogation from the fast-track obligations, recognising that the compliance cost may not be proportionate for the smallest markets.

## Who is affected

- **Institutional and retail cross-border investors** holding EU-listed shares (and, where covered, bonds) through custodians in a different Member State from the issuer.
- **Custodian banks, investment firms and central securities depositories** acting as intermediaries in the dividend/interest payment chain, who face new registration, due-diligence and reporting obligations if they seek certified-intermediary status.
- **National tax administrations**, which must build the automated eTRC issuance systems, the certified-intermediary registers, and the fast-track relief workflows.
- **Asset managers and pension funds** with diversified EU equity portfolios, for whom faster, cheaper relief reduces a long-standing drag on net returns from cross-border holdings.
