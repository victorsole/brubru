# Medical Devices Regulation (MDR)

## QUICK FACTS
- Full name: Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices
- CELEX: 32017R0745
- Replaces: Medical Device Directive 93/42/EEC (MDD) and Active Implantable Medical Device Directive 90/385/EEC (AIMDD)
- In force since: 26 May 2021 (after COVID delay from 2020)
- Responsible DG: DG SANTE (Health and Food Safety)
- Responsible Commissioner: Oliver Varhelyi (Health and Animal Welfare)
- Key bodies: Medical Device Coordination Group (MDCG), Notified Bodies
- EUDAMED: European database on medical devices (partially operational, full deployment delayed)
- UDI: Unique Device Identification system for traceability
- Classification: 4 risk classes (I, IIa, IIb, III) per Annex VIII
- Clinical evidence: mandatory clinical evaluations, post-market clinical follow-up (PMCF)
- Post-market surveillance (PMS): enhanced reporting obligations
- Notified Bodies: stricter designation and oversight requirements
- Transition: certificates under MDD/AIMDD valid until 31 December 2028 (extended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860)
- In Vitro Diagnostics: separate regulation, IVDR (EU) 2017/746 (CELEX 32017R0746)
- Simplification: Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 extended transition deadlines, reduced burden on legacy devices
- Related: European Biotech Act COM(2025)1022 (amends clinical trial aspects)

## Overview

The Medical Devices Regulation (MDR) is the cornerstone EU legal framework governing medical devices placed on the internal market. It replaced the previous directive-based system with a directly applicable regulation to ensure uniform application across all Member States. The MDR applies to a wide spectrum of products: from simple bandages and contact lenses to pacemakers and surgical robots. Its key objectives are patient safety, clinical evidence requirements, increased transparency through EUDAMED, and stricter oversight of Notified Bodies.

The regulation applies to manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers, and distributors. Economic operators must comply with general safety and performance requirements (GSPR) set out in Annex I.

## Key Changes from MDD

The MDR introduced substantially more stringent requirements compared to the predecessor Medical Device Directive:

| Area | MDD (old) | MDR (new) |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical evidence | Clinical data often based on equivalence | Mandatory clinical evaluation; stricter equivalence criteria |
| Notified Body oversight | Variable designation standards | Harmonised designation under EUDAMED, MDCG oversight |
| Post-market surveillance | Lighter obligations | Mandatory PMS plans, PMCF, periodic safety update reports (PSUR) |
| UDI | Not required | Mandatory for all devices |
| Transparency | Limited | EUDAMED public interface; device certificate database |
| Implant card | Not required | Mandatory for implantable devices |
| Legacy devices | MDD certificates accepted | Extended to 31 December 2028 (Regulation 2024/1860) |

## EUDAMED

The European database on medical devices (EUDAMED) is the central pillar of the MDR transparency architecture. It comprises six interconnected modules: actor registration, UDI/device registration, Notified Bodies and certificates, clinical investigations, vigilance and post-market surveillance, and market surveillance.

EUDAMED has faced repeated deployment delays. As of early 2026, several modules remain in phased rollout. The Commission has extended mandatory use deadlines accordingly. The public-facing interface allows healthcare professionals and patients to search device certificates, Notified Body decisions, and safety notices.

## Device Classification

Devices are classified by risk level according to rules in Annex VIII:

- **Class I:** low risk (e.g., bandages, non-sterile devices). Self-declaration by manufacturer. Some Class I devices with measuring function or sterile presentation require Notified Body involvement.
- **Class IIa:** medium risk (e.g., dental fillings, hearing aids). Notified Body conformity assessment required.
- **Class IIb:** medium-high risk (e.g., ventilators, infusion pumps). Enhanced Notified Body scrutiny.
- **Class III:** high risk (e.g., cardiac implants, drug-device combinations). Mandatory clinical investigation data; Notified Body scrutiny plus potential SCENIHR/MDCG expert panel opinion for novel high-risk devices.

## Transition Timeline

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 26 May 2021 | MDR entered into application (delayed from May 2020) |
| 26 May 2024 | Class IIb implantable legacy devices: MDD certificates expire unless extended |
| 31 December 2027 | Class III and Class IIb non-implantable legacy devices: certificates expire |
| 31 December 2028 | Class IIa and Class I sterile/measuring legacy devices: certificates expire (Regulation 2024/1860 extension) |
| TBC | EUDAMED full mandatory use following confirmed readiness |

Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 (adopted June 2024) introduced further transition relief, including sell-off periods for legacy devices already placed on the market and reduced obligations for certain well-established technologies.

## In Vitro Diagnostics (IVDR)

The companion IVDR (Regulation (EU) 2017/746, CELEX 32017R0746) applies to diagnostic devices such as blood tests, COVID-19 rapid tests, and genetic tests. It also introduced phased transition deadlines that have been extended. The IVDR is more stringent than the previous IVD Directive (98/79/EC), particularly for high-risk tests previously self-certified by manufacturers.
