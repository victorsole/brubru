# Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) under Horizon Europe

## QUICK FACTS
- **What it is**: MSCA (Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions) is the researcher training and mobility strand of Horizon Europe Pillar 1 (Excellent Science), alongside the European Research Council (ERC) and research infrastructures
- **Budget**: approximately EUR 6.6 billion for 2021-2027 (Horizon Europe MFF period)
- **Named after**: Marie Sklodowska-Curie, the Polish-French physicist and chemist, two-time Nobel laureate (Physics 1903, Chemistry 1911); the programme marked its 30th anniversary in 2026
- **Five sub-actions**: (1) Doctoral Networks (DN), including DN-ID (industrial doctorates) and DN-JD (joint doctorates); (2) Postdoctoral Fellowships (PF), split into European Fellowships and Global Fellowships; (3) Staff Exchanges (SE); (4) COFUND, co-funding new or existing regional, national and international doctoral and postdoctoral fellowship programmes; (5) MSCA and Citizens, including European Researchers' Night and the MSCA Awards
- **The mobility rule**: recruited researchers must not have resided or carried out their main activity in the country of the recruiting organisation for more than 12 months in the 3 years immediately before the recruitment deadline (36 months for Global Fellowships' outgoing phase); the rule is the structural core of MSCA and distinguishes it from purely domestic doctoral funding
- **Researcher-experience definitions**: a "doctoral candidate" is not yet awarded a doctoral degree; a "postdoctoral researcher" holds a doctoral degree and has, for Postdoctoral Fellowships, a maximum of 8 years of full-time equivalent research experience since the doctorate (extended for career breaks, parenthood, illness, national service, refugee status)
- **Managed by**: the European Research Executive Agency (REA), a Commission executive agency; REA also manages other parts of Horizon Europe (Cluster 2 Culture/Creativity/Inclusive Society elements, MSCA, research infrastructures actions); see `eu_commission_executive_agencies.md`
- **Policy DG**: DG Education, Youth, Sport and Culture (DG EAC) holds policy responsibility for MSCA; REA handles the operational funding and grant administration
- **Link to EURAXESS**: the EU's pan-European job portal and support network for researcher mobility (jobs, housing, visa/immigration guidance, family support); MSCA fellowship vacancies are typically advertised there; see `euraxess_researcher_mobility_portal.md` (planned)
- **Link to ERC**: the European Research Council funds frontier, investigator-driven research for established or emerging research leaders (Starting, Consolidator, Advanced, Synergy grants); MSCA is complementary, focused on training and mobility rather than funding a single principal investigator's research programme
- **Seal of Excellence**: high-scoring MSCA proposals (typically Postdoctoral Fellowships) that pass all quality thresholds but cannot be funded due to budget limits receive a Seal of Excellence quality label, which some Member States, regions and universities use to unlock alternative (national, regional, or Horizon Europe COFUND) funding without a fresh evaluation; see `seal_of_excellence.md` (planned)
- **MSCA Alumni**: the MSCA Alumni Association connects past and present MSCA fellows across disciplines and countries, providing a network for career development, mentoring and policy engagement
- **Widening participation dimension**: MSCA COFUND and Doctoral Networks both carry provisions to widen participation from Widening countries (the lower-R&I-performing Member States and Associated Countries under the "Widening Participation and Spreading Excellence" logic); COFUND explicitly targets less research-intensive institutions and regions
- **Primary URL**: https://marie-sklodowska-curie-actions.ec.europa.eu/
- **REA funding and grants URL**: https://rea.ec.europa.eu/funding-and-grants_en
- **Application route**: all MSCA calls are published and applied for via the Funding & Tenders Portal (ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/); see `horizon_europe_grant_management.md`

Comprehensive guide to the Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions, Horizon Europe's dedicated instrument for researcher training, career development and cross-border, cross-sector mobility.

## What MSCA Is and Its Place in Horizon Europe Pillar 1

Horizon Europe (2021-2027, Regulation (EU) 2021/695) is structured in three pillars plus a horizontal "Widening Participation and Strengthening the European Research Area" part. Pillar 1, Excellent Science, funds research and researchers on the basis of scientific excellence rather than predefined thematic priorities. It has three components:

1. The European Research Council (ERC): frontier research grants to individual principal investigators
2. Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA): doctoral training, postdoctoral fellowships, staff exchanges and structural support for researcher careers and mobility
3. Research infrastructures: funding for pan-European research infrastructure development and access

MSCA is best understood as the "people" instrument of Pillar 1. Where the ERC backs an individual's research idea, MSCA backs the researcher's training, skills and international, intersectoral and interdisciplinary exposure, at every career stage from doctoral candidate through experienced postdoctoral researcher. MSCA is deliberately bottom-up: it does not prescribe research topics, applicants (or host institutions on their behalf) propose the research and training plan.

MSCA traces its lineage to the "Marie Curie Actions" of FP6 and FP7 and to the "People" specific programme of FP7, later renamed to honour Marie Sklodowska-Curie under Horizon 2020 and continued into Horizon Europe.

## The Five Sub-Actions in Detail

### 1. Doctoral Networks (DN)

Doctoral Networks fund collaborative doctoral training implemented by partnerships of universities, research institutions, businesses and other socio-economic actors across countries. Recruited doctoral candidates are enrolled in a doctoral programme and typically hold a fully-funded fellowship contract for up to 3 years.

Three variants exist within DN:
- **Standard DN**: at least three beneficiaries from three different countries
- **DN-ID (Industrial Doctorates)**: doctoral candidates split their time between an academic and a non-academic (usually private-sector) partner, gaining a joint academic and industrial doctoral experience
- **DN-JD (Joint Doctorates)**: candidates work towards a joint, double or multiple doctoral degree awarded by at least two degree-awarding institutions in the network

### 2. Postdoctoral Fellowships (PF)

Postdoctoral Fellowships support individual experienced researchers (holding a doctoral degree, within the eligibility window for research experience) to gain new skills through advanced training and international, interdisciplinary and intersectoral mobility. Two strands exist:

- **European Fellowships**: hosted for 1-2 years at an organisation in an EU Member State or Horizon Europe Associated Country
- **Global Fellowships**: an outgoing phase of 1-2 years at a host organisation in a non-associated Third Country, followed by a mandatory 1-year return phase at an EU/Associated Country organisation

Both strands can include a non-academic secondment placement to build intersectoral experience.

### 3. Staff Exchanges (SE)

Staff Exchanges fund short-term staff exchanges (from academia to industry and vice versa, and between countries) within a consortium of organisations from different sectors and countries. Unlike DN and PF, SE does not fund an individual researcher's salary for a sustained period; it funds mobility allowances for staff (at any career stage, including non-research staff such as technical or administrative personnel) to spend periods, typically 1 to 12 months (cumulative over the project's duration), seconded at a partner organisation.

### 4. COFUND

COFUND co-funds new or existing regional, national or international doctoral programmes and postdoctoral fellowship programmes, injecting EU funding and MSCA quality standards (mobility rule, open, transparent and merit-based recruitment, career development support) into programmes run by universities, research funding organisations, and other bodies. COFUND is the main channel through which MSCA supports institutional or national capacity-building rather than funding a single multinational consortium; it is also a key vehicle for widening participation, since Widening countries can use COFUND to strengthen their own doctoral and fellowship schemes with MSCA co-financing and branding.

### 5. MSCA and Citizens

This strand connects researchers with the public to promote the image and understanding of research careers and the role of research in society. Its flagship activity is the **European Researchers' Night**, a Europe-wide public event (usually held on the last Friday of September) featuring hands-on demonstrations, talks and activities that bring researchers face-to-face with the public, especially children and young people, in hundreds of cities simultaneously. The strand also runs the **MSCA Awards**, recognising outstanding contributions by MSCA-funded researchers to European society, science communication, entrepreneurship or innovation.

## The Mobility Rule and Eligibility

The mobility rule is the defining eligibility condition across nearly all MSCA actions (DN, PF, COFUND-recruited fellows): at the recruitment deadline, the researcher must not have resided or carried out their main activity (work, studies) in the country of the recruiting host organisation for more than 12 months in the 3 years immediately before that deadline. Compulsory national service and short stays such as holidays are not counted.

For Global Fellowships, the outgoing phase requires that the researcher must not have resided or worked in the Third Country of the outgoing host for more than 12 months in the 3 years before the deadline, and the mandatory return phase brings the researcher back to an EU Member State or Associated Country.

Career-stage definitions that gate eligibility:
- **Doctoral candidates (DN)**: at the date of recruitment, must not already hold a doctoral degree
- **Postdoctoral researchers (PF)**: must hold a doctoral degree and have, at the call deadline, a maximum of 8 years of full-time equivalent research experience measured from the date of obtaining the doctorate; the 8-year ceiling is extended on a case-by-case basis for documented career breaks (parental leave, long-term illness, national service, clinical training, refugee status, and similar)

Applicants and beneficiaries must also comply with MSCA's open, transparent and merit-based recruitment principles (broadly advertised vacancies, at least 30 days' notice, published selection criteria) reflecting the European Charter for Researchers and Code of Conduct for the Recruitment of Researchers (the "HR Excellence in Research" logic).

## Indicative Budgets

MSCA's overall Horizon Europe 2021-2027 envelope is approximately EUR 6.6 billion, funded from Pillar 1 (Excellent Science). Annual and call-specific budgets are set out in the biennial Horizon Europe Work Programme (the current cycle covers 2023-2024 and 2025-2027 tranches) under the "Marie Sklodowska-Curie Actions" part. Indicative annual calls typically run:
- Doctoral Networks: one call per year, largest single budget line within MSCA (frequently over EUR 400-500 million per call)
- Postdoctoral Fellowships: one call per year (European and Global strands combined)
- Staff Exchanges: one call per year, smaller budget line
- COFUND: one call per year, split between doctoral programmes and fellowship programmes
- MSCA and Citizens: separate, smaller calls for European Researchers' Night and MSCA Awards

Exact figures shift between work programme cycles; always confirm current-year amounts against the live Horizon Europe Work Programme and Funding & Tenders Portal call pages rather than citing a fixed number as permanent.

## REA as Manager

The **European Research Executive Agency (REA)** is the Commission executive agency responsible for the operational implementation of MSCA: publishing calls, running proposal evaluation with independent expert reviewers, negotiating and signing grant agreements, and monitoring projects through to closure. Policy ownership of MSCA sits with **DG EAC** (Directorate-General for Education, Youth, Sport and Culture), which sets the programme's strategic direction within the Horizon Europe Work Programme, while REA executes the grant-management lifecycle described generically in `horizon_europe_grant_management.md` (Grant Agreement Preparation, Model Grant Agreement, reporting, audits).

REA also manages several other Horizon Europe strands beyond MSCA (parts of Cluster 2, research infrastructures actions, and elements of Widening Participation), making it one of the largest of the Commission's executive agencies by grant portfolio. See `eu_commission_executive_agencies.md` for the full map of executive agencies (REA, EISMEA/European Innovation Council and SMEs Executive Agency, HaDEA, CINEA, ERCEA, EDEA) and their respective programme responsibilities.

## Link to EURAXESS, ERC and Seal of Excellence

**EURAXESS** is the European Commission's pan-European portal and support network for researcher mobility. It hosts a jobs board where MSCA-funded positions (particularly Doctoral Network and COFUND vacancies) are commonly advertised, alongside practical guidance on visas, social security, family relocation, housing and career development in over 40 countries via a network of EURAXESS Service Centres. Universities and research institutions applying for or hosting MSCA fellows routinely use EURAXESS both to advertise positions (a requirement of MSCA's open recruitment principle) and to support incoming researchers' relocation.

**ERC (European Research Council)** funds frontier, investigator-driven research through Starting, Consolidator, Advanced and Synergy Grants, plus Proof of Concept grants for near-market validation of ERC-funded results. The distinction from MSCA is structural: ERC grants fund a principal investigator's research programme and team; MSCA funds training, mobility and career development for the researcher(s) themselves, often as part of someone else's programme (a DN network, a host institution's COFUND scheme). A researcher's career path might involve an MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship building the track record for a later ERC Starting Grant.

**Seal of Excellence** is a quality label awarded to proposals, most commonly MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships, that pass all evaluation thresholds and are ranked highly by the independent evaluation panel but cannot be funded because the call's budget is exhausted. The label allows the applicant and host institution to seek alternative funding (some Member States and regions have dedicated Seal of Excellence co-funding schemes; some universities self-fund Sealed proposals; MSCA COFUND programmes can also absorb Sealed proposals) without requiring a new evaluation, since the scientific quality has already been independently certified.

## MSCA and Citizens: European Researchers' Night

European Researchers' Night is MSCA's flagship public engagement activity, held simultaneously in hundreds of cities across Europe (and beyond) on a single night, usually the last Friday of September. Events combine interactive science demonstrations, talks, experiments and games designed to bring the public, especially young people and families, into direct contact with researchers and demystify what a research career looks like. National consortia of universities, research institutions, museums and local authorities apply for and run the events under MSCA funding. The strand also funds the MSCA Awards, recognising individual MSCA-funded researchers or projects for outstanding contributions to science communication, entrepreneurship, or societal impact.

## Brubru Engagement Angles

MSCA is a high-frequency, recurring funding stream (annual calls across all five sub-actions) with a distinct user base from other Horizon Europe strands:

- **Universities and research institutions**: doctoral schools and international offices coordinate Doctoral Networks and Postdoctoral Fellowship applications, manage the mobility-rule eligibility checks for incoming candidates, and run EURAXESS-listed recruitment; grant and legal offices manage the Model Grant Agreement obligations once awarded
- **University tech-transfer and knowledge valorisation offices**: MSCA Doctoral Networks with an industrial component (DN-ID) and Staff Exchanges with non-academic partners intersect directly with knowledge valorisation strategy; see `knowledge_valorisation_tech_transfer.md`
- **SMEs and companies hosting fellows or seconded staff**: non-academic partners in DN-ID, PF secondments, and SE need to understand intersectoral secondment rules, IP and access-rights provisions (shared with the general MGA, see `horizon_europe_grant_management.md`), and the mobility-rule implications for hiring internationally
- **Individual researchers**: doctoral candidates and postdoctoral researchers evaluating eligibility (career-stage windows, the 12-month mobility rule, career-break extensions) before applying, and MSCA alumni tracking career-development resources
- **Regional and national funding bodies**: COFUND is the main channel for co-financing domestic doctoral and fellowship schemes with MSCA branding and quality standards, relevant to Widening-country research ministries and funding agencies building capacity
- **Public engagement and science communication teams**: applying for European Researchers' Night funding and MSCA Awards nominations
