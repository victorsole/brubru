# EU Joint Clinical Assessment of Medical Devices: the First Two Devices Selected (2 October 2026)

## QUICK FACTS
- **What happened:** the Commission adopted **Implementing Decision C(2026) 7113 final of 2 October 2026**, its **first decision selecting medical devices for joint clinical assessment (JCA)** under the **HTA Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2021/2282** (CELEX `32021R2282`), on the basis of **Article 7(4)**. DG SANTE announced it on **6 October 2026**: "Today marks the beginning of joint work on medical devices under the HTA regulation."
- **The two devices (both for cardiovascular disease, both mitral valve replacement systems):** (1) the **SAPIEN M3 Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement System**, manufacturer **Edwards Lifesciences LLC**; assessor the Dutch National Health Care Institute (ZIN), co-assessor the Norwegian Medical Products Agency (DMP). (2) the **Highlife Trans-septal Mitral Valve Replacement System**, manufacturer **Highlife SAS**; assessor the French National Authority for Health (HAS), co-assessor the Polish Agency for Health Technology Assessment and Tariff System (AOTMiT).
- **Why these two:** the **Member State Coordination Group on HTA recommended them on 22 June 2026**; the decision says both meet the selection criteria (a), (b), (c), (e) and (f) of Article 7(4): **unmet medical need, first in class, potential impact on patients or health systems, cross-border dimension and Union-wide added value** (the decision finds there is no equivalent device with the same mechanism). Criterion (d), the use of artificial intelligence, is not claimed.
- **Cadence:** DG SANTE says selection of medical devices "is expected to occur on a **quarterly** basis".
- **Scope of device JCAs:** selected high-risk medical devices (class IIb and III under the Medical Devices Regulation) and class D in vitro diagnostics, chosen by the Commission after a Coordination Group recommendation. This is a **small, selective** group, NOT every device.
- **What a JCA produces:** a joint clinical assessment report and summary report on clinical benefit compared with existing technologies; Member States use them in national decisions. **Pricing and reimbursement stay national.** The reports contain no value judgement on overall clinical added value.
- **Deadlines for the manufacturer (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2086 of 17 October 2025, CELEX `32025R2086`):** the assessment scope is finalised **10 days after the selection decision**; the **dossier is due 100 days after the first request** (the HTA secretariat can extend by at most 30 days in justified cases); the Commission confirms the dossier within **15 working days**; the revised draft reports are finalised within **165 days** after that confirmation.
- **Medicines in comparison:** DG SANTE says **15 JCAs for medicinal products are ongoing and 4 JCA reports are published**.
- **Do not state:** that these devices are banned, restricted or reimbursed differently because of the selection; that all devices now need a JCA; or a risk class for the two devices (the decision text does not give one).

## Why it matters
Manufacturers of the selected devices must submit their clinical evidence to the Commission for the joint assessment and cannot be asked for the same information separately by Member States. Health technology developers of other high-risk devices should watch the quarterly selections.

## FAQ
**Is the JCA a marketing authorisation or CE-mark replacement?** No. CE marking under the Medical Devices Regulation is separate; the JCA is a clinical-benefit comparison for national decisions.
**Who decides reimbursement?** The Member States.
