## Introduction to the EU Transparency Register

The **EU Transparency Register** is a mandatory database managed jointly by the **European Parliament**, the **Council of the European Union**, and the **European Commission**. It lists organisations, associations, groups, and self-employed individuals ("interest representatives") who carry out activities aimed at influencing EU policy and decision-making processes.

**Legal Basis:**
- Interinstitutional Agreement of 20 May 2021 (in force since 1 July 2021)
- Published in OJ L 207, 11.6.2021

**Governing Institutions:**
- European Parliament
- Council of the European Union
- European Commission

**Management Structure:**
- **Management Board**: Secretaries-General of the three signatory institutions
- **Joint Transparency Register Secretariat**: Operational management

---

## What is the Transparency Register?

The Transparency Register is designed to show European citizens:
- **Which interests** are being represented at EU level
- **By whom** these interests are represented
- **On whose behalf** representation activities are carried out
- **What resources** are devoted to these activities (financial and human)

### Key Features

1. **Public Database**: All registered information is publicly accessible
2. **Code of Conduct**: 16 rules governing interaction with EU institutions
3. **Complaints Mechanism**: Administrative inquiry process for Code violations
4. **Conditionality Principle**: Registration required for certain activities

### Purpose

The register promotes:
- **Transparency**: Clear visibility of lobbying activities
- **Accountability**: Public scrutiny of influence attempts
- **Balanced Representation**: Equal access for all interest groups
- **Ethical Conduct**: Standards for interaction with EU institutions

## Why Register?

### Mandatory Requirements

Since 1 September 2025, registration is **MANDATORY** for interest representatives who want to:

**With European Parliament:**
- Request access authorisations to EP premises
- Speak at committee hearings
- Participate in intergroup and unofficial grouping activities on EP premises
- Organise or co-host events in Parliament

**With Council of the EU:**
- Meet the Secretary-General and Directors-General of General Secretariat
- Participate in subject-specific briefings for interest representatives
- Participate as speakers in public events organised by General Secretariat

**With European Commission:**
- Meet Commissioners, Cabinet members, Directors-General, and management staff
- Participate in Commission expert groups
- All meetings relating to law or policy formulation or implementation

### Benefits of Registration

- **Access to decision-makers**: Direct engagement with EU officials
- **Credibility**: Demonstrates transparency and compliance
- **Participation in policy process**: Expert groups, consultations, hearings
- **Networking opportunities**: Events and forums
- **Early access to information**: Policy developments and legislative proposals

### Consequences of Non-Registration

- **No meetings** with covered officials and decision-makers
- **No access** to EU institutions' premises
- **No participation** in expert groups
- **No speaking** at official events
- **Limited influence** on EU policy-making

## Who Must Register?

### Definition of Interest Representative

**Any natural or legal person, formal or informal group, association, or network** engaged in activities to influence:
- Formulation or implementation of EU policy or legislation
- Decision-making processes of EU institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies

### Covered Activities

Activities include (but are not limited to):
- **Direct contact** with EU officials, MEPs, or staff
- **Communication**: Letters, information materials, position papers
- **Events**: Organising conferences, meetings, briefings
- **Campaigns**: Public opinion, media engagement
- **Intermediaries**: Using consultants, lobbying firms
- **Think tanks and research**: Policy analysis and publications
- **Grassroots initiatives**: Mobilisation campaigns
- **Social media and online**: Digital advocacy

### Types of Organisations That Must Register

**If engaging in covered activities:**
- Professional consultancies and law firms (lobbying on behalf of clients)
- In-house lobbyists and trade/business associations
- Non-governmental organisations (NGOs)
- Think tanks, research institutions, and academia (when lobbying)
- Organisations representing churches and religious communities (when lobbying)
- Platforms, networks, and partnerships (when lobbying)
- Self-employed consultants and individual lobbyists

---

## Who is Exempt from Registration?

The following entities are **NOT required to register**:

### Permanent Exemptions

1. **Political parties** at national and EU level
2. **Member States' government services** and permanent representations
3. **Third countries' governments** and diplomatic missions
4. **International intergovernmental organisations** (UN, OECD, etc.) and their diplomatic missions
5. **EU institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies**
6. **Churches and religious associations or communities** (unless engaging in lobbying)
7. **Philosophical and non-confessional organisations** (unless engaging in lobbying)

### Optional Registration

**Regional and local public authorities** may register voluntarily but are not required to do so.

### Activities NOT Requiring Registration

1. **Responding to direct requests** from EU institutions for information, data, or expertise
2. **Legal advice** (provision of professional legal counsel)
3. **Social dialogue** between social partners
4. **Public consultations** (responding to official consultations)
5. **Participation upon invitation** from EU institutions

## The Principle of Conditionality

**Conditionality** means that registration is a **necessary precondition** for interest representatives to carry out certain activities with EU institutions.

### Conditionality Measures (as of September 2025)

Each institution has adopted specific rules making registration mandatory for certain interactions:

#### European Parliament Rules

**Mandatory registration for:**
- Access badges to EP premises
- Speaking at committee hearings
- Participating in intergroup activities on premises
- Organising/co-hosting events in Parliament
- Scheduled meetings with MEPs on parliamentary business (since May 2025)

**MEP Obligations:**
- Publish information on scheduled meetings with interest representatives
- Meetings must be declared online
- Applies to in-person and remote meetings

#### Council Rules

**Mandatory registration for:**
- Meetings with Secretary-General and Directors-General of General Secretariat
- Participation in subject-specific briefings
- Speaking at public events organised by General Secretariat

#### Commission Rules

**Mandatory registration for:**
- Meetings with Commissioners, Cabinet members, Directors-General, and management staff
- **ALL meetings relating to law or policy formulation/implementation** (since Commission Decision December 2024)
- Membership in expert groups
- Participation in Commission events

**Transparency Measures:**
- Commissioners and Cabinet members publish meeting information
- Commission management staff publish meeting minutes
- Meetings only possible with registered representatives

## Categories of Registration

When registering, you must select the category that best describes your organisation:

### Section I: Professional Consultancies and Law Firms

**For:** Organisations providing professional services to third-party clients, including lobbying, public affairs, legal, and consultancy services

**Characteristics:**
- Act on behalf of clients
- Provide strategic advice
- Paid services

### Section II: In-House Lobbyists and Trade/Business Associations

**For:** 
- **In-house lobbyists**: Companies' own public affairs staff
- **Trade associations**: Organisations representing collective interests of members (e.g., industry federations)

**Characteristics:**
- Represent own interests or members' collective interests
- Business and commercial focus

### Section III: Non-Governmental Organisations

**For:** Civil society organisations, advocacy groups, charities

**Characteristics:**
- Public interest focus
- Not-for-profit
- May have members or be membership-based

### Section IV: Think Tanks, Research, and Academia

**For:** Research institutions, universities, academic centres engaged in policy-relevant research and advocacy

**Characteristics:**
- Research-based activities
- Policy analysis and publications
- When engaging in advocacy beyond pure research

### Section V: Organisations Representing Churches and Religious Communities

**For:** Religious organisations engaged in interest representation activities

**Characteristics:**
- Represent religious interests
- When engaging in EU policy advocacy

### Section VI: Organisations Representing Local, Regional, and Municipal Authorities

**For:** Regional and local government associations and representative offices

**Note:** Registration is voluntary for this category

- Membership structure (if applicable)

- EU policy areas covered by your activities
- Specific EU legislation or initiatives targeted
- Brief description of main activities

**Examples of EU policy areas:**
- Agriculture and rural development
- Climate action
- Competition
- Digital economy
- Energy
- Environment
- Financial services

### 4. Persons with Access to Registration

- Minimum 2, maximum 6 persons
- Names and email addresses

### 6. EU Activities Information

**Required:**

## Financial Disclosure Requirements

Financial disclosure requirements vary by registration category:

### General Principles

**All registrants must disclose:**
1. **Financial year**: Most recently closed financial year
2. **EU funding received**: Any grants from EU institutions
3. **Estimated costs**: Good faith estimate of activities within register scope

**For newly formed entities:**
- Provide estimates for current financial year
- Can select "newly formed entity" option for first 2 years

### Category-Specific Requirements

#### Professional Consultancies and Law Firms (Section I)

**Must disclose:**
- **Annual turnover**: Total turnover in relevant financial year
- **Individual client revenue**: For each client representing more than 10% of total turnover related to covered activities
- **Client names**: List all clients for whom covered activities conducted
- **EU funding received**: Amount and source

**Financial Bands (Annual Turnover):**
- < €10,000
- €10,000 - €24,999
- €25,000 - €49,999
- €50,000 - €99,999
- €100,000 - €199,999
- €200,000 - €299,999
- €300,000 - €399,999
- €400,000 - €499,999
- €500,000 - €999,999
- €1,000,000 - €1,499,999
- €1,500,000 - €1,999,999
- €2,000,000 - €2,999,999
- €3,000,000 - €3,999,999
- €4,000,000 - €4,999,999
- €5,000,000 - €5,999,999
- €6,000,000 - €9,999,999
- ≥ €10,000,000

#### In-House Lobbyists and Trade Associations (Section II)

**Must disclose:**
- **Estimated costs**: Good faith estimate of EU lobbying activities
- **Cost calculation**: Based on staff time, overhead, direct costs
- **EU funding received**: Amount and source
- **Membership information**: For associations - categories of members

**Cost Estimation Method:**
- Calculate % of staff time on EU lobbying
- Apply to total staff costs and overhead
- Include direct costs (travel, events, publications)

**Example Calculation:**
```
Staff member spends 30% time on EU lobbying
Annual gross salary + employer costs: €50,000
Lobbying cost: 0.30 × €50,000 = €15,000

Total organisational overhead: €200,000
15% of total work time on lobbying
Overhead allocation: 0.15 × €200,000 = €30,000

Direct costs (travel, events): €5,000

Total estimated cost: €15,000 + €30,000 + €5,000 = €50,000
```

#### Non-Governmental Organisations (Section III)

**Must disclose:**
- **Total annual budget**: Complete organisational budget
- **Breakdown by source**:
  - Public funding
  - Private donations
  - Membership contributions
  - Other sources
- **EU funding received**: Amount and source
- **Major contributors**: Any contribution exceeding €10,000 AND exceeding 10% of total budget
  - Must name the contributor

**Financial Bands (Total Budget):**
- < €10,000
- €10,000 - €49,999
- €50,000 - €99,999
- €100,000 - €199,999
- €200,000 - €499,999
- €500,000 - €999,999
- €1,000,000 - €4,999,999
- €5,000,000 - €9,999,999
- ≥ €10,000,000

#### Think Tanks, Research, and Academia (Section IV)

**Must disclose:**
- **Total annual budget**: Complete budget for policy-relevant activities
- **Funding sources breakdown**:
  - Public funding (government, EU)
  - Private donations
  - Corporate contributions
  - Research grants
- **EU funding received**: Amount and source
- **Major contributors**: Following NGO rules (>€10,000 and >10% of budget)

#### Other Categories

Disclosure requirements similar to relevant category based on nature of activities.

### New September 2025 Rule: Automatic Cross-Checking

**From 1 September 2025:**
The Register automatically compares financial information between:
- Clients' declarations
- Intermediaries' declarations

**Purpose:**
- Improve data quality
- Spot inconsistencies
- Enhance transparency

**Implication:**
Ensure your financial disclosure matches with any intermediaries or clients you work with.

## The Code of Conduct

All registrants must observe the **Code of Conduct** (Annex I of Interinstitutional Agreement).

### The 16 Rules and Principles

#### 1. Identification and Objectives

**Registrants must:**
- Clearly identify themselves by name and organisation
- Declare interests and objectives they promote
- Specify clients or members they represent

#### 2. Accuracy of Information

**Registrants must:**
- Ensure information provided is complete, up-to-date, and not misleading
- Accept that information is subject to review
- Cooperate with requests for updates and complementary information

#### 3. Honesty in Information Acquisition

**Registrants must:**
- Not obtain or try to obtain information dishonestly
- Not use fraudulent means

#### 4. No Undue Influence

**Registrants must NOT:**
- Induce MEPs, EU officials, staff, or assistants to contravene their rules and standards of behaviour
- Offer gifts, hospitality, or benefits inappropriate to the circumstances

#### 5. Former EU Staff

**When employing former EU officials, staff, or assistants:**
- Respect obligations under confidentiality rules
- Respect cooling-off periods and restrictions

#### 6. Misrepresentation

**Registrants must NOT:**
- Misrepresent themselves or their relationship with EU institutions
- Suggest special relationships with EU institutions
- Misuse EU logos, emblems, or symbols without authorisation

#### 7. Financial Transactions

**Registrants must NOT:**
- Sell to third parties copies of documents obtained from EU institutions

#### 8. Respect for Rules

**Registrants must:**
- Respect all rules, codes, and good governance practices of EU institutions
- Not obstruct implementation of such rules

#### 9. Provision of Information

**Registrants must:**
- Cooperate fully with Register Secretariat
- Respond to information requests
- Participate in investigations if required

#### 10. Conflicts of Interest

**Registrants should:**
- Avoid conflicts of interest
- Where conflicts exist, disclose them
- Take appropriate measures to address them

#### 11. Public Funds

**Registrants receiving EU funding must:**
- Ensure transparency in use of funds
- Comply with EU financial regulations
- Report use of funds appropriately

#### 12. Intellectual Property

**Registrants must:**
- Respect intellectual property rights
- Not misuse copyrighted materials

#### 13. Environment and Sustainability

**Registrants should:**
- Consider environmental impact of their activities
- Promote sustainable practices

#### 14. Data Protection

**Registrants must:**
- Comply with EU data protection rules (GDPR)
- Protect personal data appropriately

#### 15. Ethical Standards

**Registrants must:**
- Maintain high ethical standards
- Conduct activities with integrity and professionalism

#### 16. Reporting Violations

**Registrants should:**
- Report suspected Code violations
- Cooperate with investigations

### Consequences of Non-Compliance

**Measures for Code violations:**
1. **Warning**: Formal notice of violation
2. **Suspension**: Temporary removal from register (loss of conditionality benefits)
3. **Removal**: Permanent deletion from register
4. **Publication**: Name published on register as suspended/removed
5. **Badge withdrawal**: Loss of access to EP premises

## Step-by-Step Registration Process

### Prerequisites

**Before starting registration:**

1. **EU Login Account**: Create account at [EU Login](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/cas/)
2. **Prepare Information**: Gather all required data
3. **Financial Data**: Calculate costs and prepare budget information
4. **Designate Access Persons**: 2-6 persons who will manage registration

### Step 1: Access Registration Portal

1. Visit [https://transparency-register.europa.eu](https://transparency-register.europa.eu)
2. Click **"Register your organisation"**
3. Log in with EU Login credentials
4. Select **"New registration"**

### Step 2: Choose Registration Category

Select the category that best describes your organisation:
- Section I: Professional consultancies/law firms
- Section II: In-house lobbyists/trade associations
- Section III: NGOs
- Section IV: Think tanks/research/academia
- Section V: Churches/religious organisations
- Section VI: Local/regional authorities

### Step 3: Select Interests Represented

Choose which interests you represent:
- Your own interests
- Collective interests of members
- Clients' interests (for consultancies)

### Step 4: Complete Registration Form

**The registration form has multiple headings:**

**Heading 1: Identification**
- Organisation name and acronym
- Contact details
- Legal form and status
- Registration number (auto-assigned)

**Heading 2: Person Responsible**
- Legal representative
- Head of EU representation

**Heading 3: Goals and Remit**
- Mission statement
- Main activities
- Areas of work

**Heading 4: Membership Information**
- Members (if applicable)
- Membership categories
- Affiliations

**Heading 5: Areas of Interest**
- EU policy areas
- Specific legislation targeted
- Main EU activities

**Heading 6: Persons with Access**
- Names and emails (2-6 persons)
- These manage the registration

**Heading 7-13: Staff and Financial Information**
- Number of staff
- Financial year
- Budget/turnover information
- Estimated costs
- EU funding
- Major contributors (if applicable)

**Heading 14: Complementary Information**
- Additional relevant details
- Explanations if needed

**Heading 15: Communication Activities**
- Publications
- Events organised
- Campaigns
- Social media presence

**Heading 16: EU Expert Groups**
- Participation in Commission expert groups
- Other EU platforms

**Heading 17: Clients/Intermediaries**
- For consultancies: list of clients
- Use of intermediaries

**Heading 18: Code of Conduct**
- Acknowledgement and acceptance

### Step 5: Review and Submit

1. **Review all information carefully**
2. **Ensure accuracy and completeness**
3. **Check financial calculations**
4. **Accept Code of Conduct**
5. **Submit application**

### Step 6: Validation

**After submission:**
1. **Secretariat review**: Joint Transparency Register Secretariat reviews application
2. **Processing time**: Typically 1-2 weeks
3. **Queries**: May request additional information
4. **Decision**: Approval or rejection with reasons

### Step 7: Publication

**Upon approval:**
- Registration number (REG number) assigned
- Information published on public register
- You receive confirmation email
- Can now access conditionality benefits

### No Cost

**Registration is FREE** - there is no charge for registering or maintaining your registration.

## Annual Updates and Maintenance

### Mandatory Annual Update

**All registrants must update their registration at least once per year** to:
- Keep information current
- Reflect changes in activities
- Update financial data for new financial year
- Maintain accuracy

### Update Requirements

**Must update:**
1. **Financial information**: New financial year data
2. **Staff numbers**: Current staffing levels
3. **Activities**: New clients, areas of interest, initiatives
4. **Contact details**: Any changes
5. **EU funding**: New grants received
6. **Persons with access**: Staff changes

### Update Process

1. **Log in**: Use EU Login credentials
2. **Select "Update registration"**
3. **Review and modify** relevant sections
4. **Submit update**
5. **Validation**: Secretariat reviews

### Update Timeline

**When to update:**
- **Mandatory**: At least once per year (anniversary of registration)
- **Best practice**: Within 3 months of financial year close
- **As needed**: When significant changes occur

### Consequences of Not Updating

**Failure to update may result in:**
- Warning from Secretariat
- Suspension from register
- Loss of conditionality benefits
- Potential removal

### Continuous Updates

**Best practice:**
- Update when major changes occur
- Don't wait for annual deadline
- Keep information as current as possible
- Shows commitment to transparency

## Complaints and Enforcement

### Complaints Mechanism

**Anyone can file complaints** about registered interest representatives for:
- Code of Conduct violations
- Inaccurate or misleading information
- Failure to update registration
- Other non-compliance

### Types of Complaints

**1. Alerts**
- Factual errors in registration
- Ineligible entries
- Technical issues

**2. Formal Complaints**
- Suspected Code violations
- Requires evidence
- More serious than alerts

### Filing a Complaint

**Process:**
1. Access register website
2. Find registrant's entry
3. Click "Lodge a complaint"
4. Complete complaint form with:
   - Nature of complaint
   - Evidence
   - Supporting documents
5. Submit

**Requirements:**
- Must be backed by evidence
- Factual basis
- Specific Code rule violated

### Investigation Process

**Stage 1: Initial Review**
- Secretariat assesses complaint
- Determines admissibility
- May request additional information

**Stage 2: Investigation**
- Registrant notified
- Given opportunity to respond
- Secretariat examines evidence
- May request further information from both parties

**Stage 3: Decision**
- Secretariat makes recommendation
- Management Board approves measures
- Decision communicated to both parties

### Possible Measures

**For Code violations:**

**Level 1: Warning**
- Formal warning letter
- Request for corrective action
- Time limit for compliance

**Level 2: Suspension**
- Temporary removal from register
- Loss of conditionality benefits
- Cannot meet EU officials
- Published as suspended
- Duration: typically 6-12 months

**Level 3: Removal**
- Permanent deletion from register
- Cannot re-register for specified period (typically 2 years)
- Loss of all benefits
- Published as removed

**Additional Measures:**
- For MEP-related violations: withdrawal of EP access badges
- Public naming in annual report
- Notification to relevant authorities

### Appeal Rights

**Registrants can:**
- Challenge decisions
- Request review
- Provide additional information
- Appeal to EU courts if procedural errors

### Transparency

**Complaint outcomes:**
- Published in annual reports
- Statistics on complaints, investigations, measures
- Names of suspended/removed registrants published

## New Rules from September 2025

### 1. Automatic Financial Cross-Checking

**Effective: 1 September 2025**

**What changed:**
- Register now automatically compares financial data between:
  - Clients' declarations
  - Consultancies/intermediaries' declarations
- System flags inconsistencies
- Improved data quality checks

**Implication:**
- Ensure financial figures match across all related registrations
- Greater scrutiny of financial disclosures
- Higher risk of detecting discrepancies

### 2. Enhanced Meeting Rules

**Effective: 1 September 2025 (European Parliament) and December 2024 (Commission)**

**European Parliament:**
- Scheduled meetings with Parliament managers now require registration
- Managers must declare meetings with interest representatives
- Applies to meetings relating to parliamentary business

**European Commission:**
- ALL meetings relating to law or policy formulation/implementation require registration
- No exceptions (except those covered by exemptions)
- Published in Commission Decision December 2024

**Implication:**
- Even informal meetings now subject to conditionality
- Greater enforcement of registration requirement
- Wider scope of covered meetings

### 3. Registration Re-activation Feature

**New feature:**
- Organisations removed from register can reactivate
- Same registration (REG) number can be reused
- Faster re-entry process for previously registered entities

**Conditions:**
- Must address reasons for previous removal
- May require additional checks
- Subject to Secretariat approval

## Best Practices

### Registration Best Practices

1. **Be Comprehensive**
   - Provide detailed, complete information
   - Don't leave fields blank unnecessarily
   - Use complementary information box for explanations

2. **Be Accurate**
   - Double-check all data before submission
   - Ensure financial calculations are correct
   - Use proper cost estimation methods

3. **Be Transparent**
   - Provide more information rather than less
   - Clearly explain your activities
   - Disclose all relevant financial relationships

4. **Be Up-to-Date**
   - Update promptly when changes occur
   - Don't wait for annual deadline
   - Keep contact information current

5. **Be Well-Structured**
   - Organise information clearly
   - Use links to additional information on your website
   - Make it easy for public to understand your activities

### Financial Disclosure Best Practices

1. **Document Your Calculations**
   - Keep records of how you calculated estimated costs
   - Be able to justify your methodology
   - Maintain audit trail

2. **Be Conservative**
   - When in doubt, include rather than exclude
   - "Good faith estimate" doesn't mean minimum possible
   - Aim for realistic reflection of actual costs

3. **Consider All Costs**
   - Staff time (including senior management)
   - Overhead allocated to EU activities
   - Direct costs (travel, events, materials)
   - External consultants and services
   - Office space and utilities

4. **Consistency**
   - Use same methodology year over year
   - Ensure figures match with any clients/intermediaries
   - Align with other public disclosures

### Compliance Best Practices

1. **Know the Code**
   - Ensure all staff understand Code of Conduct
   - Provide training on compliance
   - Have internal procedures

2. **Document Activities**
   - Keep records of meetings
   - Document policy positions
   - Track EU lobbying activities

3. **Review Regularly**
   - Internal annual review of registration
   - Check for needed updates
   - Verify continued accuracy

4. **Designate Responsibility**
   - Assign specific staff to manage registration
   - Clear accountability
   - Sufficient resources

### Communication Best Practices

1. **Proactive Disclosure**
   - Go beyond minimum requirements
   - Voluntary transparency
   - Publish additional information on your website

2. **Respond Promptly**
   - Reply to Secretariat queries quickly
   - Cooperate with investigations
   - Address complaints seriously

3. **Be Honest**
   - If you make a mistake, correct it
   - Don't try to hide errors
   - Transparency builds trust

## FAQs

### General Questions

**Q: Is registration mandatory?**
A: Registration is mandatory if you want to carry out certain activities with EU institutions (meeting officials, speaking at events, etc.). Registration itself is voluntary, but the conditionality principle makes it effectively mandatory for serious lobbying.

**Q: How much does registration cost?**
A: Registration is completely free. There are no fees.

**Q: How long does registration take?**
A: Initial registration approval typically takes 1-2 weeks after submission. Preparation time depends on how quickly you can gather required information.

**Q: Do I need a Brussels office to register?**
A: No. Registration is open to organisations worldwide. Physical presence in Brussels is not required.

**Q: Can individuals register?**
A: Yes. Self-employed consultants and individuals engaged in lobbying can register.

### Financial Disclosure Questions

**Q: What financial year should I use?**
A: Use your most recently closed financial year. For newly formed entities, provide estimates for current year.

**Q: Do I need an audit?**
A: No. Financial disclosures are "good faith estimates" and don't require formal auditing or conventional accounting standards.

**Q: What if I make a mistake in financial disclosure?**
A: Update your registration immediately with correct information. Honest mistakes are acceptable if corrected promptly.

**Q: How do I calculate "lobbying costs"?**
A: Calculate percentage of staff time on EU lobbying activities, apply to total staff costs and allocated overhead, add direct costs. Document your methodology.

### Activities Questions

**Q: Is responding to public consultations lobbying?**
A: No. Responding to official EU consultations doesn't require registration.

**Q: What about conferences and events?**
A: Organising events to influence EU policy requires registration. Simply attending does not.

**Q: Do social media activities count?**
A: Yes, if aimed at influencing EU policy through public opinion or digital campaigns.

**Q: What about academic research?**
A: Pure research doesn't require registration. Policy advocacy based on research does.

### Update Questions

**Q: How often must I update?**
A: Minimum once per year. Best practice is to update whenever significant changes occur.

**Q: What happens if I forget to update?**
A: You may receive a warning. Continued failure may lead to suspension.

**Q: Can I delete my registration?**
A: Yes. You can request removal if you no longer conduct covered activities. But you'll lose all conditionality benefits immediately.

### Access Questions

**Q: How many people can access our registration?**
A: Minimum 2, maximum 6 persons with EU Login accounts.

**Q: Can I change access persons?**
A: Yes. You can update the list of persons with access at any time through your registration.

**Q: What if we lose access?**
A: As long as one person on the access list has credentials, they can add others. Otherwise, contact the Secretariat.

### Code of Conduct Questions

**Q: What are common Code violations?**
A: Misrepresenting information, failing to update, inappropriate conduct with EU officials, dishonest information gathering.

**Q: Can complaints be anonymous?**
A: Yes, but complaints must include sufficient evidence regardless of source.

**Q: How long do investigations take?**
A: Varies by complexity. Simple cases may be resolved in weeks; complex investigations may take months.
