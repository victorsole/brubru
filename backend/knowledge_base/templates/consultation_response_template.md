# EU Consultation Response Template

Use this to draft a response to an EU consultation. First identify which kind it is, because the audience, the format and what happens next differ.

## 1. Which consultation is this?

| Kind | Where | Who responds | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call for evidence | Have Your Say (Commission) | Anyone | Early feedback on the problem, options and evidence, before a proposal |
| Public consultation | Have Your Say (Commission), usually a questionnaire | Anyone | Structured views on options; feeds the impact assessment |
| Feedback on an adopted proposal or draft act | Have Your Say (Commission) | Anyone | Comments on the text itself; summarised for Parliament and Council |
| First-stage social partner consultation (Article 154(2) TFEU) | Commission letter to the recognised EU social partners | Social partners | On the POSSIBLE DIRECTION of EU action in social policy |
| Second-stage social partner consultation (Article 154(3) TFEU) | Commission letter to the recognised EU social partners | Social partners | On the CONTENT of the envisaged proposal |
| Agency consultation (EBA, ESMA, EIOPA, EASA, ECHA, EMA, ACER, and others) | The agency's own site | Anyone, often industry | Technical standards, guidelines, opinions |

**Social partner consultations only:** at either stage the social partners may tell the Commission they wish to negotiate an agreement themselves (Article 154(4) TFEU); that process may not exceed nine months unless extended jointly with the Commission. An agreement can then be implemented by a Council decision on a Commission proposal (Article 155 TFEU). If you represent a social partner, say explicitly whether you want to negotiate.

## 2. Before you write
- Read the consultation document in full, including any annex listing the questions.
- Check the deadline and the time zone on the page. Responses after the deadline are usually not taken into account.
- Check the form's character limit and whether an attachment is accepted. Put the essentials in the text field; an attachment may not be read as closely.
- Organisations should give their EU Transparency Register ID. Contributions are normally published; say what, if anything, is confidential and why.

## 3. Structure

**Title:** Response of [organisation] to [exact consultation title], [reference if any]

**1. Who we are** (3 to 4 lines)
Organisation, sector, members or size, Member States covered, Transparency Register ID.

**2. Our position in brief** (3 bullets)
- What we support.
- What we oppose or want changed.
- The one change that matters most to us.

**3. Answers to the consultation questions**
Follow the consultation's own numbering. For each question: answer, reason, evidence.

**4. Evidence**
Data, cost estimates, case studies, surveys. Cite sources and dates. Quantify wherever possible; the Commission weighs evidence over assertion.

**5. Specific proposals**
Where a draft text exists, propose wording at article level:

| Provision | Current text | Proposed text | Justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Article X(y) | ... | ... | ... |

**6. Impact**
On SMEs, on specific Member States or regions, on administrative burden, on competitiveness. Say who bears the cost and when.

**7. Conclusion**
Restate the priority change and offer further input (contact person, availability for a meeting).

## 4. Quality checks
- Every claim is attributable (source, date) or clearly marked as the organisation's view.
- Answers use the consultation's own terms and numbering.
- No institutional jargon the reader must look up; spell out acronyms once.
- The position is consistent with anything the organisation has already published on the file.
- Coordinate with allies where useful: a coalition response carries more weight than several identical ones.

## 5. After submitting
- Save the confirmation and the submitted text.
- Track the file in My EU Bubble (My Tracked Files) to follow the proposal, and check the summary report of the consultation when the Commission publishes it.
- Use the same arguments, updated, for MEPs and the Council once a proposal is tabled: see the MEP briefing and position paper templates.
