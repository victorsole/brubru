## Guidance Notes

### ISC Process Overview

1. **Lead DG** launches ISC via Decide (internal decision-making system), sending the draft proposal to all associated DGs
2. **Associated DGs** have **10 working days** (standard) or **15 working days** (for major proposals) to submit contributions
3. Contributions submitted via **Ares** (document management system) or Decide
4. **Lead DG** reviews all contributions and either accepts changes or calls bilateral meetings to resolve disagreements
5. If disagreements persist, the matter is **escalated to cabinet level** (Hebdo or Special Chefs meeting)
6. Final text adopted by the **College of Commissioners** at its weekly meeting

### Tips for Effective ISC Contributions

- **Be specific**: Point to exact articles and propose alternative text, not just general concerns
- **Be constructive**: Offer solutions, not just objections
- **Be early**: Contact the lead DG informally before the ISC if you anticipate significant concerns
- **Prioritise**: Distinguish between essential comments (blocking) and desirable improvements
- **Cross-reference**: Cite your DG's own legal acts and policy positions to substantiate your arguments
- **Keep it concise**: ISC contributions should be actionable; lengthy policy analyses belong in separate notes

### Common Reasons for Disagreement

| Reason | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Competence overlap** | Two DGs both claim policy ownership of a provision |
| **Inconsistent definitions** | Proposal uses a term differently from your DG's acquis |
| **Proportionality** | Proposal imposes obligations disproportionate to the objective |
| **Implementation burden** | Proposal creates unrealistic reporting or enforcement requirements for your DG |
| **Missing consultation** | Lead DG did not informally consult your DG before ISC launch |
