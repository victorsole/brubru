# MiCA: The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation

**The First Comprehensive Regulatory Framework for Crypto-Assets, Stablecoins, and Crypto-Asset Service Providers**

**Author:** Victor Sole Ferioli, Founder & Director, Beresol

**Date:** February 2026

**Location:** Brussels, Belgium

---

## Executive Summary

The Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, or MiCA) is the EU's landmark regulatory framework for the crypto-asset industry. Published in the Official Journal on 9 June 2023, MiCA brings crypto-assets, their issuers, and crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) under a comprehensive regulatory framework for the first time, creating an EU-wide regime comparable in ambition to MiFID II for traditional financial instruments.

**Key Findings:**

- **Three-category classification:** MiCA distinguishes between utility tokens/other crypto-assets (lightest regime), asset-referenced tokens (ARTs -- medium regime), and e-money tokens (EMTs -- strictest regime, issued only by credit institutions or e-money institutions)
- **Phased application:** ART and EMT rules applied from 30 June 2024; full application (CASPs, market abuse, utility tokens) from 30 December 2024
- **CASP authorisation:** 10 categories of crypto-asset services require authorisation, with EU-wide passporting rights
- **Stablecoin regulation:** ARTs require reserve of assets backing, and significant ARTs/EMTs are supervised directly by EBA
- **Market abuse:** Insider dealing, market manipulation, and unlawful disclosure provisions mirror the MAR framework for traditional securities
- **Grandfathering:** Existing CASPs may continue operating under national law for up to 18 months (until 1 July 2026 maximum)
- **Penalties:** Up to EUR 15 million or 15% of annual turnover for market abuse violations

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

The explosive growth of crypto-assets -- from Bitcoin and Ethereum to stablecoins and utility tokens -- created a regulatory vacuum in the EU. Before MiCA, crypto-assets that did not qualify as financial instruments under MiFID II or electronic money under EMD2 fell outside the EU's regulatory perimeter entirely. Some Member States (France, Malta, Germany) developed national frameworks, but this created a fragmented landscape with regulatory arbitrage risks.

MiCA was proposed by the European Commission in September 2020 as part of the Digital Finance Package, alongside DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act) and a pilot regime for DLT-based market infrastructures. After nearly three years of negotiation, MiCA was adopted in May 2023 -- making the EU the first major jurisdiction to implement a comprehensive crypto-asset regulatory framework.

### 1.2 What MiCA Changes

MiCA creates a **single EU-wide authorisation regime** for crypto-asset service providers, eliminating the need for CASPs to obtain separate licences in each Member State. It establishes disclosure requirements for all crypto-asset issuers (through mandatory white papers), prudential requirements for stablecoin issuers (through reserve-of-assets rules), and market integrity rules (through a crypto-specific market abuse framework).

## 3. Scope and Exclusions

### 3.1 In-Scope Entities

| Entity Type | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) | Legal persons providing crypto-asset services professionally |
| ART Issuers | Entities issuing asset-referenced tokens to the public |
| EMT Issuers | Credit institutions or e-money institutions issuing e-money tokens |
| Token Offerors | Natural or legal persons offering crypto-assets to the public |
| Persons Seeking Admission to Trading | Entities seeking admission of crypto-assets to a trading platform |

### 3.2 Key Exclusions (Article 2(4))

- Crypto-assets qualifying as financial instruments under MiFID II
- Deposits (including structured deposits)
- Funds under PSD2 (except e-money tokens)
- Securitisation positions
- Insurance/pension products
- The ECB and national central banks (when acting as monetary authorities)
- Fully decentralised services without any intermediary

**Important note:** Where crypto-assets have no identifiable issuer (e.g., Bitcoin), Titles II, III, and IV do not apply -- but CASPs dealing in such assets must still be authorised.

## 4. Crypto-Asset Classification

MiCA defines three categories:

| Category | Key Characteristic | Regulatory Burden |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Utility Tokens / Other Crypto-Assets** | Provide digital access to a good or service on DLT; catch-all for non-ART, non-EMT crypto-assets | Light: white paper + notification |
| **Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs)** | Maintain stable value by referencing another value, right, or combination (including fiat currencies) | Medium: authorisation + reserve of assets + prudential requirements |
| **E-Money Tokens (EMTs)** | Maintain stable value by referencing exactly one official (fiat) currency | Heavy: only credit institutions or e-money institutions may issue; dual compliance with EMD2 |

### 4.1 "Significant" Classification

Both ARTs and EMTs can be classified as "significant" by EBA, triggering enhanced supervision:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Customer base | >10 million holders |
| Value issued / market cap | >EUR 5 billion |
| Number of transactions | >10 million per day |
| Aggregate value of transactions | >EUR 5 billion per day |
| Cross-border use | >7 Member States |
| Interconnectedness | Significant links to the financial system |

Significant ARTs/EMTs are supervised **directly by EBA** rather than national competent authorities.

## 5. Utility Tokens and Other Crypto-Assets (Title II, Articles 4-15)

The lightest regulatory tier. Issuers must:

- **Publish a Crypto-Asset White Paper (CA-WP)** containing: project description, issuer details, rights and obligations, underlying technology, risks, and environmental impact of the consensus mechanism
- **Notify the NCA** at least 20 working days before publication
- Ensure **marketing communications** are fair, clear, not misleading, and consistent with the CA-WP
- Provide a **14-day right of withdrawal** for retail holders (Article 13)
- Accept **liability** for damage caused by incomplete, unfair, or misleading CA-WP information

**Exemptions:** Offers to fewer than 150 persons per Member State; offers totalling less than EUR 1 million over 12 months; offers exclusively to qualified investors; crypto-assets offered free of charge.

## 6. Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs) (Title III, Articles 16-47)

### 6.1 Authorisation

ART issuers must obtain authorisation from their NCA before offering ARTs to the public or seeking admission to trading. Credit institutions may issue ARTs without separate authorisation but must notify the NCA at least 90 working days before first issuance.

### 6.2 Key Obligations

| Obligation | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Reserve of assets** | Must fully back the token value at all times; invested only in low-risk, liquid financial instruments |
| **Reserve custody** | Held by credit institutions or authorised CASPs in segregated arrangements |
| **Own funds** | Higher of EUR 350,000 or 2% of average reserve (3% for significant ARTs) |
| **Redemption rights** | Permanent right of redemption at market value of referenced assets |
| **Governance** | Clear organisational structure with defined responsibilities |
| **Conflicts of interest** | Policies to identify, prevent, manage, and disclose conflicts |
| **No interest** | Issuers and CASPs cannot grant interest related to the holding period |
| **Recovery/redemption plans** | Required for significant ARTs |

### 6.3 Significant ARTs

When an ART crosses the significance thresholds, supervision transfers to EBA. Additional requirements include enhanced own funds (3% of reserve), stricter liquidity management, and recovery/redemption plans. If daily transactions exceed EUR 5 million or 1 million transactions, EBA may restrict further issuance.

---

## 7. E-Money Tokens (EMTs) (Title IV, Articles 48-58)

### 7.1 Issuer Eligibility

Only **credit institutions** or authorised **electronic money institutions** may issue EMTs. This is the strictest issuer requirement under MiCA.

### 7.2 Key Obligations

| Obligation | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Redemption at par** | Holders have a right to redeem at par value in the referenced fiat currency at any time |
| **No interest** | No interest or benefits related to the holding period |
| **Dual compliance** | EMTs are deemed electronic money under Directive 2009/110/EC |
| **Safeguarding** | Funds received must be safeguarded per E-Money Directive rules |
| **Significant EMTs** | At least 60% of reserves in bank deposits spread across multiple credit institutions |
| **Own funds (significant)** | Higher of EUR 350,000, 2% of average funds received, or a quarter of fixed overheads |

## 8. Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) (Title V, Articles 59-85)

### 8.1 The 10 Crypto-Asset Services

| Service | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Custody and administration | Safekeeping of crypto-assets on behalf of clients |
| Operation of a trading platform | Operating a multilateral system for crypto-asset trading |
| Exchange: crypto-to-fiat | Fiat-to-crypto exchange services |
| Exchange: crypto-to-crypto | Crypto-to-crypto exchange services |
| Execution of orders | Concluding agreements to buy/sell on behalf of clients |
| Placing of crypto-assets | Marketing newly issued crypto-assets to purchasers |
| Reception and transmission of orders | Receiving and transmitting orders on behalf of clients |
| Providing advice | Personalised recommendations on crypto-assets |
| Portfolio management | Discretionary management of portfolios containing crypto-assets |
| Transfer services | Transferring crypto-assets between DLT addresses on behalf of clients |

### 8.2 Authorisation Process

- NCA acknowledges receipt within 5 working days
- Assesses completeness within 25 working days
- Decides within 40 working days (extendable to 90)
- Once authorised, **EU-wide passporting** is available

### 8.3 General Obligations

- Minimum own funds or insurance policy covering liabilities (Article 67)
- Segregation of client crypto-assets and funds from CASP's own assets (Article 70)
- ICT security and business continuity (DORA compliance) (Article 68)
- Free complaints handling procedure (Article 71)
- Conflicts of interest policies (Article 72)

### 8.4 Entities Exempt from Separate Authorisation

Credit institutions, CSDs, investment firms, market operators, e-money institutions, UCITS management companies, and AIFMs may provide crypto-asset services by **notifying** their NCA at least 40 working days before commencing, without separate CASP authorisation.

## 9. Market Abuse Prevention (Title VI, Articles 86-92)

MiCA establishes a crypto-specific market abuse framework mirroring the Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) for traditional securities:

| Prohibition | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Inside information** | Issuers must publicly disclose inside information; unlawful disclosure prohibited |
| **Insider dealing** | Prohibited: using inside information to acquire or dispose of crypto-assets |
| **Market manipulation** | Prohibited: wash trading, spoofing, layering, pump-and-dump schemes |

Title VI applies immediately to all crypto-assets admitted to trading, with **no grandfathering period**.

## 10. The Role of EBA and ESMA

### 10.1 EBA

- **Direct supervision** of significant ART and EMT issuers
- Determines significance thresholds and classifications
- Establishes supervisory colleges for significant token issuers
- Direct fining power over significant ART/EMT issuers
- Develops RTS/ITS/guidelines for ARTs, EMTs, reserves, own funds, and stress testing

### 10.2 ESMA

- Maintains the **public register of all authorised CASPs** in the EU
- Ensures consistent authorisation and supervision practices across NCAs
- Coordinates enforcement of market abuse provisions
- Develops RTS/ITS for CASPs, trading platforms, and transparency requirements
- Product intervention powers (temporary prohibition/restriction of crypto services)
- Manages the cross-border CASP passporting notification framework

## 12. Penalties and Enforcement

### For Legal Persons

| Infringement | Maximum Fine |
|-------------|-------------|
| Title II (utility token/CA-WP rules) | EUR 700,000 or 5% of annual turnover |
| Title III (ART rules) | EUR 5,000,000 or 12.5% of annual turnover |
| Title IV (EMT rules) | EUR 5,000,000 or 12.5% of annual turnover |
| Title V (CASP rules) | EUR 5,000,000 or 12.5% of annual turnover |
| **Title VI (market abuse)** | **EUR 15,000,000 or 15% of annual turnover** |

### For Natural Persons

| Infringement | Maximum Fine |
|-------------|-------------|
| Title II violations | EUR 500,000 |
| Title III/IV/V violations | EUR 700,000 |
| **Title VI (market abuse)** | **EUR 5,000,000** |

NCAs may also impose public statements, cease-and-desist orders, temporary bans from management functions, and withdrawal of authorisation.

## 13. Transitional Provisions and Grandfathering

### CASP Grandfathering (Article 143(3))

CASPs providing services under national law before 30 December 2024 may continue for up to 18 months (until 1 July 2026), or until MiCA authorisation is granted/refused. Member States may shorten this period.

**Grandfathering periods by Member State:**

| Period | Member States |
|--------|---------------|
| Full 18 months (until 1 July 2026) | France, Malta, Luxembourg, Estonia, Belgium, Cyprus, Romania |
| 12 months (until 31 December 2025) | Germany, Austria, Ireland, Greece, Spain, Liechtenstein |
| 6 months (until 30 June 2025) | Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Hungary, Netherlands, Poland, Slovenia |

**Limitations:** Grandfathered CASPs are **not MiCA-authorised** and **cannot passport** to other Member States. Title VI (market abuse) and the Transfer of Funds Regulation apply immediately with no grandfathering.

## 15. Practical Implications

### 15.1 For Crypto Exchanges and Service Providers

- Must obtain CASP authorisation or rely on grandfathering until July 2026 maximum
- Segregation of client assets is mandatory from day one
- DORA compliance for ICT systems and cybersecurity
- Market abuse surveillance systems must be in place

### 15.2 For Stablecoin Issuers

- ARTs require full authorisation and reserve-of-assets backing
- EMTs can only be issued by credit institutions or e-money institutions
- Significant stablecoins face direct EBA supervision
- Interest payments on stablecoins are prohibited

### 15.3 For Traditional Financial Institutions

- Banks, investment firms, and e-money institutions can enter the crypto market through simplified notification (no separate CASP authorisation needed)
- Provides regulatory clarity for offering crypto custody, trading, and advisory services
- Must comply with both MiCA and their existing prudential frameworks

### 15.4 For Fund Managers and Asset Managers

- MiCA intersects with DORA for ICT risk management
- Crypto-asset investments in fund portfolios may trigger MiCA disclosure requirements
- ART/EMT holdings require understanding of reserve backing and redemption mechanisms

---

## 16. Conclusions

MiCA represents a watershed moment for the global crypto-asset industry. By creating the first comprehensive regulatory framework for crypto-assets, the EU has established a template that other jurisdictions are watching closely. The regulation balances innovation with investor protection, creating clear rules for market participants while preserving the possibility for new business models to emerge.

The phased application approach -- with stablecoin rules taking effect six months before the full framework -- reflects the particular systemic importance of stablecoins to financial stability. The market abuse provisions, applying immediately with no grandfathering, signal the EU's zero-tolerance approach to market manipulation in crypto markets.

For EU policy professionals, MiCA is now the central reference point for all discussions about digital assets in the European Union -- and its influence on the global regulatory landscape is already significant.
