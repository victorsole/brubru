# US-EU AI Regulatory Divergence: Executive Order 14365 vs the EU AI Act

**Comparative Analysis of the Trump Administration's AI National Policy Framework and the European Union's AI Act**

**Author:** Victor Sole Ferioli, Founder & Director, Beresol

**Date:** February 2026

**Location:** Brussels, Belgium

---

## Executive Summary

On 11 December 2025, President Trump signed Executive Order 14365, titled "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence", which represents the most significant US federal action on AI regulation to date. The EO aims to preempt state AI laws through indirect mechanisms -- DOJ litigation, federal funding restrictions, and agency rulemaking -- rather than through direct federal legislation.

This analysis compares the US approach with the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) and examines the implications of the widening transatlantic regulatory divergence.

**Key Findings:**

- **Opposite directions:** The US moves toward deregulation and federal preemption of protective state measures; the EU maintains comprehensive binding regulation with mandatory risk classification
- **Indirect preemption:** The EO cannot directly preempt state law (only Congress can). It uses BEAD broadband funding conditions, a DOJ litigation task force, and FTC/FCC rulemaking as coercive mechanisms
- **Legal challenges expected:** 36 state attorneys general sent a bipartisan letter opposing federal preemption; California and Colorado AGs have threatened litigation
- **Brussels Effect prevails:** US companies serving EU markets must still comply with the AI Act regardless of domestic deregulation. The EU's extraterritorial scope means US deregulation does not exempt US companies from EU obligations
- **Brubru opportunity:** The widening divergence creates massive demand for EU regulatory intelligence tools

## 2. Key Provisions

### 2.1 AI Litigation Task Force (Section 3)

The Attorney General must establish a task force (by 10 January 2026) to challenge state AI laws deemed inconsistent with federal policy. Grounds include:
- Unconstitutional regulation of interstate commerce (Dormant Commerce Clause)
- Preemption by existing federal regulations
- Other constitutional deficiencies

### 2.2 Evaluation of State AI Laws (Section 4)

The Secretary of Commerce must publish within 90 days (by 11 March 2026) an evaluation identifying "onerous" state AI laws, specifically laws that:
- Require AI models to alter their truthful outputs
- Compel disclosures violating the First Amendment

### 2.3 Federal Funding Restrictions (Section 5)

States with "onerous" AI laws become ineligible for non-deployment funds under the BEAD Program (approximately $21 billion in broadband funding). Federal agencies must condition discretionary grants on states not enforcing conflicting AI laws.

### 2.4 FCC and FTC Actions (Sections 6-7)

- **FCC:** Must initiate proceedings to adopt a federal AI reporting/disclosure standard preempting state laws
- **FTC:** Must issue a policy statement explaining when state laws requiring alterations to "truthful AI outputs" are preempted by the FTC Act

### 2.5 Legislative Recommendations (Section 8)

The Special Advisor for AI and Crypto must prepare a legislative recommendation for a uniform federal AI framework. **Carved out from preemption:** child safety protections, AI compute infrastructure, and state government procurement of AI.

## 3. State AI Laws Under Threat

### 3.1 The State AI Landscape

- All 50 states introduced AI bills during 2025
- Over 160 state AI laws tracked by Orrick's US AI law tracker
- 38 states enacted AI laws in 2025 specifically addressing generative AI
- Over 500 AI bills proposed in Q1 2025 alone

### 3.2 Key State Laws in the Crosshairs

| State Law | Effective Date | Key Provisions |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| Colorado AI Act (SB24-205) | 30 June 2026 | Bias audits, prohibition of algorithmic discrimination in high-risk AI |
| California SB 53 | 1 January 2026 | Large AI developers must publish safety plans for "catastrophic risk" |
| California CCPA ADMT Regulations | In force | Automated decision-making technology regulations |
| Illinois HB 3773 | 1 January 2026 | Prohibits AI discrimination in employment decisions |

The EO explicitly singles out the Colorado AI Act, claiming its ban on "algorithmic discrimination" may "force AI models to produce false results."

### 3.3 Legal Vulnerabilities of the EO

- **Supremacy Clause:** Only an act of Congress can preempt state law -- an executive order cannot
- **NFIB v. Sebelius (2012):** The funding-conditioned-on-compliance mechanism may be unconstitutionally coercive (analogous to the Medicaid expansion case)
- **Tenth Amendment:** States retain reserved powers to regulate within their borders
- **36 state AGs' letter** (25 November 2025): Bipartisan coalition opposing federal preemption
- **Bipartisan opposition:** Florida's Republican Governor DeSantis stated "an executive order doesn't/can't preempt state legislative action"

## 4. US vs EU: A Structural Comparison

| Dimension | US Executive Order | EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Legal Nature** | Executive order (agency directive) | Binding EU regulation, directly applicable |
| **Core Philosophy** | "Minimally burdensome" -- innovation first | Rights-based -- fundamental rights protection |
| **Regulatory Approach** | Deregulatory -- prevent regulation | Comprehensive -- create obligations |
| **Risk Framework** | None | Four-tier (unacceptable/high/limited/minimal) |
| **Prohibited Practices** | None | Social scoring, manipulative AI, biometric surveillance |
| **Algorithmic Discrimination** | Frames anti-discrimination as "forcing false outputs" | Mandates high-quality datasets to minimise bias |
| **Transparency** | Opposes compelled disclosure (First Amendment) | Mandatory documentation, conformity assessments |
| **Enforcement** | DOJ task force to attack state laws | EU AI Office + national authorities; fines up to 7% revenue |
| **Scope** | Domestic (federal vs. state) | Extraterritorial -- any AI used in the EU market |
| **International Stance** | Unilateral -- "US dominance" | Multilateral -- aligned with OECD principles |
| **Children** | Carved out from preemption | Included in prohibitions (emotion recognition) |
| **Data Governance** | Not addressed | Mandatory training dataset quality requirements |

### 4.1 The Philosophical Gulf

- **EU:** The AI Act is rooted in the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights. Protects human dignity, non-discrimination, privacy. Conformity assessments required before deployment.
- **US:** The EO frames AI rights through the First Amendment (freedom of speech of AI developers) and commerce (interstate trade). Anti-discrimination requirements are characterised as "ideological bias" and "censorship."

### 4.2 The Enforcement Asymmetry

- **EU:** Centralised oversight via EU AI Office and European AI Board, complemented by national authorities. Fines up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global turnover.
- **US:** No centralised AI regulator. The EO creates an offensive mechanism (DOJ task force to challenge state laws) rather than a compliance mechanism. No fines for AI misuse at federal level.

## 5. The Brussels Effect

The "Brussels Effect" -- where EU regulation becomes the de facto global standard because companies prefer uniform compliance over fragmented approaches -- is likely to prevail:

- **Extraterritorial scope:** The EU AI Act applies to any entity that places an AI system on the EU market or whose AI system's output is used in the EU, regardless of where the entity is established
- **450 million consumers:** The EU market is too large for US companies to abandon
- **Precedent:** GDPR became the de facto global privacy standard through the same mechanism
- **Compliance logic:** Companies building for global markets will adopt EU-compatible standards as their baseline

As analysts note: "When American AI giants inevitably choose EU compliance over fragmented regulatory strategies, the US deregulatory AI agenda clashes against 450 million European consumers' rights."

## 6. Industry and Political Reactions

### 6.1 Industry Support

- **ITI (Information Technology Industry Council):** "ITI welcomes President Trump's Executive Order to advance a federal AI framework and prevent a patchwork of competing state AI regulations"
- **Big tech companies** broadly lobbied for federal preemption, arguing multi-state compliance burdens hinder innovation
- **Article III Project** (conservative legal org) praised the EO as creating "a single, constitutional framework"

### 6.2 State and Civil Society Opposition

- **36 state attorneys general** (bipartisan) urged Congress to reject federal preemption (25 November 2025)
- **California AG Rob Bonta:** Will "examine the legality or potential illegality" of the EO
- **Florida Governor DeSantis (R):** "An executive order doesn't/can't preempt state legislative action"
- **Colorado AG Phil Weiser** and California Senator **Scott Wiener:** Threatened litigation
- Consumer rights advocates warned about unregulated harms including "discriminatory AI price-fixing"

### 6.3 Legal Expert Consensus

- **White & Case:** "Executive Order 14365 would likely not independently displace state AI laws"
- **Alston & Bird:** The EO "does not purport to preempt state law. Instead, it adopts various indirect measures"
- **Seyfarth Shaw:** "Employers should anticipate a prolonged period of legal uncertainty"
- **Multiple firms recommend:** Continue complying with state AI laws until there is greater clarity

## 7. The TRUMP AMERICA AI Act

In January 2026, Senator Marsha Blackburn introduced the "TRUMP AMERICA AI Act" (The Republic Unifying Meritocratic Performance Advancing Machine Intelligence by Eliminating Regulatory Interstate Chaos Across American Industry Act), which seeks to codify the EO into law.

**Key provisions:**
- Federal preemption of state frontier AI catastrophic risk laws
- Preemption of state digital replica laws
- Child safety duty of care on AI developers
- Federal right to sue for AI training on personal/copyrighted data without consent
- AI-generated derivative works without authorisation deemed infringing
- Training Data Use Records and Inference Data Use Records transparency requirements

**Irony noted by analysts:** The bill's "most striking feature is the gap between its stated deregulatory purpose and its actual regulatory density" (Jones Walker). Despite anti-regulation rhetoric, the bill creates significant new federal compliance obligations.

## 8. Impact on Transatlantic Companies

| Company Type | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| US-only AI startups | May benefit from reduced regulatory burden if state laws are preempted |
| US Big Tech (Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI) | Still must comply with EU AI Act for EU operations |
| EU AI companies | Subject to EU AI Act; US EO has no direct impact |
| Transatlantic enterprises | Must maintain parallel compliance programmes; highest complexity |
| AI deployers (banks, healthcare, HR tech) | Must navigate state + EU requirements until clarity emerges |

**Strategic recommendations from major law firms:**
- Continue complying with state AI laws until resolution
- Proactively align with EU AI Act requirements ahead of August 2026 enforcement
- Voluntarily adhere to the GPAI Code of Practice for both compliance and reputational benefits
- Monitor the AI Litigation Task Force for precedent-setting actions

## 9. Implications for EU Policy Professionals

### 9.1 Increased Demand for EU Regulatory Intelligence

The widening US-EU divergence creates massive demand for tools and services that help organisations understand and comply with EU AI regulation. Companies operating in both jurisdictions need sophisticated compliance and monitoring tools.

### 9.2 AI Act Implementation Phase

As the EU AI Act enters implementation (high-risk obligations August 2026, product safety August 2027), there will be extensive delegated acts, codes of practice, and guidance documents. The ability to track, analyse, and amend these texts is critical for stakeholders.

### 9.3 Comparative AI Regulatory Tracking

A new category of analysis is emerging: comparative AI regulatory tracking across jurisdictions. EU policy professionals who understand both frameworks will be in high demand.

### 9.4 Public Consultations

The EU's "Have Your Say" portal will be active with AI-related consultations as the AI Act is implemented. AI companies needing to comply with EU rules will want to shape them through consultation responses.
