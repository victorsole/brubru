# EC Targeted Consultation on Venture and Growth Capital Funds Reform

**Analysis of the European Commission's Review of EuVECA, AIFMD Proportionality, and Cross-Border Fundraising Barriers**

**Author:** Victor Sole Ferioli, Founder & Director, Beresol

**Date:** February 2026

**Location:** Brussels, Belgium

---

## Executive Summary

On 15 January 2026, the European Commission launched a targeted consultation on venture and growth capital funds reform, with a response deadline of **12 March 2026**. This consultation directly addresses the structural barriers that keep EU venture capital at approximately one-sixth of US levels relative to GDP.

The consultation reviews three core areas: the **EuVECA Regulation** (Regulation (EU) No 345/2013), **AIFMD proportionality** for smaller fund managers, and **cross-border fundraising** obstacles. Legislative proposals are expected in **Q3 2026**, meaning responses submitted by March 2026 will shape regulations affecting European venture capital for the next decade.

**Key Findings:**

- **Threshold review:** The consultation asks whether the EUR 500M AuM threshold triggering full AIFMD licensing is still appropriate, with options ranging up to EUR 10B
- **EuVECA flexibility:** Proposals to expand EuVECA investment flexibility to include venture debt, post-IPO shareholdings, and fund-of-funds structures
- **Cross-border barriers:** Divergent national implementations undermine passport benefits; cumulative host authority fees erode smaller funds
- **Pension fund allocation gap:** EU pension funds allocate 0.02-0.12% to VC versus 1.9-2% in the US; unlocking this could release EUR 124 billion
- **EuSEF assessment:** The consultation asks whether the European Social Entrepreneurship Funds regime should be repealed due to poor uptake

## 2. Background: EU VC Market Gap

### The scale of the problem

- EU venture capital investment runs at approximately **one-sixth** of US levels relative to GDP
- American firms raise **7x more** in VC than European counterparts
- European pension funds allocate **0.02-0.12%** to venture capital versus **1.9-2%** in the US
- Two-thirds of EU startup exits through IPOs occur **outside the EU**
- The EU has 3x fewer unicorns per capita than the US

### Root causes identified

The Civitta study commissioned by DG FISMA identified several structural barriers:

1. **Regulatory costs:** Reporting obligations, supervision fees, registration expenses, and depositary requirements consume resources that could fund portfolio company growth
2. **Fragmented markets:** 27 national regulatory regimes despite passporting frameworks
3. **Threshold cliff-edge:** The EUR 500M AIFMD threshold creates binary regulatory jumps that discourage fund managers from scaling
4. **Limited investment flexibility:** EuVECA restrictions on qualifying investments limit fund strategies
5. **Institutional investor barriers:** Pension fund allocation restrictions and lack of harmonised rules

## 3. Key Questions Asked

The consultation asks detailed questions across six thematic areas:

### 3.1 Scope and definitions

- Should the scope of EuVECA qualifying investments be expanded?
- Should EuVECA funds be allowed to invest in venture debt, mezzanine financing, post-IPO shareholdings?
- Should fund-of-funds and master-feeder structures be permitted?

### 3.2 Thresholds and proportionality

- Is the EUR 500M AuM threshold for full AIFMD licensing still appropriate?
- Options presented: EUR 500M (unchanged), EUR 1B, EUR 2B, EUR 5B, EUR 10B
- Should there be graduated compliance requirements based on AuM?

### 3.3 Cross-border operations

- Do divergent national implementations undermine EuVECA passport benefits?
- Should a "full-scope management passport" be created for registered AIFMs?
- Should host authority fees be harmonised or capped?

### 3.4 Depositary requirements

- Are depositary costs proportionate for smaller funds?
- Should a simplified depositary regime exist for funds below EUR 250M AuM?

### 3.5 Institutional investor access

- Should the IORPs Directive be reformed to enable greater VC allocations?
- Should Solvency II capital charges for VC investments be reduced?

### 3.6 EuSEF future

- Should the European Social Entrepreneurship Funds (EuSEF) regime be repealed?
- If maintained, should it be merged with an expanded EuVECA framework?

## 4. AIFMD Threshold Review

The EUR 500M AIFMD threshold, set in 2011, determines when a fund manager must obtain full AIFMD authorisation (with associated costs, reporting, and compliance burden). Below this threshold, managers can operate under a simplified registered regime.

### Arguments for raising the threshold

- **Inflation:** EUR 500M in 2011 is approximately EUR 620M in 2026 purchasing power
- **Market evolution:** Typical VC fund sizes have grown significantly since 2011
- **Cost burden:** Full AIFMD compliance costs EUR 200,000-500,000 annually, disproportionately affecting smaller managers
- **Competitive disadvantage:** US VC managers face no comparable threshold

### Graduated approach (emerging consensus)

Rather than a binary threshold, the consultation explores graduated compliance:

| AuM Range | Compliance Level |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Below EUR 500M | Registered (current light-touch regime) |
| EUR 500M - EUR 2B | Enhanced registered (some AIFMD obligations) |
| Above EUR 2B | Full AIFMD authorisation |

This would reduce "cliff-edge effects" when managers cross thresholds and remove disincentives to scale.

## 5. EuVECA Modernisation

The European Venture Capital Fund (EuVECA) regulation, adopted in 2013, created a passport for smaller VC fund managers. However, uptake has been limited due to restrictive qualifying investment criteria.

### Current restrictions

- Limited to "qualifying portfolio undertakings" (primarily unlisted SMEs)
- Cannot hold post-IPO positions
- No venture debt or mezzanine financing
- No fund-of-funds structures
- No master-feeder arrangements

### Proposed expansions

| Feature | Current | Proposed |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Post-IPO holdings | Not permitted | 24-month holding period after IPO |
| Venture debt | Not permitted | Permitted as qualifying investment |
| Mezzanine financing | Not permitted | Permitted |
| Fund-of-funds | Not permitted | Permitted |
| Master-feeder | Not permitted | Permitted |

### Rationale

Allowing post-IPO holdings would align manager and LP interests during the critical transition period and remove artificial pressure to exit successful companies. Venture debt expands the toolkit available to smaller managers.

## 6. Cross-Border Fundraising

### Current barriers

- **Divergent national implementations:** Despite the EuVECA passport, national registration processes, fees, and requirements vary significantly
- **Host authority fees:** Cumulative fees from multiple host jurisdictions erode smaller funds' economics
- **Marketing notification delays:** Some NCAs take weeks to process cross-border marketing notifications
- **Language requirements:** Documentation requirements in local languages add costs

### Proposed solutions

- **Full-scope management passport** for registered AIFMs (not just marketing passport)
- **Harmonised fee schedule** or cap on host authority fees
- **Standardised notification procedure** with maximum processing times
- **English-language documentation** accepted across all jurisdictions

## 7. Institutional Investor Mobilisation

### The allocation gap

| Investor Type | EU Allocation to VC | US Allocation to VC |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Pension funds | 0.02-0.12% | 1.9-2.0% |
| Insurance companies | Minimal (Solvency II barriers) | Moderate |
| Sovereign wealth | Varies by country | Active participants |

### Potential impact

If EU pension funds reached just 10% allocation to alternatives (VC, PE, infrastructure), this would unlock approximately **EUR 124 billion** in new capital for European venture and growth companies.

### Regulatory barriers

- **IORPs Directive:** Fragmented national transposition creates "considerable heterogeneity" in what pension funds can invest in
- **Solvency II:** Capital charges for equity investments (including VC) discourage insurance company participation
- **National restrictions:** Several Member States impose additional limitations on institutional investor allocations

## 8. EuSEF Assessment

The European Social Entrepreneurship Funds (EuSEF) regulation, adopted alongside EuVECA in 2013, has seen minimal uptake. As of 2026, fewer than 30 EuSEF funds exist across the EU.

### Consultation options

1. **Repeal:** Remove the EuSEF framework entirely
2. **Merge:** Fold EuSEF social enterprise objectives into an expanded EuVECA with optional impact/sustainability classification
3. **Reform:** Maintain but modernise the EuSEF framework

### Emerging view

The merger option appears to have the most support: rather than maintaining a separate underutilised framework, integrate social enterprise investment objectives into a broader, more flexible EuVECA.

## 9. Connection to Draghi and Letta Reports

### Draghi Report (September 2024)

The Draghi report on EU competitiveness explicitly identified fragmented capital markets and insufficient venture capital as key barriers to European innovation. It recommended:

- Accelerating Capital Markets Union
- Harmonising insolvency frameworks
- Creating pan-European investment products
- Mobilising institutional investor capital

### Letta Report (April 2024)

The Letta report on the future of the Single Market recommended:

- Deepening the Single Market for financial services
- Reducing barriers to cross-border investment
- Creating a genuine EU savings and investment union

### Connection to this consultation

This consultation is a direct response to both reports. The Commission's review of EuVECA, AIFMD thresholds, and cross-border barriers implements the specific recommendations on venture capital from both the Draghi and Letta reports.

## 10. Stakeholder Positions

### Industry associations

- **Invest Europe:** Supports threshold increase and EuVECA modernisation; advocates for graduated compliance
- **ASCRI (SpainCap):** Supports cross-border harmonisation and pension fund mobilisation
- **France Invest:** Supports EuVECA flexibility expansion, particularly venture debt

### Fund managers

- Broad support for raising the AIFMD threshold (most favour EUR 1-2B)
- Strong support for EuVECA investment flexibility expansion
- Concerns about depositary costs for smaller funds
- Mixed views on EuSEF: some support repeal, others prefer merger with EuVECA

### Institutional investors

- European pension funds generally open to increased VC allocations but cite regulatory barriers
- Insurance companies highlight Solvency II capital charges as main obstacle
