# SFDR and SFDR 2.0: The EU's Sustainable Finance Disclosure Framework

**From Light Green/Dark Green to Transition/ESG Basics/Sustainable -- How the EU is Reshaping ESG Product Classification**

**Author:** Victor Sole Ferioli, Founder & Director, Beresol

**Date:** February 2026

**Location:** Brussels, Belgium

---

## Executive Summary

The Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088, or SFDR) has been the cornerstone of the EU's sustainable finance framework since March 2021. By requiring financial market participants to disclose how they integrate sustainability risks and consider adverse sustainability impacts, SFDR created the now-ubiquitous Article 6/8/9 product classification that reshaped the European investment fund landscape. However, widespread misuse of these categories as de facto product labels -- rather than mere transparency tools -- led the European Commission to propose a major overhaul in November 2025: **SFDR 2.0**.

**Key Findings:**

- **SFDR 1.0** applies to all financial market participants in the EU (asset managers, insurers, pension funds, investment firms) with entity-level and product-level disclosure requirements
- **Article 8 and Article 9** classifications became market labels despite being designed as transparency mechanisms -- over EUR 5 trillion in assets were classified under Article 8 alone
- **Principal Adverse Impacts (PAIs)** reporting remains complex, with 18 mandatory indicators and additional optional ones
- **SFDR 2.0** (COM(2025)841, procedure 2025/0361(COD)) proposes replacing Article 6/8/9 with three new mandatory categories: **Transition** (Art. 7), **ESG Basics** (Art. 8), and **Sustainable** (Art. 9)
- All three new categories require a **70% minimum investment threshold**, mandatory exclusions, and indicator-based measurement
- SFDR 2.0 is expected to apply around **2028**, with 18 months after entry into force
- The EU Taxonomy Regulation (2020/852) provides the underlying classification of environmentally sustainable activities that SFDR references

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Context

The EU's sustainable finance agenda, launched with the 2018 Action Plan on Financing Sustainable Growth, aims to redirect capital flows towards sustainable investments. SFDR is one of the three pillars of this framework, alongside the EU Taxonomy Regulation and the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD):

- **CSRD** requires companies to report on their sustainability impacts
- **EU Taxonomy** classifies which economic activities are environmentally sustainable
- **SFDR** requires financial market participants to disclose how their products integrate sustainability

Together, these form a disclosure chain: companies report sustainability data (CSRD), activities are classified (Taxonomy), and financial products disclose their sustainability characteristics (SFDR).

### 1.2 The Problem SFDR Was Designed to Solve

Before SFDR, there was no harmonised approach to sustainability disclosures for financial products across the EU. This led to greenwashing, where products claimed sustainability credentials without standardised evidence, and fragmentation, where different Member States applied different disclosure standards.

## 2. SFDR 1.0: The Current Framework

### 2.1 Legal Identification

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Full title** | Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 November 2019 on sustainability-related disclosures in the financial services sector |
| **CELEX number** | 32019R2088 |
| **OJ reference** | OJ L 317, 9.12.2019, p. 1-16 |
| **Application date** | 10 March 2021 |
| **Level 2 RTS** | Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1288, applicable from 1 January 2023 |

### 2.2 Scope: Who Must Comply

SFDR applies to **financial market participants (FMPs)** and **financial advisers**:

**Financial market participants:**
- Insurance undertakings offering insurance-based investment products (IBIPs)
- Investment firms providing portfolio management
- Institutions for occupational retirement provision (IORPs)
- UCITS management companies
- Alternative investment fund managers (AIFMs)
- Pan-European Personal Pension Product (PEPP) providers
- Venture capital (EuVECA) and social entrepreneurship (EuSEF) fund managers
- Credit institutions providing portfolio management

**Financial advisers:**
- Insurance intermediaries providing advice on IBIPs
- Investment firms providing investment advice
- Credit institutions providing investment advice

### 2.3 The Article 6/8/9 Classification

Although designed as a transparency regime, SFDR's product-level articles became the de facto labelling system for ESG funds in Europe:

**Article 6 -- Sustainability Risk Integration (All Products)**
- Applies to all financial products
- Must disclose how sustainability risks are integrated into investment decisions
- Must describe the likely impacts of sustainability risks on returns
- If sustainability risks are deemed not relevant, must explain why

**Article 8 -- "Light Green" Products (Environmental/Social Characteristics)**
- Products that promote environmental or social characteristics (E/S)
- Must disclose how those characteristics are met
- Must disclose the proportion of investments aligned with the characteristics
- No binding commitment to make sustainable investments (though many do)
- Became the most popular category: over EUR 5 trillion in AuM

**Article 9 -- "Dark Green" Products (Sustainable Investment Objective)**
- Products with sustainable investment as their objective
- Must designate a reference benchmark (if one exists) or explain how the objective is achieved
- All investments must be sustainable investments (with exceptions for hedging and liquidity)
- Must apply the "do no significant harm" (DNSH) principle
- More restrictive: approximately EUR 400 billion in AuM

## 3. Principal Adverse Impacts

### 3.1 Entity-Level PAI Reporting (Article 4)

Financial market participants with more than 500 employees **must** publish a PAI statement describing the principal adverse impacts of their investment decisions on sustainability factors. Smaller entities may opt out on a "comply or explain" basis.

### 3.2 The 18 Mandatory PAI Indicators

The Level 2 RTS specifies 18 mandatory indicators across environmental and social categories:

**Climate and Environment (8 indicators):**
1. GHG emissions (Scope 1, 2, and 3)
2. Carbon footprint
3. GHG intensity of investee companies
4. Exposure to companies active in the fossil fuel sector
5. Share of non-renewable energy consumption and production
6. Energy consumption intensity per high-impact climate sector
7. Activities negatively affecting biodiversity-sensitive areas
8. Emissions to water

**Social and Governance (6 indicators):**
9. Violations of UNGC principles and OECD Guidelines
10. Lack of processes/compliance mechanisms for UNGC/OECD
11. Unadjusted gender pay gap
12. Board gender diversity
13. Exposure to controversial weapons
14. Exposure to countries involved in social violations (sovereigns)

**Additional indicators (4 from optional lists, to be selected):**
15-18. Chosen from extended environmental and social indicator lists

### 3.3 Product-Level PAI (Article 7)

Article 8 and Article 9 products must additionally disclose how the product considers PAIs at the investment level, using the same indicator framework.

## 4. Disclosure Requirements

SFDR mandates disclosures at three levels:

### 4.1 Entity-Level Disclosures (Website)

- Integration of sustainability risks into investment decision-making (Article 3)
- PAI statement or explanation of non-consideration (Article 4)
- Integration of sustainability risks into remuneration policies (Article 5)

### 4.2 Pre-Contractual Disclosures (Before Investment)

- Article 6: How sustainability risks are integrated and their likely impact on returns
- Article 8: Description of E/S characteristics, investment strategy, proportion of investments, monitoring, and index designation
- Article 9: Description of sustainable investment objective, DNSH assessment, and index designation

### 4.3 Periodic Reporting (Annual)

- Article 8: Extent to which E/S characteristics were met, asset allocation, top investments, and Taxonomy alignment
- Article 9: Overall sustainability-related impact, asset allocation, and Taxonomy alignment

**Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1288** (the "RTS") provides the detailed templates and methodologies:

| Element | Delegated Regulation Reference |

| Article 8 periodic reporting template | Annex IV |
| Article 9 periodic reporting template | Annex V |
| DNSH methodology | Article 2(17) criteria |
| Taxonomy alignment disclosure | Graphical representation required |

The templates include standardised graphs showing the proportion of sustainable investments, Taxonomy-aligned investments, and investments that do not meet the sustainability criteria.

## 6. Relationship with the EU Taxonomy

The EU Taxonomy Regulation (2020/852) and SFDR are deeply interconnected:

- **SFDR Article 2(17)** defines "sustainable investment" -- which includes investments in economic activities that contribute to environmental or social objectives, provided they do no significant harm and the investee follows good governance practices
- **The EU Taxonomy** provides a detailed classification of which economic activities are **environmentally sustainable**, establishing six environmental objectives and technical screening criteria
- **Article 8 and 9 products** must disclose the proportion of their investments that are **Taxonomy-aligned** (i.e., meeting the Taxonomy's criteria for environmental sustainability)
- Products with environmental objectives must show a graphical representation of Taxonomy alignment
- The Taxonomy effectively provides the "green list" that SFDR products reference to substantiate their environmental claims

## 7. SFDR 2.0: The Commission's Proposal

### 7.1 Why a Reform?

The Commission's targeted consultation (September 2023) and comprehensive assessment identified fundamental problems:

- **Misuse as labels:** Article 8/9 were designed for transparency, not as product labels, but the market treated them as such
- **Greenwashing risk:** The broad Article 8 category allowed products with minimal sustainability ambition to sit alongside genuinely green products
- **Complexity:** The disclosure templates were overly complex for both preparers and investors
- **Legal uncertainty:** The interaction between SFDR definitions ("sustainable investment" in Article 2(17)) and Taxonomy definitions created confusion

### 7.2 The Proposal

On 21 November 2025, the Commission published **COM(2025)841** -- a proposal to amend SFDR (Regulation 2019/2088) and the PRIIPs Regulation (1286/2014). The proposal carries procedure number **2025/0361(COD)** and is now in the ordinary legislative procedure (co-decision between Parliament and Council).

**Core change:** Replace the Article 6/8/9 transparency framework with **three mandatory product categories** that function as genuine labels.

---

## 8. New Product Categories in Detail

### 8.1 Transition Category (New Article 7)

Products that support the transition of companies, economic activities, and assets towards more sustainable practices.

**Requirements:**
- **70% minimum** investment in transition-related assets
- Investments must meet a clear and measurable transition objective related to sustainability factors
- Eligible investments include:
  - Investments in portfolios replicating/managed in reference to EU Climate Transition Benchmarks or EU Paris-Aligned Benchmarks
  - EU Taxonomy-aligned investments (minimum 15% Taxonomy-aligned triggers eligibility)
  - Investments in undertakings with credible transition plans
  - Investments in undertakings with credible science-based targets
  - Investments accompanied by credible sustainability-related engagement strategies with defined milestones
- **Mandatory exclusions:** Fossil fuel-related activities (exploration, mining, extraction, distribution, refining of hard coal and lignite), controversial weapons
- Must explain transition objective, types of investments, phase-in period, indicators used, and how underperforming assets are addressed

### 8.2 ESG Basics Category (New Article 8)

Products that integrate sustainability factors in their investment strategy beyond sustainability risk management, but do not pursue a specific transition or sustainability objective.

**Requirements:**
- **70% minimum** investment in assets that integrate sustainability factors, including:
  - Investments with an ESG rating outperforming their universe/reference benchmark
  - Investments outperforming the average on a specific sustainability indicator
  - Investments favouring companies with a proven positive track record on sustainability processes, performance, or outcomes
- **Baseline exclusions** only (less restrictive than Transition or Sustainable)
- No specific transition or sustainability KPIs required
- Must explain the ESG integration approach and methodology

### 8.3 Sustainable Category (New Article 9)

Products with a clear and measurable sustainability objective.

**Requirements:**
- **70% minimum** investment in sustainable assets
- Must meet a clear and measurable objective related to sustainability factors (both environmental and social)
- **Full PAB exclusions** applied (the strictest of all categories):
  - Full climate-benchmark and Taxonomy-related exclusions
  - Exclusions against investments in companies developing new fossil fuel projects
  - Controversial weapons exclusions
  - All exclusions applicable to public sector entity investments (European Green Bond regime)
- Must designate appropriate sustainability indicators for measurement

### 8.4 Comparison with Current Framework

| Current Framework | New Framework | Key Difference |
|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Article 6 (all products) | No category (default) | Products without sustainability claims need only basic sustainability risk disclosure |
| Article 8 ("light green") | ESG Basics (Art. 8) or Transition (Art. 7) | Split into two distinct categories with 70% thresholds |
| Article 9 ("dark green") | Sustainable (Art. 9) | Tighter criteria with full PAB exclusions and mandatory indicator measurement |

### 8.5 Impact on Existing Products

All products currently classified under Article 8 or Article 9 will need to be **reassessed and reclassified** under the new regime. Fund managers must determine whether each product genuinely meets the criteria for Transition, ESG Basics, or Sustainable -- including the 70% threshold, mandatory exclusions, and indicator-based measurement.

## 10. Practical Implications for Financial Institutions

### 10.1 For Asset Managers

- **Immediate (SFDR 1.0):** Maintain Article 8/9 disclosures and PAI reporting
- **Prepare for SFDR 2.0:** Inventory all products, assess which new category each product could qualify for, and identify gaps against the 70% threshold and exclusion requirements
- **Data challenge:** The 70% thresholds require granular investee-level ESG data -- a significant operational burden, especially for fixed-income and multi-asset funds
- **Reclassification risk:** Some current Article 8 products may not meet any of the three new categories and would fall to "uncategorised"

### 10.2 For Banks and Insurance Companies

- Banks providing portfolio management are FMPs under SFDR and must comply at both entity and product level
- Insurance companies offering IBIPs (insurance-based investment products) must apply SFDR disclosures to those products
- Mandatory PAI reporting at entity level (for entities with 500+ employees)
- SFDR 2.0's new categories will require updates to product documentation, KIDs, and client communications

### 10.3 For Pension Funds

- IORPs are in scope as FMPs
- PAI reporting requirements apply
- Pension fund product offerings will need reclassification under SFDR 2.0
- Particular challenge: pension funds often have long-dated, illiquid investments where ESG data may be limited

### 10.4 Key Compliance Actions

1. Maintain current SFDR 1.0 disclosures until SFDR 2.0 takes effect
2. Begin product-level assessment against new category criteria (70% threshold, exclusions)
3. Ensure ESG data sourcing capabilities can support indicator-based measurement
4. Update governance frameworks for product classification decisions
5. Prepare client communication strategy for reclassification

## 11. Conclusions

SFDR has fundamentally reshaped the European investment landscape. The original regulation, despite its shortcomings as an unintended labelling regime, succeeded in creating transparency where none existed and in mainstreaming sustainability considerations across the financial sector.

SFDR 2.0 represents the natural evolution: from transparency to genuine product classification. By introducing three mandatory categories with clear criteria -- including 70% investment thresholds, mandatory exclusions, and indicator-based measurement -- the Commission aims to eliminate the greenwashing risk inherent in the current broad Article 8 category while creating a more meaningful framework for investors.

For financial institutions, the transition will be significant. Products will need to be reclassified, data capabilities upgraded, and client communications updated. But the direction is clear: the EU is moving from "disclose what you do" to "prove what you claim." This shift will reward genuinely sustainable products and create challenges for those that have relied on the flexibility of the current framework.
