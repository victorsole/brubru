# AIFMD II: The Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive Revision

**Analysis of Loan Origination, Liquidity Management Tools, Delegation, and Cross-Border Depositary Rules**

**Author:** Victor Sole Ferioli, Founder & Director, Beresol

**Date:** February 2026

**Location:** Brussels, Belgium

---

## Executive Summary

Directive (EU) 2024/927, known as AIFMD II, is the most significant reform of the EU alternative investment fund regulatory framework since the original AIFMD in 2011. Published on 26 March 2024 and requiring transposition by **16 April 2026**, AIFMD II amends both the AIFMD (2011/61/EU) and the UCITS Directive (2009/65/EC) across six core areas.

**Key Findings:**

- **Loan origination framework:** For the first time, EU law explicitly recognises and regulates AIFs' right to originate loans, creating a harmonised framework that replaces 27 fragmented national regimes
- **Nine liquidity management tools (LMTs):** Open-ended AIFs must select at least two LMTs from a standardised list (Annex V), harmonising investor protection across the EU
- **Enhanced delegation rules:** Two EU-resident senior persons required; expanded scope covering MiFID top-up services; ESMA peer reviews every two years
- **Cross-border depositary option:** Limited derogation allowing depositaries from other Member States, but only where local services are inadequate (markets below EUR 50 billion)
- **Transposition status:** As of February 2026, only Romania has formally transposed; Germany, Luxembourg, Ireland, Austria, and the Netherlands have published draft legislation

## 2. Scope and Applicability

**Entities in scope:**

- **EU AIFMs** (Alternative Investment Fund Managers authorised under AIFMD) -- all provisions
- **UCITS Management Companies** (authorised under Directive 2009/65/EC) -- provisions on delegation, LMTs, supervisory reporting, and investor disclosure
- **Non-EU AIFMs** marketing AIFs in the EU under National Private Placement Regimes (NPPRs) -- enhanced reporting requirements and new third-country restrictions
- **Depositaries** of AIFs -- cross-border depositary provisions

**Fund types affected:**

- All AIFs (open-ended and closed-ended)
- Loan-originating AIFs (new sub-category with specific rules)
- UCITS funds (for LMT, delegation, and reporting provisions)
- Employee share schemes (new cross-border marketing rights)

**Key exclusion:** Marketing by distributors acting on their own behalf under MiFID or the Insurance Distribution Directive is excluded from AIFMD delegation requirements.

## 3. Loan Origination Framework

This is the single most significant change in AIFMD II, creating a harmonised EU-wide framework for private credit funds.

### 3.1 Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Loan origination** | Granting of a loan directly by an AIF as original lender, or indirectly through a third party/SPV where the AIFM/AIF is involved in structuring the loan |
| **Loan-originating AIF** | An AIF whose investment strategy is mainly to originate loans, or whose originated loans represent at least 50% of its NAV |
| **Shareholder loan** | A loan granted to an undertaking in which the AIF holds at least 5% of capital/voting rights, which cannot be sold independently |

### 3.2 Rules for ALL AIFs Engaging in Loan Origination

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Risk management** | AIFMs must implement effective policies, procedures and processes for granting loans |
| **Prohibited borrowers** | AIFs may not grant loans to the AIFM itself, the AIFM's delegates, the depositary, or entities in the same group |
| **Concentration limit** | Notional value of loans to any single financial undertaking, AIF, or UCITS may not exceed 20% of capital |
| **5% retention** | Loan-originating AIFs must retain at least 5% of the notional value of loans they originate and transfer |
| **Originate-to-distribute prohibition** | Having a strategy of originating loans solely to sell them is prohibited |
| **Consumer lending** | Member States may prohibit AIFs from originating loans to consumers (Germany, Luxembourg, Ireland have exercised this option) |

### 3.3 Additional Rules for Loan-Originating AIFs (>= 50% NAV)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Leverage limit (open-ended)** | 175% of NAV |
| **Leverage limit (closed-ended)** | 300% of NAV |
| **Closed-ended presumption** | Must be closed-ended unless AIFM demonstrates to NCA that liquidity risk management is compatible with open-ended structure |
| **Shareholder loan exception** | Leverage limits do not apply where loan origination consists solely of shareholder loans not exceeding 150% of capital |

### 3.4 Grandfathering

| Category | Rule | Deadline |
|----------|------|----------|
| AIFs still raising capital (pre-15 April 2024) | Deemed compliant with concentration/leverage/closed-ended requirements | 16 April 2029 |
| AIFs no longer raising capital (pre-15 April 2024) | Deemed compliant indefinitely | No deadline |
| Loans originated before 15 April 2024 | Exempt from new rules | Permanent |
| AIFMs | No grandfathering -- must obtain updated authorisation | 16 April 2026 |

## 4. Liquidity Management Tools

### 4.1 The Nine LMTs (New Annex V)

| No. | Tool | Category | Pre-selection |
|-----|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Suspension of subscriptions/repurchases/redemptions | Exceptional | Always available (no pre-selection needed) |
| 2 | Redemption gates | Quantitative | Must pre-select |
| 3 | Extension of notice periods | Quantitative | Must pre-select |
| 4 | Redemption fees | Anti-dilution | Must pre-select |
| 5 | Swing pricing | Anti-dilution | Must pre-select |
| 6 | Dual pricing | Anti-dilution | Must pre-select |
| 7 | Anti-dilution levy | Anti-dilution | Must pre-select |
| 8 | Redemption in kind | Other | Must pre-select (professional investors only) |
| 9 | Side pockets | Exceptional | Always available (NCA notification required) |

### 4.2 Key Rules

- AIFMs managing open-ended AIFs must select **at least two LMTs** from items 2-8
- Cannot select only swing pricing and dual pricing as the two LMTs
- Selected LMTs must be in the fund's constitutional documents
- NCA notification required when activating suspension or side pockets
- Member States may allow additional LMTs beyond Annex V (Luxembourg has exercised this option)

## 5. Delegation and Substance Requirements

### 5.1 Substance/Authorisation Enhancements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Two senior persons** | Business must be conducted by at least two natural persons who are full-time employees or executive members, **domiciled in the EU** |
| **Enhanced application** | Must provide: senior management time allocation, detailed delegation arrangements, due diligence measures, human and technical resources |
| **Letter-box entity prevention** | ESMA required to conduct peer reviews at least every 2 years |

### 5.2 Framework Changes

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Expanded scope** | Delegation rules now cover ALL Annex I functions plus MiFID top-up services |
| **New Annex I items** | (6) originating loans on behalf of an AIF, (7) servicing securitisation SPEs |
| **New MiFID top-up services** | Administration of benchmarks, credit servicing, other ancillary services |
| **Enhanced reporting** | Detailed delegate identity, resources, percentage of assets delegated, dates, sub-delegate information |
| **Reporting timeline** | Delegation-related Annex IV reporting applies from **16 April 2027** |

---

## 6. Depositary and Custody Requirements

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Cross-border depositary** | Case-by-case derogation allowing depositaries from a different Member State |
| **Conditions** | Must demonstrate lack of appropriate local depositary services AND aggregate national market below EUR 50 billion |
| **CSD carve-out** | Central Securities Depositories providing custody are treated as delegation of custody functions |
| **Member State discretion** | States may choose NOT to permit foreign depositaries (Luxembourg has exercised this option) |

---

## 7. Cross-Border and NPPR Changes

| Change | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **NPPR restrictions** | Non-EU AIFMs/AIFs prohibited from marketing if established in: (a) EU AML high-risk third country, or (b) EU tax non-cooperative jurisdiction |
| **Replaced FATF reference** | Original AIFMD referenced FATF list; AIFMD II uses EU's own AML blacklist |
| **Tax cooperation** | Third countries must have signed qualified agreement on exchange of tax information |
| **Currently blacklisted** | AML: UAE, Panama (among others); Tax: US Virgin Islands, Russia, Panama |
| **Employee share schemes** | New cross-border marketing rights for authorised EU AIFMs |
| **Expanded NPPR reporting** | Non-EU AIFMs face reporting obligations aligned with EU AIFMs |

---

## 8. Investor Disclosure Enhancements

AIFMD II significantly strengthens Article 23 pre-contractual disclosures:

- **Fee transparency:** Detailed list of all fees, charges, and expenses borne directly or indirectly by investors
- **Annual cost reporting:** Periodic disclosure of all costs to investors
- **Loan portfolio composition:** AIFMs must periodically disclose originated loan portfolio details
- **Delegation arrangements:** Enhanced disclosure of delegation and sub-delegation structures

## 9. Relationship to Other EU Regulations

| Regulation | Interaction |
|-----------|-------------|
| **DORA** | ICT risk management and incident reporting requirements apply to AIFMs since 17 January 2025. Potential overlap between DORA's third-party risk rules (EBA) and AIFMD's delegation framework (ESMA) |
| **MiCA** | Crypto-asset funds managed by EU AIFMs must comply with both frameworks |
| **SFDR** | Sustainability disclosure requirements apply alongside AIFMD II. SFDR 2.0 (COM(2025)841) would require AIFMs to meet prescriptive sustainability criteria |
| **ELTIF 2.0** | ELTIFs already permit loan origination with retail marketing passport. AIFMD II provides similar capabilities for non-ELTIF AIFs |
| **MiFID II** | AIFMs providing ancillary services with financial instruments subject to MiFID II rules |
| **Securitisation Regulation** | AIFMD II adds servicing securitisation SPEs to AIFM functions |

## 10. Impact on Private Credit

### 10.1 Positive Impacts

- **Regulatory certainty:** First explicit EU recognition of AIFs' right to originate loans
- **Cross-border lending:** Harmonised framework creates implicit cross-border loan origination passport
- **Open-ended structures:** Opens the door for semi-liquid/evergreen private credit funds (subject to conditions)
- **Market growth:** Expected to significantly expand the European private credit market

### 10.2 Compliance Burdens

- **Leverage caps:** 175% (open-ended) / 300% (closed-ended) may constrain leveraged strategies
- **Concentration limits:** 20% single-borrower limit for financial counterparties
- **Closed-ended default:** Must justify open-ended status to NCA
- **Consumer lending restrictions:** Multiple Member States prohibit consumer lending by AIFs
- **Retention requirements:** 5% skin-in-the-game on loan transfers

## 11. Transposition Status

*As of February 2026 -- two months before the 16 April 2026 deadline.*

### 11.1 Completed Transposition

| Member State | National Law | Entry into Force |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Romania** | Legea nr. 243/2025 (amending OUG 32/2012, Law 297/2004, Law 74/2015) | 16 April 2026 |

### 11.2 Draft Legislation Published

| Member State | Status | Approach |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| **Germany** | Fund Risk Limitation Act (Fondsrisikobegrenzungsgesetz) published 8 Aug 2025 | No gold-plating; prohibits consumer lending |
| **Luxembourg** | Bill No. 8628 submitted to Parliament 3 Oct 2025 | No gold-plating; prohibits consumer lending; no foreign depositaries for Luxembourg AIFs |
| **Netherlands** | Draft implementing legislation published | No gold-plating indicated |
| **Ireland** | CBI Consultation Paper CP162 (9 Sep 2025) | No gold-plating; consumer lending prohibition |
| **Austria** | Ministerial draft published 22 Jan 2026 | Focus on loan origination, LMTs, delegation |

### 11.3 Not Yet Published

Belgium, France, Italy, Spain, and all other Member States have not yet published implementing legislation. Zero national measures have been notified to EUR-Lex by these Member States.
