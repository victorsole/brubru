# Questions made by Members of the European Parliament

Since the European Parliament lacks legislative initiative (Members of the European Parliament, or MEPs, can't propose legislation; only the European Commission can), they can nevertheless make questions to the European Commission on any topic. These are called either "questions", or "parliamentary questions", or "written questions", or "written parliamentary questions". In EU bubble jargon, when an MEP has made a question, it means that she or he has made a parliamentary question to the European Commission.

These questions must be, at most, 200 characters long and follow the EU vocabulary. They need to refer to current legislation and show sources (e.g., laws, newspaper articles, etc.). They have an introductory part and then up to 3 sub-questions.

All questions can be fetched at https://www.europarl.europa.eu/plenary/en/parliamentary-questions.html?tabType=wq#sidesForm, tabs "Most recent questions" (with no answer from the Commission yet) and "Most recent questions with no answers".

### Conclusions on Unanswered Questions

**A. Structure and Format**

Every question follows the same rigid structure:
1. **Title**: A descriptive headline (typically 10-20 words). Formal and specific; never vague.
2. **Header block**: Reference number (E- for standard, P- for priority), addressee (Commission, Council, or VP/HR), Rule 144, author(s) with political group.
3. **Context/introduction**: 2-4 paragraphs of factual background. This is the bulk of the question. It establishes the problem with specific evidence: legislation references (Regulation (EU) 2021/2116, Directive (EU) 2022/2041, etc.), facts (EUR 34 million in funding, 47,000 eID cards issued), news reports, audit findings, and institutional positions.
4. **Sources**: Footnoted references (marked [1], [2], etc.) linking to legislation, news articles, court rulings, Commission documents, or previous parliamentary questions.
5. **Sub-questions**: Introduced by a bridge phrase ("With the above in mind:", "In light of the above:", "In connection with the above:") followed by 1-3 numbered questions. The sub-questions themselves were NOT visible in the HTML extraction (they appear to be in a separate section below the fold or rendered differently), but from the answered questions we can see they are always direct, specific, and demand concrete answers.

**B. Content Patterns**

- **Legislative anchoring is mandatory**: Every question cites at least one piece of EU legislation, Treaty article, or Council Common Position. This is not optional -- it is the convention that legitimises the question.
- **Evidence-first approach**: Questions lead with facts (audits, court rulings, news investigations, statistics) before asking anything. The ratio is roughly 80% context, 20% question.
- **Cross-referencing**: Some questions are follow-ups to previous written questions (e.g., P-000710 follows up on E-004181/2025). This creates chains of accountability.
- **Political group signatures**: Questions are often co-signed by 2-3 MEPs from the same group. This gives political weight and suggests coordinated action within groups.
- **National vs EU tension**: Many questions highlight friction between national implementation and EU law (Poland pharmacy rules vs Treaties, German eID vs eIDAS, Spanish CAP vs cross-border coherence).

**C. Two Question Types**

| Type | Prefix | Response time | Usage |
|------|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Written question** | E- | 6 weeks | Standard; allows multiple co-signatories; broader topics |
| **Priority question** | P- | 3 weeks | Urgent/time-sensitive; typically single MEP or smaller group |

Priority questions (P-) outnumber standard written questions (E-) in the sample, suggesting MEPs prefer the faster response track.

**D. Tone and Register**

- Formal but not neutral. Questions are clearly framed to push the Commission into a specific position.
- Loaded phrasing: "bypassing of Parliament", "serious procedural concerns", "significant security risk" -- these are not innocent inquiries but political pressure instruments.
- Third-person institutional voice: MEPs do not write "I think" but "the above raises concerns regarding the freedoms guaranteed by the Treaties."

**E. Topic Diversity**

The 10 questions span: trade policy (FORGE, Mercosur, India ceramics), security (Islamic Relief, ETA, eID fraud, car safety), agriculture (CAP cross-border), labour rights (prison inmates), data protection (GDPR broadcasters), and geopolitics (Canada/Davos). Parliamentary questions cover the entire EU policy spectrum.

**F. Length**

The introductory description notes "200 characters" but this appears to refer to the **title** only. The body text of each question is substantially longer -- typically 200-400 words of context plus sub-questions. The full document (context + questions + sources) runs 300-600 words.

### Conclusions on Commission Answers

**A. Structure and Format of Answers**

Commission answers follow a consistent pattern:
1. **Attribution line**: "Answer given by [Commissioner name] on behalf of the European Commission" -- always on behalf of the institution, not personal.
2. **Date**: Typically 3-6 weeks after submission for priority questions, up to 6 weeks for standard.
3. **Body**: 2-6 paragraphs of substantive response. No numbered sub-answers corresponding to sub-questions -- the Commission addresses the overall topic rather than answering point-by-point.
4. **Sources**: Footnoted references to legislation, Commission communications, reports, and online tools (EUR-Lex URLs, scoreboards, registers).

**B. Response Strategies**

The Commission uses several recurring rhetorical strategies:

| Strategy | Example | Frequency |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| **Deflect to Member States** | "It is primarily the responsibility of the Member States to ensure correct application" (prison inmates) | Very common |
| **Cite existing frameworks** | "European Digital Identity Wallets will undergo comprehensive security certification" (eID) | Always |
| **Acknowledge without committing** | "The Commission acknowledges that energy-intensive industries face challenges" (ceramics) | Common |
| **Point to ongoing processes** | "The legal framework is under legislative negotiation" (eID/business wallets) | Common |
| **Redirect to reports** | "All KPIs are taken up in this annual reporting process" (Clean Industrial Deal) | Common |
| **Assert EU position diplomatically** | "The EU has been unequivocal that the international NGO registration framework cannot be implemented in its current form" (Israel/NGOs) | When politically charged |

**C. Tone Compared to Questions**

- Questions are **politically assertive** ("bypassing Parliament", "serious security risk"). Answers are **bureaucratically measured** ("the Commission is not aware of...", "the Commission stands ready to monitor...").
- Questions push for action. Answers describe processes already in place.
- Questions cite media investigations and audits as evidence. Answers cite EU legislation and Commission publications as authority.
- The asymmetry is striking: MEPs use questions as a **political accountability tool**; the Commission uses answers as a **status-quo defence mechanism**.

**D. Specificity**

Some answers are substantively detailed (the ceramics/India answer gives exact tariff percentages: 5% EU vs 16.5% India, antidumping 6.7-8.7%, 22-year safeguard period). Others are vague redirects ("this is a Member State responsibility"). The quality varies significantly by Commissioner and topic.

**E. Response Time**

For the sample analysed:
- Priority questions (P-): Answered within 14-28 days (fastest: P-000150, 14 days; slowest: P-000300, 28 days)
- Standard questions (E-): Answered within 23 days (E-000356)
- The stated deadlines (3 weeks for P-, 6 weeks for E-) are generally respected.

**F. Format Issue**

Three of the eight answers were only available as PDF/DOC, not in HTML. This is relevant for Brubru's scraping strategy: we will need a PDF text extractor (or the DOC endpoint) to capture all answer content. The HTML versions are more machine-readable, but not all answers are published in HTML format.

**G. Named Commissioners**

Answers are signed by the responsible Commissioner, which reveals the DG handling the topic:
- EVP Virkkunen (Digital) -- eID/digital identity
- Mr McGrath (Democracy, Justice) -- GDPR
- Ms Lahbib (Humanitarian) -- NGO funding/Israel
- EVP Sejourne (Industry) -- Clean Industrial Deal
- Mr Sefcovic (Trade) -- EU-India/ceramics

This mapping is useful for Brubru: when generating a question, the system can predict which Commissioner will respond based on the policy area.

### Summary: Key Insights for Brubru's Question Generator

| Aspect | Finding | Implication for Brubru |
|--------|---------|----------------------|
| **Structure** | Rigid: title + header + context + sources + sub-questions | Template-based generation is feasible |

| **Evidence requirement** | Facts, statistics, audits, news sources | Users must supply evidence or Brubru must surface it from RSS/knowledge base |
| **Sub-questions** | 1-3 direct, specific questions | AI can generate these from the context |
| **Tone** | Formal, politically loaded, institutional voice | Distinct from chat -- needs a "parliamentary register" prompt |
| **Title** | Descriptive, max ~200 characters | Auto-generated from context |
| **Co-signatories** | 1-3 MEPs from same group | Not relevant for Brubru users (they won't be MEPs) |
| **Answer format** | Some HTML, some PDF-only | Scraper needs PDF extraction for complete coverage |
| **Commissioner mapping** | Policy area determines respondent | Can predict who will answer based on topic classification |
| **Response strategies** | Commission often deflects to Member States | Brubru could pre-warn users which topics may get deflected |

The feature has two entry points: the Document Generator wizard in My EU Bubble's Documents tab, and the Brubru chatbot with intent detection.

**1.2 Request schema**

File: `backend/schemas/document_generation.py`

New Pydantic model:

```python
class GenerateEPQuestionRequest(BaseModel):
    topic: str                          # What the question is about (required)
    addressee: str = "commission"       # "commission" | "council" | "vp_hr"
    question_type: str = "standard"     # "standard" (E-) or "priority" (P-)
    context_description: str            # Background facts, evidence, concerns
    legislation_references: Optional[List[str]] = None  # Specific EU laws to cite

    celex_number: Optional[str] = None
```

**1.3 Generator method**

The prompt will also inject relevant EU legislation from the knowledge base when `policy_area` is provided (via context_builder's existing EUR-Lex/OEIL data).

### Phase 3: Chatbot Integration

**3.1 Intent detection**

File: `backend/services/ai/context_builder.py`

Add EP Question intent detection to the context builder's system prompt. Two paths:

**Ambiguous triggers** (require clarification):
- "make a question"
- "write a question"
- "parliamentary question"
- "I want to ask the Commission"

Brubru responds: *"Do you mean you want to make a written question from the European Parliament to the European Commission?"*

**Specific triggers** (proceed directly):
- "written question to the Commission"
- "EP question to the Commission"
- "written parliamentary question"
- "question for written answer"
- "priority question to the Commission"

**3.2 Conversational generation flow**

When intent is confirmed, the chatbot collects the required information conversationally:

1. "What topic would you like to address?" (if not already provided)
2. "What evidence or concerns do you want to highlight?" (context)
3. "Are there specific EU laws or regulations you want to reference?" (optional)
4. "Should this be a standard or priority question?" (optional, default standard)

Then generates the question using the same `generate_ep_question()` service.

**3.3 Save to Documents**

After generation, the chatbot:
1. Displays the question in the chat with markdown formatting
2. Asks: *"Would you like to save this to your Documents in My EU Bubble?"*
3. On confirmation, calls `POST /api/documents` to save as `document_type: 'ep_question'`
4. Returns a link: *"Saved! You can find it in My EU Bubble > Documents."*

### How It Works

**Via Document Generator Wizard:**
1. User clicks "Generate with AI" in Documents tab
2. Selects "EP Written Question" card (Brubru blue)
3. Fills in: topic, addressee, context/evidence, sub-questions count, tone
4. Optionally adds legislation references, evidence sources, policy area
5. AI generates question in authentic EP format
6. Saved to Documents with tag `ep_question`

**Via Chatbot:**
1. User says "write a question" (ambiguous) -- Brubru asks for clarification
2. User says "written question to the Commission" (specific) -- Brubru proceeds directly
3. Brubru collects topic, evidence, legislation references conversationally
4. Generates and displays the question in chat
5. Saves to Documents tab automatically

### Storage

EP Questions are stored as `document_type: "note"` with `tags: ["ep_question", "generated", "standard"|"priority"]` and full metadata in `doc_metadata` JSONB:
```json
{
  "generated": true,
  "document_subtype": "ep_question",
  "topic": "...",
  "addressee": "commission",
  "question_type": "standard",
  "tone": "assertive",
  "num_sub_questions": 3
}
```

No database migration was required.
