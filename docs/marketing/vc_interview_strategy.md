## 2. What questions will they ask?

### Universal questions (all three funds)

**"What does your company do?"**
- Why they ask: clarity of vision
- Demo it: open Brubru, show the 5 modules in 60 seconds

**"Who desperately needs this?"**
- Why they ask: market pull
- Answer: show Brussels job postings requiring these exact skills (already in Start it application). Name specific consultancies (FTI, Burson, Kreab) and their pain.

**"Why you? Why are you the right person?"**
- Why they ask: founder-market fit
- Answer: "I did this manually for 17 years. 7 years inside European Parliament. I built the tool I wished I had."

**"Who are your competitors?"**
- Why they ask: market awareness
- Demo it: ask Brubru "Who are your competitors in EU policy tech?" -- it should give an informed, honest answer citing Dixit, POLITICO Pro, SAVOIRR, FiscalNote EUIT, Moonlit.ai

**"How do you make money?"**
- Why they ask: business model
- Answer: tier structure. Break-even at 23 subscribers. Show the unit economics.

**"Why now?"**
- Why they ask: timing thesis
- Answer: AI Act, NIS2, DORA, MiCA, CSRD deadlines 2026-2027. LLMs crossed quality threshold. EU market completely empty.

**"What's your traction?"**
- Why they ask: proof of demand
- Answer: honest -- pre-revenue, 13 test users. But: product is fully built (186K lines), platform cost EUR 115/month, 87-month runway.

## 3. Training Brubru to answer evaluator questions live

The most powerful demo is making evaluators use Brubru themselves. Here's how to prepare Brubru's Main Chat to handle questions that fund evaluators will naturally ask.

### Scenario: evaluator opens Brubru and asks questions

Fund evaluators will test the product by asking questions from their own domain. We need Brubru's context builder and knowledge base to handle these gracefully.

---

These are consumer/gaming/fintech people. They'll test whether Brubru can answer questions outside pure EU policy.

## 7. Key narratives to prepare

### The "Harvey for EU Policy" narrative (for all funds)

> Harvey AI is worth $3B+ because it understands legal language, case law, and legal workflows better than generic AI. Brubru is the same thing for EU legislative language, institutional procedures, and policy workflows. Harvey serves lawyers. Brubru serves the people who shape the laws lawyers interpret.

### The "solo founder built it all" narrative (for Speedrun and YC)

> One person. EUR 10K. 213,000 lines of code. 32 scrapers. 18 AI services. 23 EU languages. A fully deployed production platform that replaces EUR 50K/month consultancy retainers. This is what happens when 17 years of domain expertise meets AI. The question isn't whether the product works — it does. The question is how fast we can sell it.

### The "AI-native agency" narrative (for YC, Aaron Epstein angle)

> A Brussels consultancy charges EUR 5,000 for a position paper. They spend 3-5 days on it with 15-25% margins. Brubru produces the same deliverable in minutes with 70% margins. We're not selling software subscriptions -- we're selling finished deliverables at consultancy prices, produced at software costs. That's 4x the margin at 100x the speed.

### The "KBC understands our pain" narrative (for Start it @KBC)

> KBC is registered in the EU Transparency Register. You have lobbyists in Brussels tracking ESMA, EBA, the Commission. Your compliance team reads Official Journal entries, monitors regulatory consultations, drafts position papers. That's exactly what Brubru automates. You don't just understand our market -- you ARE our market.

### The "empty market" narrative (for all funds)

> In the US, FiscalNote IPO'd at $1.5B. Quorum raised $200M. Bloomberg Government is a billion-dollar division. In the EU -- which has MORE regulatory complexity across 27 jurisdictions and 24 official languages -- the market for AI-powered policy tools is... zero. Nobody. Dixit monitors amendments but can't draft them. POLITICO Pro is media, not workflow. FiscalNote tried and failed to localise. The market is EUR 3-5B, growing with every new regulation, and completely empty.
