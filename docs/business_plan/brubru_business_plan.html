How it works
  • Load any EU legislative text via EUR-Lex CELEX number or URL
  • Two-column layout displays original text alongside your proposed changes
  • Click on any article, paragraph, recital, or annex to propose an amendment
  • AI drafting: describe changes in natural language, Brubru produces legal text
  • Track changes visualisation: bold for additions, strikethrough for deletions
  • Export in XML, HTML, PDF, or Word format
Amendment workflow
  • Candidate: draft amendment, still being refined
  • Tabled: officially submitted for consideration
  • Withdrawn: pulled from consideration
Technical compliance
  • Akoma Ntoso XML: OASIS LegalDocumentML standard used by EU institutions
  • Automatic position references (Article X, paragraph Y, point (a))
  • Handles both OJ (Official Journal) and COM (Commission) document formats
  • Supports recitals, articles, annexes, and transitional provisions
  • Link amendments to Legislative Train carriages for tracking
Why it matters
  • Legislative formatting is arcane: get it wrong and amendments are rejected on procedural grounds
  • No other tool combines AT4AM-style editing with AI drafting, real MEP amendment data, and AI comparative scoring
  • EP's own AT4AM is internal-only, not available to external stakeholders who draft amendments for MEPs
MEP amendments: real parliamentary data

Brubru fetches and parses actual EP committee amendment documents from the European Parliament Open Data Portal, giving users access to the real amendments tabled by MEPs for any legislative procedure.

  • Browse all committee amendments by procedure, with political group breakdown and hotspot analysis
  • Filter by political group, amendment type, element reference, or MEP name
  • Word-level diff highlighting: additions in bold-italic, deletions struck-through
  • Group activity bars showing which political groups are most active on a dossier
  • Justification text and "on behalf of group" indicators
AI-powered comparative analysis

Users provide their policy position and Brubru's AI scores every MEP amendment on a -2 to +2 alignment scale, then computes three strategic outputs:

  • Best Allies: top 15 MEPs ranked by alignment with the user's position. Identifies who to approach for coalition-building
  • Coverage Gaps: articles MEPs are amending that the user hasn't touched (blind spots) and user-unique contributions no MEP has proposed
  • Political Landscape: classifies groups as supportive, mixed, or opposed. Instant strategic read on political dynamics

Feedback system: the improvement engine

Every user interaction generates structured signals that systematically improve Brubru's AI quality, data coverage, and product roadmap.

1
Collect: 4 feedback channels
Feedback is captured at every touchpoint with minimal friction.
Brubru Chat
Three-button inline feedback on every AI response:
Helpful Not helpful Hallucination
Hallucination reports include optional correction text for precision.
My EU Bubble
Feedback invitation cards embedded in the dashboard. Card and sidebar variants. Links to structured form or direct email to hello@beresol.eu.
General Feedback Form
Accessible from Amendator, EU Law Comply, and Tenderator. Structured fields: type (bug, feature request, general), title, description, category, affected feature, screenshot URL. Auto-captures browser and device metadata.
Passive Analytics
Every AI interaction automatically logs: provider used, tokens consumed, response time, sources cited, context tiers, and knowledge gap indicators. No user action required.
2
Process: where feedback lives
Feedback is stored, scored, and surfaced for action across three systems.
feedback_submissions
Central table storing all feedback with type, priority (low/medium/high/critical), status (new > in_review > in_progress > resolved), admin notes, assignment, and full audit trail via admin_activity_log.
Conversation quality service
Automated scoring (0-10) per conversation. Weighted signals: positive feedback (+2.0), negative (-1.0), hallucination report (-3.0), knowledge gap (-1.0), follow-up engagement (+0.5), high-tier sources (+0.5 each). Ratings: Excellent (9-10), Good (7-8), Neutral (4-6), Poor (2-3), Critical (0-1).
Knowledge gap tracker
When the AI cannot answer, the gap is categorised by missing data type (legislation, procedure, MEP, date, statistic, document) and root cause (not_indexed, not_fetched, not_exists, outdated). Each gap is tracked until resolved.
3
Improve: systematic quality loops
Feedback drives five concrete improvement loops that compound over time.
1
Knowledge base expansion
Knowledge gaps (not_indexed, not_fetched) trigger new scraper runs or source additions. When a gap is marked as not_exists, the topic is flagged for future data partnerships. Resolved gaps include resolution notes for institutional memory.
2
Context builder tuning
Source tier usage analytics reveal which data sources produce the highest-quality answers. High-tier sources (Tier 1-2: OEIL, EUR-Lex) boost quality scores by +0.5 each. Under-performing sources are deprioritised or replaced.
3
Hallucination suppression
Hallucination reports carry the highest negative weight (-3.0). Patterns in flagged responses reveal prompt weaknesses, insufficient context injection, or source gaps. Correction text from users provides ground truth for targeted fixes.
4
Provider optimisation
Chat analytics track quality and cost per AI provider. If Mistral's quality drops on specific query types, the fallback chain routing is adjusted. Response time, token usage, and user satisfaction are correlated per provider to optimise the cost-quality tradeoff.
5
Product roadmap prioritisation
Feature requests and bug reports flow into the admin panel with filtering by type, priority, and category. Aggregate statistics (by_status, by_type, by_priority) surface the most impactful improvements. The admin assigns, tracks, and resolves each item through a full lifecycle.
4
Admin dashboard: command centre
All feedback, analytics, and quality metrics converge in the admin panel.

The daily pipeline

Every morning, the founder runs this 7-phase routine using AI orchestration (Claude Code). Each phase builds on the previous one, creating a compound improvement loop.

Phase 1 EU News Scan
Scrape 44 EU institutional news portals. Prioritise headlines. Update chatbot example questions with today's hot topics.
Phase 2 Query Audit
Pull every real user query from the previous day. Diagnose each answer against a quality checklist: factual accuracy, language match, citation integrity, completeness, follow-up quality.
Phase 3 Knowledge Improvements
Implement fixes identified from the audit. Create new knowledge guides, add keyword triggers, update system prompt rules, fix context builder logic. Every diagnosed failure becomes a permanent improvement.
Phase 4 Deploy
Push backend improvements to production (Railway.app). Build and pre-render frontend if changed (SiteGround). Brubru is live with today's improvements before noon.
Phase 5 Daily Brief Email
Compose 10 curated EU policy headlines in Brubru style. Send to all subscribers and the Brussels network. Product news items highlight what improved today.
Phase 6 News Alerts
Targeted policy alerts to EU Transparency Register organisations whose lobbying area matches today's hot topics. 3,000+ organisations across 12 policy clusters.
Phase 7 Session Summary
Review what changed, what worked, what broke. Update institutional memory files. Propose new rules to prevent recurring mistakes. The AI learns from every session.
The Brubru Trainers programme

Brubru Trainers are trusted beta users who actively test the chatbot and report quality issues. They are the human feedback loop that makes the daily pipeline effective.

Each Trainer receives a distinctive gold shield badge (BT monogram) that appears next to their name throughout the platform. Their avatar is surrounded by a rotating ring of 12 gold stars, and their name shimmers with a gradient glow effect.

Trainers help identify:

  • Hallucinated facts and wrong CELEX numbers
  • Language switching errors
  • Incomplete answers and missing policy domains
  • Broken citations and outdated references

Every piece of feedback becomes a permanent fix: a new knowledge guide, a system prompt rule, a context builder improvement. The goal: every user query that fails today works perfectly tomorrow.

Stakeholder CRM gap is universal
Dixit, DeHavilland, POLITICO Pro, Quorum, SAVOIRR, nearly every competitor has a contact database. Brubru is the only serious platform without one. #1 new feature priority.
Retention comes from daily habits
POLITICO Pro's 91% renewal comes from morning briefings. Brubru needs a "daily touch": AI-generated morning emails summarising overnight changes to tracked files.
Missing step: engage
Brubru covers monitor, analyse, draft, comply, predict, bid. But misses engage: contacting officials, tracking interactions. Adding it closes the full advocacy loop.
Spaak is the closest direct competitor, but a different category
Danish-origin Spaak (EUR 1.5M pre-seed, 4 co-founders, 20+ customers incl. Danish Government) is aggressively expanding from national Danish political monitoring into Brussels. Their strength is distribution and proven national PMF funding EU push. But the product category is different: Spaak is an observation platform (dashboards, feeds, social media tracking). Brubru is an action platform (amendment drafting, document generation, compliance analysis, legislative predictions). Their core team lacks deep EU institutional knowledge (EP procedure, trilogue dynamics, Akoma Ntoso formatting. Brubru is built by someone with 8 years inside the EU institutions doing the actual work. As Calacanis and Sacks describe it (All In Podcast, Mar 2026): the future belongs to "Agent Maestros" who orchestrate AI agents within professional workflows. Brubru is the agent EU policy professionals maestro. Race is on, but in different lanes.
