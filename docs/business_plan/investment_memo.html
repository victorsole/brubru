2. Problem and market

EU public affairs professionals (lobbyists, trade associations, MEP assistants, law firms, consultancies) must track 500+ active legislative procedures across seven institutions: the European Parliament, Council, Commission, Court of Justice, Committee of the Regions, Economic and Social Committee, and national transposition. The regulatory machine never stops.

Today this work is done manually (spreadsheets, email chains, PDF monitoring) or outsourced to expensive consultancies charging 200+ per hour. Junior consultants spend 60% of their time on information retrieval rather than strategic analysis.

Market size: approximately 50,000 EU affairs professionals in Brussels and national capitals. 12,000+ entities on the EU Transparency Register. Competitor Spaak (VC-backed, EUR 1.5M pre-seed, 100+ PA teams) has proven paying demand exists in this market. FiscalNote (US, NYSE-listed) validates the adjacent regulatory intelligence category at scale. Policy-Insider.AI (Hamburg, bootstrapped) reached $660K revenue with six people, confirming the market responds to lean, focused tooling.

4. Go-to-market

Bottom-up, product-led. Brussels is a small, dense, word-of-mouth market. The strategy is to create daily touchpoints that demonstrate value before asking for payment:

  • Daily brief emails to 100+ recipients (registered users + Brussels contacts): curated EU policy headlines, written in Brubru's voice, with direct links to the platform
  • News alerts to 4,000+ organisations on the EU Transparency Register, targeted by policy cluster (digital, climate, finance, trade, health, energy, and more)
  • Direct outreach campaigns to Brussels advocacy firms and Catalan institutions
  • 14-day free trial on all plans, no credit card required

The EP Plan (discounted for MEP assistants at 49/month) is a deliberate wedge: parliamentary assistants who adopt Brubru become long-term users and generate institutional word of mouth.

North star metric: WAPU (Weekly Active Paid Users). A paid subscriber who performs at least one core action (AI chat query, document generated, file tracked, amendment drafted, or compliance report run) in a 7-day window. WAPU is the only honest signal of product-market fit: it requires both payment and usage, simultaneously. Not MRR (trailing indicator), not subscriber count (masks churn), not raw active users (free trial noise). Current phase: Activation, 3/10 WAPU achieved (GovClipping, Ferrmed, Bo).

Moat thesis (a16z Seven Powers framework). Brubru's strongest moat is process power: encoding deep EU institutional workflow knowledge that cannot be replicated by throwing a better model at the problem. Second is cornered resources: 52 bespoke scrapers and the EU Vocabularies layer (9-identifier resolver, multilingual NAL authority labels, DCAT-AP self-description, citation verification on every model output), each with edge cases that took weeks to solve. Third is counterpositioning: Spaak sells per-seat monitoring dashboards; Brubru sells action tools that produce deliverables. Fourth is the emerging switching cost: as users accumulate dossiers, tracked files, annotations, and prediction history inside Brubru, the system becomes a system of record they cannot easily migrate. The Agent Maestro framing (Calacanis, All In Podcast, March 2026) captures the product category precisely: Brubru is the agent EU policy professionals orchestrate. Dashboards vs. Agents is a fundamentally different product category, not a feature comparison.

8. Vision

Brubru becomes the default AI policy advisor for every EU affairs professional: the agent they orchestrate, not a tool they consult. The path from workflow platform to system of record follows three phases of growth, each anchored to a WAPU target.

  • Now (Brussels, 3/10 WAPU paying): GovClipping (B2B API), Ferrmed (trade association), Bo (single-seat advocate); plus a pipeline of lobbyists, trade associations, MEP assistants using Brubru for daily monitoring, drafting, and document generation
  • Next (national capitals, 25 WAPU): 27 EU member states where national transposition creates the same monitoring and compliance needs; team collaboration layer multiplies WAPU per customer
  • Later (platform lock-in, 50 WAPU): stakeholder CRM, activity logging with ROI reporting, dossier workspaces where tracking, amendments, contacts, predictions, and documents accumulate into an irreplaceable asset the user owns inside Brubru

The context layer compounds over time. Every knowledge guide, every scraper, every keyword trigger, every user query that gets audited and fixed makes the system smarter. This is not a product that gets disrupted by a better model; it gets stronger as the orchestration layer absorbs model improvements and user-accumulated data deepens the switching cost.

The Cherny Paradigm: the method used to build Brubru (founder as fleet commander, orchestrating AI coding agents with institutional memory) is also the method users will adopt. Brubru is built by an Agent Maestro for Agent Maestros.

Deferred (post-50 WAPU): P7 MEP social media monitoring, P8 grant proposal AI drafting, P9 national parliament monitoring, P10 LEOS interoperability. These are feature-parity plays that do not drive activation at the current stage; they are scheduled only after the core WAPU target is established.
