Summary

Every competitor has at least one thing Brubru can learn from, even if they are weaker overall. This document maps specific learnings and emulation opportunities to Brubru's existing features, with a timeline for implementation.

Brubru features referenced: Brubru Chat, My EU Bubble (incl. Predictions, Tracked Files), Amendator, EU Law Comply, Document Generator, Tenderator/GrantBru, Platform (cross-cutting).

13. Spaak (Copenhagen/Brussels): VC-Backed AI-Native PA Platform

What Brubru can learnSpaak is Brubru's most direct competitor and the highest strategic threat due to VC backing (EUR 1.5m pre-seed from Node.vc, March 2025) and rapid team scaling (15+ open roles across engineering, product, commercial, and operations as of January 2026). Founded in early 2024 in Copenhagen, they expanded to Brussels in December 2025 (Rue du Commerce 31) with Cyrille Mai Thanh as Managing Director Europe. They claim 100+ PA teams onboarded and aggregate 698+ sources (social media, parliaments, municipalities, organisations). Their product covers political monitoring, an AI assistant for stakeholder mapping/briefs/bios, public consultations tracking (launched February 2026), cross-platform debate aggregation, and politician activity analytics. However, Spaak lacks Brubru's deeper legislative workflow tools: no amendment editor (Amendator), no compliance checker (EU Law Comply), no tender/grant tool (Tenderator), no AI document generator (position papers, MEP briefings, talking points), no legislative predictions with EP group vote breakdown, no QMV calculator, and no multi-source EU institutional calendar. Their strength is execution speed backed by capital and a growing team; Brubru's advantage is feature depth and institutional workflow integration that would take Spaak significant time to replicate. Key lesson: Spaak's aggressive go-to-market (events, webinars, content marketing, hiring dedicated sales staff) shows that product alone is not enough, distribution and brand presence in Brussels matter enormously.
ShortMy EU Bubble: Match Spaak's source aggregation breadth, ensure Brubru's RSS/monitoring covers social media channels (X, BlueSky, Instagram) alongside institutional sources. Their "698 sources, one overview" is a strong marketing claim.
ShortPlatform: Improve public-facing content marketing and LinkedIn presence. Spaak's highest-engagement posts are about team growth and product launches: Brubru should showcase unique features (Amendator, Predictions, EU Law Comply) that Spaak cannot match.
MidBrubru Chat: Develop a dedicated "AI Assistant" mode similar to Spaak's "The Assistant": a co-pilot for everyday PA tasks (stakeholder mapping, policy briefs, MEP bios) that is prominently positioned and easy to discover, not buried within the chat interface.
MidPlatform: Build a demo/booking flow (Spaak uses spaak.ai/booking) and webinar programme targeting Brussels PA community. Spaak's Brussels Sustainability Club webinar model, partnering with existing communities for live demos, is highly effective and low-cost.
LongPlatform: Consider strategic positioning for acqui-hire or partnership. Spaak's VC-backed growth trajectory means they will need to expand feature coverage; Brubru's unique modules (Amendator, EU Law Comply, Tenderator, Predictions) represent exactly the capabilities they would need to build or acquire. Maintain LinkedIn visibility with CEO and let the orbit strategy play out from a position of feature strength.

Strategic Insight

Five patterns emerge across all competitors:

  1. The Stakeholder CRM gap is universal. Dixit, DeHavilland, POLITICO Pro, Quorum, SAVOIRR -- nearly every competitor has a contact/stakeholder database. Brubru is the only serious platform without one. This should be the #1 new feature priority.
  2. Retention comes from daily habits. POLITICO Pro's 91% renewal comes from the morning briefing. Policy-Insider.AI's PQ Insights newsletter drives engagement. Brubru needs a "daily touch" -- an AI-generated morning email summarising overnight changes to tracked files.
  3. The advocacy workflow has a missing step. Brubru covers monitor > analyse > draft > comply > predict > bid. But it misses engage -- actually contacting officials, tracking interactions, measuring impact. Adding it closes the full loop.
  4. The real competitive threat is foundation models, not SaaS competitors. No SaaS competitor will replicate 35 scrapers and 10 workflow verticals quickly. The existential risk is Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google adding EU policy plugins via MCP, custom GPTs, or Gems. Defence is threefold: (1) depth of structured pipelines that general plugins cannot match, (2) domain engineering (Akoma Ntoso XML, QMV calculation, compliance gap analysis) beyond data access, and (3) the system-of-record layer: user-accumulated dossiers, contacts, and documents that live inside Brubru and nowhere else.
  5. Nobody has a dossier management system. The system of record for EU affairs today is fragmented across Outlook, Excel, shared drives, and mental models. No competitor -- not Dixit, not POLITICO Pro, not FiscalNote -- offers a user-owned dossier workspace where tracking, amendments, contacts, predictions, and documents accumulate into an irreplaceable asset. Brubru's path from workflow platform (Layer 3) to system of record (Layer 4) requires one architectural shift: tie all features together around the dossier concept. A lobbyist with 50 annotated dossiers, 200 MEP contacts, and 18 months of prediction history inside Brubru will never leave.
