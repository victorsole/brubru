The End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation: circularity from the factory floor to the scrapyard

Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 merges two ageing directives, the 2000 ELV Directive and the 2005 3R type-approval Directive, into a single directly-applicable law covering the whole life of a vehicle: circular design and recycled-content targets verified at type-approval, a Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport, extended producer responsibility, mandatory collection and treatment of end-of-life vehicles, and EU-wide controls on exporting used vehicles to third countries. Signed at Strasbourg on 8 July 2026, it entered into force today.

Overview

What the End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation is, why it replaces two directives, and how it is structured

One law for the whole vehicle life cycle

Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 lays down circularity requirements on vehicle design and production, and on the management of end-of-life vehicles. Article 1 states its subject matter plainly: circularity requirements on vehicle design and production related to reusability, recyclability and recoverability and the use of recycled content, to be verified at type-approval of vehicles; information and labelling requirements on parts, components and materials; extended producer responsibility; collection and treatment of end-of-life vehicles; and the export of used vehicles from the Union to third countries. It is the first single instrument to cover design, production, use and end-of-life treatment in one place.

Ordinary legislative procedure, adopted by a large margin

Unlike a Commission implementing act, this is a Regulation adopted through the ordinary legislative procedure on the joint legal basis of Article 114 TFEU, the internal market, and Article 192(1) TFEU, the environment, in relation to Articles 14 to 36. The Commission proposed the text as COM(2023) 451 final on 13 July 2023, procedure 2023/0284(COD). The joint ENVI-IMCO committee, co-rapporteurs Jens Gieseke (EPP, Germany) and Paulius Saudargas (EPP, Lithuania), steered it through committee, trilogue and a provisional agreement reached on 12 December 2025. The European Parliament adopted the final text on 18 June 2026 by 437 votes to 112 with 20 abstentions. It was signed at Strasbourg on 8 July 2026, published in OJ L, 2026/1738 on 24 July 2026, and entered into force on 13 August 2026, the twentieth day after publication (Article 59(1)).

In force today, but only one article actually binds today

Entry into force and application are two different things under this Regulation, and the gap between them is unusually wide. Article 59(1) brings the Regulation into force on the twentieth day after OJ publication, 13 August 2026. Article 59(2) then states it "shall apply from 1 September 2028", with the delegated-act empowerment articles applying earlier, from 14 September 2026, Article 55 applying later, from 1 September 2032, and Article 53 applying immediately, from 13 August 2026. Article 53 replaces Annex I of the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 with the new Annex XII to this Regulation, extending mercury, cadmium and lead concentration limits explicitly to batteries incorporated in vehicles. In parallel, Article 57(1)(a) switches off four specific entries, 5(a), 5(b)(i), 5(b)(ii) and 16, in Annex II to the old ELV Directive 2000/53/EC from the same date. Everything else in this Regulation, circular design, recycled content, the vehicle passport, extended producer responsibility, collection and treatment duties, and export controls, is still on a phased-in clock reaching as far out as 1 September 2036.

Replacing two directives with one directly-applicable Regulation

Directive 2000/53/EC on end-of-life vehicles and Directive 2005/64/EC on the type-approval of vehicles for reusability, recyclability and recoverability, the "3R Directive", are both repealed by this Regulation, on staggered clocks set out in Article 57: the 2000 Directive with effect from 1 September 2028, with several of its provisions kept alive on their own separate clocks running as far as 31 August 2032; the 2005 Directive with effect from 31 August 2032. Recital 118 explains the deferral for the 3R Directive: since the type-approval rules on reusability, recyclability and recoverability are now all contained in this Regulation, keeping the old Directive alive a little longer gives manufacturers and type-approval authorities time to transition.

Context

Scope and definitions (Chapter I, Arts 1-3)

Who this Regulation applies to, when, and the load-bearing terms it is built from

Phased scope by vehicle category (Art 2)

The Regulation applies from the start to cars and vans, categories M1 and N1. Special purpose vehicles of those categories join from 1 September 2029. Buses, lorries, trailers, categories M2, M3, N2, N3 and O, and L-category two- and three-wheelers, join from 1 September 2031, though several core articles, including Articles 4 to 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 21, 22, 33 and 35(2), simply do not apply to those later categories at all (Article 2(3)-(4)). Article 2(2) excludes small-series vehicles, vehicles for the armed services only, vehicles for civil defence, fire and public-order services, vehicles of historical interest, vehicles of special cultural interest, trailer caravans, and certain small-volume special purpose vehicles and pedal cycles.

Fifty-plus definitions, Article 3

Article 3 defines the vocabulary the rest of the Regulation runs on: vehicle, end-of-life vehicle, reusability, recyclability, recoverability, plastic, pre-consumer and post-consumer waste, removal, e-drive motor, authorised treatment facility, shredding, treatment, repair and maintenance operator, producer, producer responsibility organisation, and more. An end-of-life vehicle is, at its core, a vehicle that is waste under Article 3, point (1), of the Waste Framework Directive 2008/98/EC, or that is waste according to the criteria set out in Annex I to this Regulation.

Two Treaty legal bases, deliberately

The Regulation rests on Article 114 TFEU, harmonising the internal market for vehicle type-approval, and Article 192(1) TFEU, environmental protection, for the provisions on management of end-of-life vehicles, Articles 14 to 36. Recital 509 of the preamble explains the reasoning: replacing two directives with one Regulation covering design, manufacturing, placement on the market and end-of-life treatment in an integrated way avoids barriers to trade, distortions of competition, and supports the Union market for secondary raw materials used in new vehicles.

Circularity by design (Chapter II, Arts 4-7)

What a vehicle has to be built like before it can be type-approved from 1 September 2032

Reusability, recyclability and recoverability rates (Art 4)

Each vehicle belonging to a type-approved type from 1 September 2032 must be constructed so that it is reusable or recyclable to a minimum of 85% by mass, and reusable or recoverable to a minimum of 95% by mass. Manufacturers must collect supply-chain material data, verify it, and calculate the rates using a Commission methodology due by 28 February 2029 or, until then, international standard ISO 22628:2002 combined with Annex III Part A.

Substances of concern, lead, mercury, cadmium and hexavalent chromium (Art 5)

From 1 September 2032, no type-approved vehicle type, or new part or component placed on the market for it, may contain lead, mercury, cadmium or hexavalent chromium, subject to derogations in Annex IV up to fixed maximum concentration values. These substance rules do not apply to batteries incorporated in vehicles, which are governed instead by the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (Article 5(5)). A separate Commission report, due by 14 February 2028, will examine substances of concern more broadly, assisted by the European Chemicals Agency.

Minimum recycled plastic content (Art 6)

The plastic in each new vehicle type type-approved from 1 September 2032 must contain a minimum of 15% recycled content by weight from post-consumer plastic waste, rising to 25% from 1 September 2036. At least 20% of that target must come specifically from plastics recycled from end-of-life vehicles or from parts removed during the use phase, not from generic post-consumer plastic waste (Article 6(2)). Elastomers from tyres and thermosets other than polyurethane cushioning foams are excluded from the calculation. From 14 August 2030, recycled content may also come from an installation in a third country, provided it meets the Annex XIII criteria and is independently audited at least every five years. The Commission must also assess, by 30 September 2027, whether to set a separate minimum-recycled-steel requirement.

Designed so batteries and e-drive motors can come out again (Art 7)

From 1 September 2032, new vehicle types must be designed so that authorised treatment facilities can remove, without hindrance, the parts and components listed in Part C of Annex VIII during the waste phase, and so that electric vehicle batteries, battery packs and e-drive motors can be removed and replaced in a readily and non-destructive manner during both the use phase and the waste phase, by authorised treatment facilities or by repair and maintenance operators. Manufacturers may not use software updates to hinder that removal and replacement, unless another piece of Union law says otherwise (Article 7(5)).

Manufacturer obligations and the vehicle passport (Chapter III, Arts 8-13)

Documentation, a public circularity strategy, disclosure, labelling, and a fourteenth EU product passport

Type-approval documentation (Art 8)

Manufacturers must demonstrate that new vehicle types are type-approved under Regulation (EU) 2018/858, Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 and this Regulation, and must submit documentation showing compliance with Articles 4 to 7, the recycled-content declaration under Article 10, and the removal-and-replacement information under Article 11, to the type-approval authority alongside the application for type-approval.

A public circularity strategy, updated every five years (Art 9)

From 1 September 2029, each manufacturer must draw up a circularity strategy at manufacturer level, describing the actions it will take to meet the Chapter II circularity requirements, and provide a copy to type-approval authorities and the Commission within 30 days of drawing it up. The strategy is updated at least every five years, and the Commission makes strategies and their updates publicly available, except for confidential information. The Commission itself must publish a report on the circularity of the automotive sector by 31 August 2033, and at least every six years after that.

Recycled-content declaration (Art 10)

Manufacturers must declare, per vehicle type, the recycled content share of neodymium, dysprosium, praseodymium, terbium, samarium, nickel, cobalt and boron in permanent magnets in e-drive motors, plus aluminium, magnesium, steel and plastics, distinguishing pre-consumer from post-consumer waste for plastic components heavier than 100 grams. The declaration requirement lapses for a given material once a binding recycled-content target starts applying to it under Article 6.

Removal and replacement information, free of charge (Art 11)

From 1 September 2029, manufacturers must give waste management operators, publishers of technical information, and repair and maintenance operators unrestricted, standardised, non-discriminatory access to information enabling safe removal and replacement of electric vehicle batteries, fluids and liquids, the Part C Annex VIII parts, critical-raw-material-bearing parts, and digitally coded components that would otherwise block third-party repair. That information must be provided free of charge, though reasonable administrative charges for maintaining communication platforms are allowed.

Coded labelling for parts, components and materials (Art 12)

Manufacturers and their suppliers must use the component and material coding standards listed in Annex VII to label and identify parts, components and materials of vehicles, so that dismantlers and recyclers can identify what they are handling without guesswork.

The Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport, the fourteenth EU product passport (Art 13)

From 1 September 2032, every vehicle placed on the market must carry a Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport, accessible free of charge, aligned, interoperable and, where possible, integrated with other environmental passports required by Union law: the battery passport under Article 77 of the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, the passport referred to in Article 3, point (68), of the Euro 7 Regulation (EU) 2024/1257, and passports established under the ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781. It must carry the Article 11 removal-and-replacement information, the parts containing lead, mercury, cadmium or hexavalent chromium under the Article 5(5) derogation, the Article 10 recycled-content declaration, and the official spare-parts catalogue. Information already accessible through another of those passports is not duplicated, provided interoperability is ensured (Article 13(3)). By 14 August 2030, the Commission must adopt implementing acts on the passport's technical design, access rules and data-carrier location.

Extended producer responsibility (Chapter IV, Section 2, Arts 16-22)

Who pays for collection and treatment, and how those costs are shared out fairly

Competent authorities and authorised treatment facilities (Arts 14-15)

Member States designate competent authorities responsible for monitoring compliance under this Chapter, and must notify the Commission of their names and addresses by 30 June 2027. Any establishment intending to treat end-of-life vehicles needs a permit under Directive 2008/98/EC and must have the capacity to meet the Article 26 treatment obligations; permits also indicate whether the facility can issue the electronic certificate of destruction under Article 25.

Producer responsibility, individually or collectively (Arts 16-18)

From 1 September 2029, producers have extended producer responsibility for vehicles they make available on a Member State's market for the first time, consistent with Articles 8 and 8a of the Waste Framework Directive 2008/98/EC. Producers may fulfil that responsibility individually or appoint a producer responsibility organisation, which itself needs authorisation from the competent authority and must keep proprietary producer data confidential.

National producer registers, by 31 August 2029 (Art 19)

By 31 August 2029, each Member State must establish, or repurpose, a register of producers linked to other national registers, and the Commission must publish a website linking to all of them. Producers must register in every Member State where they first make a vehicle available, and competent authorities must grant registrations within 12 weeks of receiving a complete application.

Financial responsibility and fee modulation (Arts 20-21)

Producers' financial contributions must cover collection, transport and treatment costs, awareness campaigns, the certificate-of-destruction notification system, and administrative reporting costs (Article 20). Where fulfilment is collective, Article 21 requires those contributions to be modulated using at least six criteria: the vehicle's weight excluding its electric vehicle battery, its recyclability and reusability rate, the time needed to dismantle it at an authorised treatment facility, the share of materials that prevent high-quality recycling, the percentage of recycled content under Articles 6 and 10, and the presence and amount of the Article 5(4) restricted substances.

Cross-border cost allocation (Art 22)

Where an M1 or N1 vehicle becomes end-of-life in a Member State other than where it was first placed on the market, and no local producer can be identified, the manufacturer itself must ensure collection and reporting costs are covered, through a designated authorised representative for the cross-border mechanism in each Member State.

Collection and treatment (Chapter IV, Sections 3-4, Arts 23-35)

From free delivery at the collection point to reuse, recycling and recovery targets

Free collection and delivery (Arts 23-24)

Producers must ensure every vehicle they first made available becomes collectable when it reaches end-of-life, through collection systems covering the whole Member State. A vehicle owner must deliver an end-of-life vehicle to an authorised treatment facility or collection point without undue delay, and that delivery is free of charge unless the vehicle is missing essential parts, such as its e-drive motor, battery, engine or major bodywork, or contains added waste (Article 24).

The electronic certificate of destruction (Art 25)

Authorised treatment facilities issue a certificate of destruction, in the Annex X template, to the last vehicle owner upon delivery, in electronic format, notified through an electronic procedure to the relevant national authorities. A vehicle registration authority may only cancel a registration once it has received that certificate, and a certificate issued in one Member State is recognised in every other Member State.

Depollution, mandatory part removal and shredding rules (Arts 26-30, 34)

Authorised treatment facilities must depollute every end-of-life vehicle within 30 days of delivery, removing fluids, batteries and restricted-substance-bearing parts under Article 28. From 1 September 2029, Article 29 requires the Part C Annex VIII parts, those with reuse, remanufacturing or refurbishment potential, to be removed in a non-destructive manner before shredding, unless a facility demonstrates disproportionate cost or that post-shredder technology separates materials just as efficiently. Removed parts fit for reuse must be assessed, labelled and are not treated as waste (Article 30). From 1 September 2029, non-inert shredder residue exceeding Annex VIII limit values may not go to landfill (Article 34).

Trading used, remanufactured and refurbished parts (Art 31)

From 1 September 2029, any economic operator selling used, remanufactured or refurbished spare parts must keep the labelling applied by the authorised treatment facility, and, where sold to consumers, must ensure the parts function and perform as required and comply with Directive (EU) 2019/771 on the sale of goods, whatever sales channel is used, including online.

Reuse, recycling and recovery targets (Art 33)

From 1 January 2030, Member States must ensure waste management operators achieve a combined reuse-and-recovery rate of at least 95%, and a combined reuse-and-recycling rate of at least 85%, both measured by average weight per vehicle per year and excluding batteries. From 1 January 2032, a separate yearly target requires at least 30% of the total average weight of plastics in end-of-life vehicles to be recycled, excluding elastomers, non-cushioning thermosets, and plastics contaminated above the Regulation (EU) 2019/1021 persistent-organic-pollutant thresholds.

Transfer of ownership and vehicle status (Chapter V, Arts 36-37)

Proving a used vehicle is not, in fact, an end-of-life vehicle

Documentation on every ownership transfer (Art 36)

An economic operator transferring ownership of a used vehicle within the Union, whatever the sales channel, including dedicated auctions, distance contracts or online platforms, must present documentation showing the vehicle is not an end-of-life vehicle, either proof it is roadworthy or an Annex I assessment. Private sellers only need to provide that documentation where the vehicle has been declared a total economic loss by an insurer, or the sale happens entirely online without a physical handover.

Who assesses "end-of-life" status (Art 37)

When assessing accident damage, insurers or their automotive experts must also assess whether the vehicle is now an end-of-life vehicle under the Annex I criteria. Competent authorities may separately require an owner to prove a used vehicle is not end-of-life, through an Annex I assessment or a valid roadworthiness certificate. The Commission may later narrow the Annex I criteria by delegated act, if traceability, repairability and safety technology justifies it (Article 37(3)).

Export of used vehicles to third countries (Chapter VI, Arts 38-46)

Automated customs checks against roadworthiness and end-of-life status, from 1 September 2031

Only roadworthy, non-end-of-life vehicles may leave the Union (Art 39)

From 1 September 2031, used vehicles may only be exported if they are not end-of-life vehicles and are roadworthy at the date of lodging the export declaration, unless they have been recognised as vehicles of special cultural interest under Article 2(2)(f) and Annex II. Exporters must provide the vehicle's VIN, the Member State where it was last registered, and a statement or certificate confirming those conditions are met.

Automated verification and the MOVE-HUB system (Arts 40-46)

Before releasing a vehicle for export, customs authorities must electronically and automatically verify roadworthiness using the VIN, through electronic systems built around the Commission's new MOVE-HUB, which interconnects national vehicle registers and roadworthiness systems with the EU Single Window Environment for Customs. Where the automatic check flags a mismatch, or where there are reasonable grounds to suspect non-compliance, customs authorities must suspend release and notify the competent authorities, who then have four working days to intervene before the vehicle is automatically released (Article 43). A release for export is explicitly not proof of compliance with Union law (Article 43(2)). The Commission must adopt the MOVE-HUB implementing acts by 30 September 2028, with the interconnection with national systems operational within two years after that, and the customs interconnection within four years.

Enforcement and delegated powers (Chapters VII-VIII, Arts 47-52)

Risk-based inspections, cross-border cooperation, penalties by 2029, and how the Commission may update the annexes

Inspections and enforcement cooperation (Arts 47-48)

Member States must run inspection strategies covering authorised treatment facilities, collection points, and any other operator that might be involved in collection, treatment or export of end-of-life vehicles, or in selling used spare parts. At least 10% of treatment facilities and collection points must be inspected every calendar year. Member States must also cooperate bilaterally and multilaterally to prevent illegal treatment and export, and to address the issue of missing vehicles, notifying the Commission of the staff responsible for that cooperation.

Penalties, effective, proportionate and dissuasive, by 1 September 2029 (Art 49)

By 1 September 2029, Member States must lay down penalties for infringements of a long, named list of core provisions, including Articles 15, 16, 18, 22 to 31, 33, 34, 36, 37 and 39, and notify the Commission of those rules without delay, and of any subsequent amendment.

Reporting to the Commission (Art 50)

From 1 September 2029, Member States must make aggregated annual data publicly available, machine-readable and searchable, within 18 months of the reporting period's end, covering everything from vehicle registrations and certificates of destruction issued to Article 33 target attainment and critical-raw-material quantities recovered. A separate five-yearly report, first due by 1 September 2032, covers reuse incentives, penalties applied, inspection results and practical difficulties applying the end-of-life and used-vehicle definitions.

Delegated acts and the committee procedure (Arts 51-52)

The power to adopt delegated acts amending or supplementing named articles, including the substance restrictions, recycled-plastic derogations, removal-and-replacement annex, labelling annex, fee-modulation criteria, and cross-border cost mechanism, is conferred on the Commission for five years from 13 August 2026, tacitly renewable. Neither the European Parliament nor the Council may object within two months of notification, extendable by a further two months. Implementing acts go through the committee established under Article 39 of the Waste Framework Directive 2008/98/EC, acting under the examination procedure of Regulation (EU) No 182/2011.

Amendments to other EU laws (Chapter IX, Arts 53-55)

Three separate laws amended, one of them, Article 53, already applying today

Article 53: the Batteries Regulation, applying from today, 13 August 2026

Article 53 replaces Annex I of the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 in accordance with the new Annex XII to this Regulation, extending the mercury, cadmium and lead concentration limits that previously governed batteries generally so that they expressly cover batteries incorporated in vehicles too. Under Article 59(2), Article 53 is the only substantive provision of this Regulation applying from entry into force, 13 August 2026, rather than from a later phase-in date.

Article 54: the Market Surveillance Regulation, from 1 September 2028

Article 54 deletes points 10 and 11 of Annex II to the Market Surveillance Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, tidying up cross-references now superseded by this Regulation's own compliance and enforcement architecture.

Article 55: type-approval frameworks, from 1 September 2032

Article 55 amends Annex II to the Type-Approval Framework Regulation (EU) 2018/858 and Annex II to the Motorcycles Regulation (EU) No 168/2013, in accordance with Annex XI to this Regulation, aligning both frameworks' type-approval annexes with the new circularity requirements once they take full effect at type-approval level.

A staggered repeal, not a clean break

Article 57 does not simply switch off the old ELV Directive on a single date. Several of its provisions survive independently: Article 4(2) and Annex II until 31 August 2032, though four specific Annex II entries ceased on 13 August 2026; Article 5(4) second subparagraph, Article 6(3) second subparagraph, Article 7(1), and Article 8(3)-(4) until 31 August 2029; Article 6(3) first subparagraph and Annex I until 31 August 2029; Article 7(2)(b) until 31 December 2030; and parts of Article 9 until 31 August 2029 or 31 August 2031. Article 57(3) directs that references to the repealed directives be read as references to this Regulation, using the correlation tables in Annex XV. By 31 December 2035, the Commission must report to Parliament and Council on the Regulation's application and impact, with an evaluation covering, among other things, whether to extend scope to L-category and heavier vehicle categories sooner, whether Article 33 targets need revising, and how effective the Article 49 penalties have proven.

Key definitions (Art 3)

The load-bearing concepts that drive the rest of the Regulation

Six definitions worth memorising

End-of-life vehicle
A vehicle that is waste under Article 3, point (1), of Directive 2008/98/EC, or that is waste according to the criteria set out in Annex I to this Regulation (Art 3(2)).
Reusability, recyclability, recoverability
The possibility, respectively, for reuse, recycling or recovery of parts, components or materials diverted from an end-of-life vehicle (Art 3(5)-(7)).
Producer
Any manufacturer, importer or distributor who, whatever selling technique is used, makes available a vehicle for the first time for distribution or use within a Member State on a professional basis (Art 3(23)).
Authorised treatment facility
Any establishment or undertaking permitted, under Directive 2008/98/EC and this Regulation, to carry out collection, storage and treatment operations on end-of-life vehicles, including their parts and components (Art 3(16)).
Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport
The free, interoperable digital record every vehicle placed on the market must carry from 1 September 2032, aligned with the battery, Euro 7, and ESPR product passports (Art 13).
Extended producer responsibility
The obligation, from 1 September 2029, on producers to ensure the vehicles they first make available on a Member State's market are collected and treated when they become end-of-life, and that the Article 33 targets are met (Art 16).

Same recovery ambition, far more machinery underneath it

The headline 85% recyclability and 95% recoverability figures are not new; they carry over from the old 3R Directive. What changes is everything wrapped around them: a binding recycled-content floor tied specifically to end-of-life vehicles, a digital passport that has to talk to three other EU product-passport regimes, harmonised producer registers and fee modulation, an EU-wide electronic certificate of destruction, and, for the first time, an automated customs check on every used vehicle leaving the Union.

Glossary

Load-bearing terms as used in Regulation (EU) 2026/1738

Vehicle
Any vehicle as defined in Article 3, point (15), of Regulation (EU) 2018/858 or listed in Article 4(2) of Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 (Art 3(1)).
End-of-life vehicle
A vehicle that is waste under Article 3, point (1), of Directive 2008/98/EC, or that is waste according to the Annex I criteria (Art 3(2)).
Producer responsibility organisation
A legal entity that financially, or financially and operationally, organises the fulfilment of extended producer responsibility obligations on behalf of several producers (Art 3(24)).
Authorised treatment facility
Any establishment or undertaking permitted, under Directive 2008/98/EC and this Regulation, to collect, store and treat end-of-life vehicles, including their parts and components (Art 3(16)).
Certificate of destruction
The electronic document, in the Annex X template, an authorised treatment facility issues to the last owner on delivery of an end-of-life vehicle, needed before de-registration (Art 25).
Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport
The free, interoperable digital record every vehicle placed on the market must carry from 1 September 2032, aligned with the battery, Euro 7 and ESPR passports (Art 13).
MOVE-HUB
The Commission's electronic system exchanging vehicle registration and roadworthiness data between Member States and the EU Single Window Environment for Customs, to automate export checks (Art 46).
Extended producer responsibility
The obligation, from 1 September 2029, on producers to ensure vehicles they first made available are collected and treated at end-of-life, and that the Article 33 targets are met (Art 16).
Authorised representative for the cross-border mechanism
A natural or legal person appointed by a manufacturer, in a Member State, to fulfil cross-border cost obligations under Article 22.
Circularity strategy
The manufacturer-level document, from 1 September 2029, describing the actions taken to meet the Chapter II circularity requirements, updated at least every five years and made public by the Commission (Art 9).

Legislative timeline

From today's entry into force to the Commission's first full review, due by the end of 2035

24 July 2026
Published in OJ L, 2026/1738.
13 August 2026
Entry into force. Article 53 amends the Batteries Regulation; four Annex II entries of the old ELV Directive cease to apply.
14 September 2026
Delegated-act empowerments apply.
1 September 2028
The Regulation as a whole applies. Directive 2000/53/EC repealed, subject to survivor provisions.
31 August 2029 / 1 September 2029
Producer registers established; extended producer responsibility, circularity strategy, removal information, used-parts labelling and national penalties all start applying.
1 January 2030
Reuse-and-recovery (95%) and reuse-and-recycling (85%) targets apply.
14 August 2030
Third-country recycling installations can count towards recycled-content targets, subject to Annex XIII and independent audits.
1 September 2031
Export controls on used vehicles apply; heavier vehicle categories and L-category two- and three-wheelers enter scope.
1 September 2032
Type-approval circularity, recycled-plastic content (15%), the Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport, and Article 55 all apply.
1 September 2036
Minimum recycled-plastic content rises to 25%.
31 December 2035
Deadline for the Commission's first full review report, Article 56.

Art 13 is the 14th EU product passport
Batteries Regulation
CELEX 32023R1542, amended by Art 53 today
First of a growing vehicle-circularity family, but already cross-linked twice

This is the first canon entry Brubru has written in the vehicle-circularity legal family. It does not stand alone, though. Article 13 creates the Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport, the fourteenth distinct EU product passport, aligned and interoperable with the passports required under the ESPR, the Euro 7 Regulation and, above all, the Batteries Regulation, so it belongs alongside Brubru's Digital Product Passport regime hub. And Article 53, the only provision of this Regulation that actually applies today, 13 August 2026, directly amends Annex I of the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, extending mercury, cadmium and lead limits to batteries incorporated in vehicles, so the two laws are legally entangled from day one, not just thematically related.
