Overview

What the Digital Product Passport is, and why the EU built it as a registry, not a document

A framework, not a single law

The Digital Product Passport (DPP) is not one regulation: it is a regime built by the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, whose Articles 9 to 15 create the passport itself, the central registry, the public web portal and the customs check. ESPR sets no product requirement on its own: those arrive sector by sector through delegated acts, with the battery sector, under the pre-existing Batteries Regulation, going first.

The result is a single electronic record that travels with a product for its whole life: composition and material origin, repair and disassembly information, carbon footprint, and compliance data, all reachable from one QR code or similar data carrier and readable in different depth depending on who is asking.

The registry: live since 20 July 2026

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 built the central passport registry required by ESPR Article 13, and the production environment went live on 20 July 2026, one day inside the 19 July legal deadline. Every economic operator obliged to issue a passport must register it there, either through a secure user interface or an API, and the registry hands back a unique registration identifier that must accompany the product through customs.

Why sectors join on a rolling timetable

ESPR is a framework Regulation: it hands the Commission the power to make products passport-ready sector by sector through delegated and implementing acts, rather than mandating every product category on the same day. Batteries move first because the Batteries Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, already had its own passport article, Article 77, predating ESPR itself. Iron and steel, construction products, textiles, aluminium, tyres, furniture, mattresses, toys, detergents and ICT products each get their own delegated act and their own mandatory date.

How the registry works

The central database every passport is registered in, live since 20 July 2026

DPP Registry: production environment

The live registry where economic operators register each digital product passport.

  • Legal basis: Reg. (EU) 2024/1781 Art. 13; Reg. (EU) 2026/1778
  • Live since: 20 July 2026
  • Legal deadline to establish: 19 July 2026 (ESPR Art. 13(1))
  • Registration pathways: secure user interface, or API
  • Who must register: the economic operator placing the product on the market or putting it into service (Art. 13(4))
DPP Registry: acceptance and testing environment

The testing environment operators use to integrate before going live.

  • Purpose: integration testing without writing to the production registry
  • Available since: 20 July 2026
The unique registration identifier

What the registry returns once an operator uploads the required data, and what it does not mean.

  • Created by: ESPR Article 13(5)
  • Returned: automatically on upload of the Art. 13(1) and 13(2) data
  • Not proof of compliance: Art. 13(5) states the communication is not proof of compliance with the ESPR or other Union law
  • Customs use: must be provided to customs for release for free circulation (Art. 15(1))
Which products the registry covers

Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 Article 1 is the authoritative scope list.

  • ESPR delegated acts: Reg. (EU) 2024/1781 Art. 4
  • Batteries: Reg. (EU) 2023/1542 Art. 77
  • Construction products: Reg. (EU) 2024/3110 Art. 76
  • Toys: Reg. (EU) 2025/2509 Art. 19
  • Detergents and end-user surfactants: Reg. (EU) 2026/405 Art. 21
  • Catch-all: any other Union law requiring a passport and its registration (Art. 1(1)(f))
Customs controls and the Single Window

How the passport becomes a border condition rather than a documentation exercise.

  • Legal basis: ESPR Article 15
  • Requirement: provide the unique registration identifier to customs for release for free circulation
  • Customs check: identifier and commodity code must match the registry
  • Interconnection: EU CSW-CERTEX under Reg. (EU) 2022/2399
  • Deadline for interconnection: within four years of the entry into force of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778

The 71 battery data points

The battery passport is the first sector's full schema, and the template later sectors are expected to follow

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 77, sets out 71 individual data points a battery passport must be able to carry, from a unique identifier through to end-of-life instructions. Each one has a legal source in the Regulation and its own applicability rule across the three battery categories the passport already covers: electric vehicle, light means of transport, and industrial batteries. Because batteries are the first product group with a binding passport deadline, this schema is also the closest thing the regime has to a worked example of what a full digital product passport looks like in practice.

What it means per audience

The same passport, read at different depth depending on who is asking

Economic operators

What the passport requires of manufacturers, importers and distributors: create the passport, register it, keep the data accurate, complete and up to date (ESPR Art. 9(1)), and give customs the registration identifier.

Consumers

What consumers can read from a passport before buying: composition, circularity, environmental impact, repair and end-of-life information, accessible before the buyer is bound by a contract (ESPR Art. 9(2)(e)).

Repairers and recyclers

What the passport gives the people who keep a product alive or take it apart: material composition, disassembly and repair information, and end-of-life routes.

Public authorities

How market surveillance and customs authorities use the registry: direct access under ESPR Art. 13(6) and verification at release for free circulation.
