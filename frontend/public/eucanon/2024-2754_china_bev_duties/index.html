EU's countervailing duties on Chinese battery electric vehicles

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 imposes definitive anti-subsidy duties on Chinese BEV imports, finding that three major producers (BYD, Geely, SAIC) and Tesla's Chinese factory received countervailable state support, and that Chinese imports threatened to materially injure the EU automotive industry.

What the Regulation does

Five operative effects in three articles and one annex

Imposes definitive countervailing duties
Article 1 lays down additional ad valorem duties ranging from 7.8% to 35.3% on new BEVs from China (TARIC code 8703 80 10 10), differentiated by exporting group and applied on top of the standard 6.5% customs duty.
Replaces provisional Regulation 2024/1866
The provisional countervailing duty imposed by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1866 of 8 July 2024 is superseded and amended. Definitive rates take effect from 31 October 2024.
Releases provisional duty securities
Article 2 directs customs authorities to release amounts secured under provisional measures. Because the injury finding is "threat" (not actual injury), Article 16(2) of the Basic Regulation bars retroactive collection.
Confirms threat-of-material-injury finding
The Commission finds the EU BEV industry is not yet materially injured but faces an imminent threat under Article 8(8) of the Basic Regulation, based on Chinese market share reaching ~25%, price undercutting of 12.7%, and EU industry profitability of -20.5% in Q3 2023.
Rejects all five undertaking offers
The Commission declines price undertaking offers from the CCCME (collective), BYD, Geely, SAIC, and other cooperating producers, citing product complexity, high monitoring risk, cross-compensation risk, and SAIC's limited cooperation.

Why no lesser-duty rule?

In EU anti-dumping law, the "lesser-duty rule" can cap the duty at the level needed to remove injury rather than the full dumping margin. The Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation (Reg (EU) 2016/1037) contains no equivalent provision. Countervailing duties are therefore imposed at the full subsidy rate found, which is why BYD's 17.0% duty equals exactly BYD's 17.0% countervailable subsidy rate and SAIC's 35.3% duty equals SAIC's 35.3% subsidy rate.

Investigation procedure and timeline

From the start of the period considered to definitive measures -- approximately 4 years and 10 months

1 January 2020
Start of the "period considered" for injury trend analysis. Chinese BEV market share in the EU was at low single-digit percentages at this point.
1 October 2022
Start of the formal investigation period (IP). The one-year window 1 Oct 2022 to 30 Sep 2023 is used for all subsidy benefit calculations and price comparisons.
4 October 2023
Investigation initiated ex officio by the Commission. Notice of initiation published as OJ C/2023/160. Complaint lodged by the European Electric Vehicle Board (EEVB) on behalf of the EU BEV industry. Sampling initiated for exporting producers (BYD, Geely, SAIC selected) and EU producers (three sampled).
30 September 2023
End of the investigation period. By this point, Chinese BEVs had reached approximately 25% of EU BEV registrations. EU industry profitability had deteriorated to approximately -20.5% in Q3 2023.
6 March 2024
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/785 published. Imports registered for potential retroactive duty collection pending the investigation's conclusion. Registration does not automatically trigger retroactive duties.
8 July 2024
Provisional measures imposed. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1866 published. Provisional countervailing duties applied, with securities collected at provisional rates pending the definitive determination.
29 October 2024
This Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 adopted by the European Commission. Definitive countervailing duty rates fixed; all five undertaking offers rejected. Investigation concluded approximately 12 months and 27 days after initiation.
30 October 2024
Regulation published in the Official Journal of the European Union as OJ L, 2024/2754.
31 October 2024
Definitive countervailing duties enter into force. Provisional duty securities released (not collected) under Article 2, consistent with the "threat" (not actual injury) finding. Definitive measures expected to remain in place for five years under Article 22 of the Basic Regulation, subject to review.

Product scope

What the duties apply to -- and what is excluded

Product definition (Article 1)

The product under investigation is "new battery electric vehicles, i.e. motor vehicles for the transport of persons, which are propelled exclusively by one or more electric motors, which have a battery pack attached to the vehicle and which are designed to be charged exclusively from an external power source (fully electric vehicles)", classifiable under:

  • CN code: ex 8703 80 10 (the "ex" prefix indicates the TARIC sub-code applies)
  • TARIC code: 8703 80 10 10 (with additional codes C179/C184-C190 for specific companies)

The Commission confirmed that Chinese-produced BEVs and EU-produced BEVs are "like products" within the meaning of Article 2(c) of the Basic Regulation -- they share the same basic physical and technical characteristics and the same end uses.

Products excluded from the duties

  • Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) -- which also have an internal combustion engine
  • Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) -- charged externally but with an internal combustion engine
  • Range-extended electric vehicles where the internal combustion engine is the primary propulsion source
  • Battery electric vehicles designed for the transport of goods (different CN code)
  • BEVs not originating in the People's Republic of China
  • Imports registered before the relevant registration date where specific conditions under Art 16(4) are not met (and Article 2 explicitly releases those provisional securities)

Product Control Numbers (PCNs)

To enable like-for-like price comparisons across a product category with enormous model diversity, the investigation used Product Control Numbers (PCNs). Each PCN encodes key technical characteristics including battery capacity, vehicle segment, and drive configuration. All undercutting and price suppression calculations in the injury analysis were carried out at PCN level, ensuring the 12.7% undercutting figure reflects genuine model-by-model comparisons rather than a simple average across very different vehicle types.

Subsidies found

Three categories of countervailable support identified across all sampled groups

Category A: preferential financing
Chinese BEV producers received loans from policy banks (China Development Bank, Export-Import Bank, Agricultural Development Bank) and state-owned commercial banks at below-market interest rates. The Commission used international benchmarks for comparable risk profiles to calculate the LTAR benefit.
  • Policy bank loans at sub-benchmark rates
  • State-owned commercial bank loans
  • Bonds issued at below-benchmark yields (where applicable)
Category B: direct grants and revenue foregone
Grants at all levels of government (central, provincial, local) and tax concessions form the second category of countervailable support. These are specific to the BEV and New Energy Vehicle (NEV) sector.
  • New Energy Vehicle (NEV) production subsidies
  • Technology innovation and R&D grants
  • Industrial development fund grants
  • Export-promotion grants
  • Corporate income tax rate reduced to 15% for high-tech enterprises (versus standard 25%)
  • CIT "two-free, three-half" holidays for new investments
  • VAT exemptions or reductions on inputs
Category C: goods and services for LTAR
Identified as the most significant category. Key inputs and factors of production provided at less than adequate remuneration, distorting production costs relative to a market benchmark.
  • Battery cells and battery packs (LFP -- Lithium Iron Phosphate) below international market benchmarks
  • Land use rights for production facilities below market-comparable reference prices
  • Industrial electricity tariffs below unsubsidised market levels in certain provinces
  • Aluminium and other raw materials through state-influenced supply chains

The legal finding: threat of injury, not actual injury

The central and most consequential legal conclusion of the investigation

Why the distinction matters: Article 16(2) of the Basic Regulation

The Commission found that the EU BEV industry was not yet experiencing material injury but faced a threat of material injury within the meaning of Article 8(8) of the Basic Regulation. This is not a technicality -- it has two major legal consequences:

  • No retroactive duties: Article 16(2) requires that provisional duty securities be released rather than collected when the finding is "threat" rather than "actual injury". This is implemented in Article 2 of this Regulation.
  • No collection under Registration Regulation 2024/785: The conditions of Article 16(4) (critical circumstances, massive import surge undermining remedial effects) were not met, so no retroactive duties were applied for the registration period.

For importers who paid provisional duty securities, this means a refund of those deposits.

Key injury indicators supporting the threat finding

Market share trajectory: Chinese BEV imports grew from low single-digit market share in 2020 to approximately 25% of EU BEV registrations by the end of the investigation period (September 2023). The pace of growth, not merely its endpoint, was central to the threat finding.

Price undercutting: Chinese BEV imports undercut EU industry prices by approximately 12.7% on a PCN-weighted average basis. This sustained price pressure constrains EU producers' ability to price for profitability and investment recovery.

EU industry profitability: The sampled EU producers saw profitability deteriorate from positive margins in 2020-2021 to approximately -20.5% by Q3 2023, driven by the combination of import volumes and price undercutting.

Forward-looking capacity: Substantial Chinese production capacity -- well in excess of current EU export volumes -- exists with clear intent and ability to export further. Without measures, the trajectory would imminently convert threat into actual material injury.

Investment at risk: EU producers committed large capital investments in BEV capacity during the period based on projected market growth. Subsidised Chinese competition threatened to undermine the return on those investments, with systemic industrial consequences for the EU automotive sector.

Causation and non-attribution

Six alternative causes assessed -- all rejected as breaking the causal link

Causal link established

The surge in subsidised Chinese BEV imports correlates directly in timing and magnitude with the deterioration in EU industry profitability and market share erosion. The 12.7% price undercutting is of sufficient magnitude to constrain EU pricing and erode margins. Market share growth from negligible to ~25% over three years is consistent with subsidisation enabling below-cost or below-reasonable-margin pricing.

Macro-economic conditions
Post-pandemic economic conditions affected all automotive producers. The Commission found this insufficient to explain the specific price undercutting and market share dynamics attributable to subsidised imports.
EU producers' own decisions
Arguments that EU producers' own pricing and model strategy choices contributed to their competitive difficulties were assessed and rejected as insufficient to break the causal link between subsidised imports and threat of injury.
Consumer preferences
Arguments about consumer preference for Chinese BEV brands were assessed. The Commission found that Chinese BEVs were gaining EU market acceptance, in part driven by price competitiveness attributable to subsidisation.
Other third-country imports
Imports from third countries other than China were assessed and found to be negligible or priced at market rates. They are not a contributing cause of the threat of injury.
EU producers' high structural costs
EU BEV production is structurally more expensive than Chinese production (higher labour costs, different supply chain). The Commission acknowledged this but found the subsidy-derived advantage is a distinct additional factor on top of any natural cost differential.
GOC consultations outcome
The Government of China requested and received consultations under Article 10(7) of the Basic Regulation. These did not result in a mutually agreed solution or in any finding that breaks the causal link.

Union interest

Article 31 of the Basic Regulation -- the interests of all EU stakeholders weighed

EU industry interest

The EU automotive sector (OEMs and their suppliers) directly benefits from measures that address the competitive distortion created by subsidised Chinese imports. Protection of EU BEV production capacity supports the EU's strategic industrial transition objectives. The Commission found no compelling reason in the Union interest to depart from this.

Importer and dealer interest

Importers and dealers of Chinese BEVs face higher costs under definitive measures. The Commission assessed their submissions and found that while margin compression would occur, the scale of the competitive distortion in the Union interest justifies measures. The BEV market has multiple domestic and non-Chinese sources that remain competitively available.

Consumer interest

Higher duties may raise the price of Chinese BEVs for EU consumers. The Commission found that the BEV market is competitive with multiple available sources, and that the long-term consumer interest -- in a viable EU BEV industry providing choice and competition over the full vehicle lifecycle -- outweighs short-term price effects from the duties.

Green Deal and strategic transition

The EU's European Green Deal (Fit for 55 package) and the transition away from internal combustion engines require a viable EU BEV manufacturing industry. The Commission explicitly found that allowing subsidised Chinese competition to undermine the EU industry's investment capacity would jeopardise the strategic transition objectives that underpin the Green Deal. The conclusion was that no compelling reason exists against imposing definitive measures in the Union interest.

Undertakings rejected

Five separate price undertaking offers -- all declined by the Commission

The five offers

The following parties submitted undertaking offers structured around a Minimum Import Price (MIP) mechanism, under which the exporter would commit not to sell below an agreed minimum price in exchange for exemption from the countervailing duty:

  • CCCME (China Chamber of Commerce for Import and Export of Machinery and Electronic Products): collective offer on behalf of a group of Chinese exporting producers
  • BYD: individual price undertaking offer
  • Geely: individual price undertaking offer
  • SAIC: individual price undertaking offer (noting SAIC's limited cooperation in the investigation itself)
  • Other cooperating producers: offer from certain non-sampled cooperating companies

Five reasons for rejection

  • Product complexity: BEVs are sold in a large number of different variants, configurations, and specifications. Setting a single MIP or a range that accurately reflects the full diversity of BEV models is practically very difficult.
  • High monitoring risk: The large number of BEV variants and the rapid pace of model evolution (new PCNs regularly introduced) makes it practically impossible to monitor MIP compliance with sufficient rigour.
  • Cross-compensation risk: Diversity of models creates the risk that an undertaking is complied with on some PCNs while being evaded on others, undermining the remedial effect.
  • SAIC's limited cooperation: Given SAIC's non-cooperation during the investigation (especially on input suppliers), the Commission found it particularly difficult to accept a price undertaking from SAIC, where verifying compliance would require the same type of transparency that was withheld during the investigation.
  • GOC non-cooperation: Where the Government of China declined to fully cooperate in establishing the subsidy picture, an undertaking removes the disciplining effect of the duty without full confidence that the subsidy benefit is adequately neutralised.

The recitals note that consultations with the GOC and certain producers were ongoing at the time of adoption, without prejudice to the definitive measures imposed.

Glossary

Key terms as used in Regulation (EU) 2024/2754 and EU anti-subsidy law

BEV
Battery electric vehicle -- a motor vehicle propelled exclusively by one or more electric motors, with a battery pack charged exclusively from an external power source (fully electric, not hybrid).
Basic Regulation
Regulation (EU) 2016/1037 -- the EU's framework law for anti-subsidy (countervailing duty) investigations. Equivalent of the Basic Anti-Dumping Regulation for subsidies.
CIF
Cost, insurance and freight -- the customs value of goods at the EU border, used as the base on which ad valorem countervailing duties are calculated.
Countervailing duty (CVD)
A duty imposed to offset a subsidy granted by a third country government that confers a benefit on exported goods, enabling those exports to undercut or undersell domestic producers.
Facts available (Art 28)
Where a party fails to cooperate or provides incomplete information, the Commission may use best available facts, including adverse inferences. Applied to SAIC and in part to GOC in this investigation.
GOC
Government of China -- the responding government in the anti-subsidy investigation. GOC partially declined to cooperate, triggering facts available methodology for several subsidy programmes.
IP (Investigation Period)
1 October 2022 to 30 September 2023. The one-year window used for all subsidy benefit calculations and price comparisons. Distinct from the longer "period considered" (2020-IP) used for injury trends.
LTAR
Less than adequate remuneration -- when a government provides goods, services, or land use rights at below-market prices, the difference constitutes a countervailable subsidy benefit (WTO SCM Art 14(d)).
LFP
Lithium Iron Phosphate -- the battery chemistry used in many Chinese BEVs. LFP battery cells provided to BEV producers at LTAR were identified as a significant subsidy category in this investigation.
LUR
Land use right -- in China, land is state-owned; enterprises acquire rights to use land for defined periods. When provided below market reference prices, the benefit is an LTAR subsidy counted in the total rate.
MIP
Minimum Import Price -- the mechanism proposed in the undertaking offers. Exporters commit not to sell below an agreed floor price. All five offers were rejected due to monitoring and evasion risks.
NEV
New Energy Vehicle -- the Chinese regulatory and subsidy category covering BEVs and PHEVs. Central NEV subsidies (purchase incentives, production grants, R&D funding) form a significant portion of the countervailable benefits found.
PCN
Product Control Number -- a code encoding key technical characteristics of a specific BEV model or variant. Used to ensure like-for-like price comparisons across a product category with enormous model diversity.
Specificity
WTO SCM Art 2 requirement that a subsidy be specific to an enterprise, industry, or group to be countervailable. General infrastructure or generally available measures are not countervailable unless shown to benefit specific industries disproportionately.
TARIC additional code
10-digit EU tariff code extension used to identify individual companies subject to different duty rates. Codes C179, C184-C190 identify the BEV producer groups for customs declaration purposes.
Threat of material injury
An imminent threat to the domestic industry, not yet actual injury, established under Art 8(8) of the Basic Regulation. Triggers Art 16(2): provisional duties are released rather than collected retroactively.

Related canon entries

Other EU Canon deep-dives in the same legal family

Regulation (EU) 2020/776: China and Egypt GFF Countervailing Duties

Same legal family. The 2020 anti-subsidy case on woven and stitched glass fibre fabrics (GFF) from China and Egypt is the closest sibling to the BEV file. Both use the same Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation (Reg (EU) 2016/1037), apply the same no-lesser-duty rule introduced by Reg (EU) 2018/825, and follow the same investigative architecture (sampling, facts available, undertaking offer treatment, Union interest test).

The GFF case is the landmark precedent that introduced cross-border subsidy attribution: countervailing in Egypt subsidies granted by China to production located in the China-Egypt Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone (SETC-Zone), using Article 11 of the ILC Articles on State Responsibility (acknowledgement and adoption doctrine). The duty rates are 30.7 % for the CNBM Group (China), 17.0 % for the Yuntianhua Group (China), and 10.9 % for Egyptian CNBM operations.

Regulation (EU) 2022/433: India and Indonesia Stainless Steel Countervailing Duties

Same legal family. The 2022 anti-subsidy case on stainless steel cold-rolled flat products from India and Indonesia. It applied the same Basic Anti-Subsidy Regulation (Reg (EU) 2016/1037) and extended the cross-border subsidy doctrine to Chinese financing channelled to Indonesian producers through the Morowali Industrial Park. Countervailing duties range from 4.3 % to 21.4 %, reaching 42.8 % combined with anti-dumping.
