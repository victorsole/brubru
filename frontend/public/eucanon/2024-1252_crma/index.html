EU Canon / Circular Economy & Critical Raw Materials

CRMA: fast-tracking the mines Europe's green and digital transition depends on

Regulation (EU) 2024/1252 is the EU's framework law for securing a supply of critical raw materials it does not itself produce enough of. It sets four 2030 benchmarks for extraction, processing, recycling and import diversification, fast-tracks a handful of Strategic Projects through permitting, builds a supply-risk monitoring dashboard, and, unusually for an upstream instrument, reaches into products directly through new circularity duties for permanent magnets.

Overview

What the CRMA is, why it exists, and how it is structured

A supply-chain security framework, not a products regulation

The Critical Raw Materials Act is the EU's framework law for securing the supply of the non-energy, non-agricultural raw materials that underpin the green transition, the digital transition, and defence and aerospace applications. Article 1(1) sets its general objective: improving the functioning of the internal market by ensuring the Union's access to a secure, resilient and sustainable supply of critical raw materials, including through efficiency and circularity throughout the value chain.

Article 1(2) does this through three strands: lowering the risk of supply disruptions by supporting Strategic Projects and moderating demand growth; improving the Union's ability to monitor and mitigate supply risk through a dashboard, stress tests and company risk assessments; and ensuring free movement of compliant raw materials and products while raising circularity, notably for permanent magnets.

From the 2020 Action Plan to a binding Regulation

The CRMA grew out of the Commission's 2020 Action Plan on Critical Raw Materials and the 2021 EU principles for sustainable raw materials. The Commission proposed COM(2023) 160 on 16 March 2023, OEIL 2023/0079(COD). The European Parliament adopted its position on 12 December 2023, the Council took its decision on 18 March 2024, and the Regulation was signed at Brussels on 11 April 2024 by President Metsola for Parliament and Minister Lahbib for the Council.

It was published in the Official Journal on 3 May 2024, as OJ L, 2024/1252 (54 pages), and entered into force on 23 May 2024, twenty days after publication, per Article 49(1). It is directly applicable in all Member States, no national transposition required, except Articles 40 and 41, the type-approval Annex amendments, which apply only from 24 May 2028 (Article 49(2)).

What the CRMA amends

The CRMA amends four existing Regulations through its Chapter VIII: (EU) No 168/2013 on two/three-wheel vehicle and quadricycle type-approval (Art 40), (EU) 2018/858 on motor vehicle type-approval (Art 41), (EU) 2018/1724, the Single Digital Gateway (Art 42), and (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance (Art 43). Only the two type-approval amendments carry the delayed 24 May 2028 application date; the Single Digital Gateway and market surveillance amendments apply from the Regulation's own entry into force.

One of the 13 acts in the EU Digital Product Passport architecture

Unlike the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) or the Batteries Regulation, the CRMA regulates the upstream supply chain, extraction, processing, recycling, stockpiling, monitoring, rather than end-products. Its own product-facing rules are narrow, permanent-magnet labelling, recycled content and critical-raw-material environmental footprint declarations, Articles 28 to 34. But it sits alongside the Batteries Regulation and ESPR as one of the 13 acts in the EU Digital Product Passport legal architecture: it does not create a DPP itself, but its recycled-content and provenance data for critical raw materials is the upstream input the battery passport and other product passports depend on.

Chapter I: general provisions (Arts 1-2)

Subject matter, the three-strand objective, and 64 defined terms

Subject matter and objectives (Art 1)

Article 1(1) frames the CRMA as an internal-market instrument: it improves the functioning of the market by establishing a framework to ensure the Union's access to a secure, resilient and sustainable supply of critical raw materials, including by fostering efficiency and circularity throughout the value chain. The three strands set out in Article 1(2), lowering supply-disruption risk, improving monitoring and mitigation, and ensuring free movement while raising circularity and sustainability, run through every later chapter of the Regulation.

Definitions (Art 2)

Article 2 sets out 64 defined terms covering the raw materials value chain, from strategic and critical raw materials themselves through to Strategic Projects, benchmarks, strategic stocks, large companies and Strategic Partnerships. References to strategic and critical raw materials cover the entire value chain, including unprocessed form and at all processing stages, with an explicit clarification for the aluminium value chain, bauxite, alumina, aluminium. By-product raw materials, for example those recovered from ferrous scrap, are eligible on the same basis as primary-extracted materials.

Chapter II: strategic and critical raw materials (Arts 3-4)

The two overlapping lists that decide which duties apply to which materials

Strategic raw materials, the narrower list (Art 3)

Strategic raw materials, Annex I, Section 1, are raw materials of high strategic importance for strategic technologies underpinning the green and digital transitions or defence and aerospace applications, characterised by a potentially significant gap between global supply and projected demand and by difficulty in increasing production. Only strategic raw materials are subject to the Article 5 capacity and diversification benchmarks, the Strategic Project mechanism, joint purchasing, strategic stock reporting and company risk-assessment duties.

Critical raw materials, the wider list (Art 4)

Critical raw materials, Annex II, Section 1, is the wider list: it automatically includes every strategic raw material, plus any other raw material that reaches or exceeds the threshold of 1 for supply risk and 2.8 for economic importance, calculated per the Annex II, Section 2 methodology. The measures on the single point of contact, planning, exploration, monitoring, circularity and sustainability apply to all critical raw materials, not only the narrower strategic subset.

Annex-only figures, verify before quoting

The CRMA's Annex I and Annex II lists themselves, and the exact number of substances on each, sit in the Annexes rather than the operative Articles. The figures commonly cited externally are 17 strategic raw materials and 34 critical raw materials, but these should be confirmed against the current consolidated Annex I/II text on EUR-Lex before being treated as authoritative, particularly after any list update from 24 May 2027 onward.

Both lists are maintained by the Commission via delegated act and reviewed by 24 May 2027 and at least every three years thereafter, or at any time on a reasoned request from the European Critical Raw Materials Board following monitoring or stress testing.

Chapter III: strengthening the Union raw materials value chain (Arts 5-19)

Five sections: benchmarks, Strategic Projects, permitting, enabling conditions and exploration

Section 1: the 2030 benchmarks (Art 5)

Article 5(1) sets Union-level capacity and diversification benchmarks for strategic raw materials, to be approached or reached by 2030: extraction capacity capable of extracting at least 10% of annual consumption; processing capacity capable of producing at least 40%; recycling capacity capable of producing at least 25%; and a diversification cap so that no third country accounts for more than 65% of annual consumption of any strategic raw material at any relevant processing stage.

By 1 January 2027 the Commission must adopt a delegated act supplementing this with Union recycling capacity benchmarks expressed as a share of the strategic raw materials available in specific waste streams. Alongside the numerical benchmarks, the Commission and Member States must incentivise technological progress and resource efficiency to moderate consumption growth below a reference projection due by 24 November 2026. Progress is monitored and reported from 24 May 2027, at least every three years.

Section 2: Strategic Projects (Arts 6-8)

The Commission recognises a raw material project, in the Union or in a third country or overseas country or territory, as a Strategic Project if it makes a meaningful contribution to security of supply, is technically feasible within a reasonable timeframe, is implemented sustainably, and delivers cross-border benefits, for Union projects, or is mutually beneficial to the Union and the third country, for projects in emerging markets or developing economies (Art 6).

Project promoters apply through a single template, on an open call with cut-off dates at least four times a year. The Commission notifies applicants within 30 days whether the application is complete, and adopts its recognition decision within 90 days of acknowledging completeness, extendable once by up to 90 more days in exceptional cases (Art 7). A Member State, or a third-country government, can veto recognition of a project on its own territory. Every two years after recognition, promoters must report on implementation progress, delays and financing; recognition can be withdrawn if criteria are no longer met (Art 8).

Section 3: streamlined permitting (Arts 9-14)

Every Member State had to establish or designate a single point of contact for critical raw material permitting by 24 February 2025 (Art 9). Strategic Projects in the Union are considered to serve the public interest or public health and safety for the purposes of the Habitats Directive, the Water Framework Directive and the Birds Directive, subject to the usual conditions, and receive the highest national-significance status available under national law plus urgent handling in dispute-resolution and judicial procedures (Arts 10-12).

The permit-granting process itself is capped at 27 months for extraction and 15 months for processing or recycling (Art 11(1)). Shorter derogated caps of 24 months and 12 months apply where the project was already in a national permitting process before recognition, or is an extension of an already-permitted Strategic Project (Art 11(2)). Member States may extend these caps in exceptional cases by up to 6 months (extraction) or 3 months (processing/recycling) (Art 11(4)). The time needed for the environmental impact assessment report is excluded from these clocks (Art 11(8)).

Section 4: enabling conditions (Arts 15-18)

The Commission supports Strategic Projects with access to finance, including facilitating offtake agreements between promoters and industrial buyers, and maintains online information on Union and Member State financing instruments available to the raw materials sector.

Section 5: exploration (Art 19)

Member States with realistic potential for exploration of critical raw materials had to draw up a national general-exploration programme by 24 May 2025, covering territory-wide geological data and mapping to identify areas suitable for further exploration.

Chapter IV: risk monitoring and mitigation (Arts 20-25)

Watching the pipeline: a dashboard, stress tests, strategic stocks, company risk assessments and joint purchasing

Monitoring dashboard and stress tests (Art 20)

The Commission must monitor supply risk for critical raw materials, trade flows, demand and supply, supply concentration, production capacities, price volatility and bottlenecks, and publish a continuously updated, free-access monitoring dashboard, supported by national authorities in a dedicated Board subgroup. The Commission, working with Member States, must ensure a stress test is carried out for each strategic raw material supply chain at least every three years, or sooner if monitoring detects a significant increase in supply risk.

Strategic stocks (Arts 22-23)

Member States must report annually, from 24 May 2026, on the state of their strategic stocks, levels, five-year trend and release or allocation rules, though the Regulation explicitly creates no obligation to hold or release strategic stocks (Art 23(8)). Every two years from 24 May 2026, the Commission shares with the Board a draft benchmark for a safe level of Union strategic stocks and may issue non-binding opinions to Member States on increasing convergence.

Company risk preparedness (Art 24)

By 24 May 2025, and within 12 months of any update to the strategic raw materials list, Member States must identify the large companies, more than 500 employees and net worldwide turnover above EUR 150 million, that manufacture strategic technologies on their territory using strategic raw materials, covering batteries, hydrogen equipment, renewable energy generation, aircraft, traction motors, heat pumps, data transmission and storage equipment, mobile devices, additive manufacturing, robotics, drones, rocket launchers, satellites and advanced chips.

Those large companies must, at least every three years, carry out a supply-chain risk assessment, mapping origins, analysing supply-affecting factors, assessing vulnerabilities, and take mitigation efforts where significant vulnerabilities are found. Member States may require the report to be presented to the company's board of directors, but the report itself is not made public.

Joint purchasing (Art 25)

The Commission must set up and operate a demand-aggregation system, modelled on the gas joint-purchasing precedent, letting interested Union undertakings pool demand for strategic raw materials and seek matching offers from suppliers. Before launching the system for a given material, the Commission must assess the likely market impact after consulting the Board. Participating undertakings may jointly negotiate prices and terms, subject to Union competition law; entities targeted by Article 215 TFEU restrictive measures are excluded.

Chapter V: sustainability (Arts 26-34)

Circularity, permanent magnets, certification schemes and environmental footprint declarations

National circularity programmes and extractive waste recovery (Arts 26-27)

Within two years of the Commission's implementing act on recovery-potential products and waste streams, due by 24 May 2025, each Member State must adopt national programmes to moderate demand, promote reuse and repair, increase collection and sorting of high-recovery-potential waste, and support skills for the circular value chain (Art 26). Operators of extractive waste facilities must submit, by 24 November 2026, a preliminary economic assessment study on the potential recovery of critical raw materials from stored and newly generated extractive waste, unless they can show no technically recoverable materials are present (Art 27).

Permanent magnets, the CRMA's most product-facing rules (Arts 28-29)

Products incorporating permanent magnets, magnetic resonance imaging devices, wind energy generators, industrial robots, motor vehicles, light means of transport, cooling generators, heat pumps, electric motors, automatic washing machines, tumble driers, microwaves, vacuum cleaners and dishwashers, must be labelled to disclose whether they incorporate permanent magnets and of which type, neodymium-iron-boron, samarium-cobalt, aluminium-nickel-cobalt or ferrite, from two years after the relevant Commission labelling-format implementing act (Art 28).

By 24 May 2027, for magnets exceeding 0.2 kg total weight, operators must publicly disclose the share of neodymium, dysprosium, praseodymium, terbium, boron, samarium, nickel and cobalt recovered from post-consumer waste present in the magnet (Art 29(1)). A later delegated act, due by 31 December 2031 at the latest, will set binding minimum recycled-content shares (Art 29(3)). Products designed primarily for defence or space applications are exempt.

Recognised certification schemes and environmental footprint (Arts 30-31)

Governments, industry associations and other scheme owners can apply for Commission recognition of their sustainability certification scheme against Annex IV criteria; recognition is reassessed at least every three years and can be withdrawn if deficiencies are not remedied within up to 12 months (Art 30). The Commission may, following a prioritisation report due by 24 November 2026, adopt delegated acts setting calculation and verification rules for the environmental footprint of specific critical raw materials, at which point anyone placing that material on the market must make available a free-access environmental footprint declaration (Art 31).

Free movement, conformity and market surveillance (Arts 32-34)

Member States cannot restrict, prohibit or impede the market access of compliant permanent-magnet products or critical raw materials on recycling or footprint-information grounds (Art 32). Conformity assessment and market surveillance for the permanent-magnet and environmental-footprint duties follow the standard New Legislative Framework pattern, coordinated with Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, which the CRMA itself amends via Article 43.

Chapter VI: governance (Arts 35-37)

The European Critical Raw Materials Board and international Strategic Partnerships

The European Critical Raw Materials Board (Arts 35-36)

The Board, established by Article 35, advises the Commission and carries out the tasks assigned to it throughout the Regulation. It is chaired by the Commission, composed of one high-level representative per Member State, only Member States have voting rights, one vote each, with the European Parliament invited as an observer and industry, SMEs, civil society, academia, trade unions, local and regional authorities, third countries, overseas countries or territories and relevant EU agencies invitable case by case.

The Board meets at least every three months to assess Strategic Project applications, every six months to develop Chapter IV monitoring, and at least once a year to discuss exploration-obligation progress. It must establish at least six standing subgroups: financing of Strategic Projects, public knowledge and acceptance, national exploration-programme coordination, circularity, resource efficiency and substitution, monitoring and stress-testing support, and strategic-stock coordination (Art 36).

Strategic Partnerships (Art 37)

The Board periodically discusses Strategic Partnerships with third countries, non-binding commitments to cooperate on the raw materials value chain, their contribution to security of supply, consistency with Member States' bilateral cooperation, and criteria for prioritising future partners, without prejudice to the Council's Treaty prerogatives over international agreements.

Chapter VII: delegated powers and committee procedure (Arts 38-39)

An eight-year delegation, tacitly renewable, exercised under Article 290 TFEU

Eight years, tacitly renewable

The power to adopt delegated acts under Articles 3(2), 4(2), 5(3), 6(2), 28(12), 29(2)-(3), 31(1) and (8), and 34(1) is conferred on the Commission for 8 years from 24 June 2024, tacitly renewable unless Parliament or Council object at least three months before expiry; either institution can revoke at any time, and either can object within two months, extendable by two more, of notification of an individual delegated act (Art 38). Comitology implementing acts, single-application templates, certification scheme recognition criteria, reporting formats, permanent-magnet labelling format, run through either the advisory or the examination procedure depending on the empowering Article, per Article 39 and Regulation (EU) No 182/2011.

Chapter VIII: amendments (Arts 40-43)

Four existing Regulations amended, only two on a delayed clock

Type-approval amendments, applying from 2028 (Arts 40-41)

Article 40 amends Regulation (EU) No 168/2013 on the approval and market surveillance of two or three-wheel vehicles and quadricycles, and Article 41 amends Regulation (EU) 2018/858 on motor vehicle type-approval, both to build in critical-raw-material recyclability and recycled-content considerations. Both amendments apply only from 24 May 2028, the CRMA's single longest lead time.

Single Digital Gateway and market surveillance (Arts 42-43)

Article 42 amends Regulation (EU) 2018/1724, the Single Digital Gateway, to add the CRMA's permitting and information procedures to the Gateway's coverage. Article 43 amends Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance to bring the CRMA's permanent-magnet and environmental-footprint duties within the standard EU market surveillance regime. Both apply from the Regulation's own entry into force, no delayed clock.

Chapter IX: final provisions (Arts 44-49)

Reporting, confidentiality, penalties, evaluation and entry into force

Monitoring progress and Member State reporting (Arts 44-46)

The Commission must publish a reference projection for critical raw materials consumption by 24 November 2026 and report on progress towards the Article 5 benchmarks from 24 May 2027, at least every three years (Art 44). Member States report to the Commission on implementation across the Regulation (Art 45), and confidential business information gathered under the Regulation is protected accordingly (Art 46).

Penalties and evaluation (Arts 47-48)

Member States must lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties for infringements by 24 November 2026, and notify the Commission of those rules (Art 47). The Commission must evaluate the whole Regulation and report to Parliament, Council and the European Economic and Social Committee by 24 May 2028, covering, among other things, the case for maximum environmental-footprint thresholds, 2040/2050 benchmarks, consistency with EU environmental law given the Strategic Project public-interest status, and the joint-purchasing system's competition impact (Art 48).

Entry into force (Art 49)

The Regulation entered into force on 23 May 2024, twenty days after its 3 May 2024 OJ publication, and is directly applicable in every Member State from that date, except Articles 40 and 41, the type-approval Annex amendments, which apply only from 24 May 2028.

Six definitions worth memorising

Strategic raw material
A raw material on the narrower Annex I list, selected for its strategic importance to green, digital, defence or aerospace technologies, high forecast demand growth, and difficulty of increasing production (Art 3).
Critical raw material
A raw material on the Annex II list, automatically including every strategic raw material, plus any material meeting the Annex II, Section 2 supply-risk and economic-importance thresholds (Art 4).
Strategic Project
A raw material project, in the Union, a third country or an overseas country or territory, formally recognised by the Commission as meeting the Article 6 criteria, unlocking streamlined permitting, financing support and public-interest status (Arts 6-8).
Benchmark
The 2030 Union-level capacity, extraction, processing, recycling, and diversification, single-country dependency, targets set in Article 5.
Single point of contact
The authority or authorities each Member State had to establish or designate by 24 February 2025 to coordinate the entire permit-granting process for a critical raw material project (Art 9).
Permit-granting process
The full sequence of permits, assessments and authorisations for a critical raw material project, running from acknowledgement of a complete application to the comprehensive decision (Art 2(18)).

CRMA vs the 2020 Action Plan

Regulation (EU) 2024/1252 versus the non-binding policy document it grew out of

Feature CRMA (Reg (EU) 2024/1252) 2020 Action Plan on Critical Raw Materials
Instrument type Regulation, directly applicable in all Member States, no national transposition required Commission Communication, a non-binding policy document
Targets Four binding 2030 benchmarks, 10% extraction, 40% processing, 25% recycling, 65% single-country dependency cap Soft, aspirational objectives with no numerical benchmarks or legal deadlines
Permitting Hard permit-granting caps for Strategic Projects, 27 months extraction, 15 months processing or recycling No permitting mechanism, general call for faster national procedures
Monitoring Statutory monitoring dashboard, stress tests at least every three years, mandatory large-company risk assessments No statutory monitoring obligation, ad hoc Commission analysis only

Same strategic diagnosis, a legally binding answer

The 2020 Action Plan correctly diagnosed the EU's raw-material import dependency but had no legal teeth. The CRMA keeps the diagnosis, then attaches numerical benchmarks, capped permitting timelines, a monitoring dashboard, and enforceable circularity duties, turning a policy ambition into a Regulation with dates, thresholds and penalties.

The authority or authorities each Member State had to establish or designate by 24 February 2025 to coordinate the entire permit-granting process for a critical raw material project (Art 9).
Permit-granting process
The full sequence of permits, assessments and authorisations for a critical raw material project, running from acknowledgement of a complete application to the comprehensive decision (Art 2(18)).
Joint purchasing
The Commission-run demand-aggregation system letting undertakings pool demand for strategic raw materials and match it against supplier offers (Art 25).
Stress test
The Commission's periodic assessment of a strategic raw material supply chain's vulnerability to disruption, covering extraction, processing and recycling location, market structure, and diversification options (Art 20(3)).
Recognised scheme
A sustainability certification scheme for critical raw materials formally recognised by the Commission against the Annex IV criteria (Art 30).
Strategic stock
A quantity of raw material stored by a public or private operator to be released in the event of a supply disruption; reporting on strategic stocks is mandatory, holding or releasing them is not (Art 2(28), Arts 22-23).
Large company
For the purposes of Article 24, a company with more than 500 employees on average and net worldwide turnover exceeding EUR 150 million in its most recent financial statements (Art 2(29)).
Strategic Partnership
A non-binding commitment between the Union and a third country or OCT to cooperate on the raw materials value chain (Art 2(63)).
