The Construction Products Regulation: a digital passport for everything Europe builds

Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 is the revised EU rulebook for the internal market in construction products: doors, insulation, cement, structural steel, cladding and thousands of other items permanently incorporated into buildings and civil engineering works. It replaces the 2011 Construction Products Regulation, reintroduces binding product requirements dropped since 1989, phases in a new environmental performance layer, and creates a dedicated construction digital product passport under Chapter X.

Overview

What the CPR is, why it exists, and how it is structured

An internal-market rulebook, not a set of building codes

The Construction Products Regulation is the EU's framework law for the internal market in construction products, doors, insulation, cement, structural steel, cladding, prefabricated kits and thousands of other items incorporated permanently into buildings and civil engineering works. Adopted on 27 November 2024, it pursues three linked objectives (Article 1): harmonised rules for expressing the safety and environmental performance of construction products against "essential characteristics", including life-cycle assessment; new environmental, functional and safety product requirements; and free movement of compliant products across the single market.

Construction works themselves stay a national competence: the CPR sets the rules for the products used in them, not for the buildings or infrastructure Member States choose to build.

Replacing the 2011 CPR

The Regulation repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) No 305/2011, itself the successor to the 1989 Construction Products Directive, following a 2019 Commission evaluation that found the old CPR underperforming on standardisation speed, market surveillance consistency and legal clarity (recital 3). The Commission proposed COM(2022) 144 on 30 March 2022, OEIL 2022/0094(COD), part of the same sustainable products legislative package as the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). The European Parliament adopted its position on 10 April 2024, the Council took its decision on 5 November 2024, and the Regulation was signed at Strasbourg on 27 November 2024.

It was published in the Official Journal on 18 December 2024, as OJ L, 2024/3110 (77 pages), and entered into force on 7 January 2025, twenty days after publication, but only for a front-loaded set of provisions. The remaining obligations, including the core Chapter I-III duties on economic operators, apply from 8 January 2026, phasing in further, product family by product family, as each harmonised technical specification becomes mandatory.

Companion to the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation

Recital 32 and Article 12(2) make the CPR the explicit sector-specific counterpart to the horizontal Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 (ESPR). Construction products are, with narrow named exceptions such as heaters, boilers, heat pumps and photovoltaic products, which fall primarily under ESPR, regulated under the CPR rather than the horizontal framework. Where the two Regulations conflict, the CPR prevails.

One of the 13 acts in the EU Digital Product Passport architecture

Unlike the 2011 CPR, which addressed only performance declaration, the 2024 CPR reintroduces binding product requirements not seen since the 1989 Directive, creates a construction-specific digital product passport, sets out a detailed simplified-procedures regime for SMEs and micro-enterprises, and rewrites the obligations of manufacturers, importers, distributors, authorised representatives, fulfilment service providers and online marketplaces to align with the EU's newer product-legislation template, Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance, the "New Legislative Framework". Chapter X (Articles 75-80) creates the construction digital product passport, built to be interoperable with the ESPR's own passport system and registered in the same Commission-run DPP registry, making the CPR one of the 13 acts in Brubru's EU Digital Product Passport legal architecture.

General provisions (Chapter I, Arts 1-12)

Scope, 64 defined terms, and the standardisation system behind harmonised technical specifications

Scope and definitions (Arts 2-3)

Article 2 applies the Regulation to construction products, including used products, key parts and materials a manufacturer voluntarily submits. It excludes lifts and escalators (Directive 2014/33/EU) and drinking-water-quality aspects (Directive (EU) 2020/2184). Article 3 sets out 64 defined terms, including construction product, placing on the market versus making available on the market, essential characteristics, product requirement, key part, kit, used and remanufactured products, and harmonised technical specifications.

The standardisation system (Arts 4-11)

Article 4 establishes the CPR Acquis Expert Group, which supports a rolling three-year working plan for harmonised technical specifications, with the first plan due by 8 January 2026. The primary route is performance harmonised standards (Article 5): the Commission requests European standardisation organisations to draft standards, then makes conforming standards mandatory by implementing act.

Article 6 provides a fall-back mechanism: where a standardisation request is rejected, a standard is not delivered within three years, or a delivered standard does not comply, and no mandatory standard has existed for five years, the Commission may set essential characteristics directly, bypassing standardisation. Article 7 lets the Commission adopt delegated acts establishing binding product requirements once a product family is covered by a standard or Article 6 act. Article 11 defines the resulting harmonised zone: within it, Member States cannot impose additional national requirements, though they retain full competence over the design and dimensioning of construction works themselves.

Declaration and CE marking (Chapter II, Arts 13-19)

The declaration of performance and conformity, its exemptions, and the CE marking rules

The declaration of performance and conformity, DoPC (Arts 13, 15-16)

Article 13 requires a manufacturer to draw up a DoPC before placing on the market any product covered by a harmonised technical specification, following the applicable Annex IX assessment-and-verification system. By drawing it up, the manufacturer assumes contractual and extra-contractual liability for the declared performance. The DoPC uses the Annex V model, states performance against essential characteristics, confirms fulfilment of applicable product requirements, and must cover the Annex II environmental sustainability characteristics on the phased timetable set out in Article 15(3). It may be supplied electronically or on a website, provided the content is unamendable, human- and machine-readable, free to access, and linked to the product via its unique identification code.

Exemptions (Art 14)

Individually manufactured or custom-made products installed in a single identified construction work under national supervision, and products manufactured exclusively for heritage-conservation renovation in a non-series process, do not require a DoPC. Manufacturers meeting these criteria may instead opt into the simplified procedure under Article 61.

The CE marking (Arts 17-19)

The CE marking is the only marking permitted to attest performance against essential characteristics and compliance with the Regulation; it may be affixed only where a DoPC exists, and it must also be affixed to key parts. Article 18 sets its required content: the last two digits of the affixing year, the manufacturer's name and address, the unique product-type identification code, the DoPC's declaration code, the notified body identification number where applicable, and, where a digital product passport is available, a connecting data carrier. Other markings, including recognised EN ISO 14024 Type I ecolabels, may be affixed only if they do not impair the CE marking's visibility or imply an alternative assessment method.

Economic operators (Chapter III, Arts 20-30)

Differentiated obligations for every actor in the construction products supply chain

All economic operators (Art 20)

Every economic operator must take all necessary measures to ensure continued compliance, identify upstream suppliers and downstream recipients on request, keep documents and information for 10 years after supply, provide consumer communication channels for accidents or incidents, and immediately inform competent authorities where a non-conforming product presents a health, safety or environmental risk.

Manufacturers (Arts 21-22)

Manufacturers determine the product type, assess performance against essential characteristics and product requirements, draw up technical documentation and the DoPC, affix CE marking, maintain factory production control, provide instructions and safety information in the required language, make the digital product passport available on the Article 80 timeline, ensure specified spare parts remain available for 10 years where required, and, where a product presents a risk, notify downstream operators and authorities within three working days, withdrawing and recalling at their own cost if the risk is serious. A person 3D-printing and placing a product on the market assumes full manufacturer obligations.

Importers, distributors, authorised representatives (Arts 23-26)

Authorised representatives act only within a written mandate, keep the DoPC and technical documentation available, and verify at documentary level that CE marking and labelling are in order. Importers place on the market only compliant products, verify technical documentation, CE marking and DoPC availability before doing so, and add their own contact details. Distributors verify CE marking, labelling and the DoPC before making a product available, and take corrective action where a product is suspected non-compliant. An importer or distributor becomes subject to full manufacturer obligations if it places a product under its own name, modifies it materially, or changes its declared use, and this extends automatically to anyone placing a used or remanufactured product on the market.

Fulfilment providers and online marketplaces (Arts 27-29)

For the first time under the CPR, fulfilment service providers must ensure CE marking, the DoPC and product information accompany the product, and that warehousing and dispatch conditions do not jeopardise compliance. Online marketplaces must design their interface so operators can meet Digital Services Act disclosure obligations, maintain a single contact point for national authorities, and act on orders to remove non-compliant listings. An offer is deemed targeted at EU customers, and therefore "made available on the market", if it uses a Member State's currency, an EU-referencing domain, or ships to a Member State, unless the operator explicitly excludes the EU market (Art 29).

Assessment infrastructure (Chapters IV-VII, Arts 31-62)

European assessment documents, technical assessment bodies, notified bodies, and simplified procedures for SMEs

European assessment documents and technical assessment bodies (Chapters IV-V, Arts 31-41)

For products not, or not yet, covered by harmonised standards, a European assessment document (EAD), developed by a technical assessment body (TAB), provides the reference for an individual European technical assessment (ETA), an alternative route to CE marking. TABs are designated, monitored and coordinated by their Member State's designating authority.

Notifying authorities and notified bodies (Chapter VI, Arts 42-58)

Chapter VI, the CPR's longest, sets out notification, requirements for notifying authorities and notified bodies, subcontracting rules, the application and notification procedure, and operational and information obligations, along with coordination between notified bodies across the Union.

Simplified procedures for SMEs and micro-enterprises (Chapter VII, Arts 59-62)

Chapter VII consolidates the CPR's SME-facing flexibility, responding to recital 79's finding that the 2011 CPR's simplification provisions "often remained misunderstood or not used". Any manufacturer may replace type-testing or calculation with a technical-documentation statement in defined cases (Art 59); a micro-enterprise may replace type-testing under Assessment System 3 with equivalent data (Art 60); manufacturers of custom-made non-series products may substitute equivalent data for full assessment (Art 61); and a notified body may recognise another notified body's assessment of the same operator or shared supplier, a mechanism that also covers duplicate testing avoided under the ESPR (Art 62).

Market surveillance and cooperation (Chapters VIII-IX, Arts 63-74)

A Commission complaint portal, national enforcement, cost recovery, and cross-border coordination

Complaint portal and national enforcement (Arts 63-67)

Article 63 creates a Commission-run complaint portal letting any person report suspected non-compliance. Each Member State designates at least one competent market surveillance authority and a single liaison point (Art 64). Where non-compliance is confirmed, the authority requires corrective action, withdrawal or recall within a reasonable period (Art 65); a national restrictive measure is deemed justified if no objection is raised within two months. Where a Member State or the Commission objects, the Union safeguard procedure runs on further two-month consultation and adoption cycles (Art 66), and even a compliant product can be required to be adjusted or withdrawn if it presents a health, safety or environmental risk (Art 67).

Coordination, cost recovery and benchmarking (Arts 68-70)

The administrative cooperation group (ADCO) organises joint surveillance projects, common training and enforcement guidelines (Art 68). Market surveillance authorities may recover the justified costs of document inspection and physical testing from non-compliant operators (Art 69). Every four years, the Commission publishes a report on checks performed, non-compliance levels and penalties imposed, with the first due by 9 January 2030 (Art 70), a response to the 2019 evaluation's finding that market surveillance quality varied widely between Member States.

Information and administrative cooperation (Chapter IX, Arts 71-74)

Chapter IX establishes the information and communication system supporting harmonised decision-making across Member States, the network of national product contact points for construction, training obligations, and shared implementation roles between Member States.

The construction digital product passport (Chapter X, Arts 75-80)

The CPR's most structurally new element, entirely absent from the 2011 CPR

Setting up the system and its content (Arts 75-76)

Article 75 requires the Commission to adopt a delegated act setting up a dedicated construction digital product passport system, built to be compatible and interoperable with the ESPR digital product passport (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) without compromising interoperability with Building Information Modelling (BIM). Article 76 sets out its content: the DoPC and its accompanying REACH documentation, general product information and safety instructions, technical documentation, the environmental sustainability label, unique identifiers, and the data carriers of key parts that themselves carry a passport. It must be connected to a data carrier, free to access for economic operators, clients, users and authorities, with differentiated access levels.

Technical requirements and timeline (Arts 78, 80)

Article 78 sets essential technical requirements: full interoperability between passports, free easy access according to access rights, protection of trade secrets and personal data, no end-user personal data without explicit GDPR consent, data authentication and security, and continued availability, including via a back-up system, even after the creating operator's insolvency.

Under Article 80, the system must be fully operational six months after the Article 75(1) delegated act enters into force, and the manufacturer's duty to make a passport available applies from eighteen months after that same act, with voluntary use permitted in the interim. The system must remain accessible for 25 years after the last product of a given type is placed on the market, with the economic operator obliged to keep the passport available for at least 10 years of that period. As of this reading, the Article 75(1) delegated act had not yet been adopted, so these clocks had not started, treat any specific calendar date for mandatory construction-sector DPP use as indicative pending that act.

Environmental sustainability (Arts 7, 15, 22, 83, Annex II)

A new layer that did not exist under the 2011 CPR, tied to the European Green Deal and Circular Economy Action Plan

Predetermined environmental essential characteristics (Art 15(2)-(3), Annex II)

Manufacturers must calculate mandatory life-cycle-based environmental characteristics using free Commission-published software, based on European standard EN 15804 methodology, and declare them in the DoPC. Article 15(3) phases in the obligation on three dates: Annex II points (a)-(d) from 8 January 2026, points (e)-(m) from 9 January 2030, and points (n)-(s) from 9 January 2032. The precise characteristics behind each letter are annex-only content, verify against the current consolidated Annex II before naming specific characteristics tied to a specific phase-in date.

Binding product requirements (Art 7, Annex III)

Once a delegated act is adopted for a product family, products must meet binding functionality, safety and environmental-protection requirements before being placed on the market, not merely declare performance, reversing the 2011 CPR's "declare and let the market decide" model.

Circularity, reuse and spare parts (Art 22(8))

The Commission may require manufacturers to keep specified spare parts available, at a reasonable, non-discriminatory price, within a reasonably short delivery period, for 10 years after the last product of that type is placed on the market. Dedicated harmonised technical specifications may extend to used and remanufactured products, and remanufactured products benefit from an environmental-impact calculation that starts only from the product's last deinstallation, not from original raw-material extraction.

Green public procurement (Art 83)

The Commission must adopt delegated acts fixing mandatory minimum environmental sustainability requirements for public contracts under Directives 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU, usable as technical specifications, selection criteria, contract-performance clauses or award criteria, with the first impact assessment due to launch by 31 December 2026. EU public procurement represents roughly 14% of Union GDP, underscoring the demand-side lever this creates (recital 98).

Final provisions and repeal (Chapters XI-XV, Arts 81-96)

International cooperation, incentives, emergency procedures, and the staged repeal of the 2011 CPR

International cooperation, incentives, and emergency procedures (Chapters XI-XIV, Arts 81-88)

Article 81 covers cooperation with third countries and international organisations. Chapter XII lets Member States incentivise high-performing products and requires minimum environmental sustainability requirements in green public procurement (Art 83). Chapter XIII gives the Commission power to determine, by implementing act, whether an item is a "product" under the Regulation (Art 84). Chapter XIV prioritises assessment, verification and market surveillance for crisis-relevant construction products during an internal market emergency (Arts 85-88).

Delegated and implementing powers (Arts 89-90)

The power to adopt delegated acts is conferred on the Commission for five years from 7 January 2025, tacitly renewable unless Parliament or Council object at least three months before expiry. The Commission is assisted by the Committee on Construction Products; most implementing acts follow the advisory procedure, while the fall-back standardisation mechanism, common specifications, and regulatory-status determinations follow the examination procedure, with urgent cases triggering immediately applicable acts.

Penalties, evaluation and entry into force (Arts 92-93, 96)

Member States must lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties, notified to the Commission by 8 December 2026, applying from 8 January 2027. The Regulation entered into force on 7 January 2025 for a front-loaded set of provisions, with the remainder applying from 8 January 2026 (Art 96).

The staged repeal of the 2011 CPR (Arts 94-95)

Article 94 repeals Regulation (EU) No 305/2011 with effect from 8 January 2026, except a defined subset of its Articles and Annexes III and V, which stay in force until 8 January 2040. Article 95 bridges the transition: product contact points, TABs and notified bodies designated under the old CPR are deemed designated under the new one but must be re-assessed by 8 January 2030; old-CPR European assessment documents remain valid until 9 January 2031, with placing on the market under resulting ETAs closing on 9 January 2036; and Chapter I-III obligations phase in by product family, one year after the relevant implementing act, unless a later date is specified.

Key definitions (Art 3)

Article 3 contains 64 defined terms. The load-bearing concepts that drive the rest of the Regulation:

Six definitions worth memorising

Construction product
Any formed or formless physical item, including 3D-printed products, or a kit, placed on the market for permanent incorporation into construction works (Art 3(1)).
Placing on the market vs making available on the market
Placing on the market is the first supply to the EU market, or for a used product, the first supply after deinstallation; making available on the market is any subsequent commercial supply (Art 3(4)-(5)).
Essential characteristics
Characteristics relating to the Annex I basic requirements for construction works, plus the Annex II predetermined environmental essential characteristics (Art 3(7)).
Product requirement
A binding characteristic, set out in Annex III, that a product must have before it can be placed on the market, distinct from a declared essential characteristic (Art 3(8), Art 7).
Harmonised technical specification
The umbrella term for performance harmonised standards made mandatory under Article 5(8), fall-back implementing acts under Article 6(1), and related delegated acts under Articles 7(1), 9(3) and 10(2) (Art 3(42)).
Key part
A component or spare part specified by a harmonised technical specification as essential to a product's characterisation, safety or performance, subject to its own CE marking obligation (Art 3(16), Art 17(2)).

CPR 2024 vs the 2011 CPR

Regulation (EU) 2024/3110 versus Regulation (EU) No 305/2011, the Regulation it repeals

Feature 2024 CPR (Reg (EU) 2024/3110) 2011 CPR (Reg (EU) No 305/2011)
Product requirements Reintroduces binding functionality, safety and environmental requirements via delegated act, Art 7 Performance declaration only, no binding product requirements since the 1989 Directive was dropped
Environmental layer Mandatory predetermined environmental essential characteristics, phased 2026/2030/2032, Annex II No environmental essential characteristics
Digital product passport Chapter X, Arts 75-80, creates a dedicated construction digital product passport No equivalent existed
Standardisation delay Article 6 fall-back mechanism lets the Commission set essential characteristics directly where standards fail to arrive No equivalent fall-back, standardisation could stall indefinitely
E-commerce and fulfilment Explicit obligations for fulfilment service providers and online marketplaces, Arts 27-29 Not addressed
SME simplification Chapter VII codifies replacement testing, the micro-enterprise regime, custom-made products and mutual recognition Equivalent provisions existed but were, per recital 79, "often misunderstood or not used"
Market surveillance Commission complaint portal, cost recovery, four-yearly benchmarking, Arts 63, 69-70 Market surveillance quality varied widely between Member States, no complaint portal
Repeal and transition Staged repeal of the 2011 CPR running to 8 January 2040, Art 94 Repealed the 1989 Directive outright on entry into force

Same architecture, a heavier rulebook

The 2024 CPR keeps the core architecture of the 2011 Regulation, declaration of performance, CE marking, harmonised standards, notified bodies, but adds the elements a decade of implementation experience and the European Green Deal made unavoidable: binding product requirements, an environmental performance layer, a digital product passport, and a stronger e-commerce and market surveillance regime.

Glossary

Load-bearing terms as used in Regulation (EU) 2024/3110

Construction product
Any formed or formless physical item, including 3D-printed products, or a kit, placed on the market for permanent incorporation into construction works (Art 3(1)).
Harmonised technical specification
The umbrella term for performance harmonised standards, fall-back implementing acts, and related delegated acts that together fix a product family's essential characteristics and assessment method (Art 3(42)).
Declaration of performance and conformity (DoPC)
The manufacturer's mandatory statement of a product's performance against its essential characteristics and its compliance with applicable product requirements, drawn up before placing the product on the market (Art 13, Art 15, Annex V).
Assessment and verification of constancy of performance
One of the Annex IX numbered systems fixing which combination of manufacturer self-assessment and notified-body third-party tasks applies to a given essential characteristic or product requirement (Art 10).
Essential characteristics
Characteristics of a product relating to the Annex I basic requirements for construction works, plus the Annex II predetermined environmental essential characteristics (Art 3(7)).
European assessment document (EAD)
The reference document developed by a technical assessment body providing the route to CE marking outside harmonised standards; the European technical assessment (ETA) is the individual assessment issued against it (Art 3(18)-(19), Chapter IV).
Technical assessment body (TAB)
A body designated by a Member State to issue European technical assessments on the basis of European assessment documents (Art 3(57), Chapter V).
Notified body
A conformity assessment body authorised and notified to carry out third-party assessment, verification and validation tasks under the Regulation (Art 3(55), Chapter VI).
Digital product passport
The product-level digital record, accessible via a data carrier, bundling the DoPC, product information, technical documentation and labelling for a construction product, hosted in the system set up under Article 75 (Arts 75-76).
Predetermined environmental requirements
Mandatory life-cycle-based environmental essential characteristics in Annex II, calculated with free Commission software and phased in from 2026 to 2032 (Art 15(3)).
Basic requirements for construction works
The Annex I categories, fire safety, mechanical resistance, hygiene, health and environment, accessibility, energy economy and heat retention, sustainable use of natural resources, that link products to construction works but create no direct obligation on economic operators or Member States (recital 113).
Economic operator
The umbrella term for manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor, fulfilment service provider and online marketplace, each carrying differentiated obligations under Chapter III.
