Solvency II: the EU Prudential Framework for Insurance

Directive 2009/138/EC is the risk-based prudential rulebook for EU insurance and reinsurance. A recast of 14 earlier directives, it introduced a three-pillar architecture: quantitative capital requirements anchored to a 99.5% Value-at-Risk, a governance and supervisory pillar including the ORSA, and a transparency pillar built on the SFCR and supervisory reporting.

Overview

The risk-based prudential framework that replaced 14 earlier directives

From Solvency I to Solvency II

Solvency II replaced a patchwork of national insurance supervisory regimes and EU minimum-harmonisation directives that dated from 1973. The older system, informally called Solvency I, was based on fixed factor-driven capital charges bearing no direct relationship to an undertaking's actual risk profile. Premium income and claims reserves were used as simple proxies for required capital. By the mid-2000s this system was widely recognised as inadequate to capture the risks of modern insurance groups engaged in complex asset-liability management, derivative use, and cross-border reinsurance.

The Solvency II project, launched under the Lamfalussy process, drew on the Basel II/III framework used in banking and on work by the International Association of Insurance Supervisors. Its central innovation is an economic, mark-to-market valuation of the entire balance sheet -- assets and liabilities -- and a risk-calibrated Solvency Capital Requirement. The directive was adopted on 25 November 2009 and published in OJ L 335 on 17 December 2009.

Legal basis and objectives

The directive is based on Articles 47(2) and 55 EC Treaty (now Articles 53(1) and 62 TFEU), the internal market provisions relating to the taking-up and pursuit of self-employed activities. It was adopted via the codecision procedure. The stated primary objective, set out in Article 27, is the protection of policyholders and beneficiaries. Financial stability and fair markets are secondary objectives and may not override policyholder protection (Article 28). This hierarchy is enforced throughout the supervisory framework: a supervisor may not justify inaction on grounds of financial stability if policyholder protection requires intervention.

Omnibus II (Directive 2014/51/EU) subsequently amended Solvency II, most importantly by introducing a long-term guarantee package, extending the transposition deadline to 31 March 2015, and setting 1 January 2016 as the application date. A further major review (Solvency II 2020 Review) led to Directive 2025/2 amending various provisions including the long-term guarantee measures and proportionality framework.

The Three-Pillar architecture

Solvency II is structured around three interdependent pillars that together form a comprehensive supervisory system:

  • Pillar 1 covers quantitative financial requirements: market-consistent valuation of technical provisions, a tiered own-funds regime, the Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR) calibrated at 99.5% Value-at-Risk over one year, the Minimum Capital Requirement (MCR) as the absolute intervention floor, and the prudent-person investment principle.
  • Pillar 2 covers qualitative governance and supervisory requirements: the mandatory system of governance including four key functions, the Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA), fit-and-proper requirements for key function holders, the supervisory review process, and the capital add-on power.
  • Pillar 3 covers public and supervisory transparency: the annual Solvency and Financial Condition Report (SFCR) and Regular Supervisory Reporting (RSR) covering governance, valuation, risks, risk management, and capital structure.

Pillar 1: Quantitative requirements

The economic balance sheet, own funds, SCR, MCR and the prudent-person investment principle

The economic balance sheet (Article 75)

The entire Solvency II quantitative framework is built on a market-consistent economic balance sheet. Assets are measured at the amount for which they could be exchanged between knowledgeable willing parties in an arm's-length transaction -- effectively fair value. Liabilities are measured at the amount for which they could be transferred or settled between knowledgeable willing parties, with no adjustment for own credit standing. Own funds are the residual of market-consistent assets over market-consistent liabilities, and the SCR protects that residual against unexpected losses over a one-year horizon.

Technical provisions (Arts 76-86)

Best estimate plus risk margin: the market-consistent value of insurance obligations

The fundamental requirement (Art 76)

Technical provisions represent the current amount an undertaking would need to transfer its insurance and reinsurance obligations to another undertaking in an arm's-length transaction immediately (Article 76(2)). They must be market-consistent and are calculated as the sum of a best estimate and a risk margin (Article 77(1)).

Best estimate (Art 77(2))

The probability-weighted average of future cash flows, taking account of the time value of money using the relevant risk-free interest rate term structure. It is calculated gross of reinsurance: recoverable amounts from reinsurance contracts and special purpose vehicles are calculated separately and adjusted for expected losses due to counterparty default (Article 81). Segmentation by homogeneous risk groups and lines of business is mandatory (Article 80).

Risk margin (Art 77(3) and (5))

The Cost-of-Capital (CoC) approach: the cost of providing eligible own funds equal to the SCR necessary to support the insurance and reinsurance obligations over their lifetime. The CoC rate must reflect the additional rate above the risk-free rate that an undertaking would incur holding eligible own funds, and is identical for all undertakings (set at 6% in Commission Delegated Regulation 2015/35, Article 39).

Replicating portfolio exception (Art 77(4))

Where future cash flows can be reliably replicated using financial instruments with observable market values, technical provisions may be set equal to the market value of those instruments without a separate best-estimate/risk-margin split. This exception is narrow: both reliable replication and observable market values are required.

Own funds and tiering (Arts 87-99)

Basic own funds, ancillary own funds, and the three-tier eligibility system

Structure of own funds (Arts 87-91)

Basic own funds (Article 88): (1) excess of assets over liabilities both valued under Article 75; and (2) subordinated liabilities. Ancillary own funds are items callable to absorb losses that are not basic own funds -- including unpaid share capital, letters of credit, and guarantees (Article 89). These require prior supervisory approval (Article 90). Surplus funds -- accumulated profits not made available for distribution -- are deemed Tier 1 where they meet the permanence criteria (Articles 91 and 96).

Tier Key characteristics (Arts 93-96) SCR eligibility limit (Art 98(1)) MCR eligibility limit (Art 98(2))
Tier 1 Permanent availability; fully subordinated on winding-up; no fixed charges; no incentive to redeem. Most loss-absorbing category. Must exceed 1/3 of total eligible own funds Must exceed 1/2 of total eligible basic own funds
Tier 2 Substantially possesses subordination but not necessarily full permanence. Fills the gap above Tier 1 Balance after Tier 1 applied
Tier 3 Items meeting neither criterion fully. Deferred tax assets are the principal Tier 3 item. Must be less than 1/3 of total eligible own funds Not eligible for MCR coverage

Solvency Capital Requirement (Arts 100-127)

The 99.5% VaR central capital requirement: standard formula and internal models

The SCR calibration (Art 101(3))

The SCR corresponds to the Value-at-Risk of the basic own funds of an insurance or reinsurance undertaking subject to a confidence level of 99.5% over a one-year period. This is the verbatim requirement of Article 101(3). As Recital 64 explains, ruin should occur no more than once in every 200 cases -- i.e., the undertaking should be able to meet its obligations over the next 12 months with at least 99.5% probability. All undertakings must hold eligible own funds covering the SCR at all times (Article 100). SCR non-compliance triggers immediate notification and a recovery plan within two months; restoration within six months, extendable to nine months in exceptional market conditions (Article 138).

Risks the SCR must cover (Art 101(4))

At a minimum the SCR must cover:

  • Non-life underwriting risk
  • Life underwriting risk
  • Health underwriting risk
  • Market risk
  • Credit risk (counterparty default risk)
  • Operational risk -- includes legal risk but excludes strategic and reputational risk
Standard formula structure (Arts 103-111)

The standard formula SCR = Basic SCR + operational risk capital charge + adjustment for loss-absorbing capacity of technical provisions and deferred taxes (Article 103). The Basic SCR (Article 104) aggregates five risk modules using a correlation matrix, each calibrated at 99.5% VaR one-year (Article 104(4)):

  • Non-life underwriting risk module (Art 105(2)): sub-modules for premium and reserve risk and catastrophe risk.
  • Life underwriting risk module (Art 105(3)): sub-modules for mortality, longevity, disability/morbidity, life expense, revision, lapse, and life catastrophe.
  • Health underwriting risk module (Art 105(4)): covers expense risk, claims risk, and epidemic/catastrophe risk.
  • Market risk module (Art 105(5)): sub-modules for interest rate, equity, property, spread, currency, and market risk concentrations.
  • Counterparty default risk module (Art 105(6)): covers reinsurance arrangements, securitisations, derivatives, and intermediary receivables.

Key calibration constraints: the operational risk charge is capped at 30% of the Basic SCR for non-life business (Art 107(3)); the equity symmetric adjustment may not deviate from the standard charge by more than plus or minus 10 percentage points (Art 106(3)).

Undertakings may replace a subset of standard formula parameters with undertaking-specific parameters derived from their own data, subject to supervisory approval (Art 104(7)).

Internal models (Arts 112-127)

Full or partial internal models may replace the standard formula after supervisory approval (Article 112). Partial models may cover one or more risk modules or sub-modules, or specific business units (Article 112(2)). Five approval standards must be satisfied:

  • Use test (Art 120): The model must be widely used in risk management and decision-making and play an important role in the governance system.
  • Statistical quality standards (Art 121): Methods actuarially and statistically sound; data accurate, complete, and updated at least annually.
  • Calibration standards (Art 122): Results equivalent to the 99.5% VaR one-year measure; alternative measures permissible only where resulting capital provides equivalent policyholder protection.
  • Validation standards (Art 124): Regular validation cycle including back-testing, statistical testing, and sensitivity analysis.
  • Documentation standards (Art 125): Full documentation of theory, assumptions, and known limitations.

Once approved, reversion to the standard formula requires duly justified circumstances and supervisory approval (Article 117). A capital add-on may be imposed where the standard formula risk profile deviates significantly from assumptions and internal modelling is inappropriate or under development (Article 37).

Minimum Capital Requirement (Arts 128-131)

The absolute floor below which authorisation must be withdrawn: the 85% VaR linear function

Intervention ladder

The SCR and MCR form a two-rung intervention ladder. Non-compliance with the SCR triggers a recovery plan but does not immediately require withdrawal of authorisation. Non-compliance with the MCR is the absolute floor: failure to restore own funds within three months leads to mandatory withdrawal of authorisation (Article 144). MCR non-compliance must be notified immediately; a short-term finance scheme must be submitted within one month (Article 139).

MCR calibration and calculation (Art 129)

The MCR is calibrated to a Value-at-Risk of 85% confidence over one year -- a lower threshold than the SCR reflecting the final intervention point (Article 129(1)(c)). It is calculated as a simple, auditable linear function of technical provisions, written premiums, capital-at-risk, deferred taxes, and administrative expenses (Article 129(2)). It must be calculated quarterly (Article 129(4)).

MCR corridor (Art 129(3)): The MCR must not fall below 25% nor exceed 45% of the SCR. This corridor maintains the intervention ladder: the MCR can never exceed the SCR, and the gap between them cannot become trivially small.

MCR absolute floors (Art 129(1)(d))
  • Non-life insurance (standard): EUR 2,200,000
  • Non-life (liability, credit, suretyship classes 10-15): EUR 3,200,000
  • Life insurance: EUR 3,200,000
  • Reinsurance: EUR 3,200,000
  • Captive reinsurance: EUR 1,000,000
  • Composite (life + non-life): sum of the respective floors

Prudent-person investment principle (Arts 132-135)

Asset management: security, quality, liquidity and profitability -- no mandatory allocations

Article 132: the prudent person standard

All assets must be managed in accordance with the prudent person principle (Article 132). The key requirements are:

  • Only invest in assets whose risks can be properly identified, measured, monitored, managed, controlled, and reported.
  • Assets must ensure security, quality, liquidity, and profitability of the portfolio as a whole.
  • Assets covering technical provisions must be invested appropriately to the nature and duration of the liabilities and in the best interest of policyholders and beneficiaries.
  • Derivatives are permissible only to reduce risk or facilitate efficient portfolio management.
  • Unregulated market assets must be kept at prudent levels; assets must be properly diversified.

Critically, Article 133 prohibits Member States from requiring undertakings to invest in particular categories of assets (no mandatory asset allocation) and from requiring prior approval or systematic notification of investment decisions. This principle-based approach replaced the prescriptive asset-by-asset limits of Solvency I.

Pillar 2: System of governance (Arts 41-50)

Four mandatory governance functions, fit-and-proper requirements, and the ORSA

The system of governance requirement (Art 41)

Every undertaking must have an effective system of governance providing for sound and prudent management of its business (Article 41). The system must be proportionate to the nature, scale, and complexity of the undertaking's operations. It must include: adequate organisational structure with clear allocation of responsibilities; transparent decision-making processes; written policies on risk management, internal control, internal audit, and outsourcing reviewed at least annually; and continuity and contingency plans.

The management body bears ultimate responsibility for the system of governance and for ensuring ongoing compliance with Solvency II requirements.

Risk management function (Art 44)

Must cover all material risk categories: underwriting and reserving, asset-liability management, investment (including derivatives), liquidity and concentration risk, operational risk, and reinsurance and other risk-mitigation techniques. For internal model users, the risk management function also oversees model design, implementation, testing, documentation, and performance monitoring.

Compliance function (Art 46)

Advises the management body on compliance with applicable laws and regulations and assesses the possible impact of changes in the legal environment on the undertaking's operations. Reports findings to the management body.

Internal audit function (Art 47)

Evaluates the adequacy and effectiveness of the internal control system and other elements of the system of governance. Must be objective and independent from all operational functions. Audit findings and recommendations reported directly to the management body. This function cannot be combined with other governance functions (Recital 32).

Actuarial function (Art 48)

Coordinates the calculation of technical provisions; ensures appropriateness of methodologies, underlying models, and assumptions; assesses the sufficiency and quality of data; compares best estimates against experience; informs the management body and supervisors of the reliability and adequacy of the technical provisions calculation; expresses an opinion on the overall underwriting policy and on the adequacy of reinsurance arrangements.

Multiple governance functions may be combined in smaller undertakings -- except the internal audit function, which must remain independent at all times (Recital 32).

Fit and proper (Art 42)

All persons who effectively run the undertaking or hold other key functions must at all times be: (a) professionally qualified, knowledgeable, and experienced relative to their role (fit); and (b) of good repute and integrity (proper). These are ongoing requirements: a person who ceases to meet them must be replaced. Changes to key function holders must be notified to the supervisory authority.

Outsourcing (Art 49)

Undertakings remain fully responsible for discharging all their obligations when outsourcing. Outsourcing of critical or important functions must not: impair the quality of the governance system; increase operational risk; impair supervisory monitoring; undermine continuous service to policyholders. Supervisors must be notified prior to outsourcing critical or important functions.

Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (Art 45)

The undertaking's prospective self-assessment: not a capital requirement, but a strategic governance tool

What the ORSA must cover

As an integral part of the risk management system, every undertaking must conduct its own risk and solvency assessment covering three elements (Article 45(1)):

  • (a) Overall solvency needs with respect to its specific risk profile, approved risk tolerance limits, and business strategy -- the forward-looking assessment of the capital the undertaking needs given its own risks.
  • (b) Continuous compliance with SCR, MCR, and technical provision requirements -- how the undertaking will remain solvent throughout its planning horizon.
  • (c) Degree of deviation of the undertaking's risk profile from the SCR assumptions -- which standard formula or internal model assumptions do not reflect the undertaking's actual risks, and why.

The ORSA must be conducted at least annually and following any significant change in risk profile (Article 45(5)). Results are reported to the supervisory authority as part of supervisory reporting. Critically, the ORSA does not serve to calculate a capital requirement different from the SCR or MCR (Article 45(7)). It is a governance and strategy tool, not an additional capital floor.

Pillar 3: Transparency and reporting

The SFCR (public disclosure) and RSR (supervisory reporting): making the balance sheet legible

Solvency and Financial Condition Report (Arts 51-56)

Every undertaking must publish an annual SFCR within two weeks of supervisory approval. The SFCR must contain five sections (Article 51):

  • (a) Business description and performance.
  • (b) System of governance and assessment of its adequacy.
  • (c) Risk exposure, concentration, mitigation, and sensitivity by risk category.
  • (d) Valuation bases for assets, technical provisions, and other liabilities with explanation of material differences from financial statement valuations.
  • (e) Capital management: structure and amount of own funds; SCR and MCR amounts; SCR calculation method; significant deviations from standard formula or internal model assumptions; any MCR non-compliance or significant SCR non-compliance.

Supervisors may permit non-disclosure where information would give competitors significant undue advantage or is subject to confidentiality obligations (Article 53), but this exemption cannot apply to capital management disclosures (Article 53(4)). MCR breach or significant SCR breach triggers additional immediate public disclosure (Article 54).

Regular Supervisory Reporting (Art 35)

Undertakings must submit to supervisors all information necessary for supervision: governance system assessments, valuation principles and technical provision details, risk exposures, risk management systems, and capital structure. Reporting occurs at predefined periods, on predefined events, or during enquiries (Article 35(1)-(2)).

The RSR is not publicly disclosed (unlike the SFCR) and may contain commercially sensitive information. It forms the basis of the supervisory review process and enables the supervisor to determine whether a capital add-on under Article 37 is warranted.

EIOPA collects aggregate data from national supervisors and reports annually to the European Parliament, the Council, and the Commission on supervisory convergence and the application of Solvency II (Article 52).

Structure of the directive: Title by Title

312 articles across 6 Titles: from authorisation to winding-up

Title I -- General rules on taking-up and pursuit (the main operative Title)

Chapter I (Arts 1-13): Subject matter, scope, definitions. Article 2 defines scope as all direct life and non-life insurance and reinsurance undertakings established in the EU. Article 3 excludes statutory social security schemes. Article 4 excludes small undertakings with gross premium income below EUR 5 million, technical provisions below EUR 25 million, no liability/credit/suretyship business, and reinsurance below EUR 0.5 million or 10% of gross premiums -- but only where no threshold has been exceeded for three consecutive years. Article 13 provides 39 definitions, including qualifying holding (10% or more of capital or voting rights).

Chapter II (Arts 14-26): Taking-up of business. Authorisation is required before any insurance or reinsurance business may commence (Article 14). A single EU authorisation covers both freedom of establishment and freedom to provide services across all Member States (Article 15). Authorisation conditions include: limitation of objects; scheme of operations demonstrating financial soundness; eligible basic own funds at least equal to the MCR absolute floor; governance system compliance.

Chapter III (Arts 27-39): Supervisory authorities. Primary objective of supervision: policyholder and beneficiary protection (Art 27). Home Member State bears exclusive financial supervision responsibility for the entire business (Art 30). General supervisory powers include preventive and corrective measures, on-site inspections, and information requests extending to outsourced service providers (Art 34). Capital add-on power (Art 37) is a last resort following the supervisory review process.

Chapter IV (Arts 40-74): Conditions governing business. The governance pillar (Arts 41-50), public disclosure pillar (Arts 51-56), qualifying holdings (Arts 57-63), professional secrecy and information exchange (Arts 64-71), and auditor duties (Art 72). Auditors must promptly report MCR/SCR non-compliance and refusal to certify accounts to supervisors.

Chapters VI-VIII (Arts 75-177): Quantitative requirements and cross-border business. The Pillar 1 core covered above. Chapter VII (Arts 136-144) covers the intervention ladder for undertakings in difficulty. Chapter VIII (Arts 145-177) covers freedom of establishment via branches (three-month clearance procedure) and freedom to provide services (one-month notification). Third-country branches must hold assets equal to at least half the MCR absolute floor and deposit one quarter as security (Art 162).

Title II -- Specific provisions for insurance and reinsurance (Arts 178-211)

Rome I applies to insurance contracts (Art 178). Member States may not require prior approval or systematic notification of policy conditions or premium scales (Arts 181-182), though life undertakings may be required to notify technical bases for actuarial compliance verification. Life insurance policyholders must receive pre-contractual information on benefits, terms, options, and unit-linking; a 14-30 day cooling-off period applies to individual life contracts (Art 186).

Non-life specific provisions cover community co-insurance for large risks (Arts 190-196), tourist assistance as class 18 (Art 197), legal expenses insurance (Arts 198-205 -- independent claims management and free choice of lawyer guaranteed), and health insurance as an alternative to social security (Art 206). Special purpose vehicles require prior supervisory approval (Art 211).

Title IV -- Reorganisation and winding-up (Arts 267-296)

Only the competent authorities of the home Member State may take reorganisation decisions or open winding-up proceedings (Arts 269, 273). Both are automatically recognised and effective throughout the EU without formalities. Insurance claims have priority over other claims in winding-up (Art 275). Supervisory authorities of all other Member States must be informed urgently of reorganisation decisions. The Title builds on the mutual-recognition principle established by Dir 2001/17/EC, which Solvency II recasts.

Title III: Group supervision (Arts 212-266)

Group solvency, the college of supervisors, and the group supervisor

Scope and group solvency

Group supervision under Title III applies in addition to solo supervision -- it does not replace it (Article 213). Participating undertakings in an insurance or reinsurance group must ensure that eligible own funds within the group are always at least equal to the group SCR (Article 218(1)). The group SCR must be calculated at least annually; continuous monitoring is required.

Two calculation methods are available:

  • Method 1 (default, Arts 230-232): Accounting consolidation. The group SCR is calculated on the basis of consolidated accounts. The consolidated group SCR must be at least equal to the sum of individual MCRs -- it cannot be lower than the worst-case floor.
  • Method 2 (alternative, Arts 233-234): Deduction and aggregation. The group SCR is the sum of individual SCRs at proportional shares. Used where Method 1 is not appropriate.

Double-counting of own funds across group entities is prohibited (Arts 222-223). Reciprocal financing -- intra-group creation of own funds -- is excluded.

Group supervisor (Art 247)

One supervisor is designated from the supervisory authorities of the Member States concerned. Selection is based on the location of the EU ultimate parent undertaking (or, for groups not headed by an insurer, the entity with the largest balance sheet total). The group supervisor coordinates supervisory activity, chairs the college, and leads on group solvency assessments and group internal model approvals.

College of supervisors (Art 248)

A college of supervisors must be established for every insurance group, chaired by the group supervisor. Membership includes supervisors of all Member States where subsidiary head offices are located. Supervisors of significant branches may participate with limited roles. The college facilitates cooperation, information exchange, consultation, and convergence of supervisory decisions. Disagreements between college members may be referred to EIOPA for binding mediation.

Third-country equivalence (Arts 260-264)

Where a group is headed by a third-country parent, supervisory authorities verify whether the third country's group supervision is equivalent to Title III. The Commission may formally assess equivalence. Where equivalent, supervisors rely on the third-country regime. Where not equivalent, Article 218-258 applies mutatis mutandis -- i.e., the EU group supervision framework is applied as if the EU supervisory authority were the group supervisor.

Equivalence decisions have been adopted for Switzerland, Bermuda, Japan, and several other jurisdictions, enabling substantial deference to those regimes for groups headquartered there.

Key definitions (Art 13)

Article 13 provides 39 definitions; the most operationally significant are set out below

SCR
Solvency Capital Requirement. The principal capital standard: the Value-at-Risk of basic own funds at a 99.5% confidence level over one year (Art 101(3)). Eligible own funds must cover it at all times.
MCR
Minimum Capital Requirement. The absolute floor: a simple linear function calibrated to 85% VaR over one year (Art 129). Must remain between 25% and 45% of the SCR. Breach triggers mandatory withdrawal of authorisation if not restored within three months.
ORSA
Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (Art 45). The undertaking's own prospective assessment of its solvency needs, continuous compliance, and risk profile deviation from SCR assumptions. Not a capital requirement; a governance and strategy tool.
SFCR
Solvency and Financial Condition Report (Arts 51-56). The mandatory annual public disclosure covering business performance, governance, risk profile, valuation, and capital management including SCR and MCR amounts.
Best estimate
Art 77(2). The probability-weighted average of future cash flows, discounted at the risk-free interest rate term structure. Gross of reinsurance; calculated net of reinsurance only after separate adjustment for counterparty default risk.
Risk margin
Art 77(3). The Cost-of-Capital component of technical provisions: the cost of holding eligible own funds equal to the SCR needed to support the obligations over their lifetime. CoC rate set at 6% under Delegated Regulation 2015/35.
Basic own funds
Art 88. The excess of assets over liabilities (both at market-consistent values under Art 75) plus subordinated liabilities. The starting point for the own-funds tiering framework.
Qualifying holding
Art 13(21). A direct or indirect holding in an undertaking representing 10% or more of the capital or voting rights, or which makes it possible to exercise a significant influence over the management. Acquisition at 20%, 30%, and 50% thresholds requires supervisory notification.
EIOPA
European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority. The Level 3 supervisor established by Regulation 1094/2010, replacing CEIOPS. Issues binding regulatory and implementing technical standards under Solvency II; chairs mediation between college members; reports annually to EU institutions.
Home Member State
Art 13(8). The Member State in which the undertaking's registered office is located. The home Member State bears exclusive responsibility for financial supervision of the undertaking's entire business, including cross-border branches and services (Art 30).
Prudent person principle
Art 132. The investment standard: assets whose risks cannot be properly identified, measured, monitored, managed, controlled, and reported may not be held. Assets covering technical provisions must match the nature and duration of liabilities.
Omnibus II
Directive 2014/51/EU. The principal post-2009 amendment: extended transposition deadline to 31 March 2015; set application date as 1 January 2016; introduced long-term guarantee measures (matching adjustment, volatility adjustment, transitional measures for risk-free rates and technical provisions).

Small undertaking exclusion Art 4 Gross premium income below EUR 5 million and technical provisions below EUR 25 million (both conditions must hold; exceeded for three consecutive years = applies from fourth year).
ORSA Art 45 Prospective; covers solvency needs, continuous compliance, and risk-profile deviation. Not a capital requirement. Annual minimum.
Group supervisor Art 247 Single designated supervisor for the group; chairs the college of supervisors.
Group solvency -- Method 1 Arts 230-232 Accounting consolidation; consolidated group SCR at least equal to sum of solo MCRs.
Prudent person principle Art 132-133 All assets; no mandatory allocation; no prior approval required; derivatives only to reduce risk or for efficient portfolio management.
Home-state supervision Art 30 Exclusive financial supervision responsibility for entire business including cross-border.
Repeal of predecessors Art 310 13 predecessor directives repealed effective 1 November 2012.

Legislative timeline

From Solvency I patchwork to risk-based framework applied from 1 January 2016

1973-2005
Solvency I era: national-minimum-harmonisation directives govern EU insurance prudential requirements. Capital requirements use fixed factors applied to premiums and claims reserves, with no direct link to actual risk profile. The patchwork includes Dir 73/239 (First Non-Life), Dir 2002/83 (Life Assurance), Dir 2005/68 (Reinsurance), and nine others.
2001-2009
The Solvency II project develops under the Lamfalussy four-level process. CEIOPS conducts Quantitative Impact Studies (QIS1 to QIS5) to calibrate the framework, involving hundreds of undertakings across all Member States.
25 November 2009
Directive 2009/138/EC adopted by the European Parliament and the Council. Published in OJ L 335, 17.12.2009, p. 1. Original transposition deadline: 31 October 2012.
16 April 2014
Directive 2014/51/EU (Omnibus II) amends Solvency II. Transposition deadline extended to 31 March 2015; application date set to 1 January 2016; long-term guarantee package introduced (matching adjustment, volatility adjustment, transitional measures); EIOPA formally embedded as Level 3 supervisor issuing binding technical standards.
31 March 2015
EU transposition deadline. All 27 Member States notified transposition measures to the Commission. See the interactive map in the Transposition section below.
1 January 2016
Solvency II enters into full application. Full SCR, MCR, SFCR, ORSA, and group supervision requirements take effect. Predecessor directives had been formally repealed effective 1 November 2012 (Art 310), with transitional arrangements bridging the gap.
2021-2022
Solvency II 2020 Review: Commission publishes legislative proposal COM(2021)581 following EIOPA technical advice. Focus areas: long-term guarantee measures, proportionality, macro-prudential tools, and reporting simplification.
2025
Directive (EU) 2025/2 (Solvency II Review Directive) published, amending the 2009 framework. Key changes include revised long-term guarantee measures, proportionality improvements, macro-prudential tools, and sustainability risk provisions.

Glossary

Technical terms from the Solvency II framework explained

VaR (Value-at-Risk)
Value-at-Risk. The maximum loss that will not be exceeded with a given probability (confidence level) over a defined time period. Under Solvency II, the SCR is set at the 99.5% VaR over one year: the own-funds loss that occurs with at most 0.5% probability over the next 12 months.
Technical provisions
The market-consistent value of insurance liabilities: best estimate plus risk margin (Art 77). The central balance-sheet liability under Solvency II. Must reflect the current transfer value of obligations, not historic premium calculations.
Cost-of-Capital (CoC)
The rate used to compute the risk margin in technical provisions. Represents the additional return above the risk-free rate that an acquirer would require for holding the SCR-level own funds needed to run off the portfolio. Set at 6% under Commission Delegated Regulation 2015/35, Article 39.
Basic SCR (BSCR)
The aggregate of the five standard-formula risk modules -- non-life underwriting, life underwriting, health underwriting, market risk, and counterparty default risk -- using a correlation matrix. The operational risk charge and loss-absorbing capacity adjustment are added to the BSCR to give the overall SCR (Art 103).
Matching adjustment
An Omnibus II LTG measure: permits life insurers with matched portfolios of fixed-income assets and predictable liabilities to adjust the risk-free rate upward by the portion of the credit spread on the assets that reflects the undertaking's own liquidity premium. Requires supervisory approval and strict asset-liability matching conditions.
Volatility adjustment
A second Omnibus II LTG measure: a country-specific or currency-specific adjustment to the risk-free rate term structure, derived from the spread of assets backing technical provisions in the relevant market, to dampen artificial volatility from short-term spread movements.
Captive reinsurer
A reinsurance undertaking owned by a non-financial undertaking (the parent), the purpose of which is to provide reinsurance coverage exclusively for the risks of the group or of the parent. Subject to a lower MCR absolute floor of EUR 1,000,000 (Art 129(1)(d)).
Composite undertaking
An undertaking authorised to carry on both life and non-life insurance (Art 73). Composites are largely prohibited under Solvency II; grandfathering applies only for undertakings active at specified accession dates. Where permitted, both activities must be managed separately and separately financed at the MCR level (Art 74).
College of supervisors
A permanent coordination structure established for every insurance group (Art 248), chaired by the group supervisor. Facilitates supervisory cooperation, joint assessments, and convergence of supervisory decisions. EIOPA participates as observer and may mediate binding disputes between members.
Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)
An entity that assumes risks from insurance undertakings via reinsurance or other mechanisms, and fully funds its exposure through debt issuance, letters of credit, or other financing. Must receive prior supervisory approval (Art 211). Securitisation of insurance risk (insurance-linked securities / catastrophe bonds) uses SPVs under Solvency II.
Surplus funds
Accumulated profits not made available for distribution (Arts 91 and 96). Treated as Tier 1 own funds where they meet the permanence and subordination criteria. Common in mutual insurers and with-profits life funds where policyholders hold a beneficial interest in accumulated reserves.
Loss-absorbing capacity (LAC)
The adjustment under Art 108 by which the SCR may be reduced to reflect the potential for future discretionary benefits (e.g., profit-participation reductions) and deferred tax liabilities to absorb losses under stress, reducing the own funds needed to cover the net SCR charge.

The EU financial regulation family in the Brubru EU Canon

EU Financial Regulation family
MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU)
The Markets in Financial Instruments Directive II: the governance, conduct of business, transparency, and market structure framework for investment firms and trading venues. MiFID II applies to insurers distributing investment products; the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) applies in parallel for IBIP products.
EU Financial Regulation family
European Electronic Communications Code (Dir 2018/1972)
The horizontal regulatory framework for electronic communications networks and services: spectrum, access obligations, universal service, and NRA powers. Relevant to insurers as operators of telecommunications-dependent digital infrastructure.
EU Financial Regulation family
Insurance Distribution Directive (Directive 2016/97/EU)
The conduct-of-business and distribution rules for insurance intermediaries and direct insurers. Solvency II governs the prudential soundness of the undertaking; IDD governs the distribution process -- the two directives are complementary pillars of EU insurance law.
Banking parallel
Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and Directive (CRD)
The banking prudential counterpart to Solvency II: the CRR/CRD IV and CRR2/CRD V framework implements Basel III for EU credit institutions and investment firms. Solvency II drew on Basel II/III methodology (particularly the VaR approach and tiered capital) and developed in parallel with banking reform. Group supervisors of mixed financial conglomerates must coordinate across both frameworks.
EU Financial Supervision
EIOPA Founding Regulation (Regulation 1094/2010)
Regulation 1094/2010 established EIOPA as the European Supervisory Authority for insurance and occupational pensions, replacing CEIOPS. EIOPA issues binding regulatory and implementing technical standards under Solvency II, conducts stress tests, publishes opinions on supervisory convergence, and acts as binding mediator in college of supervisors disputes.
EU Pensions
IORP II Directive (Directive 2016/2341/EU)
The prudential and governance framework for Institutions for Occupational Retirement Provision (pension funds). IORP II follows Solvency II's governance architecture (own risk assessment, fit-and-proper, key functions) while maintaining a separate, less prescriptive quantitative framework for pension-specific risks and national pension regulatory traditions.
