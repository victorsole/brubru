01

What it does

A medicine's full safety profile is only known after it reaches patients. Directive 2010/84/EU, paired with Regulation (EU) 1235/2010, delivered the biggest overhaul of EU pharmacovigilance since the system began: it rewrote Title IX of the Community Code and built the modern safety architecture we use today.

It clarified roles, made reporting proportionate, and created the tools that run post-marketing safety: the PRAC, EudraVigilance single reporting, risk management plans, the additional-monitoring black triangle, single PSUR assessment and the urgent Union procedure.

02

Pharmacovigilance systems, the QPPV and risk management plans

Both Member States and marketing-authorisation holders must operate an audited pharmacovigilance system. Every holder must have a Qualified Person Responsible for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV) resident in the Union, keep a pharmacovigilance system master file, and run a risk management system with a risk management plan for each product. Authorities can attach post-authorisation safety studies (PASS) and efficacy studies (PAES) as conditions of the authorisation (Articles 21a, 22a, 22b).

03

EudraVigilance, single reporting and the black triangle

Suspected adverse reactions are reported to a single Union database, EudraVigilance: within 15 days for serious reactions and 90 days for non-serious ones. Medicines under additional monitoring (all new active substances and biologicals, among others) carry an inverted black triangle and a standard sentence, with a public EMA list. National medicines web-portals make assessments and leaflets public.

04

PSURs, signals and the urgent Union procedure

Periodic safety update reports were reoriented from case listings to a risk-benefit analysis, with a single assessment across Member States and common Union reference dates. The PRAC leads signal detection. For fast-moving safety concerns the directive created the urgent Union procedure (Articles 107i to 107k): a Member State or the Commission can trigger an EU-wide assessment and take immediate interim measures.

05

The PRAC

The Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (PRAC) runs through the whole text and is formally created by the companion Regulation (EU) 1235/2010. It gives the scientific safety recommendation whenever action is based on pharmacovigilance data, which the CHMP or the coordination group then act on.

How the Member States transposed it

A directive is not directly applicable: each Member State must write it into its own national law by a transposition deadline. For the Pharmacovigilance Directive (Directive 2010/84/EU) the EU transposition deadline was 21 July 2012.
