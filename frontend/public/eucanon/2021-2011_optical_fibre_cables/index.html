Overview

The EU's first anti-dumping entry in the canon, and a textbook application of the Article 2(6a) significant-distortions methodology

What this Regulation does

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2011 of 17 November 2021 imposes definitive anti-dumping duties on imports of single-mode optical fibre cables originating in the People's Republic of China. The legal basis is Article 9(4) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036, the EU's basic anti-dumping Regulation.

The investigation was initiated on 24 September 2020 following a complaint by Europacable, the European association of wire and cable manufacturers, on behalf of Union producers representing more than 25% of total Union production. The investigation covered the period 1 July 2019 to 30 June 2020.

This is the first anti-dumping measure in the Brubru EU Canon. It complements the anti-subsidy (countervailing-duty) family that includes the China BEV, China-Egypt glass-fibre, India-Indonesia stainless-steel and Morocco aluminium-wheels cases. Anti-dumping offsets below-normal-value pricing; anti-subsidy offsets government subsidies. A separate anti-subsidy investigation ran in parallel on the same product.

The product and its strategic importance

Optical fibre cables are the backbone of modern telecommunications and internet infrastructure. Single-mode optical fibre cables (CN code ex 8544 70 00, TARIC 8544700010) consist of one or more individually sheathed single-mode fibres with protective casing, whether or not containing electric conductors. The product is distinct from cables where all fibres are individually fitted with operational connectors at both extremities (excluded) and from submarine cables (excluded).

The cables are used in long-distance and metropolitan-area fibre networks, connecting exchanges, data centres and ultimately households and businesses. Chinese producers had become dominant global suppliers by offering prices far below those achievable without State-distorted inputs, and by the investigation period their imports were significantly undercutting Union producers' prices.

New-exporter review mechanism (Article 2)

A genuine new exporting producer that did not export the product during the investigation period and is not related to any cooperating exporter may apply to the Commission to be added to the Annex I (cooperating) rate of 31.2%. This mechanism prevents new-market entrants from automatically receiving the higher residual rate while ensuring the process is robust against circumvention.

How dumping was measured: the Article 2(6a) significant-distortions methodology

Chinese domestic prices were State-distorted, so normal value was constructed from Argentine costs

Why China's domestic prices could not be used

In a standard anti-dumping investigation the normal value is the domestic selling price in the exporting country, or a constructed value based on costs in that country. For China, the Commission found that the domestic prices and costs for optical fibre cables were significantly distorted by State intervention, including preferential access to raw materials, subsidised energy and distorted factor costs more broadly, pursuant to the analysis of significant distortions in China set out in the Commission's country report on China under Article 2(6a) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036 (as introduced by Regulation (EU) 2017/2321).

Because of these distortions, using Chinese domestic prices would have produced a misleading normal value, understating the "fair" price at which the product should have been sold. The Commission therefore applied the Article 2(6a) methodology: constructing the normal value using undistorted costs from a representative country.

Argentina as the representative country

The Commission selected Argentina as the representative country for the normal-value construction. Argentina was chosen on the basis of an analysis of its economic development level, the existence of relevant production in Argentina, the availability of undistorted data, and the absence of significant State distortions affecting the relevant sector.

Each factor of production was priced using Argentine commodity and labour-market data. Key inputs included: optical fibre (the highest-value input, at 660.5 CNY/kg equivalent), aluminium alloy wire, aramid yarn (used in protective sheathing), sheath materials, steel strand, labour (at 40.1 CNY/hour equivalent), and electricity (at 0.339 CNY/kWh equivalent). These Argentine values replaced the distorted Chinese costs in the constructed normal value calculation.

Legal basis: Article 2(6a) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036

Article 2(6a) was introduced by Regulation (EU) 2017/2321 following the expiry of China's transitional WTO Protocol of Accession provision. It allows the Commission to depart from using domestic prices and costs in a country where significant distortions exist due to State intervention in the economy. The provision was upheld by the General Court and has been consistently applied in investigations involving Chinese products, including solar glass, biodiesel, electric bicycles and, as here, optical fibre cables. This case is a textbook application of the methodology.

Dumping, injury and causation

Margins established, material injury found, causal link confirmed

Dumping margins established
The Commission compared the constructed normal value (based on Argentine costs) with the export price to the EU for each sampled group. The resulting dumping margins were:
  • FTT group (FiberHome): 44.0%
  • ZTT group (Jiangsu Zhongtian): 19.7%
  • Other cooperating companies: 31.2%
  • All other companies: 44.0%
Price undercutting in the Union market
The Commission analysed undercutting by comparing the Chinese import prices with Union industry selling prices, on a like-for-like basis. The sampled groups undercut Union prices by:
  • FTT group: 30.0%
  • ZTT group: 33.2%
  • Overall weighted-average undercutting: 31.5%
  • Tender analysis confirmed systematic undercutting
Volume of dumped imports
Chinese imports of optical fibre cables increased significantly in both absolute terms and as a proportion of Union consumption during the investigation period, displacing Union producers from their own market.
Price depression
The Union industry was forced to lower its prices to compete with dumped Chinese imports, resulting in price depression. The overall undercutting of 31.5% exerted sustained downward pressure on Union selling prices.
Loss of sales
Union producers suffered a loss of sales volume and market share as buyers shifted to lower-priced Chinese imports, undermining the Union industry's ability to operate at viable production levels.
Causal link confirmed
The Commission established a causal link between the dumped imports and material injury to the Union industry. Other factors examined under Article 3(7) (third-country imports, demand, export performance) did not break the link.
Union interest
Under Article 21 of the basic Regulation the Commission confirmed that imposing measures was in the Union interest, weighing Union industry interests against importers and users. The Government of China's argument that measures would harm digital-field cooperation was rejected.
Registration discontinued
Imports had been registered by Regulation (EU) 2021/548 (29 March 2021). Article 3 of this Regulation discontinued that registration on the imposition of definitive duties, as the provisional measure was not extended to a retroactive application.

The lesser-duty rule

In anti-dumping the duty is capped at whatever removes the injury; here that meant the dumping margins

How the lesser-duty rule works in anti-dumping

Article 7(2) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036 requires that the anti-dumping duty shall be less than the dumping margin if such lesser duty is adequate to remove the injury to the Union industry. In practice this means the duty is the lower of two figures: the dumping margin (the amount by which the export price is below the normal value) and the injury elimination level (the amount needed to bring the import price up to a level that removes injury to the Union industry, calculated as the Union industry's cost of production plus a target profit, compared with the landed import price).

The contrast with anti-subsidy is important: the lesser-duty rule no longer applies in anti-subsidy investigations under the current Regulation (EU) 2016/1037. Countervailing duties are set at the full subsidy amount. This divergence means anti-dumping duties can be lower than anti-subsidy duties for the same product if the injury margins are the binding constraint in anti-dumping, as is frequently the case.

Injury margins exceeded dumping margins: the lesser duty is the dumping margin

In this investigation the Commission established the injury elimination levels for each sampled group. An injury elimination level is higher than the dumping margin when the import price needs to rise by more than the dumping amount simply to eliminate the injury. This can occur when Union producers are suffering additional pricing pressure beyond the dumping itself (for example, due to overcapacity).

The injury elimination levels were established as follows, all higher than the corresponding dumping margins:

  • FTT group: injury margin 61.3% vs dumping margin 44.0% (lesser duty: 44.0%)
  • ZTT group: injury margin 42.0% vs dumping margin 19.7% (lesser duty: 19.7%)
  • Other cooperating: injury margin 52.7% vs dumping margin 31.2% (lesser duty: 31.2%)
  • All others: injury margin 61.3% vs dumping margin 44.0% (lesser duty: 44.0%)

Because the lesser of the two figures is always the dumping margin, the definitive anti-dumping duties are set at the dumping margins. This means the duties are adequate to remove injury but are not set above what is necessary to offset the dumping itself.

Anti-dumping vs anti-subsidy: two parallel instruments

This case ran alongside a separate anti-subsidy investigation on the same product; the two instruments have different legal bases, methodologies and duty-setting rules

Anti-dumping (this Regulation)
Offsets dumping: selling in the export market at a price below the normal value (the price in the domestic market or a constructed value). Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2016/1036. Duty-setting: lesser-duty rule applies, duty is the lower of the dumping margin and the injury elimination level. Measure: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/2011.
Anti-subsidy (countervailing, parallel case)
Offsets government subsidies: financial contributions by a government conferring a benefit on exported goods. Legal basis: Regulation (EU) 2016/1037. Duty-setting: lesser-duty rule does not apply; duty equals the full countervailable subsidy. Parallel investigation on the same optical fibre cables was initiated on 21 December 2020 and led to a separate countervailing-duty Regulation.

Why two separate investigations on the same product?

Dumping and subsidisation are distinct legal wrongs under EU trade-defence law and WTO rules, each governed by its own basic Regulation (2016/1036 for anti-dumping, 2016/1037 for anti-subsidy), its own investigation procedure and its own duty-setting methodology. A product can be both dumped and subsidised simultaneously; the Commission may investigate both in parallel. The resulting measures can apply cumulatively, provided there is no double-counting of the same benefit.

In the optical fibre cables case the Commission initiated the anti-dumping investigation on 24 September 2020 and the parallel anti-subsidy investigation on 21 December 2020. This pattern of parallel investigations is also visible in the Morocco aluminium road wheels case (anti-dumping duties from Reg (EU) 2023/99 were already in force when the anti-subsidy Reg (EU) 2025/500 was adopted), confirming it as a standard Commission approach for products from State-intervening economies.

Key definitions

Nine core concepts used throughout this Regulation and EU anti-dumping law

  • Anti-dumping duty: a duty imposed to offset the practice of dumping, where an exporter sells a product in the importing market at a price below the normal value. The duty eliminates the competitive advantage derived from dumping. Legal basis: Article 9(4) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036.
  • Dumping margin: the difference between the normal value and the export price, expressed as a percentage of the export price. In this case: FTT group 44.0%, ZTT group 19.7%, other cooperating 31.2%, all others 44.0%.
  • Normal value: the comparable price of the like product in the ordinary course of trade in the exporting country. Where significant distortions exist, the normal value is constructed under Article 2(6a) using undistorted costs from a representative country.
  • Constructed normal value: a normal value built up from factors of production (raw materials, labour, energy, overheads, profit) priced at undistorted values from a representative country. Used here because China's domestic prices are State-distorted. Argentina was selected as the representative country.
  • Representative country: a third country selected by the Commission to provide undistorted cost benchmarks for the normal-value construction under Article 2(6a). The country must have a similar level of economic development to the exporting country and produce the like product under undistorted market conditions. Argentina was chosen in this case.
  • Lesser-duty rule: Article 7(2) of the basic anti-dumping Regulation requires that the duty be less than the full dumping margin if that lesser amount is adequate to remove injury. The duty is therefore capped at the lower of the dumping margin and the injury elimination level. In this case the dumping margins were the lower figures and became the duties. Contrast: the lesser-duty rule does not apply in anti-subsidy investigations under Regulation (EU) 2016/1037.
  • Injury elimination level: the price increase needed to bring Chinese import prices to a level that would remove material injury to the Union industry. Calculated as the Union industry's cost of production plus a target profit margin, compared with the landed import price (CIF plus customs duty). In this case: FTT group 61.3%, ZTT group 42.0%, other cooperating 52.7%, all others 61.3%.
  • Undercutting: the extent to which Chinese import prices were lower than Union industry selling prices, on a like-for-like basis. Overall undercutting margin of 31.5% (30.0% for FTT and 33.2% for ZTT) confirmed that Chinese imports were significantly cheaper than Union production, forcing the Union industry to lower its prices or lose sales.
  • TARIC additional code: a four-character alphanumeric code appended to the standard Combined Nomenclature (CN) code in the EU's Integrated Tariff (TARIC) to identify a specific producer subject to an individual duty rate. The codes in this Regulation are C696 (FTT/FiberHome group), C697 (ZTT group) and C999 (all other imports from China).

Glossary

Key terms as used in Regulation (EU) 2021/2011 and EU anti-dumping law

Article 2(6a)
The provision of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036 permitting the Commission to construct the normal value from undistorted costs in a representative country when significant State-induced distortions affect the exporting country's domestic prices. Introduced by Regulation (EU) 2017/2321.
Basic anti-dumping Regulation
Regulation (EU) 2016/1036 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 8 June 2016 on protection against dumped imports from countries not members of the European Union. The procedural and substantive framework for all EU anti-dumping investigations. Legal basis for this Regulation: Article 9(4).
CCCME
China Chamber of Commerce for Import and Export of Machinery and Electronic Products. Participated as an interested party in this investigation on behalf of Chinese producers, making submissions on behalf of the industry.
CIF
Cost, insurance and freight: the customs value of goods at the EU border, used as the base for calculating ad valorem anti-dumping duties.
Europacable
European association of wire and cable manufacturers. Filed the complaint on behalf of Union producers on 24 September 2020, triggering this anti-dumping investigation. Represented producers accounting for more than 25% of total Union production of optical fibre cables.
FTT group
One of the two sampled Chinese exporting producer groups: FiberHome Telecommunication Technologies Co., Ltd., Nanjing Wasin Fujikura Optical Communication Ltd., and Hubei Fiberhome Boxin Electronic Co., Ltd. Subject to a 44.0% anti-dumping duty (TARIC code C696).
GOC
Government of China. Participated as an interested party and argued that anti-dumping measures would harm digital-field cooperation. The argument was rejected by the Commission under the Union interest assessment.
Reg 2021/548
Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/548 of 29 March 2021, which subjected imports of optical fibre cables from China to registration pending the outcome of this investigation. The registration was discontinued by Article 3 of this Regulation on imposition of definitive duties.
Significant distortions
State-induced distortions in the exporting country's economy that prevent domestic prices and costs from being used as the normal value. Article 2(6a) of the basic Regulation provides the framework for identifying significant distortions; the Commission's country report on China is the primary analytical document.
Single-mode optical fibre cable
The product concerned: cables made up of one or more individually sheathed single-mode fibres with protective casing. Carries data over long distances using a single ray of light per fibre, used in long-haul telecom networks and internet backbone infrastructure. CN code ex 8544 70 00 (TARIC 8544700010).
Target profit
The minimum profit margin that the Union industry could reasonably expect to achieve in the absence of dumped imports, used in calculating the injury elimination level. The injury elimination level equals cost of production plus target profit, compared with the landed import price.
ZTT group
The second sampled Chinese exporting producer group: Jiangsu Zhongtian Technology Co., Ltd. and Zhongtian Power Optical Cable Co., Ltd. Subject to a 19.7% anti-dumping duty (TARIC code C697), the lowest individual duty in this Regulation.

Procedural timeline

From complaint to definitive measures; key dates in the investigation

24 September 2020
Investigation initiated. Europacable filed a complaint on behalf of Union producers representing more than 25% of total Union production. The Notice of Initiation was published in the Official Journal on 24 September 2020. The investigation period was set as 1 July 2019 to 30 June 2020.
21 December 2020
Parallel anti-subsidy investigation initiated. The Commission opened a separate anti-subsidy (countervailing-duty) investigation on the same product, optical fibre cables originating in China, recognising that dumping and subsidisation may be investigated and addressed concurrently.
29 March 2021
Registration of imports (Regulation (EU) 2021/548). The Commission subjected imports of optical fibre cables from China to registration, allowing duties to be made retroactive if warranted. Article 3 of this definitive Regulation later discontinued the registration.
17 November 2021
This Regulation (EU) 2021/2011 adopted by the European Commission. Definitive anti-dumping duties fixed: FTT group 44.0% (TARIC C696), ZTT group 19.7% (TARIC C697), other cooperating 31.2% (Annex I), all others 44.0% (TARIC C999).
18 November 2021
Entry into force. Published in OJ L 410 on 18 November 2021. Article 4 confirms that the Regulation enters into force the day following publication, i.e. 19 November 2021.

Related canon entries

The first anti-dumping entry in the canon. Anti-dumping offsets unfair below-normal-value pricing; the related countervailing-duty cases below offset government subsidies.

Regulation (EU) 2024/2754: countervailing duties on Chinese battery electric vehicles anti-subsidy

The most high-profile anti-subsidy case in the canon. Three sampled Chinese BEV producers (BYD 17.0%, Geely 18.8%, SAIC 35.3%) and a residual rate of 35.3%. Threat of material injury (not actual injury). No lesser-duty rule applies. Contrasts with this Regulation in legal instrument, injury standard and duty-setting methodology.

Regulation (EU) 2025/500: countervailing duties on aluminium road wheels from Morocco anti-subsidy

The most recent trade-defence entry in the canon: cross-border subsidy doctrine, Chinese Belt-and-Road financing attributed to Morocco. DMA (CITIC Dicastal subsidiary) 31.4%, Hands 8 5.6%. No lesser-duty rule; duties set at full subsidy amounts. Ran in parallel with an existing anti-dumping Regulation (EU) 2023/99, a pattern also visible in this optical fibre cables case.

Read the Morocco ARW CVD deep-dive

Regulation (EU) 2020/776: countervailing duties on glass-fibre fabrics from China and Egypt anti-subsidy

The founding case of the cross-border subsidy doctrine in EU law, establishing that Chinese subsidies granted to production located in the China-Egypt Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone are attributable to Egypt as the country of export. Duty rates: CNBM Group 30.7%, Yuntianhua Group 17.0%, Egyptian CNBM operations 10.9%.

Read the GFF CVD deep-dive

Regulation (EU) 2022/433: countervailing duties on stainless steel from India and Indonesia anti-subsidy

The intermediate step in the cross-border subsidy doctrine's development: Chinese financing channelled to Indonesian producers through the Morowali Industrial Park found countervailable. Countervailing duties range from 4.3% to 21.4%. The case demonstrates that anti-dumping and anti-subsidy measures can apply cumulatively on the same product from the same country.
